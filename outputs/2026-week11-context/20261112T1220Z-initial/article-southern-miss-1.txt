Blake Anderson Weekly News Conference – The Louisiana Tech Game
11/9/2026 3:57:00 PM
|
Football
Media Availability
Share:
HATTIESBURG, Miss. –
Southern Miss football coach
Blake Anderson
addressed the media on Monday, Nov. 9, in the Duff Athletic Center ahead of the Golden Eagles' Saturday road contest at Louisiana Tech. Game time is set for 4 p.m. at Joe Aillet Stadium.
Southern Miss (3-6 overall, 2-3 Sun Belt) played for the second-straight season in Ruston against the Bulldogs (7-2, 5-0).
The game can be seen via ESPN+, while the Southern Miss Sports Network's radio pregame show begins at 2:30 p.m. with the Voice of the Golden Eagles,
John Cox
, analyst Lee Roberts and sideline reporter Jason Baker on the call.
Here is what Anderson had to say to the media Monday morning:
Opening Statement:
"It seems like it's been a while since the (James Madison) game, but the results are still the same. We played a really good first half, and then they just disappeared in that middle—those last minutes of the first half and the first five or six minutes of the third quarter. I felt like we made too many mistakes to stay in that game the way we wanted to. Beyond that, I told the team our immaturity really showed. We got frustrated, and we played even worse down the stretch as our frustration made it onto the field. They knew that was a really big game for us, given where we were after a couple of wins and what JMU is, and what a win against them would mean. I felt like we responded with frustration and disappointment during the game, and it spiraled late. We've got to learn from that and grow up. Our immaturity probably showed as much in the middle and back end of that game as it has all year. Against a really good team, if you make mistakes like we did, it doesn't take long for things to get out of hand. We fought really well in the first half, got the lead, and felt like the momentum was going in our favor. That last drive right before halftime didn't help. That first drive—and our first drive of the third quarter—set the tone. From that point, we never really gained our bearings or played like we were capable of. We'll work hard to learn from it and move forward against another really good opponent, LA Tech, who's having a great year. Sonny's doing a phenomenal job with them. We'll go on the road and try to play a better football game and see if we can find a way to win."
Q: "After watching the game, it felt like James Madison was the first team all season to really stop the run against you guys. Obviously, they have a really good front, but after watching the film what was happening with the run game?"
"Yeah, collectively, if you look at the totality, we struggled. We ran the ball well in that first stretch when we got some tempo going, but for the night, it wasn't what we were used to. They're really well built up front. They've done a great job recruiting and developing. There are a lot of guys we recruited, too, and we just didn't win in those areas. They're really well built. They've got a tremendous program and players, and financially they're able to provide depth. There wasn't a big drop-off between their ones and twos. It wasn't schematically—we run a lot of the same stuff, it was physically being able to move bodies consistently and win one-on-one. It was a struggle against a really good, solid team. To some degree, it was going to take patience. But once you get behind later in the second half, there's only so much running you can do. We started having to throw the ball a lot more than we'd like, so it's a combination of everything. They're just a really good defense. Even in the games they've lost, they haven't given up a lot of points. They turned the ball over; they didn't turn the ball over against us."
Q: "You spoke after the game a little bit about John's performance. It was on a short week too so you have to be encouraged by that, but what are you looking to see from him going into this first full week of preparation before a start?"
"I just need him to be the same guy. That's why I was comfortable putting him out there. He's prepared every week like he was a starter, and that's why I think it didn't really matter that it was a short week. He's so comfortable in the offense because of how he prepares. He's a competitor. You can see that in him. I think that was a big piece—we needed that spark. There's a competitive spark to him that, for whatever reason, we were kind of lacking. I thought we saw it early out of Liddy. Somewhere along the way, his confidence got broken. I don't know if it was a big hit or a decision he made, but he was struggling to find that spark. He'd tell you he was frustrated with it, too. But it's something we see in John every day. To be truthful, that's why I was really comfortable going with him when we did. I think it was the right time. Everybody can look back and say, 'Man, I'd love to have done it earlier,' but you really had to go through the process. I just need him to keep being him. He does everything we need him to do. He prepares the right way. Physically and mentally, he's doing all the things. He just needs to get experience and play. I was very pleased with how he played the other night. He did not put the ball in harm's way. He and the wideouts weren't on the same page all the time. There were a few times he thought they'd be somewhere and they weren't. That's something we've got to get past because that could turn into turnovers real quickly. But he did not put the ball in harm's way. He ran the ball well, made good decisions, and threw the ball well. He missed a couple that I think time and reps will help with. I thought he had a really good, solid outing. I just need him to keep getting better, growing, and learning."
Q: "You talked about how much he has grown from last year to now. Have you seen steady growth from him from the start of this season to now?"
"Absolutely. It was a true three-man race. I think everybody can see physically there are a lot of similarities between all three. Maybe now that you've seen him play a game, you can say that makes sense. Their numbers were almost identical. The other two had more experience. He had a couple more turnovers in fall camp than the other two, and I think he may have been pressing a little bit. From fall camp until now, he has continued to get better. He works out on his own and trains everywhere he can. When he's not getting enough reps, he stays after and throws with the wideouts. He prepares the right way and has known the plan every week. He has continued to develop and build confidence, even in me. I already had a lot, but he built confidence in Coach Cefalo, who didn't know him as well. It was a decision we both made. Ultimately, it's my choice, but when I said, 'Hey, I think it's time to move,' Coach Cefalo said, 'I get it completely. I trust him.' You've got to give the kid credit that he didn't go in the tank when we told him what his position was going to be early. He kept fighting and working. That's what you want every kid in the building to do at any position. If you're going to be the two or the three, you want them to attack it the way he did and be ready when their opportunity comes. I felt like he was ready for the moment the other night, against a very good team in a very tough game environment for your first start."
Q: How do you tackle the quarterback room moving forward, do you build around
John White
?
"I'm always very transparent and honest with him. I've told him the truth. We're going to recruit a guy to come in here and compete with you. You know that he knows. He knows we've got to. I mean, he knows we can't have him and just a bunch of freshmen. Like he knows that there's got to be. That's where I've been all morning. I was up there with a quarterback. He knows we're going to bring in a guy. It's your job to maintain and earn and solidify the job. I think all these reps over the next few weeks will be crucial to that. Will help him tremendously. They're reps you could not create any other way. He's earned them, but they will be helpful to him as well. I mean, these are not. I'm not giving him these reps. He earned the opportunity. He was the best option in the room Thursday night, in my opinion, without a doubt. So, he's earned that option, but these are great reps for him, and he knows in the spring he's got to battle to win the job. Like he's going to have to go head-to-head. There'll be five guys in the room, and there'll be three young guys, but there'll be five guys in the room. I don't know who that guy's going to be, but it's going to be somebody that's quality that makes the room better and makes him fight. Now I know John. He's going to fight. He's going to prepare. He's going to do everything he can to be the guy. You know, he'll get the first rep, first series with the ones at spring ball, and then it's going to be equal reps. And let's go. That's all you can ask at that position. That's all you can ask. And we'll bring a good, solid, quality guy to come in there and compete with him. I mean, it's my job to out recruit everybody in the building, at every position, including quarterback. So we're going to try to do that, and then I know him. He'll fight his tail off, and whoever's coming in better be ready for a fight because he's going to be right there every day doing exactly what he's been doing this this whole time."
Q: At this point in the season there isn't too much that can surprise you, are you anxious to see how your team plays with their backs against the wall?
"Well, I'm anxious to see how they respond to a rough night the other night. You know that's key. I mean, we're taking this one step at a time. Yeah, we all know mathematically, we win out then we go to a bowl, and that's great. But this is this has always been about one day at a time, one opponent at a time, one opportunity at a time. We didn't play our best. You know, we want to capture what that first half looked like. Find that consistently. Overcome what happened in the second half. Learn from it. Get better from it. Don't do that again. And just respond by going out and playing our best ball. And this this is a bigger process than this one season, and I've been truthful with them. I've told them all along, man. We just want to be our best, the best team we can be. So, trying to keep it in perspective, would it be cool to take this team to a bowl game? Absolutely, man. We'd celebrate like the Super Bowl. It'd be a huge task and huge to me, celebration of what these guys have done, but I don't want to wrap it all. If we don't get bowl eligible but doesn't mean this team failed. I mean, they just got to be their best. And have we grown? Yes. Are we better now than we were at the beginning? Yes. Are we building this thing to have something to carry into next year? Yes. Like they're doing all that. I don't want to label them success or failure. Now, do I want it for them? Absolutely, because I think they would it would be a great celebration of what they've done and how they've come in here and work. So, they're aware of their kids. They know they know it takes six to get there too. I mean, we don't talk about it a lot. They're aware of it, so we'll see how they handle Saturdays. All we can handle is can we go find a way to be a really good La Tech team? We'll worry about that later, if we can."
Q: It's rare in cases of
John White
in today's college football environment that players stay with a team, talk about the importance of having guys like him who stay with a program.
"I think it's you're right. It is rare, unfortunately. I think it's still needed, and I hope that more and more guys will take that approach, be a little bit more patient in this process. I know it's just been just mass turnover. There's always something better. There's always I hope more and more guys, especially in our building, will see and take that into account. Like watch what this guy's done. Working through hard things is still really important for these guys and for anybody. And not getting the immediate answer that you want, man. We're all dealing with this right now. We're not getting the immediate answer that we want out of this season. But this is bigger than one season. This is a process in working through hard and rebuilding and growing behind somebody else, waiting for my opportunity, being ready. That's still a very valuable lesson, and it makes you better. And he will be better because of it. He is already better because of it. And I'm hoping that a lot of the guys in our room, we have a chance to bring back a bunch of guys if they'll stay. We only have a handful of seniors. It's a really small class. That was intentional. I didn't want to build it up with a bunch of seniors. I wanted guys to have an opportunity to grow in the system and learn and develop still. That's the way we're going to win here: is to develop. But that means guys have to stay and fight through when they're not getting exactly what they want. And I get, in this society that's hard to come by. So, he is a rare story, but a success story all, all the same, and I'm hoping he will be an encouragement to some other guys that maybe don't have quite the role that they want to have or maybe think that just the grass is miraculously greener somewhere else. It's going to be a big emphasis for us to retain as much of this roster. That's why La Tech is really good right now. They retained a tremendous amount of that roster, and Sonny's done a great job developing guys that stayed, and that's who his best players are. And we need to take a very similar approach, and hopefully we can. And he's a good example of that."
