NDSU Notes
Hawaii Notes
Share:
THIS WEEK:
The North Dakota State football team makes its first visit to Hawaii this week when the Bison (7-2, 4-2 MW) take on the Hawaii Rainbow Warriors (3-6, 2-4 MW). Game time is 6:10 p.m. HST (10:10 p.m. CST) on Saturday, Nov. 14, at the Clarence T.C. Ching Complex (15,014) in Honolulu.
TELEVISION:
The game will be televised nationally on The CW with
Chris Fisher
(play-by-play),
Isaiah Stanback
(analyst) and
Bryan Walters
(sideline) describing the action. Streaming is available on the ESPN app with ESPN Unlimited.
RADIO:
Statewide network coverage on all 25 stations across the
Pioneer Seeds Bison Sports Network
begins 1 hour prior to game time including Bison 1660 and 107.9 The Fox in Fargo with
Sam Neidermann
(play-by-play),
Phil Hansen
(analyst) and
Noah Gindorff
(sideline) on the call. Streaming is available on
GoBison.com/allaccess
and the NDSU Athletics mobile app.
COACHES SHOW:
Fans are invited to join NDSU head coach
Tim Polasek
this Wednesday night from 6:30 to 8 p.m. for the
Bison Football Coaches Show
live from Holiday Inn Fargo with host
Sam Neidermann
and other NDSU coaches and players. Watch the show on MW+ or listen on Bison 1660 in Fargo, KCJB-AM 910 in Minot, KSJB-AM 600 in Jamestown, KYCR-AM 1440 in Minneapolis, GoBison.com and the NDSU Athletics mobile app.
MW+ STREAMING:
Visit
GoBison.com/MW+
and subscribe to MW+ to watch live Bison press conferences, weekly episodes of The Bison Football Show, and other featured content and live games from around the Mountain West Conference.
THE SERIES:
This is the first meeting between North Dakota State and Hawaii.
ANOTHER NEW STATE
: Hawaii is one of three states the Bison football program will make its first visit to this year as a new member of the Mountain West Conference. It is the 36th state the Bison have played in after crossing off Nevada (UNLV) and New Mexico from the list earlier this fall.
BISON TOP UTEP:
North Dakota State had a bye in the Mountain West Conference schedule last week after beating UTEP 31-7 in Fargo on Halloween.
Nathan Hayes
passed for 240 yards,
Jackson Williams
made a career-high 10 receptions for 111 yards, linebacker
Donovan Woolen
made a team-high 11 tackles with 2.5 tackles for loss including a sack, and the Bison forced three turnovers highlighted by cornerback
Marion Moore
's league-leading fifth interception of the year and 46-yard fumble recovery touchdown.
ROAD WARRIORS:
Hawaii is coming off back-to-back road wins for the first time since 2007 after beating Northern Illinois 56-21 and UTEP 17-14. Quarterback
Micah Alejado
, the Mountain West preseason offensive player of the year, had his second straight 300-yard passing game at UTEP going 26 of 40 for 303 yards and a touchdown. Alejado leads all MW passers with 2,489 yards and 276.6 yards per game this season and ranks third in FBS averaging 25.3 completions per game. Former record-setting Hawaii quarterback
Timmy Chang
is in his fifth season as the head coach with a 25-35 career record.
TOUCHDOWN TARGETS:
North Dakota State has two of the Mountain West's top five touchdown targets this season. Wide receiver
Mekhi Collins
and tight end
Reis Kessel
are tied for fourth in the MW with four touchdown receptions each. Kessel had career highs of six catches and 85 yards against UTEP including a 3-yard TD late in the third quarter to give the Bison a two-score lead. Kessel is NDSU's second-leading receiver this year with 27 catches and 345 yards, already surpassing last year's marks of 15 catches and 335 yards, the most receiving yards by a Bison tight end since
Jerimiah
Wurzbacher
's 388 yards in 2008.
DEFENSIVE DISRUPTORS:
Two NDSU players had historic days on defense at New Mexico. Linebacker
Gavin Sell
accounted for five of NDSU's eight tackles for loss, all solo stops, including one sack. Sell's 5.0 TFLs were the most by a Bison player since 1984 when defensive tackle
Flint Fleming
had 6.0 TFLs in a win at South Dakota. NDSU defensive tackle
Logan Larson
made three tackles (one for a loss of 6 yards and two for 0 yards) plus a career-high three pass breakups at New Mexico. It was just the fifth time in school history a Bison defensive lineman has recorded three or more PBUs in a single game, joining defensive ends
Toby Anene
(2025),
Joe Lardinois
(2006) and
Alvin Robinson
(2004). Defensive tackle
Steve Krause
's five PBUs in the 1982 season-opening win against Northern Michigan is tied with three others for the NDSU school record.
BEAR BRYANT WATCH LIST:
North Dakota State's
Tim Polasek
was named to the midseason watch list for the Bear Bryant National Coach of the Year Award. Polasek has a 33-5 overall record including a 7-2 mark this year, his third as the Bison head coach. Former Bison defensive coordinator and safeties coach
David Braun
(Northwestern) and safeties coach
Jake Dickert
(Wake Forest) are also among the 28 coaches under consideration along with Mountain West coaches
Jason Eck
(New Mexico) and
Troy Calhoun
(Air Force).
RIMINGTON TROPHY WATCH LIST:
North Dakota State's
Gage Schmidt
is on the watch list for the Rimington Trophy, presented annually to the most outstanding center in NCAA FBS football. This is Schmidt's first year as the Bison starter after appearing in 12 games as a backup last season. NDSU had four Rimington Award winners at the FCS level.
MIDSEASON ALL-AMERICAN:
North Dakota State left guard
Griffin Empey
was named to the Midseason All-America Team published by The Sporting News, one of five outlets used to determine consensus All-American status at the FBS level. Empey was one of five offensive linemen selected with Virginia tackle Monroe Mills, Houston center Anthony Boswell, Iowa guard Kade Pieper and Indiana tackle Carter Smith.
DEFENSIVE RANKINGS:
North Dakota State is ranked second in the FBS this week for scoring defense (11.9 ppg), total defense (248.8 ypg), passing defense (124.4 ypg) and red zone defense (.625), as well as third in pass interceptions (14), defensive touchdowns (4) and first downs allowed (126). The Bison are sixth in turnovers gained (18) and tied for eighth in turnover margin (+9).
TOP 10 NATIONALLY:
NDSU's
Marion Moore
is tied for second nationally with five interceptions, the most by a Bison cornerback since current defensive analyst
Marcus Williams
had seven picks twice in the 2011 and 2012 seasons.
DEFENSIVE SCORING:
North Dakota State's four defensive touchdowns rank third in FBS behind Alabama (5) and Texas Tech (5). The Bison scored on a fumble recovery in the end zone by defensive end
Blake Schiltz
, a 37-yard interception return and 46-yard fumble return by cornerback
Marion Moore
, and a 24-yard fumble recovery by safety
Darius Givance
. The 100-yard missed field goal return touchdown by
Jackson Williams
vs. Nevada counts as all-purpose yardage but is not included in NCAA rankings.
MIDDLE EIGHT:
North Dakota State has been excellent in the "middle eight" minutes under head coach
Tim Polasek
outscoring opponents 289-72 in the final 4:00 of the first half and first 4:00 of the second half. Most of that scoring has come at the end of the first half, where the Bison have a 209-63 advantage. NDSU has scored in the middle eight in 29 of 38 games since the beginning of the 2024 season.
TWO-MINUTE SUCCESS:
The Bison scored in the two-minute drill before halftime each of the first three weeks of the season.
— vs. Jacksonville State...Went 88 yards in 8 plays and 1:24...Targeted seven different receivers completing 6 of 7 attempts including a 44-yard TD pass to
Jordan McCants
for a 17-7 lead.
— vs. Fordham...Went 80 yards in 9 plays and 1:24...Completed 6 of 6 passes for four first downs before
Chris Kiel
's 8-yard TD catch for a 17-0 lead.
— at Air Force...Went 65 yards in 6 plays and 1:19...Completed 3 of 4 passes highlighted by a 51-yard play to
Jackson Williams
at the 1 before a
Myles Mitchell
1-yard TD run for a 28-13 lead.
PLAYERS OF THE WEEK:
NDSU swept all four Old Trapper Mountain West player of the week awards in Week Zero after a 33-7 win over Jacksonville State. Nine NDSU players have combined for 14 MW player of the week honors this year:
— QB
Nathan Hayes
(Offense 8/31, 9/14)...Completed 14 of 22 passes for 160 yards and two touchdowns against Jacksonville State...Passed for 228 yards and two TDs with a 17-yard rushing TD at Air Force.
— LB
Donovan Woolen
(Defense 8/31, 10/5)...Game-high 10 tackles against Jacksonville State...Team-high nine tackles with an interception, solo sack, assisted TFL and third-and-goal stop in the shutout of Wyoming.
— K
Drew Klein
(Special Teams 8/31, 9/14)...Scored 15 points with field goals of 34, 44, 42 and 48 yards plus three PAT kicks against Jacksonville State...School-record 56-yard field goal and five PATs at Air Force.
— DE
Blake Schiltz
(Freshman 8/31)...Recovered a fumble for a touchdown and added a sack in his first collegiate game against Jacksonville State.
— RB
Myles Mitchell
(Freshman 9/14)...Team-high 15 carries, 61 yards and two touchdowns at Air Force, including a 31-yard TD on the game's opening drive.
— WR/RS
Jackson Williams
(Offense 9/21; Special Teams 10/19)...Career-high seven receptions and 134 yards with one TD at Sacramento State...Scored on a 33-yard pass to cap NDSU's game-opening drive and had four receptions of 20-plus yards...Career-high 251 all-purpose yards against Nevada including a 100-yard missed field goal return touchdown and team-high five receptions for 58 yards.
— S
Darius Givance
(Defense 9/21)...Career-high seven tackles at Sacramento State including six solo stops...Scored his first collegiate TD on a 24-yard fumble recovery 3:11 into the game.
— LB
Gavin Sell
(Freshman 9/21)...Five tackles including a forced fumble on Sacramento State's first drive that resulted in an NDSU touchdown and 14-0 lead.
— CB
Marion Moore
(Freshman 10/19, 11/2)...Two interceptions and five tackles including two third-down stops in the win over Nevada...Two fourth-quarter takeaways against UTEP with a 22-yard interception return and 46-yard fumble recovery touchdown.
FBS DEBUTS:
Ten former FCS teams have reclassified to FBS in the past 10 years, and only North Dakota State and James Madison started with five straight victories. The Dukes were 5-0 in 2022 before finishing 8-3 overall and 6-2 in the Sun Belt Conference.
PRESEASON PICKS:
NDSU return specialist
Jackson Williams
was named Mountain West Preseason Special Teams Player of the Year and claimed two spots on the preseason team as kick returner and punt returner. Other NDSU selections were offensive lineman
Griffin Empey
, defensive tackle
Keenan Wilson
and safety
Darius Givance
.
BEDNARIK AWARD:
NDSU defensive tackle
Keenan Wilson
was named to the preseason watch list for the Bednarik Award, presented annually to the most outstanding defensive player in college football since 1994. Wilson was an FCS All-American last year starting all 13 games and posting 8.0 tackles for loss and 4.0 sacks.
PAUL HORNUNG AWARD:
NDSU return specialist
Jackson Williams
was named to the preseason watch list for the 17th annual Paul Hornung Award, presented to the most versatile player in major college football. Williams was one of three players in all of Division I last season to have both a kick return touchdown and a punt return touchdown. He totaled 1,106 all-purpose yards.
MAXWELL AWARD:
NDSU running back
DJ Scott
made the preseason watch list for the 90th Maxwell Award, presented annually to the most outstanding player in college football. Scott headlines the Bison backfield this year after rushing for 502 yards and six touchdowns in 2025.
FCS ERA BY THE NUMBERS:
North Dakota State competed in the Football Championship Subdivision for 22 seasons from 2004 through 2025, spending the past 18 years in the Missouri Valley Football Conference after four years in the Great West Football Conference while reclassifying from Division II. Here are the final numbers from NDSU's FCS era:
— Overall record: 251-49 (.837)
— Record from 2011-2025: 198-22 (.900)
— 13 conference championships (12 MVFC, 1 GWFC)
— 10 national championships (FCS record)
— 16 consecutive postseason appearances
— 51-6 in playoffs (FCS record for wins)
— 38-2 in playoffs at home
— 39-game winning streak (FCS record)
— 33-game winning streak (2nd in FCS)
— 9-5 record vs. FBS opponents
— 26 weeks of AP Top 25 votes
— 21 NFL draft picks (14 in past 8 years)
18 NATIONAL CHAMPIONSHIPS:
North Dakota State has won 18 football national championships. NDSU claimed three College Division national championships in 1965, 1968 and 1969 via the national polls, five Division II playoff titles in 1983, 1985, 1986, 1988 and 1990, and was the first team in college football history to win five straight national titles with FCS crowns in 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014 and 2015 before winning again in 2017, 2018, 2019, 2021 and 2024. The Bison lost to the eventual national champion in the 2010 and 2020 quarterfinals and 2016 semifinals.
CONFERENCE CHAMPS:
NDSU won 12 conference championships in 18 years of Missouri Valley Football Conference membership, including the 2024 and 2025 titles. Overall, the Bison have 39 conference championships counting 26 at the Division II level in the North Central Conference and its first FCS title in 2006 as a member of the Great West Football Conference.
NON-CONFERENCE SUCCESS:
North Dakota State is 94-6 against non-conference opponents since the beginning of its first FCS national championship season in 2011. Two losses were to FBS opponents (31-28 at Arizona in 2021 and 31-26 at Colorado in 2024) and two were in the playoffs to the eventual national champion (27-17 to James Madison in 2016 and 24-20 at Sam Houston State in 2020-21). Montana's 31-29 double-overtime win in the 2023 FCS semifinals and 38-35 victory in the 2015 FCS Kickoff are NDSU's only other non-conference losses the past 13 years.
BISON AT HOME:
The Bison have a 210-30 all-time record in the Fargodome since 1993, including 36-5 against FCS Top 10 ranked teams and 78-1 against FCS non-conference teams. North Dakota State had a 38-2 record in the Fargodome during the FCS playoffs and the Bison's 32-game home winning streak September 2017 through April 2021 is fifth longest in FCS history.
