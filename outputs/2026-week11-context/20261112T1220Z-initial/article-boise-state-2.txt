Family of Jeremy Ioane to Raise Blue Chaos Flag Saturday
11/11/2026 4:30:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
BOISE, Idaho -
The family of former Boise State safety Jeremy Ioane will raise the Blue Chaos flag ahead of Saturday's matchup with Oregon State on Saturday.
Ioane, who played for the Broncos from 2010-14, tragically passed away earlier this year in April. His parents, Matthew and Doris, will join his sister Jasmine and brother Jordan in raising the flag in his honor.
"It's truly an incredible honor and such an emotional moment for our family," Jasmine said. "To be able to raise the Blue Chaos flag in Jeremy's memory means more than words can express. Boise State was such a special part of his life, and knowing that his legacy continues to live on through Bronco Nation means so much to us. I know Jeremy would be so proud, and we're forever grateful to be part of such a special moment."
A native of Honolulu, Hawai'i, Ioane was a part of four bowl-champion teams, including the 2014 Fiesta Bowl. After redshirting in 2010, he went on to be named All-Mountain West Honorable Mention in 2012, All-Mountain West Second Team in 2013, and was a preseason Jim Thorpe Award candidate in 2014. As a Bronco, Ioane played in 41 career games, earning 29 starts. He tallied 146 tackles and five interceptions.
"Jeremy's legacy as a Bronco goes far beyond football," Jasmine said. "He was just an island boy from Hawai'i with big dreams of making a name not only for himself, but also paving the way for other local boys back home. He'll be remembered not just for his athleticism and talent on the field, but for his strength, resilience and heart. No matter what life threw his way, he never gave up. He truly embodied what it meant to be a Bronco, and we hope his story continues to inspire others to dream big, stay humble, and never give up on themselves."
Ioane battled through kidney disease during his playing days at Boise State, undergoing dialysis treatment in his final year as a Bronco. He started the first three games of his senior campaign in 2014, but was forced to a reserve role the remainder of the year due to the disease. He still managed to record two interceptions and 11 tackles his senior season.
His journey was shared through the message and hashtag of #10aneStrong, as his No. 10 jersey and play on the field was fondly remembered by so many of Bronco Nation. During his senior year in 2014, #10aneStrong shirts were worn by Boise State fans to show their support for Ioane in his final season. Following his playing career, Ioane underwent a successful kidney transplant. Jasmine, his twin sister, was his donor.
"Bronco Nation's support meant absolutely everything to Jeremy," Jasmine said. "The love and support he received from Bronco Nation was truly indescribable, especially during some of the most difficult moments of his life. Knowing he had an entire community standing behind him meant more than words could ever express. Our family will forever be grateful for the unwavering love and support Bronco Nation showed our son and brother."
Kickoff for the matchup against the Beavers is scheduled for 4 p.m. MT. The game will be televised nationally on USA Sports.
