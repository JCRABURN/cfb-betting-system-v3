Line of Trust
Harry S. Truman supposedly said, “It’s amazing what you can accomplish if you do not care who gets the credit.”
That sounds a lot like Manny Diaz’s description of his 2026 defensive line.
Just playing better team football than we were a year ago. A lot of unselfishness on that side of the ball. When it’s done well, our tackle numbers, our TFL numbers, our sack numbers are all spread amongst a large group of people because they understand that if everyone does their jobs, there’s plays for everyone to make.
~ Duke head coach Manny Diaz
Defensive end Bryce Davis provides a player’s perspective, saying after the Tulane opener that “the emphasis is on not being about yourself, being about the whole defense. When you do that, the play is going to come to you. The stats don’t concern us. Just run after the ball and make people feel us.”
Duke defensive end Bryce Davis makes a move towards the quarterback.
Anyone watching Duke’s defense on the field, on the sidelines, on the social media shared by Duke, can see this is not lip service. These players celebrate their teammates’ successes.
Another word crops up.
The word is trust. Trust from coach to coach, from coach to player, from player to player. If you have guys you can trust, you can go out and do your job because you’re not worried about your teammate.
~ Duke defensive ends coach Trent Harris
Kobe Smith adds that “even off the field, I consider everyone on the D-line my brothers. Having that connection with everyone on the D-line, it’s just so much easier to go out there and trust everyone.”
But being unselfish and trusting isn’t all that’s needed to win at the Power 4 level. You still have to have talent.
Let’s rewind back to the end of the 2025 Duke season.
From his very first press conference at Duke, Diaz has insisted that Duke will be a developmental program. Duke proved they could walk that walk after losing all four starting defensive linemen from last season’s ACC champions: ends Wesley Williams and VJ Anthony and tackles Aaron Hall and Josiah Green.
This was no ordinary quartet. Hall currently plays for the Carolina Panthers. Williams was drafted by the Jacksonville Jaguars and is fighting through some injuries. Anthony has been on several NFL practice squads. He led Duke with 7.5 sacks last season. Green went through fall camp with Tampa Bay.
Faced with that talent drain, most college teams would have hit the portal. Hard.
But that developmental pipeline is working for Duke.
We want to recruit high-school players, develop them and retain them and that helps us not have to go into the portal. Obviously, you have to at times and fix some issues when guys leave or whatever. But if you trust your evaluation out of high school, you trust the development Coach Feeley and our staff have done, then the second- and third-year guys should start producing at a high level.
~ Duke defensive coordinator Jonathan Patke
“We knew we were going to have to push some younger guys into the games and they’ve played well. I think we’re playing with more depth than we have in years past. A year ago we felt like we couldn’t take those guys off the field. Now, we can roll six D-tackles, six D-ends and they’re all really engaged, really focused and you’re getting the best out of them because they’re all getting starting reps. When you play a lot of guys, they’re better playing 20 snaps than 60 snaps. Those guys stay hungry that way.”
“Those guys” are defensive ends Davis, Tyshon Reed, Kevin O’Connor, Kobe Smith, Semaj Turner and Sampson Onuoha and tackles David Anderson, Preston Watson, Julius Columbus, Owen Wafle and Dakota Quinonez.
Duke defensive end Kevin O'Connor leads the Blue Devils in sacks in ACC games with 4.5.
Wafle (Penn State) and Quinonez (Dartmouth) are transfers. Both are in the rotation but neither starts. The other nine players are all returnees — all recruited, signed and developed by Duke. Not a single transfer at defensive end.
Going into this year, we felt like we have guys who can roll in there. We feel like anybody who can go into the game can up the level of play for us.
~ Duke defensive tackles coach Alex Devine
Duke defensive tackles coach Alex Devine talks to Blue Devil defensive tackles at Illinois.
There is one new face in the defensive ends room. The Baltimore Ravens hired Duke’s defensive end coach Harland Bower in February. Duke replaced him with Harris, who played collegiately at Miami when Diaz was a defensive coach. Harris went on to a five-year career in the NFL. Duke hired him out of Illinois.
“Trent Harris stepped in and did a fantastic job,” Diaz said over the summer.
Harris liked what he saw when he arrived at Duke.
“Coach Bower did a great job of building a culture and a standard in that room. The guys are hungry to get better and they want to perfect everything. They’ve done a great job of taking the fundamentals and the techniques of individuals in practice and translating that into games.”
Duke defensive ends coach Trent Harris points in celebration during a home 2026 football game.
Duke’s depth and talent and hunger showed up right away, as Duke defeated Tulane 17-3 in the season opener. Davis had six tackles, two sacks and three tackles for loss.
“We play hard, we’re physical, we play fast,” he said. “I think this defense fits everybody, defensive ends, defensive tackles, perfectly. That allows a lot of freedom.”
Third-year Preston Watson added four tackles, one for a loss. The defensive line combined for five TFLs.
Davis added five tackles in a road win at Illinois. Reed had a tackle for loss.
Duke came back home and opened its ACC schedule with a 35-7 win over Stanford.
Duke defensive tackle Tyshon Reed Jr. runs towards the backfield at Virginia in the Blue Devils' 19-16 victory.
Reed and O’Connor each had two sacks and O’Connor forced a fumble recovered by Duke linebacker Kendall Johnson on a play where he and Reed collided and knocked each other’s wind out for a couple of harrowing moments.
The message is stay connected. We want to pride ourselves on being the most connected team. If we’re on the same page, we can play fast, play physical.
~ Duke defensive end Kevin O'Connor
Connected. Another word that describes Duke’s success.
Everybody feasted in a 62-7 win over William and Mary. No fewer than 27 Blue Devils made a tackle.
Second-year tackle Columbus had four of those. The Gainesville, Georgia native barely saw the field last season. He might be Duke’s next D-line star.
Devine says Columbus has earned his way into a veteran rotation.
Duke defensive tackle Julius Columbus and defensive tackles coach Alex Devine.
I fell in love with Julius as a high school recruit because of his style of play and because he fits what we want to do up front — play vertical, attacking, being able to move from gap to gap, from B gap to A gap. He has all those attributes. Probably the best part of him to come out this year is his ability to play free, not worrying about making a play for himself, not worried about if something goes wrong on this play, being judged for it. His ability to do that allows him to play fast, to attack and use what the good Lord has given him.
~ Duke defensive tackles coach Alex Devine
There was some slippage in Duke’s 28-25 loss at Georgia Tech. The Yellow Jackets had several explosives, none more damaging to Duke’s cause than Trelain Maddox’s 60-yard TD run in the third period.
Defensive end Kobe Smith said “the small details” were to blame.
Despite those few plays, Duke ranked fifth in the ACC in rushing yards per game after six games, allowing only 92 yards per game. That’s more than a 50 yards per game improvement over last year.
Nothing the defensive line does exists in isolation.
A defense is a living organism. Eleven guys have to become one, no matter where the ball is or goes. They’re playing much better team ball than a year ago. We’ve got great leadership on that side of the ball.
~ Duke head coach Manny Diaz
Duke's defense celebrates a play made by Owen Wafle (91) at Virginia during the Blue Devils' 19-16 victory.
Cornerback Evan Smith gives an example of how his unit is impacted by the defensive line.
“We’ve got a lot of things to worry about on the back end and if you’re able to take away that first read, the quarterback doesn’t have time to get that second read. It eases our job up so much to have not much to worry about. You can always lean on it. If you have four D-linemen ready to hunt the football, it makes everyone’s job easier.”
Coaches are always looking toward the next practice, the next game, the next month. But also the next season. Devine says Duke knows what it’s looking for in the high school ranks and the players know what Duke is looking for.
“In recruiting, we talk about evidence. The fact that we play with a front that’s violent and physical speaks for itself. In recruiting, what’s easy is identifying the type of person for our scheme, the type of person for Duke. We want players who want to do things the right way, want to be held accountable.”
Harris says recruits notice.
The culture that Coach Diaz has set here allows you to play freely. They’re always together. It starts in practice. They’re always together. That connection makes them more powerful. It’s an attractive message.
~ Duke defensive ends coach Trent Harris
Unselfishness. Trust. Connection. Culture. Development. A recipe for success on the defensive line.
