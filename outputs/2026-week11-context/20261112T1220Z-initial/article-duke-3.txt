Duke Offensive Line a Moore Award Semifinalist
11/11/2026 5:47:00 PM
|
Football
Watch the Announcement
Share:
DURHAM, N.C. –
The Duke football offense line was named a 2026 semifinalist for the Joe Moore Award, presented annually to the most outstanding offensive line unit in college football.
The Blue Devil offensive line is tied for second nationally in fewest sacks allowed (4.0) and ranks tied for third in sack yards lost (27). Duke has allowed just four sacks, one each at Illinois, with an intentional grounding against North Carolina, at Virginia and at NC State. In six conference games, Duke has yielded just 3.0 sacks, tied for the third fewest nationally.
Duke's group also ranks tied for 15th nationally and second in the ACC in fewest tackles for loss allowed (33.0) and is sixth in the country in fewest yards lost on TFLs (84).
The unit is providing space for
Maxwell Award
and
Walter Camp Player of the Year
semifinalist
Nate Sheppard
to rush for 1,189 rushing yards (6.23 yards/carry) and 17 touchdowns with 32 receptions for 306 yards (9.56) and four scores. With the offensive line's guidance, Sheppard enters the weekend leading all FBS student-athletes in points per game (14.00), total points scored (126), total TDs scored (21) and plays from scrimmage of 10+ yards (49), while ranking second in rushing yards per game (132.11) and rushing yards, third in all-purpose yards per game (166.11) and fourth in rushing TDs.
Duke (7-2, 4-2 ACC) travels to No. 5 Miami (8-1, 6-0 ACC) Saturday for a 12 p.m. tilt on ABC.
About the Joe Moore Award
The Joe Moore Award is named after Joe Moore, widely regarded as one of the best offensive line coaches in college football history, most notably for his work at Notre Dame and the University of Pittsburgh. Over 18 seasons, Coach Moore sent 52 players on to the NFL, including Bill Fralic, Mark May, Russ Grimm, Jimbo Covert and others.
The Joe Moore Award trophy, crafted by legendary sports sculptor Jerry McKenna, is the largest trophy in college football, standing at a height of 6 feet and weighing in at 800 pounds. The perpetual trophy is made available for display by the winning university until the conclusion of the following college football season.
