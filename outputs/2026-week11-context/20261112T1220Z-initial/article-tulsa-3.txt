BACK ON THE ROAD TO OPEN NOVEMBER
• Tulsa returns from its open week to face Tulane on Saturday, Nov. 7, at Yulman Stadium in New Orleans. Kickoff is scheduled for 11 a.m. CT on ESPNU.
• The Golden Hurricane enters November at 4-4 overall and 1-3 in American Conference play. Tulsa is 2-2 on the road this season, with victories at Sam Houston and Rice.
• Saturday marks Tulsa's first trip to New Orleans since Nov. 11, 2023, when the Golden Hurricane fell 24-22 to Tulane.
• Tulsa owns a 5-5 record against Tulane in games played in New Orleans, including a 2-3 mark at Yulman Stadium.
• Tulsa's five victories in New Orleans came in 2007, 2009, 2011, 2015 and 2021.
• Tulsa's most recent victory in New Orleans was a 20-13 overtime win on Nov. 13, 2021. Shamari Brooks scored on a 15-yard run in overtime before the defense delivered a fourth-down stop to secure the victory.
• The last two meetings in New Orleans have been decided by a combined nine points, with Tulsa winning in overtime in 2021 before the two-point setback in 2023.
• Saturday will be the 22nd meeting between the programs. Tulsa leads the all-time series 13-8 and will look to snap Tulane's three-game winning streak in the series.
• The matchup begins Tulsa's final four-game stretch of the regular season. The Golden Hurricane returns home to host Florida Atlantic and Charlotte before closing the regular season at UTSA.
THOMPSON'S COMEBACK EARNS NATIONAL RECOGNITION
• Graduate linebacker
Chris Thompson Jr.
was named to the 38-player Comeback Player of the Year Award Mid-Season Watch List on Nov. 2, adding national recognition to his return to the field.
•Thompson is one of three American Conference players on the list, joining North Texas running back Jahiem White and Tulane linebacker Dickson Agu.
• After missing the entire 2025 season because of injury, Thompson has returned to lead Tulsa with 81 tackles through eight games, averaging 10.1 stops per contest.
• Thompson leads the American Conference in total tackles and tackles per game. He ranks seventh nationally in total tackles and fifth in tackles per game.
• His production has been consistent throughout the season, with at least seven tackles in every game and four double-digit performances.
• Thompson recorded a season-high 15 tackles against Army, surpassing the 14 stops he collected against North Texas. He also reached double figures against Oklahoma State and Rice, recording 10 tackles in each contest.
• Against Army, Thompson added two tackles for loss and his first sack of the season. He enters the Tulane game with 3.5 tackles for loss, one sack, one fumble recovery and one pass breakup.
• Thompson made an immediate impact in his return, earning American Conference Defensive Player of the Week honors following Tulsa's season-opening victory over Oklahoma State.
• His performance against the Cowboys included 10 tackles, a fumble recovery and contributions to two critical fourth-down stops in the Golden Hurricane's 24-10 victory.
• A team captain in both 2025 and 2026, Thompson has paired his return to competition with a continued leadership role for the Golden Hurricane.
• Saturday's matchup at Tulane features two players on the award's Mid-Season Watch List in Thompson and Agu, who rank first and second in the American Conference in tackles per game.
• Presented by College Sports Communicators in association with The Associated Press and Fiesta Sports Foundation, the award recognizes college football student-athletes who overcome injury, illness or other circumstances. Three players will be honored as award winners following the season.
ROSS EXTENDS HIS STREAK TO 27
• Wide receiver
Javon Ross
enters the Tulane game with a reception in 27 consecutive games, spanning his careers at Bethune-Cookman and Tulsa.
• Based on the latest national streak list, Ross is tied for the 10th-longest active reception streak, alongside UTSA's David Amador II and Oklahoma State's Miles Coleman.
• Ross is one of only 12 players nationally with an active streak of at least 27 games and one of three American Conference players on the list, joining Florida Atlantic's Easton Messer and Amador.
• He extended his streak against Army with five receptions for 84 yards and a touchdown, adding an eight-yard rushing score to finish with 92 yards from scrimmage.
•Ross has caught a pass in all eight games with the Golden Hurricane, totaling 28 receptions for 327 yards and three receiving touchdowns.
• Over his last three games, Ross has collected 15 receptions for 205 yards, highlighted by season highs of seven catches and 90 yards at Navy.
• He has caught a touchdown pass in consecutive games, scoring on an 11-yard reception at Rice before adding a 17-yard touchdown against Army.
• Ross will look to extend his streak to 28 consecutive games when Tulsa visits Tulane on Saturday.
• His streak is tied for 10th nationally with UTSA's David Amador II with 25 or more consecutive games with a reception.

OFFENSE BUILDING MOMENTUM IN CONFERENCE PLAY
•Tulsa has scored 143 points in four American Conference games, averaging 35.8 points per contest.
• The Golden Hurricane has scored at least 35 points in three of its four conference games, producing 44 against North Texas, 38 at Rice and 35 against Army.
• Tulsa's scoring average in conference play is 12 points higher than its 23.8-point average during the four-game nonconference schedule.
•
Baylor Hayes
has helped drive that production, passing for at least 200 yards in all four conference games while totaling 990 passing yards and nine touchdown passes.
• Hayes has thrown multiple touchdown passes in three of those four contests, including three apiece against North Texas and Rice.
•
Oran Singleton Jr.
has provided a consistent target during conference play, collecting 28 receptions for 333 yards over the four-game stretch.
• Tulsa has demonstrated its ability to respond to deficits, scoring the final 31 points in its 38-17 victory at Rice before producing 21 unanswered points against Army.
• Against the Black Knights, Tulsa erased a 28-14 third-quarter deficit to take a 35-28 lead with 13:25 remaining before falling 38-35.
• The Golden Hurricane finished the Army game with 410 yards of total offense, combining 262 passing yards with 148 rushing yards. Four different players scored Tulsa's five touchdowns.
• Tulsa's three American Conference losses have been decided by a combined 12 points, including a one-point overtime setback against North Texas and a three-point loss to Army.
HAYES FINDS HIS RHYTHM IN CONFERENCE PLAY
• Quarterback
Baylor Hayes
has passed for at least 200 yards in each of Tulsa's four American Conference games, totaling 990 passing yards and nine touchdown passes against North Texas, Navy, Rice and Army.
• Hayes has completed 88-of-139 passes during conference play, connecting on 63.3 percent of his attempts while averaging 247.5 passing yards per game.
• He returned from a two-game absence against North Texas and accounted for all five of Tulsa's touchdowns in regulation, throwing three touchdown passes and adding two rushing scores.
• Hayes followed that performance by completing 20-of-35 passes for a season-high 268 yards and one touchdown without an interception at Navy.
• At Rice, Hayes completed 20-of-27 passes for 249 yards and three touchdowns without an interception, helping Tulsa score the game's final 31 points in a 38-17 victory.
• His 74.1-percent completion rate against Rice remains his best this season, while his three touchdown passes matched his career high.
• Hayes completed 23-of-34 passes for 262 yards and two touchdowns against Army, marking his third consecutive game with at least 20 completions.
• He has thrown multiple touchdown passes in three of Tulsa's four conference games and accounted for 11 touchdowns during that stretch, combining nine passing scores with two rushing touchdowns.
• Hayes has helped Tulsa average 35.8 points in American Conference play, with the Golden Hurricane scoring at least 35 points in three of its four league games.
• His connection with Tulsa's receivers has fueled that production.
Oran Singleton Jr.
has collected 28 receptions for 333 yards during conference play, while Hayes' touchdown targets have included
Javon Ross
,
David Wells Jr.
, Singleton and
Jimmy Calloway
.
• Against Army, Hayes connected with Calloway for a 13-yard touchdown on third-and-13, completing a stretch of 21 unanswered Tulsa points and giving the Golden Hurricane a 35-28 fourth-quarter lead.
• Hayes enters the Tulane game with 1,343 passing yards, 10 touchdown passes and two interceptions in six appearances. He has topped 200 passing yards five times this season.
TURNOVERS TILT TOWARD TULSA
• Tulsa enters the Tulane game leading the American Conference in turnover margin at plus-10 overall and plus-1.25 per game.
• The Golden Hurricane has generated 15 takeaways—seven interceptions and eight fumble recoveries—while committing only five turnovers through eight games.
• Tulsa leads the conference in fumble recoveries and ranks second in total takeaways behind Memphis' 18.
• The defense has already surpassed its entire 2025 total of 11 takeaways by four, with four regular-season games remaining.
• Tulsa has doubled last season's four fumble recoveries and matched its seven interceptions from the 2025 campaign.
• Ball security has contributed to the improvement. Tulsa has committed two interceptions and lost three fumbles this season after finishing 2025 with 20 giveaways.
• The Golden Hurricane owns a plus-10 turnover margin after finishing last season at minus-nine, a 19-turnover improvement in its overall differential.
• Tulsa forced at least one turnover in each of its first seven games, highlighted by four-takeaway performances against Oklahoma State and Navy.
• Three of Tulsa's four takeaways at Navy came inside its own red zone, ending potential scoring drives.
Dax Collins
forced and recovered a fumble at Tulsa's six-yard line during that contest.
• The Golden Hurricane produced eight takeaways without committing a turnover during its three-game stretch against North Texas, Navy and Rice.
• Saturday's matchup presents a contrast in turnover production. Tulane has generated 10 takeaways while committing 13 turnovers, giving the Green Wave a minus-three margin.
• Tulsa's plus-10 margin and Tulane's minus-three mark create a 13-turnover difference between the teams' season differentials heading into Saturday.
ROSS AND SINGLETON PROVIDE CONSISTENCY
• Wide receivers
Oran Singleton Jr.
and
Javon Ross
have combined for 71 receptions, 862 receiving yards and seven touchdown catches through Tulsa's first eight games.
• Singleton leads the Golden Hurricane with 43 receptions, 535 receiving yards and four touchdown catches, while Ross has contributed 28 catches for 327 yards and three receiving scores.
• Singleton has recorded at least five receptions in five consecutive games, providing a dependable target throughout Tulsa's American Conference schedule.
• Over Tulsa's four conference games, Singleton has collected 28 receptions for 333 yards, averaging seven catches and 83.3 receiving yards per contest.
• His conference production includes a season-high 11 receptions for 104 yards against North Texas, followed by five catches at Navy, seven at Rice and five against Army.
• Ross enters the Tulane game with a reception in 27 consecutive games, spanning his careers at Bethune-Cookman and Tulsa. He has caught a pass in all eight appearances with the Golden Hurricane.
• Over his last three games, Ross has totaled 15 receptions for 205 yards, highlighted by season highs of seven catches and 90 yards at Navy.
• Ross has caught a touchdown pass in consecutive games, scoring on an 11-yard reception at Rice before adding a 17-yard touchdown against Army.
• Against Army, Ross caught five passes for 84 yards and added an eight-yard rushing touchdown, finishing with 92 yards from scrimmage and two scores.
• Both receivers have contributed beyond catching passes. Singleton threw a 10-yard touchdown pass to
David Wells Jr.
at Navy, while Ross added 36 rushing yards in that contest.
• Singleton and Ross each finished the Navy game with 126 yards from scrimmage, combining for 252 yards as receiving and rushing threats.
• Over Tulsa's last three games, the duo has combined for 32 receptions and 434 receiving yards, giving
Baylor Hayes
two consistent options heading into the November stretch.
JONES CONTRIBUTES BEYOND THE BACKFIELD
• Running back
Trequan Jones
has provided Tulsa with an explosive presence on offense and special teams, totaling 776 all-purpose yards through eight games.
• Jones has rushed for 388 yards and four touchdowns on 60 carries, averaging 6.5 yards per attempt.
• Three of his four rushing touchdowns have covered at least 45 yards, with scoring runs of 81 yards at Sam Houston, 45 yards at Rice and 59 yards against Army.
• Those three touchdown runs account for 185 rushing yards, illustrating his ability to turn an opportunity into a game-changing play.
• Jones enters the Tulane game with a rushing touchdown in consecutive contests after delivering long scoring runs against Rice and Army.
• Against Army, he rushed five times for a team-high 66 yards, averaging 13.2 yards per carry. His 59-yard touchdown tied the game at 28 during the third quarter.
• His most productive rushing performance came at Sam Houston, where he gained 125 yards and scored twice on nine carries in Tulsa's 23-17 road victory.
• Jones has also returned 16 kickoffs for 378 yards, averaging 23.6 yards per return, and added three receptions for 10 yards.
• His 98-yard kickoff-return touchdown against East Texas A&M helped Tulsa secure a 42-0 victory and earned him American Conference Special Teams Player of the Week honors on Sept. 21.
• Against Army, Jones added a 53-yard kickoff return that gave Tulsa possession at the Black Knights' 45-yard line and set up a touchdown drive.
• Jones has scored five touchdowns this season—four rushing and one on a kickoff return—giving the Golden Hurricane a scoring threat in multiple phases.

TURNING RED-ZONE TRIPS INTO TOUCHDOWNS
• Tulsa has scored on 24 of its 28 red-zone possessions this season, producing 21 touchdowns and three field goals for an 85.7-percent scoring rate.
• The Golden Hurricane has scored a touchdown on 75.0 percent of its red-zone opportunities, tied with Army for the highest touchdown rate in the American Conference.
• Tulsa's red-zone touchdown percentage has improved by 20.2 percentage points from last season, when the Golden Hurricane scored touchdowns on 23 of 42 opportunities, or 54.8 percent.
• With four regular-season games remaining, Tulsa is just two red-zone touchdowns shy of matching its entire 2025 total.
• The Golden Hurricane has demonstrated balance inside the opponent's 20-yard line, scoring 11 rushing touchdowns and 10 passing touchdowns.
• Touchdowns account for 87.5 percent of Tulsa's successful red-zone possessions, with 21 of its 24 scoring trips ending in the end zone.
• Tulsa has protected the football in scoring territory, committing no interceptions or lost fumbles during its 28 red-zone possessions.
• Saturday presents a test against Tulane, which leads the American Conference in red-zone scoring defense. The Green Wave has allowed opponents to score on 25 of 36 opportunities, a 69.4-percent rate.

RECRUIT LOCAL
• Tulsa's 2026 roster features 27 players from the Tulsa metropolitan area, demonstrating the Golden Hurricane's commitment to recruiting and retaining local talent.
• The group includes 17 players whose listed hometown is Tulsa, four from Owasso, three from Bixby and one each from Collinsville, Jenks and Mannford.
• Local players represent every phase of the roster, with Tulsa-area talent appearing on offense, defense and special teams.
• The group includes players from several of the area's most successful high school programs, including Bixby, Booker T. Washington, Cascia Hall, Collinsville, Holland Hall, Jenks, Metro Christian, Owasso, Rejoice Christian and Union.
• Tulsa's local contingent includes returning Golden Hurricane players as well as student-athletes who returned home after beginning their collegiate careers elsewhere.
• Head coach
Tre Lamb
and his staff continue to prioritize relationships with area high school coaches while providing local players an opportunity to compete at the FBS level in their hometown region.
WHAT YOU DIDN'T KNOW ABOUT TULSA'S FOOTBALL TRADITION
•
New Year's Day Bowl Streak:
Tulsa was the first school to play in five consecutive New Year's Day bowl games: Sun Bowl (1942), Sugar Bowl (1943, 1944), Orange Bowl (1945), and Oil Bowl (1946).
•
Ellis Jones:
An All-American guard in 1945, Jones played with one arm and was a standout on the College All-Star team vs. the Chicago Bears.
• Pro Football Hall of Fame:
Four former Tulsa players are enshrined: Bob St. Clair, Jim Finks, Steve Largent, and Drew Pearson.
•
Heisman Runners-Up:
Jerry Rhome (1964) and Howard Twilley (1965) finished second in Heisman Trophy voting.
•
Glenn Dobbs (1940–42):
Considered Tulsa's greatest player, Dobbs also coached the Hurricane in the 1960s. Eighth Street near H.A. Chapman Stadium is Glenn Dobbs Drive, dedicated in 2000. A statue of Dobbs was dedicated before the 2013 spring game.
•
Zaven Collins (2020):
Consensus All-America linebacker who won the Chuck Bednarik and Bronko Nagurski awards as college football's top defender.
•
NFL Head Coaches:
Four former Tulsa players have served as NFL head coaches: Tommy Hudspeth (Detroit Lions), Sam Rutigliano (Cleveland Browns), Ray Rhodes (Philadelphia Eagles/Green Bay Packers), and Lovie Smith (Chicago Bears/Tampa Bay Buccaneers/Houston Texans).
•
College Football Hall of Fame:
Tulsa has three former players, two former head coaches, and two former assistant coaches inducted:
• Players:
Glenn Dobbs (1940–42, 1961–68), Howard Twilley (1963–65), Jerry Rhome (1963–64)
•
Head Coaches:
Francis Schmidt (1919–21), John Cooper (1977–84)
•
Assistant Coaches:
Darrell Royal (1951), Sammy Baugh (1963)
