Ohio State Launches “JJ 4 It All” Mission for Jeremiah Smith
11/11/2026 2:58:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
COLUMBUS, Ohio —
College football's highest individual honors. Ohio State's top team goal. For
Jeremiah Smith
, the pursuit of one goes hand in hand with the other.
Ohio State's "JJ 4 It All" mission champions Smith for the Heisman Trophy, Fred Biletnikoff Award, Maxwell Award and Walter Camp Player of the Year Award while reinforcing Ohio State's top goal every season: winning the rivalry game at the end of the regular season.
The mission connects Smith's case for national recognition with the work, preparation,
and commitment to excellence behind his performance. Its purpose extends beyond individual awards: For Smith, success ultimately comes down to what he accomplishes alongside his teammates for The Ohio State University.
A Standalone Season Amid a Historic Career
Entering Week 11 of the 2026 season, Smith has 69 receptions for 1,200 yards
and 14 touchdowns, averaging 17.4 yards per catch and 133.3 receiving yards per game.
Smith leads the Big Ten in all five categories. Nationally, he leads in
touchdown receptions and ranks second in both receiving yards and receiving yards per game, and he leads all Power 4 receivers in each of those three categories.
Smith's production this season includes the two highest receiving yardage totals of his career: 217 yards
against Illinois on Sept. 26 and 206 yards at No. 14 Iowa on Oct. 3. He also recorded 151 receiving yards against both Ball State and No. 4 Texas.
Following the victory over Illinois, Smith was the consensus National Player of the Week, earning recognition from the AP, Walter Camp and Maxwell. Additionally, Smith has been named Big Ten Offensive Player of the Week twice this season, following his performances against Illinois and Iowa.
His performance has earned him midseason All-America recognition from The Associated Press, The Athletic, On3 and Sports Illustrated. He also was named to the preseason watch lists for the Maxwell, Walter Camp and Biletnikoff awards and received Big Ten preseason honors.
Smith's case as college football's best player gained further support this week when he was named a semifinalist for both the
Maxwell Award
and the
Walter Camp Award
, each of which recognizes the nation's most outstanding player.
Legendary Career
Smith's resume extends beyond his current season.
With 232 receptions for 3,758 yards
and 41 touchdowns entering Saturday's game against Northwestern, he holds Ohio State's career records in all three categories. His totals have surpassed the previous school records of 205 receptions by Emeka Egbuka, 2,898 receiving yards by Michael Jenkins and 35 touchdown receptions by Chris Olave.
Additionally, Smith
enters this weekend ranked among the most productive receivers in Big Ten history. He owns the conference career record with 41 touchdown receptions, ranks second in receiving yards and stands sixth in receptions.
Those milestones build on a career marked by national recognition. Smith earned first-team All-America honors from USA Today and was named the Big Ten's Freshman of the Year in 2024, when he also earned Rose Bowl offensive MVP honors. In 2025, he became a unanimous All-American. He was named the Big Ten's Richter-Howard Wide Receiver of the Year in both seasons.
His recognition also extends to the classroom and Ohio State's offseason program. Smith earned Academic All-Big Ten honors in 2025, was an OSU Scholar-Athlete in 2024 and 2025, and received Iron Buckeye recognition in each of his three seasons.
Individual Honors, Shared Purpose
"JJ 4 It All" brings those accomplishments together under one mission: pursue every honor and embrace every challenge.
The effort celebrates Smith's pursuit of college football's highest individual awards without separating it from the Ohio State team
goals that drive him most. That pursuit always begins with beating That Team Up North and earning a second pair of Gold Pants.
