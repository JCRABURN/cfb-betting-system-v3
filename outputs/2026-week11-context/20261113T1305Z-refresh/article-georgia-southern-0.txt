The Extra Down: The Work Behind Anthony Bynum's Rise
11/12/2026 10:00:00 AM
|
Football
Share:
By:
Caitlyn Griffin
STATESBORO
– Before becoming a defensive leader at Georgia Southern, fourth-year outside linebacker
Anthony Bynum
spent much of his high school career waiting for his opportunity.
A two-sport athlete at the time, Bynum played football and baseball but didn't earn his first college football offer until the end of his junior year. Even then, he remained confident that his opportunity would come.
"I didn't get my first offer until going into my senior year," Bynum said. "I played my whole junior year with no offers, and it wasn't until the end of the year that I started getting them. It was cool, and it just gave me hope for the next level."
That opportunity eventually came from Middle Tennessee, which became Bynum's first commitment. A three-star recruit, the decision offered him the chance to continue his football career while staying within a reasonable distance of home.
"It's only a four-hour drive away from home, so it was far enough, but close enough at the same time," Bynum said.
Georgia Southern, however, was actually Bynum's first offer.
The connection came through his brother, who was visiting Western Kentucky at the time. With Georgia Southern head coach
Clay Helton
's brother Tyson leading the Hilltoppers, the connection helped put the Eagles on Bynum's radar early in his recruitment.
Although his path initially led to Middle Tennessee, the foundation of his career was built on patience and preparation.
The transition to college football wasn't necessarily about the speed of the game. Instead, Bynum quickly learned that succeeding at the next level required a greater understanding of defensive schemes, formations and the smaller details that can make the difference on any given play.
"It's more the playbook and paying attention to the little details, like formations and all the nuances of the offense," Bynum said. "You just pay attention and learn from that."
As a true freshman, Bynum found himself surrounded by experienced players who helped him navigate the college game. Veterans Damonte Smith and Quindarius Dunnigan were among those who took him under their wings, helping him become comfortable within the defensive system.
Bynum appeared in four games, recording two solo tackles across 26 snaps. While his playing time was limited, he knew he was making progress behind the scenes.
"I was doing well as a freshman," Bynum said. "I knew the playbook. It was just a lot of older guys, fifth- and sixth-year guys, ahead of me. That's why I played so little. But I was doing my job. Then the next year, it was my time to shine. Spots opened up, and I haven't looked back."
Following his redshirt freshman season, Bynum began establishing himself as a consistent presence on the Blue Raiders' defense. He started 11 games in 2024 before becoming one of four players to start all 12 games in 2025.
His production continued to climb, finishing his third season with a career-high 63 tackles, including 6.0 tackles for loss and 2.5 sacks. He also recorded four pass breakups and led the team with eight quarterback hurries.
For Bynum, the approach behind that production has always been simple: fast, physical and attacking.
"Football is a man-on-man game," Bynum said. "You've got to whoop the man in front of you. It's 11-on-11, but each man has his own job and responsibility. Your other 10 teammates count on you to win your one-on-one. That's all it's about."
After establishing himself at Middle Tennessee, Bynum faced another important decision when he entered the transfer portal. This time, he wasn't making the decision alone.
He and his brother approached the process with a plan, evaluating potential destinations through a detailed list that considered everything from coaching and defensive schemes to housing, cost of living, academics and fan support.
"We created a list," Bynum said. "We did coaching, housing, cost of living, the whole thing. We made a T-chart and checked off all the schools I had. We looked at scheme-wise, coaches, living-wise, school-wise, fan base-wise. We went through everything, man."
When it came time to make a decision, Georgia Southern quickly separated itself from the rest.
"Georgia Southern was at the front, so we said, 'Let's do it!'" Bynum said.
The opportunity to play for Helton was a significant factor, but the environment surrounding the program ultimately made the decision even easier.
"Coach Helton definitely played a big role," Bynum said. "It's like coming into a real family. You can just tell by the team bonding and the way he runs things around here. It's different."
Bynum arrived in Statesboro ready to contribute, but his role continued to evolve throughout the offseason. After spending much of his career playing the joker position, he transitioned to the defensive line during the summer, taking on a new responsibility within the Eagles' defense.
The position change also presented an opportunity to establish himself as a leader in a new room.
Having spent the spring working with the joker group, Bynum gradually worked his way into the defensive line room over the summer. Rather than immediately assuming a leadership role as a transfer, he focused on earning the respect of his new teammates through his work ethic and consistency.
"I didn't get to the D-line room until the summer," Bynum said. "In the summer, I eased my way in because I'm a transfer. You can't just walk in and start leading. I wanted the guys to respect my work ethic."
Before long, his teammates began following his example.
"Sooner or later, the guys just started following behind me, and they started taking leadership," Bynum said. "We all communicate with each other. We tell each other what we're doing good and what we're not doing good. We just help each other get better."
That leadership extends beyond the defensive line room and into the atmosphere surrounding Georgia Southern football.
For Bynum, experiencing the Power of Paulson for the first time was an immediate reminder of what makes playing in Statesboro special.
"Man, walking out and seeing all the fans in the home stadium, I'm like, 'Wow. Just wow,'" Bynum said. "Having 20,000 or 30,000 fans cheering for you when you win definitely plays a role. Having that home crowd, and Georgia Southern being a small-town campus, you can tell the care around here is real. Everybody cares."
With three games remaining in the regular season, the Eagles now turn their attention to an important in-state matchup against Georgia State in Atlanta.
The stakes are significant, but Bynum and his teammates understand the importance of approaching each Saturday with the same mentality. As the coaching staff has emphasized throughout the season, every game is an opportunity to compete for a championship.
"Our coach was telling us every game is a championship game by itself," Bynum said. "This is the most important game of the season. This game right here against Georgia State is the most important game. We need y'all to come out and support us. We need Eagle Nation behind us, cheering us on."
It is a mindset Bynum knows well.
Long before the college offers, the defensive production and the leadership responsibilities, he was a high school player working his way up the depth chart. He spent his freshman season on the freshman team, started on junior varsity as a sophomore and spent time on the varsity bench before finally earning a starting opportunity as a junior.
By his senior season, he had taken another step forward.
The progression wasn't always immediate, but Bynum never allowed the lack of early recognition to dictate what he believed he could accomplish.
Now, as he continues to make his mark in Statesboro, the message he carries with him remains the same one that helped him get here.
"Keep your head down and keep believing in yourself," Bynum said. "Even if nobody else believes in you, just keep believing in yourself. You always have to put the work in. The work will always show."
