# Week 6 ranking and calibration forensic research — draft, no policy change

## 1. Executive conclusion and decision gate

**Gate F: insufficient data for a v2.9 production selection change. NO-GO.** Week 6's governed ATS Top 5 fell through an *intentional* reliability-policy tie breaker: all 58 ATS predictions had null governed uncertainty and Confidence 1, so the first five locked-line IDs won the tie. This is a real selection-objective mismatch, but two complete frozen ATS-plus-totals weeks do not prove which replacement picks the best five bets. The mixed shadow board is not a valid probability comparison: its ATS and totals scores use unrelated, empirically unvalidated transforms. Keep mandatory full-card ATS picks and their recorded historical ranking; do not promote a shadow score, post hoc shrink, fixed market allocation, or Week 6 winner-selected method into v2.8. The smallest supported *research/governance* repair is to disclose that the governed Top 5 has no strength discrimination when uncertainty is missing, preserve separate market diagnostics, and gather frozen prospective evidence before a versioned replacement.

The full [58-candidate Week 6 ATS reconstruction](week6_ats_58_reconstructed.csv), [all counterfactual picks](ats_counterfactual_top5_in_sample.csv), and [machine-readable metrics](diagnostics.json) are generated by `python -m scripts.research_week6_ranking`. Week 5 has 56 complete frozen ATS and totals candidates; Week 6 has 58 each. No source database, pregame artifact, audit artifact, model, migration, or production policy was modified.

## 2. Exact governed ATS root cause and code path

1. `contest_lines.py:list_effective_locked_lines` (line 585) queries `contest_locked_lines` using `SELECT id ... WHERE contest_id = ? ... ORDER BY id` (line 598). This guarantees lock-ID order; it is not accidental database row order.
2. `business_entities/full_card.py:generate_full_card` (line 957) iterates those lines, calls `_select_side` to fetch point-in-time model selections and fallback if needed, then calls `_rank_selections` (line 1021). `business_entities/ranking.py:confidence_for_uncertainty` (line 322) explicitly maps `None` uncertainty to Confidence 1. Frozen Week 6 `week6_ats_full_card.csv` has 58/58 null governed `uncertainty_points` and 58/58 Confidence 1; its separate `shadow_uncertainty_points` was computed after the governed card and was **not** passed to this policy. No per-game fallback code was recorded on the 58 picks.
3. `business_entities/full_card.py:_rank_selections` (line 484) sorts by `(-confidence, uncertainty_is_none, uncertainty_or_inf, locked_line_id)` (lines 508–517) and gives ranks 5→1 to the first five (lines 519–524). Thus the last key, `locked_line_id`, decided every Week 6 tie. `business_entities/ranking.py` records `RELIABILITY_METRIC=model_uncertainty_points`, `RANKING_METHOD=confidence_desc_uncertainty_asc`, `TIE_BREAKER=locked_line_id_asc` (lines 23–25). This is an **intentional v2.8-era implementation/policy fallback for missing reliability**, not a missing sort or SQL-order leak. The absence of usable governed uncertainty made the Top 5 *operationally* lock-order based. The example config calls its versions `production-confidence-v1`/`production-ranking-v1`; the frozen model manifest reports the same, but marks the Week 6 card `NOT_PUBLISHED;LOCAL_GOVERNED_DRAFT` and the example policy as proposed, so this report does not assert an owner-approved production cutover.
4. Persistence and replay: `business_entities/cards.py:list_contest_picks` uses `ORDER BY locked_line_id, id` (line 198); `business_entities/full_card.py` replays `_rank_selections` against stored picks (line 801). `scripts/finalize_week6_card.py` writes `rank`, `top_five`, `uncertainty_points`, and separate `shadow_uncertainty_points` into `week6_ats_full_card.csv` (lines 167–184), sorts the governed five by descending stored `rank` (line 211), and writes `week6_official_ats_top5.csv` (line 235). CSV order is serialization, not the selection rule. `tests/test_confidence_ranking.py:test_top_five_uses_uncertainty_not_raw_edge_and_stores_policy` and `test_fewer_than_five_games_are_all_ranked_with_stable_ties` assert this policy and lock-ID tie breaker. This research adds a frozen-data check that all 58 Week 6 governed IDs equal lock order and stored ranks 5→1.

At selection time the system also had `projected_home_margin`, immutable `locked_home_spread`, and selected-side point edge, plus the separately generated shadow uncertainty and conservative ATS score. The conservative score is exactly `min(0.60, 0.50 + 0.005 × max(0, selected-side edge points))` (`business_entities/ats_shadow_calibration.py:_evaluation_input`, lines 369–415; `scripts/finalize_week6_card.py` lines 132–145). The original `candidate_score` equals its so-called `calibrated_selected_side_probability`, but `week6_ats_shadow_calibration.csv` itself marks `NOT_EMPIRICALLY_VALIDATED`. It is a **shadow ranking score, not a validated probability**. Selected-side point edge is not expected value: no offered price/odds were sealed for a sportsbook decision. The shadow uncertainty is also a separate research quantity rather than an approved v2.8 ranking combination. Confidence alone is constant and repeats governed order; ATS score and edge are strictly monotone here and produce the same counterfactual order. No other already-supported v2.8 combination can break the null-uncertainty tie without changing policy.

## 5. ATS calibration and ordinal discrimination

For all 114 Weeks 5–6 ATS picks, the score is 0.50–0.60 by construction and has **no 60%+ examples**. Observed ATS record is 62–52; mean score is approximately 52.7%. Bucket counts and observed-versus-score gaps are in `diagnostics.json`; 50–52 has 50 picks, 26 wins; 52–54 has 36 picks, 20 wins; 54–56 has 14 picks, 7 wins; 56–58 has 12 picks, 8 wins; 58–60 has 2 picks, 1 win. There is no smooth win-rate increase. Pooled Brier-like score 0.248, log-loss-like score is mathematically defined because all scores lie strictly in (0,1), ECE-like gap 0.030, and pairwise AUC 0.531. These are *diagnostics of probability-shaped scores*, not proof of calibrated probabilities: the mapping was not fitted/validated, sample size is small, and the apparent low ECE hides cancellation and sparse upper buckets. Rank discrimination and absolute calibration must be evaluated independently.

## 6–7. Totals ranking and calibration

The totals card is 56/56 in Week 5 and 58/58 in Week 6. Independently joining the frozen totals cards to postgame grades reproduces the 60%+ bucket: Week 6 **11/23** wins against **14.6916** summed shadow scores; Week 5 **9/24** against **15.4466**; combined **20/47** against **30.1382**. Combined observed win rate 42.55%, mean score 64.12%, a **-21.57 percentage-point** gap (Wilson 95% for observed 29.5%–56.7%). This is strong evidence of upper-score inflation in these weeks, not proof of a stable future calibration slope. The top-scored totals across two weeks went 4–6 at ranks 1–5, 5–5 at 6–10, 7–13 at 11–20, and 40–34 on the remaining card. Pairwise AUC is **0.430**, so higher totals scores did **not** discriminate winners here. Pooled Brier-like 0.268, ECE-like 0.135, log loss in `diagnostics.json`. Week 5 AUC 0.423 and Week 6 0.461 both fall below chance; two weeks cannot distinguish persistent inverse ranking from noise/model misspecification.

A purely **post hoc, unfitted sensitivity check** halves every totals score's excess above 0.5. It lowers pooled Brier-like loss from 0.2684 to 0.2566 and ECE-like gap from 0.1347 to 0.1104, while AUC remains 0.430 because the order is unchanged. This shows why calibration might matter, but is **not** a candidate production curve and does not make the Top 5 more reliable.

## 8. ATS–totals comparability

ATS score: capped `0.5 + 0.005 × nonnegative selected-side margin advantage`. Totals score: `z=(projected_total−locked_total)/uncertainty_points`, standard-normal CDF, a symmetric logit-slope transform, then select over probability or `1−over probability` (`business_entities/totals.py` lines 557–566 and 782–799). Week 5–6 slope is 1.0, so it does not empirically correct the raw normal-residual assumption. `business_entities/unified_top_five.py:generate_unified_top_five` (around lines 421–435) pools both `calibrated_probability` fields, sorts descending, then by market type and source ID, enforcing at most one selection per game. `candidate_score` simply copies that field; the common name does **not** give a common empirical scale. ATS 0.58 and totals 0.58 do **not** currently mean the same demonstrated chance of a win.

## 9. Walk-forward feasibility and fixed-rule replay

A genuine calibrated walk-forward cannot be run credibly here. Week 5 has no earlier complete frozen dual-market training ledger; calibrating on Week 5 alone and evaluating Week 6 would estimate a high-variance function from one football week, including correlated games. This report fits **no** calibration function and chooses no parameters based on outcomes. The following is a fixed-rule, sealed-input replay only, ordered by week; it is **not validated walk-forward evidence**. ## 11. Objective and minimum repair

The desired selection objective is **choose the five most defensible *actual bets* from the ATS-plus-totals universe**, conditional on immutable lines, available prices, estimated win chance, uncertainty, and sportsbook policy. The current full-card side model predicts home margin, and the contest card must pick every game; `_rank_selections` ranks model *uncertainty*, not selected-side win probability or after-vig value. With null uncertainty, it ranks lock order. The totals model predicts points and converts residual-normal tail area to a shadow selected-direction score. The unified board sorts unlike scores as though they were calibrated probabilities. This is a precise prediction-to-selection mismatch. There is no frozen offered price/stake ledger, so none of the five can be certified as an expected-value bet. Preserve the mandatory contest Top 5 contract, but explicitly label it a deterministic fallback when this policy has no discriminator and keep sportsbook recommendations separate. No production patch is part of this PR.

### Methods and limits

All score ranks and probability-shaped diagnostics use only frozen pregame variables joined **afterward** to immutable grading outputs. Ties are broken by locked-line ID for ATS and game ID for totals. Bucket intervals are half-open, except 60%+; pushes are excluded from calibration and Wilson win-rate denominators. Quantiles are linear-interpolated empirical quantiles. ECE weights absolute observed-minus-mean score gaps by bucket size. AUC is win/loss pairwise concordance with half credit for ties. Brier/log loss are numerically legitimate for values in (0,1) but cannot certify a probability model without temporal calibration validation. Wilson intervals omit game/week correlation and therefore overstate precision if games are dependent. Scores may be correlated across common teams, weather, and market moves. No closing-line timestamp, reliable vig-based EV, stake path, or raw EPA component attribution is available for these frozen Week 6 cards.
