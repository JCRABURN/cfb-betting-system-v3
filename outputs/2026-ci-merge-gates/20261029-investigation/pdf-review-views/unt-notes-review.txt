STORYLINES
• Now at 5-2 overall and 3-0 in league play, head coach Neal Brown and the Mean Green look to extend their winning
streak to five games as they host Florida Atlantic on Thursday night in a nationally televised game at DATCU Stadium.
• Brown is in his first season at the helm of the UNT program after spending 2025 as the special assistant to the head
coach under Steve Sarkisian at Texas. A veteran head coach, Brown arrived in Denton with 10 seasons of head coaching
experience following previous stints at West Virginia (2019-24) and Troy (2015-18).
• In his head coaching career, Brown has compiled a 77-53 overall record and led his team to bowl appearances in seven
of 10 seasons. He has three 10-win campaigns under his belt and was named 2017 Sun Belt Coach of the Year at Troy.
• The Mean Green offense is once again one of the most dangerous units in the country, ranking second in both scoring
(43.1 PPG) and passing offense (341.0 YPG) and fourth in total offense (525.7 YPG). UNT is one of three teams in the
country to have scored 40+ points five times already this season and is tied for the FBS lead with four games of 550+
total yards of offense.
• QB Tayven Jackson has been one of the most productive quarterbacks across the country, completing 72.4% of his
passes for 2,173 yards and 18 touchdowns. He currently ranks third nationally in passing yards per game (310.4), sixth
in completion percentage (72.4%), ninth in passing touchdowns (18) and 10th in completions per game (22.9). His 492
passing yards against Texas State still remain the most by an FBS player this season.
• Right alongside Jackson is RB Jahiem White, who also stands among the nation’s best running backs. White has
carried the ball 116 times for 861 yards and 11 touchdowns, while also adding 250 yards and two more scores through
the air. He has found the end zone in every single game this season, highlighted by four scores against Houston
Christian, and is tied for the national lead with five 100-yard rushing games.
• Together, Jackson and White make UNT the country’s only team with a top-10 passer and top-10 rusher.
• WR Justin Stevenson has burst onto the scene over the past month, catching seven total touchdowns in his past four
games. He has had multiple scores three games in a row and has racked up 41 catches for 662 yards on the season.
• Matt Powledge was hired as the defensive coordinator in the offseason after spending the 2023-25 seasons in the
same role at Baylor. He has served in a coordinator capacity every year since 2014 and is currently leading the Mean
Green defense to top-50 national ranks in tackles for loss, 3rd down defense and turnovers forced.
• Thursday marks UNT’s third regular season game ever broadcast on ESPN and second this season after playing on the
network at Tulsa earlier this month. As of Thursday, six of UNT’s last 11 games will have aired on ESPN, ABC, or FOX.
• Anchor Matt Barrie will also host ‘SportsCenter on Campus’ prior to the game outside Gate 4 of DATCU Stadium, giving
fans another opportunity to showcase their excitement for UNT Athletics on ESPN’s flagship program.
• In addition, classes scheduled for noon or later on Thursday were canceled in the spirit of rallying the full Mean Green
community and due to a high volume of student ticket claims.

QUICK HITTERS
• Neal Brown is now 5-2 in his first season as head coach of the Mean Green after former head coach Eric
Morris left in December 2025 for the head coaching job at Oklahoma State. In addition to Morris, 20 former
UNT players transferred to OSU including American Offensive Player of the Year Drew Mestemaker and
Newcomer of the Year Caleb Hawkins.
• In the offseason, North Texas reloaded its roster with 70 new players. Dating back to 2024, UNT has
added at least 68 new players to its roster every season with 77 in 2024, 68 in 2025 and 70 this past year.
That accounts for a total of 215 new players, the most of any FBS program in that span.
• Per Rotowire, UNT’s average player age of 21.01 makes it the second-oldest roster in FBS in 2026.
• The Mean Green rank alongside the nation’s best in several offensive categories such as passing yards
per game (2nd, 341.0), scoring offense (2nd, 43.1) and total offense (4th, 525.7).
• QB Tayven Jackson, RB Jahiem White and WR Justin Stevenson form one of the country’s most
fearsome trios, each ranking among the FBS leaders in their respective yardage categories. Jackson is the
nation’s No. 6 passer, while White and Stevenson rank eighth and 17th in rushing and receiving yards.
• UNT received votes in the AFCA Coaches Poll for the second consecutive week.
FAU SERIES HISTORY
• Florida Atlantic holds a slim 8-7 lead in a series that dates back to 2004. Of those 15 all-time matchups, 14
have come when both teams were members of the same conference (SBC, CUSA, American).
• Since FAU’s six-game winning streak to begin the series, UNT has won three straight matchups and seven
of the last nine. That stretch includes four consecutive victories in Denton.
• The Mean Green pulled out a 41-37 road victory in their last meeting on Oct. 12, 2024. The standout per-
former that day was WR DT Sheffield, who recorded a career-high 11 catches for 126 yards and two TDs.
• A key moment of the all-time series came on Dec. 2, 2017, when UNT and FAU met in the Conference USA
Championship Game. The Owls took that game by a score of 41-17.
ACTION JACKSON
• UCF transfer Tayven Jackson has been one of the most productive quarterbacks in the country so far
this season, completing 160 of 221 pass attempts for 2,173 yards and 18 touchdownss against just 5
interceptions. He has also added a pair of touchdowns on the ground.
• Through seven games, Jackson ranks third among FBS players in passing yards per game (310.4), fifth in
passing efficiency (177.3), sixth in total passing yards (2,173), sixth in completion percentage (72.4), ninth
in passing touchdowns (18) and 10th in completions per game (22.9).
• His marks in passing yards, passing yards per game and passing efficiency all lead the American.
• After 305 more passing yards at Navy, Jackson has now thrown for 300+ yards in four of his last five
games. He had never reached the 300-yard mark in 27 career games before joining the Mean Green.
• Jackson was nearly flawless against Charlotte, leading UNT to a 50-10 rout as he went 24-of-31 for 349
yards, a career-high five touchdowns and no interceptions. He finished as just the fourth different UNT
quarterback since at least 1995 to have a game of 325+ passing yards, 5+ passing TDs and zero INTs.
• Notably, he completed his first nine passes at Navy after going 14-for-14 to begin the Charlotte game.
• Against Texas State, Jackson turned in another stellar effort with 492 yards and three TDs on 42-of-56
passing. His 492 passing yards are the most by an FBS player this season and the fourth-highest total in
program history. He also became just the seventh FBS quarterback since 1995 to complete at least 75% of
his passes when throwing 55 or more attempts and eclipsing 490 passing yards in a single game.
• Jackson joined Chandler Morris (2024) and Mason Fine (2019) as the only UNT quarterbacks since at
least 1995 to complete back-to-back games with 325+ passing yards and 4+ passing touchdowns. He did
so in the first two American Conference games versus Tulsa and Charlotte.
• Jackson has played in 34 games and made 23 starts over time at UNT, UCF, Indiana and Tennessee.
• In 27 games prior to joining the Mean Green, Jackson completed 62.8% of his passes while throwing
for 3,451 yards and 16 touchdowns. In seven games at UNT, he has already surpassed that passing
touchdown total with 18 and increased his completion percentage to 72.4%.
• Jackson comes from a family of athletes as his brother, Trayce Jackson-Davis, was a second round pick
in the 2023 NBA Draft and is currently a member of the Toronto Raptors. His father, Ray Jackson, played
cornerback for Washington State and was a member of the 1998 Pac-10 champion and Rose Bowl team.
He then played for four years in the NFL with the Panthers, Rams, and Chiefs.

“WHITE” LIGHTNING IN A BOTTLE
• Perhaps the most notable add in the offseason was former Freshman All-American RB Jahiem White, who
spent three seasons as a productive back under Brown at West Virginia.
• Through seven games, White has carried the ball 116 times for 861 yards and 11 touchdowns. He’s also
contributed in the receiving game with 27 catches, 250 receiving yards and a pair of scores.
• White sits among the nation’s leaders in several categories, ranking second in yards per carry (7.42),
third in all-purpose yards per game (158.7), fourth in total touchdowns (13), sixth in rushing yards per game
(123.0), sixth in rushing touchdowns (11) and eighth in rushing yards (861).
• White continued his hot streak in the win over Navy, scampering for 174 yards and a pair of scores on 20
carries. He also added 58 yards through the air, giving him 232 scrimmage yards on the night.
• He has recorded at least one rushing touchdown in all seven games this season, a stretch highlighted by a
career-high four scores against HCU. Dating back to Aug. 30, 2025, White has ran for a touchdown in nine
consecutive games. He has not finished a game without finding the end zone since the 2024 season.
• With two more scores against Navy, White has now had 2+ total touchdowns in four different games this
year. He entered the season with just three multiple-touchdown games in 28 career games at West Virginia.
• White has rushed for 100+ yards in four straight games and fives total times on the season, tying him for
the FBS lead. That is the longest 100-yard game streak of his career and gives him 12 total overall.
• White registered a career game against Tulsa when he ran for 219 yards and a touchdown on 32 carries.
His 219 yards were the most of his career and 10th-most by any FBS
player this season.
• Across 34 career games, White has amassed 2,680 yards and 25
touchdowns on the ground while also catching 54 passes for 512
yards and five more scores. His career rushing total of 2,680 ranks
11th among active FBS running backs.
• White is a proven big-play threat, averaging 6.8 yards per carry
throughout his career. Among active FBS players with at least 300
rushes, White only trails Louisville’s Isaac Brown nationally in yards per rush attempt.
• The 2026 season sees White reunite with his former head coach Brown, as well as running backs coach
Chad Scott. In two years together (2023-24) at WVU, the trio helped the Mountaineers rushing offense rank
27th nationally in 2024 and third in 2023.
• White started the first two games of 2025 for the Mountaineers but was sidelined with a knee injury for the
remainder of the season.

OFFENSIVE JUGGERNAUT
• The Mean Green offense has proven to be one of the most dangerous units in the country, ranking second
in both scoring (43.1 PPG) and passing offense (341.0 YPG) and fourth in total offense (525.7 YPG).
• Every year since 2023, UNT has ranked top six among FBS competition in total offense (2023-6th; 2024-
3rd; 2025-1st), top 12 in passing (2023-12th; 2024-4th; 2025-2nd) and top 25 in scoring offense (2023-21st;
2024-23rd; 2025-1st).
• North Texas is one of three teams in the country to have scored 40+ points in at least five games this
season. The only other schools to also reach that mark are top-ranked Miami (FL) and Alabama.
• Similarly, UNT is joined by Oklahoma State and Texas State as the only FBS teams to accumulate at least
550 yards of offense four times this season. It has eclipsed 600 yards twice in that span, including a season-
high 648 against Houston Christian that ranked seventh-most in program history. North Texas compiled five
550-yard games last season, the program’s most in recorded history.
• In QB Tayven Jackson and RB Jahiem White, North Texas is currently the only FBS team in the country
with a top-10 passer and top-10 rusher this season. Jackson ranks sixth at 2,173 passing yards while White
is eighth with 861 yards on the ground.

STEVENSON UNLEASHED
• Perhaps the surprise breakout player of the season has been WR Justin Stevenson, who has hauled in
41 catches for 662 yards and seven touchdowns so far this season.
• Through seven games, he ranks 10th across the FBS in receiving touchdowns (7), 14th in receiving yards
per game (94.6) and 17th in total receiving yards (662). His seven scores lead the American Conference,
while his yardage totals are third-best in the league.
• Stevenson had the best game of his career last Saturday against Navy, catching eight passes for a
personal-best 209 yards and pair of scores. That was the seventh-highest receiving total by an FBS player
this season and most by a Mean Green player since Wyatt Young’s 295 versus Rice on Nov. 22, 2025.
• He also registered 122 yards against both Texas State and Charlotte, making him one of three American
Conference players to hit the century mark three times this year.
• All seven of Stevenson’s touchdowns have come in the past four games, including multiple scores in each
of the past three. He is one of five FBS players to catch multiple touchdowns in three straight games this
season, joining the likes of Ohio State’s Jeremiah Smith and Oregon State’s Jesse Legree, and the only
Mean Green player to do so since at least 1995.
• Stevenson is in prime position to extend UNT’s streak of four straight seasons with a player recording at
least 10 receiving touchdowns. Ohio State is the only other FBS program with that distinction.

VETERANS UP FRONT

at least 20 games throughout their career.

• Even with the emergence of WR Justin Stevenson, the UNT passing attack is one of the most
balanced units in college football. It ranks second across the FBS at 341.0 yards per game and is one of
seven teams with at least nine players who have caught eight or more passes this season.
• Despite missing the past two games, WR Corri Milliner ranks third on the team in receptions (20) and
second in receiving yards (325). His best outing of the season came against Texas State, grabbing seven
passes for 80 yards and a touchdown. Each of those marks were his most of the season.
• The Mean Green have also seen a recent breakout from WR Kyle Koch, who has scored in four of the
past five games. Koch entered the season with just three career catches but has been a vital piece of the
2026 offense, racking up 18 catches for 226 yards and four touchdowns.
• TE Max Reese has provided a versatile presence to the offense, ranking third among the American
Conference’s tight ends with 250 receiving yards on 17 catches. He’s also found the end zone three
times this season, two of which have come in the past three games.
• Reese had a breakout performance in the win over Tulsa with five receptions for 101 yards and a touch-
down. That effort earned him John Mackey Award National Player of the Week.
• Reese’s performance also made him just the fourth different tight end in North Texas history to record a
100 receiving yards in a game. It was the sixth 100-yard receiving game by a UNT tight end and the first
since Andy Blount had 104 yards against ULM on Oct. 30, 2004.
• RB Jahiem White has been a major factor in the passing game out of the backfield as well with 27
catches, 250 receiving yards and two touchdowns. His catch and yardage totals both rank top five among
FBS running backs this season and are tops in the American Conference.
• Against Texas State, Stevenson, Milliner and White all had at least seven receptions. It was the first time
since Oct. 20, 2007, vs. Louisiana that UNT had three players with 7+ catches (Casey Fitzgerald, Brock
Sticker and Brandon Jackson). LA Tech is the only other FBS team to accomplish that feat this season.
CONTROL THE GAME
• UNT has an average time of possesion of 31:30 through seven games, which ranks 36th in the FBS. It
has won the time of possession battle in every game this season except against Navy.
• One of the main reasons for UNT’s TOP success has been its ability to convert on third down. The
Mean Green are converting 48.4% of their third downs, good for No. 19 in the nation.
• UNT dominated time of possession in the season opener against Indiana, holding the ball for 37:22
while the Hoosiers had just 22:38 TOP. It was the first time in head coach Curt Cignetti’s tenure at Indiana
he had lost the time possession battle by 14+ minutes. Indiana was 10th nationally in TOP during 2025.
• At the time, the only other team to record 35+ minutes of time of possession against Cignetti’s Indiana
squad was Notre Dame in the 2024 College Football Playoff.
• Against Texas State, UNT went 10 for 15 on third downs. It was the first time since Sept. 16, 2023,
against LA Tech that North Texas converted 10+ third downs in a game.
RED ZONE SUCCESS
• The Mean Green currently rank 24th among FBS competition in red zone offense (91.7%), converting
33 of 36 possible chances into points. Of those 33 scores, 26 were touchdowns and seven came through
field goals.
• It is an impressive continuation of last season’s success in the red zone despite so much roster
turnover. UNT completed the 2025 campaign as the country’s No. 18 red zone offense at 91.1%.
RUN THE DAMN BALL
• In his head coaching career, Neal Brown has found plenty of success in the win/loss column when his
teams run the ball effectively.
• Across 10+ seasons as a head coach, Brown’s teams are 30-2 when rushing for over 200 yards and 54-
11 when breaking 150 yards on the ground. UNT has eclipsed that 150-yard mark six times this season,
including a high of 248 against HCU. When held to less than 150 rushing yards, Brown is 23-42.
POWLEDGE EFFECT
• Matt Powledge was hired as defensive coordinator in the offseason after spending the 2023-25 sea-
sons in the same role at Baylor. He has been in a coordinator position every season since 2014, working
as DC at both Baylor and Oregon in addition to special teams experience at Baylor, Louisiana, ULM and
Sam Houston from 2014-21.
• Under Powledge’s watch, the Mean Green defense ranks top 50 nationally in fumbles recovered (22nd),
tackles for loss (37th), 3rd down defense (39th) and turnovers forced (42nd).
• Following Zakye Barker’s 63-yard scoop-and-score against Navy, the UNT defense now ranks seventh
in the country with two defensive touchdowns.
• In 2025, Powledge helped lead the Bears to the No. 33 passing defense in the FBS and was tied for
30th in the nation in defensive touchdowns.

SCORING IN ALL THREE PHASES
• North Texas recorded a touchdown on offense, defense and special teams in the win over Navy last week-

IN THE SECONDARY
• The UNT secondary hit the transfer portal hard in the offseason bringing in A4 transfers Cameren
Jenkins (Baylor), Jason Cross Jr. (West Virginia), Keshawn Davila (Arkansas), Taye Seymore (Auburn)
and Jaden Allen (Arkansas), as well as talent from other levels like CJ Coombes (Wofford), Peyton
Taylor (Emporia St.), Brandon Jones (McNeese) and Anthony Benford II (Missouri Western).
• Coombes was previously a standout in the secondary at Wofford where he started 17 total games over
the past two seasons. He compiled 119 tackles, 6.0 TFL, PBUs and two interceptions in that time and now
plays a key role for UNT as the team’s second-leading tackler.
• Some of Coombes’ standout performances this year include 10 tackles at both Texas State and Navy, as
well as a 21-yard interception return in the win over Charlotte.
• Jones has been a staple at safety for UNT with 45 tackles and 4.0 TFLs in the first seven games of the
year. At Summer Creek High School, Jones was a pick-six machine throughout the 2024 season and
returned seven of his 10 INTs for touchdowns. That set a national single-season pick-six record, earning
him the nickname “Mr. Pick-Six.”
• Two of the biggest surprises of the season are Benford II and Cross, who’ve each made at least one start
in the secondary. Cross has tallied 23 tackles, including four for loss, while Benford II’s efforts at the nickel
position had him put him on scholarship earlier in the year.
• Taylor was one of the most productive Division II cornerbacks in the nation throughout 2025, ranking 13th
with 12 PBUs. He has 20 tackles, 1.5 TFL and a team-high four PBUs through seven contests.
ROSTER TURNOVER

added at least 68 new players to its roster every season with 77 in 2024, 68 in 2025 and 70 this past year.

in starting OL Keon Johnson and CB Taylor Starling.

setting a new program best with seven games on linear TV in 2024, the Mean Green bested that in one
year with 10 linear contests throughout the 2025 campaign.

against defending national champion Indiana in week one.

and again this Thursday when they host FAU. Prior to 2026, UNT’s only regular season game ever broadcast
on ESPN main was its regular season finale against Temple last year.
• As of Thursday, six of UNT’s last 11 games will have aired on either ESPN, ABC or FOX. It is also one of
four teams in America to have played on both ESPN main and FOX Big Noon Kickoff this year.

Stadium. Hosted by anchor Matt Barrie, the live show begins at 4 p.m. and will showcase the university’s
atmosphere, traditions and key storylines headed into the game later that evening. Mean Green students,
faculty and fans are encouraged to attend to be part of the live broadcast.

What is Scorigami?
Scorigami	is	the	art	of	building	final	scores	that	have	never
happened	before.	Jon	Bois	developed	the	concept	to	find
unique scored in NFL history. This chart was inspired by
UCF,	the	first	known	FBS	program	to	implement	Scorigami
to track the most common scores in program history.
How do I read this chart?
The winning team’s score is along the top (horizontal axis)
and the losing team’s score is along the left (vertical axis).
Scores marked in light green have happened, scores in
white have not yet happened, scores in dark green are im-
possible. The bottom half is all dark green because the los-
ing team can not score more points than the winning team.
The number in the green square represents the amount of
UNT	games	with	that	final	score.	Scores	bordered	in	black
are results from the 2025 season.
