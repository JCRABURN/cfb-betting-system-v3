Cougars jump out fast in win
By:
Mike Brohard
PULLMAN, Wash. –
One season surmised in two plays.
The first, Colorado State running back
Durell Robinson
gets stopped in the red zone on a fourth-and-2 play on the road. It was a call of some necessity but also leaning toward high-reward. The Rams were playing from behind and they were deep in enemy territory.
On the next play, Washington State running back Kirby Vorhees broke through the middle untouched for a 98-yard touchdown run, the longest in the country this season. It was his second score of the game and the Cougars, like Vorhees, never looked back.
"Obviously, it's going to get us into that moment but us as leaders and captains, we've just got to keep their head high and just tell them to keep fighting and just go out there and play ball,"
Oumar Diomande
said. "Do what we know, do our jobs and keep fighting. So as leaders, we've just got to do our jobs, and we need to push on."
Washington State posted a 73-7 victory, it second of the season and first in Pac-12 play while the Rams fell to 3-7 and were officially eliminated from bowl consideration. It was the 10
th
consecutive road loss for the Rams, dating back to 2024.
Colorado State's inability to get stops on defense continued against a team which was averaging just 19 points per game. The Cougars, who scored on their first 10 drives, had doubled that number by halftime with a 38-7 advantage. The Rams have now allowed 476 points this season, the second most in program history; the 1981 team allowed 502.
"You have two choices -- you can quit and run and hide or you can keep fighting," CSU coach
Jim Mora
said. "Some guys have identified that maybe they've already decided to run and hide so that's why guys like Kellen (Martinez) and Jaden (Cortell) and some of those young guys are going to play.
"When I say run and hide, I don't think there's many at all, I just think that we need to get these young guys that got a real hunger that haven't gotten used to losing. They need a chance to play."
Mora also mentioned Xavier Winbush as a younger player who took advantage of his time and made plays. They will get more chances moving forward.
The 73 points allowed are the second most in a game. The nine rushing touchdown the Rams yielded were a record, as were the 535 rushing yards for Washington State.
For most of the year, Colorado State has been able to generate offense, but that production has now stalled in the past two road games. They were limited to 14 points in a loss at Utah State two weeks ago and took a step back from that, just one touchdown produced, coming in the first half. That came when
RJ Vick
took a pitch from
Hauss Hejny
and scored on a sweep.
The Rams generated just 273 yards of total offense, turning the ball over twice. As for that, Mora  turned to the defense.
"Part of it is we gave up 73 points," he said. "Our offense is really good."
As Mora noted, there wasn't much he could say about the loss and how it transpired. Just put a positive spin and try to move forward. He said do it with younger players before they learn bad habits.
He has stressed his team needs to learn how to win. The sooner it can happen, the better.
"Hopefully we get a couple wins so they can learn how to win, what it takes to win. I think that's a real problem here and has been for many, many years is the people here don't know how to win," Mora said. "The place I came from, they've won 16 national championships in basketball, so the whole school knows how to win. We've got to teach people out here how to win."
