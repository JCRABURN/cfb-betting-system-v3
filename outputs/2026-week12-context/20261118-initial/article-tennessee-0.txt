Mental and Physical Toughness on Full Display as #8 Vols Prepare for Pivotal Matchup Against #21 LSU
November 16, 2026
|

DeSean Bishop Media Availability

UT Game Notes
FB Media Center
Share:
KNOXVILLE, Tenn. –
After another gritty win last weekend on the road against Texas A&M, No. 9/9/8 Tennessee returns to the friendly confines of Neyland Stadium for a pivotal conference game this Saturday against No. 24/23/21 LSU at 3:30 p.m. on ABC.
Despite having to battle adversity in the form of injuries, as well as the challenges presented by a loaded SEC schedule, the Vols (8-2, 5-2 SEC) continue to grind out wins to remain in the College Football Playoff picture as they prepare for their final regular season home game of the year against an extremely talented Tigers (6-4, 3-4 SEC) team on Saturday afternoon.
During his Monday press conference, head coach
Josh Heupel
praised the physical and mental toughness his team has displayed throughout the season.
"I think as much as anything it's their mindset how they approach every day," Heupel said. "They've trained themselves to act and respond in that way. It's a highly competitive group and we're going to need that through the course of the week. Our preparation's got to take us to Saturday kickoff."
Tennessee's defense has been the driving force behind the team's success this season and continues to deliver week after week, holding opponents to just 284.7 yards per game and an SEC leading 14.3 points per game, which also ranks seventh in the FBS.
Over their last two games against then No. 6 Kentucky and Texas A&M, the Vols' defense has allowed just 13 total points and has posted back-to-back second half shutouts.
"I think as much as anything it's their mindset how they approach every day, they've trained themselves to act and respond in that way. It's a highly competitive group and we're going to need that through the course of the week, our preparation's got to take us to Saturday kickoff," Heupel said when asked about his team's stifling second half defense.
The Big Orange will also need a solid performance from its constantly improving offensive line, as they prepare to face a vaunted LSU defense that leads the nation with 40 sacks entering Saturday's contest.
"Really good talent, length, explosiveness, power. Their guys have multiple ways to get to the quarterback and then their pressure packages are a part of that as well," Heupel said. "So, combination of all those things is why they've been good at getting to the quarterback."
Video and a transcript of Heupel's Monday press conference can be seen below.
Head Coach
Josh Heupel
Transcript (Nov. 16, 2026)
Opening statement…
"Good to see everybody. Obviously, another big football game against a really good opponent and they've lost some close football games, but they're a talented roster, good coaches, huge test, huge opportunity this Saturday. Looking forward to getting inside that stadium with our guys and our fans and get ready to go play."
On the defense being especially good in the second half of games…
"I think it's a combination of a lot of different things, the physicality and the way our guys have approached the game in the second half, some subtle tweaks to what we're doing. It's all pieces of the puzzle."
On his assessment of the defense limiting Marcel Reed…
"Did a good job playing some of the read game where he has the opportunity to potentially get out on the edge. Holistically defensively, I thought we tackled well throughout the course of the football game. We were able to apply pressure and force him to throw it maybe before he wanted to. But I think you look at the day, the third down defense was a huge part of the football game and coverage and pressure working together and when they were running the football, being able to get stops."
On how tough it is to lose
Mike Matthews
due to injury…
"You hate it. It's the hard part, ugly part of this game. The growth that he's shown, the way that he was playing. Huge part of what we're doing offensively, hate it for him, but also know because of who he is and the competitor and how he approaches his work every single day, he'll come back on the other side of this and be better for it too. In this game the next guy's got to step up and some of those young wideouts have played some football, they're going to play a lot of football here and need to be ready to go."
On what he wants to see from the receiver room with
Mike Matthews
absence…
"Yeah, Radarious (Jackson), TK (Keys), Travis (Smith Jr.), maximize their opportunities. You got to play within the scope of it, you got to defeat man coverage in this football game, really talented corners and yeah got to play assignment sound, got to understand concepts – man, zone – and got to go make plays, attack the football."
On
George MacIntyre's
knee and what they'll need from the quarterbacks this week…
"With George (MacIntyre), he looked good this morning and we'll continue to progress through the course of the week. We've played both of them here the last few weeks once Faizon (Brandon) got healthy. At the end of the day, whoever's out there's got to go play really good football. This is a really good defense. You look at them statistically, against the run, against the pass. I think they lead the conference in total defense, they're playing extremely well."
On why this team is mentally tough…
"I think as much as anything it's their mindset how they approach every day, they've trained themselves to act and respond in that way. It's a highly competitive group and we're going to need that through the course of the week, our preparation's got to take us to Saturday kickoff."
On this year's senior class...
"Yeah, a bunch of guys who have been here for multiple years, that have played a lot of meaningful football, had been a huge part of the culture inside of the building. Some guys that haven't been here as long but have continued or got in here and indoctrinated into it and have helped it continue to grow, a lot of dynamic playmakers."
On
Shamurad Umarov's
performance vs. Texas A&M...
"Sham (Umarov) did a lot of really good things. In the totality of our run game, some things he's got to clean up, but all five of them - guys got things to clean up too. I thought he was good in pass protection majority of the day as well and he's stepped right in."
On LSU's defense and their ability to get to the quarterback...
"Really good talent, length, explosiveness, power. Their guys have multiple ways to get to the quarterback and then their pressure packages are a part of that as well. So, combination of all those things is why they've been good at getting to the quarterback."
On the depth at the center position...
"We've been working in that way. We got multiple ways that we can play it. You guys have seen it, offensive line, you never know what's going to happen. Those guys got to be prepared at multiple positions."
On building mental toughness and facing adversity in this day and age of college football...
"Everything inside your program's tougher because there's more turnover every single year and you guys have heard me say it, when you start in your offseason, typically the third week of January, you're starting at ground zero and you got to build it from there."
On LSU's pass defense and what stands out...
"They play a lot of tight man. They understand their zone principles when they get into it too, but they got really good skill. This is an extremely talented football team. Both sides of the football, as talented as you're going to see inside of this league. They've lost a couple close ball games, but it's a really good football team and they're back-end talented and can test everything."
On the engagement level of
George MacIntyre
and
Faizon Brandon
throughout the QB changes...
"I think their mental approach has been that of maturity, which is hard to do because you don't know when your opportunities coming. Both sides, both guys, have been the two (second string) and have continued to prepare and practice in a way that gives them the opportunity to go play the way they need to when they get their opportunity, and you guys have seen that for the last couple weeks."
On what he liked from
Trent Thomas
..."
"Trent's done a really good job, he continues to get better fundamentally throughout the course of the year. He's done a good job in pass protection as well, we have no problems using him in pass patterns as well. Anyways, I like the growth that he's had, came in with some experience, he's mature in the way that he approaches every day and that's why he's continued to get better."
