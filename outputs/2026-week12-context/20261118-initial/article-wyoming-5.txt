Wyoming Game Notes
Share:
Pokes have won five-straight contests, Both Teams getting Votes in AP Poll
By:
Nick Seeman
LARAMIE, Wyo. (Nov. 16, 2026) –
The Wyoming Cowboys will host the New Mexico Lobos in a high-stakes matchup that will have Mountain West Championship game implications on Friday evening at 7 p.m.
Light Up Laradise
Wyoming Athletics is calling on Cowboy Country to help create one of the most electric atmospheres in War Memorial Stadium history with
"Light Up Laradise,"
a first-of-its-kind fan experience set for Friday, Nov. 20, when the Wyoming Cowboys host nationally ranked New Mexico at 7 p.m.
As part of the Light Up Laradise initiative, every fan in attendance will receive a complimentary, interactive LED wristband that will illuminate and change colors in synchronization with the stadium's music, videoboards and in-game entertainment.
Ticket Information
Fans may order tickets online, via email or by phone at:
•Go to GoWyo.com/tickets
•Email tickets@uwyo.edu
•Call (307) 766-7220
•Stop by the UW Athletics Ticket Office on the West Side of the Arena-Auditorium on the corner of Willett Drive and 19th Street
Contest on FS1
Saturday's contest will be televised on FS1 and the Fox One App with Evan Lepler on the call with Tanner Morgan analyzing the action.
Listen Live
The game will also be available on the 26 stations of The Cowboy Sports Network with Keith Kelley on the call, Kevin McKinney on color and Erick Pauley on the sidelines.
Military Appreciation Game
Helps us celebrate our Military during Friday's contest with the Lobos. Veterans will be honored during the game and all active military and veterans can claim tickets to the game on Vet Tickets while tickets are available.
Student Tailgate
Join President Reeves for a pregame tailgate starting at 3:30 PM in the Indoor Practice Facility, featuring special food and exclusive shirts for the first 1,000 guests. Shirts and food are first come, first serve so get there early!
Wyoming Notes
Back at Home:
Wyoming is back at home for the first time in barely a month when they host New Mexico on Friday. It is the third Friday game of the season for the Pokes, as a win for the Brown and Gold would secure them a spot as well as an opportunity to host the Mountain West Championship game for the first time since 2016.
AP Poll:
Wyoming received votes in the Associated Press Poll for the first time since the final poll of the 2023 season on Jan. 9, 2024. Wyoming got five votes in the polls this week.
Five in a Row:
Wyoming earned a 27-15 win on the road at UTEP on Saturday. It marked the fifth-straight for the Pokes and the longest winning streak since the Cowboys won five-straight during the 2016 season. The Pokes have a chance to earn a six-game win streak for the longest since UW had a seven game win streak during the 1998 season.
The Last Five Games:
The Cowboys have been playing their best football over the last five games. Wyoming has averaged 30.6 points per game and are allowing 20 points per game.
The Wyoming offense is averaging 416.4 yards of total offense per game and have rushed for 247 yards and thrown for 169.4 yard per contest. The defense allows 342.8 yards per game with opponents rushing for 187.2 yards and throwing for 155.6 yards.
Sims Has Career Day:
Wyoming quarterback
Landon Sims
had a career day in his first start. He was 19-of-25 passing in the contest with a touchdown. He led the Pokes to 24 points in the opening frame, as he recorded 169 passing yards on 10-of-14 passing. He finished the day with 289 passing yards for the most by the Pokes this season.
Stuhlsatz in Double-Digits:
Wyoming linebacker
Ethan Stuhlsatz
recorded 11 tackles for the Pokes at UTEP. It marked the sixth time this season that he has recorded 10 or more tackles and the third straight-game he has recorded 10 or more stops. He has 80 tackles on the season to rank third in the MW and No. 30 in the nation at 8.9 tackles per game.
Tote Finds The Endzone Some More:
Wyoming running back Tote Harris found the endzone twice at UTEP marking back-to-back weeks with two scores. He had a 49-yard touchdown reception and a 33-yard rushing score on a fourth down play. Over the last week he has recorded three rushing scores and one touchdown reception. He has five rushing touchdowns and two receiving scores this season.
Frendt Has Career Day:
Wyoming tight end
Kyle Frendt
had a career day for the Pokes at UTEP. He recorded four catches in the contest and had 113 receiving yards for a career best. It was the first 100-yard receiving game for the Pokes since
Chris Durr Jr.
recorded 146 yards against Akron in 2025's season opener. It was the most by a tight end since
John Michael Gyllenborg
had 137 at San Jose State in 2024.
Holman Does Something Special:
Wyoming running back
Markell Holman
threw for a touchdown in the win at UTEP. He found tight end
Landon Pace
from three yards out.  It was a touchdown for the Pokes by a non-QB since Austin Conway threw a TD to Josh Allen at Nevada in 2016.  It marked the first time a running back has thrown for a touchdown for the Pokes since Wynel Seldon did so against Colorado State in 2008.
Pace Finds End Zone for First Time:
Wyoming tight end
Landon Pace
caught his first career pass at UTEP. It happened to be for a touchdown that came from running back
Markell Holman
. Pace has appeared in all 10 games for the Pokes. He is the son of NFL Hall of Famer Orlando Pace, who played offensive tackle in the NFL.
About New Mexico
The Lobos are coming off a 21-17 loss to UNLV last Saturday in Albuquerque. New Mexico, which was ranked in the Associated Press poll for the first time in program history
New Mexico quarterback Toa Fa'avae was 7-for-14 passing for 40 yards with one touchdown and one interception against UNLV. The sophomore has passed for 1,081 yards with eight touchdowns and eight interceptions this season.
Senior running back Scottre Humphrey is second in the MW with 736 yards rushing. Junior running back Kiefer Sibley has added 616 yards on the ground. The duo has a combined 18 rushing touchdowns.
Defensively, the Lobos are No. 2 in the FBS in points allowed (11.9 per game) and yards allowed (248.9 per game). Senior defensive end Darren Ago leads the MW with 16.0 tackles for loss and senior defensive end Brett Karhu is third with 10.0 tackles for loss.
The Lobos lead the nation in turnover margin (+19). New Mexico has 18 interceptions and 10 recovered fumbles while throwing eight interceptions and losing only one fumble.
