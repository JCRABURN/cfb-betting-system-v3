James Wagenseller: Flipping the Field for the Black Knights
November 16, 2026
|
Football
Share:
WEST POINT, N.Y.
– For Army senior
James Wagenseller
, punting is all about the snap and the coverage downfield by his teammates.
Based upon the Black Knights' national standing in nearly every punting-related NCAA statistical category, Wagenseller is absolutely correct.
Army is ranked second nationally in net punting with a 45.57 yards per punt average. The Black Knights are ranked just behind Texas A&M of the SEC, which averages 46.95 net yards per punt. Wagenseller's career-high 48.18 yards per punt average would rank second nationally, but his 3.1 punts per game average is just below the 3.6 punts per game average to qualify for the national rankings.
Wagenseller's career-high 62.0 yards per punt average against Air Force is the third-best single-game punting average in college football this season. He launched punts of 65 and 59 yards against the Falcons. One week earlier, Wagenseller booted his career-long punt of 74 yards in a driving rainstorm against Memphis.
Army's lofty special team's statistics have placed Wagenseller among the elite punters in the nation.
He was recently named as one of the top 10 semifinalists for the Ray Guy Award. Wagenseller is the first punter in Army football history to be named a semifinalist for the Ray Guy Award. Since 2000, the award has been presented to the nation's top punter in honor of Southern Miss graduate and Oakland Raider Hall of Fame punter Ray Guy.
"I'm just thankful for all the opportunities that the Lord and this school have given me," said Wagenseller following practice on Sunday. "I'm thankful for my teammates; I can't do it without them. I can't do it without the snap (from
Owen Walter
), and I can't do it without the coverage from my teammates. It's really a team effort."
Wagenseller, like many of the great kickers and punters in college football, comes from a soccer background. It wasn't until his junior year of high school that he became a standout kicking specialist. As a senior, he earned First Team All-League honors.
"I played soccer my whole life, through the prep school when I enrolled at West Point," said Wagenseller. "During my sophomore season in high school, my soccer coach told me that the JV football team needed a kicker and punter and that's where my kicking career took off."
As an Army Cadet, Wagenseller followed his father, MAJ Ward Wagenseller (a retired US Army officer), and his brother, Robert Wagenseller (a second lieutenant in the US Army), to the United States Military Academy in 2023.
Wagenseller became Army's starting punter in 2024. He averaged 41.67 yards per punt as a sophomore. The systems engineering major improved his punting average to 43.12 yards as a junior and to his current career-high average of 48.18 yards per punt as a senior. Wagenseller's punting average this season (48.18) ranks second in school history for a single season and is a mere one-hundredth of a yard off the single-season school record held by Zach Harding in 2019 (48.19 yards per punt).
Wagenseller averages 44.2 yards on 84 career punts—the second-best career punting average in program history. He is one of just three punters in school history to average better than 44 yards per punt for his career as a Black Knight.
"The main thing I've worked on since last season is my consistency," said Wagenseller.
"Last season there were a couple of balls that could have been better. I come out to practice every day and work on my consistency. I've also worked on my mental toughness. Punting and kicking are very mental. The two positions are as much mental as they are physical. The mental side of punting has strengthened my abilities as a punter."
For Wagenseller, punting will always be about his teammates.
"Punting is and will always be a team effort for me," said Wagenseller. "It doesn't matter how good I am. If we can't cover or if we can't tackle the returner, then my punts don't matter. The key is the discipline and the team we've built to run down and cover the punts."
For Army, ranking among the nation's leaders in net punting and punt return defense (5.5 yards per opponent return) certainly takes a Herculean effort each time the Black Knights line up in punt formation.
It also helps to have a Ray Guy Award semifinalist who puts his teammates on special teams in position to defend those punts returns.
