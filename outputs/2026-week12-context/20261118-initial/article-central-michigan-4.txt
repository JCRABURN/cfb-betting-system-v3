Chippewas take on Bulls, who are tied for first place in the MAC, on Wednesday night
By:
Andy Sneddon
MOUNT PLEASANT, Mich. –
Certainly, bowl eligibility is at the forefront for the Central Michigan football team.
More importantly, The Chippewas' Mid-American Conference game at Buffalo (7:30 p.m.) may prove to be a measuring stick as to where they stand as a program under second-year coach
Matt Drinkall
.
"This is an opportunity to really kind of propel (the program) to a different status level," Drinkall said. "What would it mean? It's kind of like as rough and up and down as the season has been, winning this game would get you bowl eligible, you beat a really good team, it kind of puts a cap on a successful season. And now the whole narrative of everything here is starting to change. So I think from that impact it has a huge significance."
The Chippewas (5-5, 3-3 MAC) have won two straight games in dominant fashion, which bodes well as they step onto the field against a Buffalo team (7-3, 5-1) that has won five consecutive and is in a four-way tie for first place in the MAC with Toledo, Ohio and Western Michigan.
Drinkall and his Chippewas can point to several affirming factors that they are, indeed, ready to a spot among the upper echelon of MAC programs.
For one, they stand on the cusp of bowl eligibility and are 5-5 while standing hard against a schedule that has proven as difficult as any team in the MAC.
They split games with Wyoming (win) and New Mexico (loss), which are now first and second, respectively, in the Mountain West Conference, and the Lobos' defense ranks second nationally. CMU also faced then-No. 6/5 Miami (Fla.) on the road and then went head to head with Ohio, Western Michigan and defending MAC-runnerup Miami (Ohio) on consecutive weekends in league games.
"The resiliency of our kids has been fantastic," Drinkall said. "Like you're going through the car wash (and) just getting hit in the face with a bat over and over and over again. There's a lot of times you can quit or have hiccups along the way; I'm incredibly impressed and, like, humbled by how our kids have been able to persevere and how resilient they have been."
Telling about how much the Chippewas have improved, and their potential, is what they have done in their last two games, a 35-7 win over Eastern Michigan and a 28-7 victory over Sacramento State.
The Chippewas have averaged 5.08 yards per rush and scored eight touchdowns on the ground. In the two games, CMU has posted a combined 2-to-1 advantage in possession time, outgained its opponents by 354 yards, and rolled up 31 more first downs than their two opponents.
"If you continue to do those things, regardless of the opponent you play, the games start to get framed in your favor," Drinkall said. "Where it's kind of like the recipe for those games -- those last two games were cooked well."
And the Chippewas, who haven't won on the road this season,will have
cook well
in order to beat the Bulls in Buffalo.
The Bulls have found a knack for winning close games. Two of their victories this season have come in overtime, and they are 5-1 in one-score games. Bulls running back Messiah Burch leads the MAC with 1,110 rushing yards.
"They're as scary of an opponent as you can run into," Drinkall said. "Their culture is really good, they're really well coached, they've got great players, good depth and they're playing really well.
"If you want to go to a bowl game, you're going to have to do everything right, and then some, in this one."
