Dominant Ducks Maintain Momentum, Beat Michigan
11/14/26
|
Football
Box Score
Photo Gallery
Share:
No. 16 Oregon used explosive offense and stifling defense to take care of the visiting Wolverines on Saturday, 31-7.
By:
Rob Moseley
EUGENE, Ore. —
The 2026 Oregon Ducks are starting to consistently look like what most expected the 2026 Oregon Ducks to look like.
No. 16 Oregon followed its wipeout win at Ohio State by suffocating Michigan on Saturday in Autzen Stadium, 31-7. The Ducks were one broken play away from a defensive shutout, and they leaned on their playmakers offensively to post the best performance of the season against the vaunted Wolverines defense.
There were moments at Ohio State, UO coach
Dan Lanning
said this week, when the Ducks looked as good as any team in the country. There were similar moments Saturday.
"We made a decision on Monday when we came in that we weren't going to let last week's game be our high point of the season," UO quarterback
Dylan Raiola
said after throwing four touchdown passes against the Wolverines. "We knew there was a lot of growth (to take from) the last game, and even this game — as good as it looks on paper — there's a lot of holes that we need to fix. … It's a good feeling knowing that you put up a good performance, but there's better out there that we can tap into."
The Ducks improved to 7-3 overall and 5-2 in the Big Ten on Saturday. Indiana's win Saturday over USC made a conference title-game berth less likely, but Oregon's path to a College Football Playoff at-large berth remains open.
To get there, Oregon will need help. The biggest thing the Ducks can do is continue to help themselves, at Michigan State on short rest this Friday before closing out the regular season at home against Washington.
"Everyone's aware of what could happen, but none of that happens if we don't handle our business," Raiola said. "So we just try to take it a week at a time and let everything else play itself out. Not worry about other people's results, but really our process and our results."
The results Saturday were largely impressive. Raiola was 20-of-28 for 341 yards and four scores, and both
Dakorien Moore
and
Jeremiah McClellan
had more than 100 receiving yards on the strength of long touchdown receptions.
Dierre Hill Jr.
rushed for 98 yards and added a 49-yard touchdown reception, as the Ducks piled up 467 yards of offense; Michigan hadn't previously allowed more than 21 points or 332 yards in a game this season.
"We have weapons out there, and it makes it really hard for people to make a decision how they're going to play coverage on us," UO coach
Dan Lanning
said. "And then obviously you have to have the ability to distribute the ball to them. Once we've cleaned up some things when it comes to protection and created some schemes that really fit our guys … I think it shows that we can be pretty dynamic."
Oregon's defense, playing for the first time without injured senior leader
Teitum Tuioti
, allowed only a long touchdown run in the second quarter. Otherwise the Ducks stifled the Wolverines, limiting them to 71 passing yards while forcing two fumbles in the second half.
Dylan Williams
started at inside linebacker and led the Ducks with 10 tackles.
Devon Jackson
continued his strong senior season with nine tackles, freshman
Jett Washington
had a pass breakup on fourth down to give Oregon a turnover on downs inside the 5-yard line in the third quarter, and
Matayo Uiagalelei
had two sacks to reach 28 in his career, two away from a new UO record.
"I thought everybody on defense played well, played hard," Uiagalelei said. "We didn't have anybody have to be a hero, (just) everybody doing our job, and the result was what it was."
The Ducks opened the game with a 60-yard touchdown reception by McClellan, and went up 14-0 later in the opening period when Raiola hit running back
Da'Jaun Riggs
for another score. Moore hauled in a 59-yard TD pass just before halftime, and Hill made it 28-7 with yet another explosion play in the fourth quarter.
McClellan's touchdown was his first since the opening game of the season, and since suffering a foot injury that sidelined him for five games.
"It feels amazing just to be back out there making plays for the Ducks, making plays for the offense," he said.
As Raiola said, the Ducks will find areas to clean up from Saturday. The offense had a mid-game lull, the defense had some sort of breakdown on the long Michigan touchdown, a field goal was missed and the team had seven penalties — all in the second half.
The performance a week earlier wasn't perfect either, but the Ducks were able to take lessons from a win and maintain their momentum Saturday. That's the goal over the next two weeks, with potentially much to play for in the postseason if Oregon continues to take care of business.
