Neal Anderson Selected to 2026-27 Florida Sports Hall of Fame Class
Wednesday, November 18, 2026
|
Football
Share:
Anderson ranks third in career rushing yards (3,234) and sixth in rushing touchdowns (30) in UF history.
By:
Sullivan Bortner
GAINESVILLE, Fla. – Florida Athletic Hall of Famer and former star Gators running back Neal Anderson headlines a seven-member 2026-27 class elected for induction into the Florida Sports Hall of Fame.
Donning the Orange & Blue from 1982-85, Anderson and the other six inductees will be formally enshrined Sunday, May 2, 2027, at the Dr. Phillips Center for the Performing Arts in downtown Orlando, Fla. The class features four different sports and is rounded out by former Tampa Bay Buccaneers quarterback Doug Williams, 2003 U.S. Open champion Jim Furyk, two-time major champion Jane Geddes, former NFL standout Anquan Boldin, broadcaster Paul Kennedy and longtime baseball manager Don Zimmer.
"We are excited to recognize such a talented and accomplished class, whose members have made tremendous contributions to their respective sports," said Keyna Cory, chair of the Florida Sports Hall of Fame board. "Their achievements on the ﬁeld, on the course and behind the microphone, along with their impact on their communities, make this a truly remarkable class. We are honored to welcome them as the newest members of the Florida Sports Hall of Fame."
Holding status as one of Florida's all-time great running backs, Anderson rushed for 3,234 yards and 30 touchdowns during his Gators career prior to being selected by the Chicago Bears in the ﬁrst round (27th overall) of the 1986 NFL Draft. He trails only Errict Rhett and Emmitt Smith in career rushing yards at Florida and is tied with current UF running back
Jadan Baugh
for sixth in career rushing touchdowns. Anderson also recorded 46 receptions for 525 yards and two receiving touchdowns during his UF tenure while throwing two touchdown passes on trick plays during his 1985 senior campaign.
Powering the Gators to back-to-back 9-1-1 seasons from 1984-85, Anderson was the recipient of numerous accolades during his career. In addition to captaining the 1985 squad, Anderson was honored as two-time AP Honorable Mention All-American (1984, 1985) 1985 First-Team All-SEC, 1985 Fergie Ferguson Award winner and two-time Second-Team All-SEC (1983, 1984).
Going on to become a cornerstone of the Bears' oﬀense, Anderson succeeded legendary running back Walter Payton as the team's starter. During eight seasons in Chicago (1986-93), Anderson earned four Pro Bowl selections and established himself as one of the NFL's premier running backs. In his NFL career, Anderson rushed for 6,166 yards and 51 touchdowns while adding 302 catches for 2,763 yards and 20 receiving scores. He ranks second in Bears franchise history in rushing touchdowns and sits third in rushing yards.
ABOUT THE FLORIDA SPORTS HALL OF FAME
Established in 1961, the Florida Sports Hall of Fame celebrates the individuals whose achievements, leadership and contributions have helped make Florida a cornerstone of the nation's sports landscape. Through its annual enshrinement ceremony, fundraising initiatives and educational outreach, the organization uses the inspirational stories of its inductees to support organizations that encourage Florida's youth to participate in sports, particularly those in underserved communities. The Florida Sports Hall of Fame attraction is located at the MidFlorida Event Center in Port St. Lucie, Fla.
