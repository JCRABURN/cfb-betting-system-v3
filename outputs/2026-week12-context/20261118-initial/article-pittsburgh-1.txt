Narduzzi Notebook: Effort Isn’t the Issue as Pitt Turns to Louisville
11/16/2026 4:52:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
By:
Abby Schnable
Share:
Pitt's 6-4 after a disappointing Friday night loss to Florida State, the Panthers' third ACC loss of a touchdown or less this season. The defense held the Seminoles to 260 total yards, the lowest figure they've had all year, but the offense couldn't keep pace after halftime.
Head Coach
Pat Narduzzi
said the film session showed a team that played hard but left too many plays on the field. Now Pitt turns to a Louisville team riding a five-game winning streak.
Dailey is still developing, and Geriner is trending the right way
Narduzzi acknowledged that freshman quarterback
Corey Dailey
isn't yet the finished product Pitt saw from
Mason Heintschel
a year ago. He said the problem is mostly vision.
When Pitt watched the 12-play highlight reel, receivers were open and Dailey missed them. The first snap of the second half is an example: C.J. Lee beat his man and was wide open, but the throw went to the flat.
"Corey is not there yet, and he's getting there. He did some nice things, like I said in the first half."
Narduzzi stressed that Dailey has the arm and the potential, and he expects a big jump after a spring and another fall camp.
As for
Holden Geriner
, Narduzzi said he's closer than he was a week ago and feels noticeably better this Monday than he did last Monday, though he wouldn't commit to anything. Either way, his message was that Dailey has to be ready.
Beau Jackson
would have been the next man up had another backup been needed last week, and Narduzzi said he got most of the second-team reps.
Narduzzi defends
Malik Knight's
effort on the late deep ball
Asked about the fourth-quarter throw up the right sideline, Narduzzi said he hasn't talked to Knight about it, but he bristled at any suggestion of a lack of effort. In his view the pass was overthrown, landing at or just inside the end line. He thinks Malik was still tracking it and didn't have a realistic chance to put his arms up.
"Just watch him run," Narduzzi said. "You don't go sprinting down the field. You loaf down the field if you don't want to catch a ball. But Malik ran his tail off."
Pitt's second-half offense fell apart
Narduzzi opened by pointing to the third-down numbers.
Pitt converted 56 percent of its money downs in the first half and one of 10 in the second. He praised the team's effort and attitude, but said the execution wasn't there, and the film shows a series of small breakdowns: a back missing a cut, a tight end or lineman not finishing a block, drops that made it harder on the quarterback.
"We just got to finish blocks up front," Narduzzi said. "That was the disappointing thing. I didn't think we played as well up front. Finishing blocks and getting the run game going like we need to and have to with our you know our quarterback situation."
He singled out Ja'Kyrian "Boosie" Turner and Damon Ferguson as the top two backs. He wishes both were on the field more. Asked how to get the run game back to its Georgia Tech level, his answer was simple: practice, with the details demanded.
Crothers' versatility on the defensive line
With Pitt down a tackle,
Zach Crothers
played both inside and outside against Florida State. Narduzzi said he's healthy, played well in both spots, and has the tools to be an NFL tackle.
When asked about a permanent move inside, he said maybe down the road, but nothing is changing now and he hasn't discussed it with Crothers. For now the goal is getting the best four linemen on the field.
"He's so strong," Narduzzi said. "He's so physical. He's got great instincts. He can get off blocks. He sheds people well. I like him on the inside. I like him on the outside. The great thing is he's tough and physical enough to play inside."
Louisville presents a challenge
Narduzzi called Louisville one of the most explosive offenses in the country and said it may be as good as Miami's.
He also praised Jeff Brohm's team for being one of the best rushing offenses nationally and for riding a five-game winning streak. Its defense has also improved since the NC State game, he said, and he called the Cardinals fast and physical.
"You stop that guy, that guy's going to beat you," Narduzzi said. "They got weapons all over the place. They're an explosive, explosive offense. So our defense, it's a great matchup. Our defense versus our offense. See what we got."
