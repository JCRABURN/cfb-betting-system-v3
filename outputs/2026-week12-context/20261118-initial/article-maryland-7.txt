Built to Belong on the Big Stage
Chris Durr Jr. arrived at Maryland with something to prove. Now, the first-year Terp is showing he belongs on the B1G stage.
Brady Ruth, Staff Writer
11/18/2026
He caught the pass behind the line of scrimmage. With a battalion of Boilermakers bearing down on him, the first-year Terp stood on his own 10-yard line.
What followed was a play Maryland fans won't soon forget.
Chris Durr Jr. weaved his way through the Purdue defense for a remarkable 87-yard touchdown, extending the Maryland lead late in the fourth quarter of the Terps’ 34-29 win.
The touchdown has come to define a highlight-reel season for Durr Jr. in his first year in the Big Ten.
I was really just thinking about getting the first down,. When I started to find the lane, I thought why not go score? Why not make a play for the team and add to the lead? I started making moves and once I found a lane, it was over from there. I knew no one was going to catch me.
Chris Durr Jr.
Much like that spectacular touchdown, Durr Jr.'s football journey has been defined by twists and turns, an ability to adapt and, above all, confidence in himself and an unwavering focus on what's next.
The Chicago native wasn’t heavily recruited. The collegiate offers weren’t flowing in, but his belief in himself never wavered. The self-driven Durr Jr. pushed himself at every opportunity to make it to the Power 4 level, and he’s already cemented the best season of his career in his debut campaign with the Terps.
“I just want to show that I belong here,” Durr Jr. said. “Being a guy that was highly under-recruited, I’ve always had that internal motivation. My dad has always told me don’t get complacent in life because at the end of the day, memory is short. Anybody can forget anything, so I’m always working to be better than last year, because the player you see today is not the player that you will see next year.”
As a high schooler, Durr Jr. hadn’t attracted much attention from recruiters, but he never lacked support. His parents and friends continually pushed him to be his best. His parents broke down games with him, highlighting both the plays he made and the opportunities he could have taken advantage of.
His friends offered encouragement and reminded him that one day, before long, everyone was going to know who he was.
His college journey began in Laramie, Wyoming, where he laced up his cleats for the Cowboys on Saturdays in the Mountain West Conference.
Across his two seasons with Wyoming, Durr Jr. tallied 76 receptions for 817 yards and five touchdowns. His on-field production was substantial, but the lessons he learned away from the game proved just as valuable.
I learned that everything is not going to go your way. You’ve got to really take the time to hone in on your body. That was something that I didn’t take too seriously in high school. I’m glad I got a chance to get the silly mistakes out of the way in Wyoming, and now everything’s professional.
Chris Durr Jr.
Small-town life in Wyoming provided a different student-athlete experience than the spotlight of the DMV. It gave him time to focus on his eating and sleeping habits, learn how to care for himself and make a name for himself in the Mountain West.
Playing for the Cowboys gave Durr Jr. the opportunity to focus solely on football without the distractions of a major market or national attention. He developed both his game and his character while preparing for a bigger stage. He didn't know where that opportunity would come. He was just waiting for a chance.
“All I needed was a foot in the door,” Durr Jr. said. “Playing against big teams like Utah and Colorado and having good games against them let me know that all I needed to do was get to a Power 4 school, and Maryland gave me a chance.”
It’s been a historic season for Durr Jr. His five games with 100-plus receiving yards in 2026 are tied for the most by a Terp in a single season. He’s already surpassed 800 receiving yards and hauled in five touchdown receptions.
Durr Jr. has played a key role in the Terps’ recent two-game winning streak, helping fuel the team's success down the stretch of the 2026 season.
“We knew we could do this,” Durr Jr. said. “All the hard work is starting to pay off. We knew we were going to put in a good week of work and get it done. We got the win, and all we needed was one for momentum. It’s only up from here.”
There have been plenty of standout moments to choose from, but his first time taking the field as a Terp is one that will stick with him for a long time.
“I low-key cried,” Durr Jr. said. “Coming out and seeing the stadium, it was huge. When I saw everyone at the game, I was like, ‘Damn, I’m finally here. It’s time to perform.’ Everything else was up to me now.”
For Durr Jr., it hasn’t just been about the numbers, but the people. Driven by the relationships he’s built with his teammates — specifically quarterback Malik Washington, who he lives just a few doors down from — Durr Jr. is finding the brotherhood within the game that Maryland prides itself on.
It’s bigger than football. It’s about connecting with people and building relationships. It usually takes years to develop relationships between quarterbacks and wide receivers. I’m glad we were able to build ours in months.
Chris Durr Jr.
Durr Jr. is finally home. Uprooting his life to make the move to the Big Ten was necessary, but it wasn't without its challenges. Now settled in with the people around him and the program he’s helping build, Durr Jr. looks forward to the years ahead in red, black and gold, continuing to prove something he’s always known: he belongs on the big stage.
