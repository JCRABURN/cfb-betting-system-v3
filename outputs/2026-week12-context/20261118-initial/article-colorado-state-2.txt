Monday Presser: Defense Getting Influx of Youth
11/16/2026 1:03:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
Mora, team hunting value in final weeks
By:
Mike Brohard
The same game
Jaden Hernandez
was getting his first defensive reps in game action,
Kallen Martinez
was playing his first game, period. That same night,
Jaylen King
was gaining more experience as a linebacker, and so was Xavier Winbush in the secondary.
The offense isn't the only unit where freshmen are earning some playing time, and
Jim Mora
said it's on the staff to have them prepared for bigger roles the final two weeks.
"Just get them more practice reps.
Jaden Hernandez
has not taken one snap with the defense," Mora said Monday at his weekly press conference. "He's been on the scout team all year and he wouldn't even play. We had to move
Khamani Potts
down from linebacker to defensive end. He had not done that. Like
Ace Brown
, same thing. So just the more football that those guys can play, the better they're going to get.
"I was excited to see the energy and enthusiasm and the effort that they played with given the fact that they haven't taken any reps. And that doesn't even include Ced (Ross) and what he's done because he's been consistent all year. So you never give up on the season. You play the best players. You play the guys that are going to give you a chance to win, but you also give some of those young players that really work hard and show them that they deserve the chance to go out on the field and play and give them that opportunity when you can."
The NCAA's five-for-five ruling opened up a different door for coaches. There was still somewhat of a buffer in the past (a player could appear in up to four games and still redshirt), but now the restrictions are off. Winbush played in his fifth game, which would have cost him a year in the past.
Not now.
Jaylen King
has been gaining more time on defense and finished with six tackles, including a half of a stop for a loss. Winbush has seen more defensive snaps as well; he had a pair of tackles.
Hernandez had three tackles, Martinez four, three of them solo. All of them earning chances based off their work in practice.
"I think you have to reward them. You ask them to work hard, and you tell them if they work hard they're going to get an opportunity, and then you have to make sure you back that up by giving them an opportunity," Mora said. "Kallen in particular, the last couple of weeks he's practiced really well. And we switched positions for him when we moved him off the service team onto the depth defense, and he's just done a great job. And then during the game, after every series, he was bringing me the iPad, and he wanted to go through every play right there in real time so he could go out in the next series and be even better. And that's impressive.
"That's the type of mindset you want in your players. Same with
Jordan Lessard
. Now he's been playing, but he's constantly striving to try to find ways to get better, and you love that, and I saw him get better during the game just from things he looked at on the iPad between series, plays he could learn in real time and apply in real time."
Still Much to Gain
The season will end in two weeks. There is no postseason game waiting for Colorado State, yet it doesn't mean the season is over. Nor the lessons.
Mora stressed there is value to be gained in these final two contests. Not just in football, but in life, and he realizes some of them wonder what he's talking about when he equates the struggles of a college season to being a father later in life.
It doesn't naturally translate to some of the younger players. Showing, Mora said, is leading.
"I think it's more you demonstrate it than anything. So you go into meetings with enthusiasm, with the same type of mindset that you went into the very first meeting before the Wyoming game," he said. "I think that you go out on the practice field and you coach with a huge amount of enthusiasm, just like you did at any other point in the season.
"It's your attitude when you walk into the meal room is, hey, it's not phony, but it's still upbeat. Because they want someone … They want people who they can look at who are demonstrating the way that they're supposed to act if they want to get through this."
To Clarify
Mora took the opportunity to walk back a comment he made after the Washington State loss when his wording suggested the university as a whole needed to learn how to win, phrasing which naturally didn't sit well with everyone in the Colorado State athletic community.
"I think I said something the other night that maybe came off wrong about winning at CSU, and I was talking specifically about football, but probably was taken wrong, which is my fault," he said. "We just have to create a culture of winning within our football program and that's going to start with that group I just talked about being on the same page and doing it the right way. There's a commitment to doing that."
Those people in particular referenced were members of athletic administration, as well as outgoing university president Amy Parsons.
More also said that night he was as determined as ever to turn the program around, and he doesn't feel he's alone at the wheel.
"They're all sitting in here, and they're sitting in that locker room, and they're sitting up in the coaches' offices, and in our personnel department and operations," Mora said. "There's a real determination. There's no give in. There's no quit. You can see it in the players' eyes this morning, the staff's eyes, and the way they reacted."
