Hot Start Fuels No. 10 BYU to Win over Bears in Provo

Baylor Postgame Notes (PDF)

PROVO, Utah –
No. 10 BYU used a hot start to drive a 28-7 win over Baylor football, in front of 63,008 on Saturday at LaVell Edwards Stadium.
Baylor (6-4, 4-3 Big 12 Conference) couldn't overcome a sluggish start as BYU (8-2, 5-2 Big 12) put up a pair of quick, first-quarter touchdowns to jump out to an early lead that the Bears couldn't recover from. The Bears rallied to cut the lead to 21-7 in the fourth quarter but saw the Cougars extend the lead to 28-7 in the closing minutes.
The loss for BU snapped a two-game winning streak under interim head coach
Joe Klanderman
, who had led the Bears to wins at UCF and vs. Iowa State over the last two weeks. The Bears entered the game needing wins in their last three games - which featured staff tests at top-10 BYU, vs. No. 10 Texas Tech and at No. 14 Houston - to vault into the Big 12 Championship Game contention.
Meanwhile, BYU entered the game needing to snap a stretch of two losses in the last three outings to remain in the hunt for the College Football Playoff. The Cougars suffered a loss at UCF, won vs. Arizona State, and last week fell 28-20 at now-No. 2 Utah.
The Baylor offense was thwarted throughout the game, as the Cougars held the Bears to just 238 yards of total offense, including 159 yards passing and 79 yards rushing. The Cougars rolled to 220 yards of offense in the first half and the Baylor defense stiffened in the second stanza, holding BYU to just 92 yards of total offense in the final two periods.
The Bears had their best chance to get back into the game in the fourth quarter, trailing 21-7, when it narrowly missed a potential pick-6 with 9:44 left on a third-down stop. The Bears forced a punt regardless but couldn't get back into the endzone. A 44-yard return on a 64-yard punt from
Palmer Williams
set up the Cougars for their eventual game-clinching TD.
The Baylor offense saw QB
DJ Lagway
lead the way on the ground and in the air. Lagway, a native of Willis, Texas, rushed 11 times for 42 yards, and threw for 159 yards and a score on 21-of-37 passing. He found WR
Gavin Freeman
six times for 37 yards, with
Jadon Porter
owning a career-high five catches with 50 yards.
DeBraun Hampton
also added a career high with three catches for 31 yards.
Lagway found TE
Matthew Klopfenstein
for a three-yard TD in the fourth quarter, his second touchdown of the year.
BYU QB Bear Beachmeier rushed for three scores and threw for another, totaling 15 carries for 54 yards, while throwing for 104 yards on 14-of-24 passing. LJ Martin rushed six times for 49 yards, Sione Moa added seven rushes for 37 yards and Preston Rex had a team-leading four catches, with Walker Lyons owning three grabs for 49 yards.
The Bears will return to action on Saturday, hosting No. 12/12/10 Texas Tech at McLane Stadium.
After a quick three-and-out on its first possession, the Bears set up BYU with its first possession starting at its own 42-yard line. The Cougs drove down the field, using a 21-yard pass to Walker Lyons to reach the red zone. Bachmeier punched it in on a scramble from nine yards out on third down, for his eighth rushing TD of the year, to put BYU up 7-0 with 9:14 left in the first.
BYU forced another Baylor punt on its second drive of the game and marched 86 yards to take a 14-0 lead with seconds left in the first quarter, with Bachmeier punching it in from five yards out for his ninth rushing score of the year.
Following another BYU defensive stop, the Cougs took over on their own four-yard line. The Cougars took it 96 yards on 18 plays, consuming over nine minutes on the clock, to score on a one-yard rush from Bachmeier to take a 21-0 lead with 1:40 left in the first half.
At the break, BYU held a 220-54 margin in total yards, including 150-31 in rush and 70-23 in passing yards. Baylor was 3-for-7 on third downs, while BYU was 6-for-8 and went 1-for-1 on fourth downs. They held a 21:07-8:53 lead in time of possession.
The Bears got on the board with 11:18 left in the fourth quarter on an eight-play, 73-yard drive that took 2:10 off the clock. It ended in a Lagway TD pass from three yards out to Klopfenstein. It marked Klopfenstein's second TD of the year and the fourth of his career.
The Cougars pushed the lead to the final margin with a three-play, 26-yard drive that ended in a Bachmeier pass to Jojo Phillips.
