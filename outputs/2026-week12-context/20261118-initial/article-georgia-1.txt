Smart, Players Talk South Carolina

ATHENS, Ga. -
Georgia head football coach
Kirby Smart
alongside junior running back
Nate Frazier
and junior defensive back
Ellis Robinson IV
spoke with the media on Monday afternoon and offered the following comments.
Head Coach
Kirby Smart
Opening Statement
"I'm excited this week to get another opportunity to go on the road at night and play at a venue that is very tough to play. I have a lot of respect for South Carolina's program and the atmosphere they create. I have played there, personally, and coached there for what seems like many times. They do an incredible job with a good game day atmosphere and make it exciting for both teams in terms of the environment you get to play. That's why you come to the SEC to get a chance to play in those kinds of games. We'll start our prep today for those guys"
On making the offense more explosive…
"It takes good players. It takes good planning. It takes execution. It takes attention to detail, and it takes buy-in from a lot of guys because not everybody gets to be the guy that touches the ball. when you are explosive. Some guys have to protect. Some guys have to make sacrifices for the team to do it, but it takes a lot of work and team effort. Guys have bought into that, and a lot of that is created by the fact that you can run the ball. When you can run the ball it opens up a lot of things. We haven't always been able to run the ball, but when we have, we've been able to be more explosive"
On playing in another hostile road environment…
"You have to prepare every time. You do not know what kind of situation you're going to get into. You don't control everything that happens in the game. It's tough every time you go on the road in our league. It's really tough there. It's a loud, great environment for college football. We don't say, 'We have done this a couple times this year.' I think it is where people get in trouble. You have to prepare for communication issues and guys playing on the road."
On South Carolina QB LaNorris Sellers…
"He's a 'wow' player. There are two or three plays on there that are just incredible. The arm strength, the speed, the talent, that ability to keep eyes downfield. He looks faster than everybody on the field, and they're really fast people on the field. He's taking angles away from a couple of the best defenses in the country, and that there's more dominant player in terms of college football at what he does when he does it. You never know what's going to happen. It's not like they're all designed runs. There are designed runs, but some of them are just incredible plays. He is a phenomenal talent. A lot of credit to their coaching staff because in the time he's been there, he's gotten better each and every year."
On keeping the players mentally healthy through the season…
"Practice. We practice in tough environments. We create tough situations. Repetition, offseason. Physical training and mental training help. Being in tough environments and recognizing that each moment has a history in life of its own. You have to go play the next one. You can't let the last one impact you in a negative way. You build your team and your culture and your program around toughness and hard so that when hard things happen, they respond the right way."
On OL
Cortez Smith's
performance against Missouri…
"He did some good things. I am not really surprised by that because he practices and goes against good players each and every day. Our guys spend a lot of time blocking different defensive looks. [Glenn] Schumann and our defensive staff challenge the offensive line in everything we do year-round. Every day, we are doing something on third down, we are doing something on first and second down trying to give a different look and presentation, so that it requires communication. He handled that well. He wasn't on the road, and this week, hopefully, Drew [Bobo] will be able to go and be able to play, but if he is not, Cortez is in a good spot. He is gaining confidence. He has played in some tough environments in our practices, as well."
On the team getting out to early leads…
"If a coach tells you he influences that he's a liar. There's no influence over that. We're not practicing any different than we have in the past. We're not preparing any different than we have in the past. I would say we're playing with confidence. I would say that our quarterback is more experienced. A lot of your team flow, your start in those kinds of games goes with the age and maturity of your team. I don't know that we're older and more mature than we were last year or the year before, but the starts have been different than some of our past ones."
On the impact of this year's team on his own growth…
"You evolve as a coach. You're always evolving. My staff has helped tremendously. I think we've got an incredible staff here. We've got incredible continuity. People sometimes chastise continuity and think that you need to make changes to grow and get better because you don't get the results you want. Sometimes the results you got were the best results you could have gotten and maybe exceeded expectations. It's not the other way around that the change makes growth. Continuity makes growth, and that's from a staff perspective. You're asking as far as the players, I think you learn something from all your players. This group has been a fun group to coach. They respond well to coaching and they respond well to challenges. I think I'm still learning from them."
On the team being player-led…
"I think that's a 'coachism.' What coach is going to tell you he doesn't have a player-led team? We've had teams that we're probably more player-led than this one. We've had teams that were probably less player-led than this one. We challenge our guys to lead every year, and some teams take it by storm and some less than others. Our job as coaches and not really just the coaches, the people in the organization, the strength coaches, the analysts, the nutrition staff, the training staff. They fill that void of leadership and communication if you don't have it completely there as players. I'm very pleased with this group in terms of leadership."
On South Carolina offensive coordinator Kendal Briles…
"They do a tremendous job. They mix tempo but are committed to the run. They've got really good backs. Their offensive line plays extremely hard physically. You can see the strain on tape. It's very obvious when you turn on the tape how hard they play and how physical they play. The hardest thing for me is that we didn't play them for two years, maybe two or three (ago). That's a hard comparison because I don't have anything to compare it to really other than overlap games in terms of Kendal versus anybody else. I can tell you they're explosive. I mean every play could go the distance. They have got speed. They have got toughness and they have got a quarterback that can get the ball anywhere on the field. I mean he throws the ball 60-70 yards on the line."
On South Carolina returner Vicari Swain…
"He's been a great returner, it's not just this year. We showed his returns last year multiple times to our team talking about the scheme and the blocking and everybody committed to getting their guy. When you have a really good returner and you got a really good return team, it's a weapon. It's what they do a phenomenal job of number one getting returns. It's hard to get returns in our league. They do a good job getting returns, and he's been a great returner for a couple years now."
On OL
Cortez Smith
off the field…
"He's a great kid. He was fun to recruit. Local to here. He was a more laid-back. He smiles, jokes, and understands football. He's got good instincts. He's a good athlete. He's got fire, passion, energy, and he shows it in different ways. I think he's got the right kind of demeanor for playing that position where it doesn't have a lot of stuff bothering him."
On wide receivers coach
James Coley
…
"James is a developer of talent. I think the level of detail he coaches in his room is unmatched in terms of receiver rooms across the country. He understands the big picture. He's not just coached one position. He's coached multiple positions: quarterbacks, tight ends, and receivers. He knows what's going on. He's a good idea guy for schemes, plans, what affects what. He's been around a lot of good football. He's coached in the National Football League, and he's coached great wideouts. He's been around it a lot. He also knows what it takes to win, and it takes a committed room. In that room, we have been very fortunate to be explosive and score points without necessarily having single-person statistics that blow you away, and that's a culture belief. He helps promote that culture along with the rest of the coaching staff that you can do more for your team than just put up statistics. We've had guys in that room buy into that."
On co-defensive coordinator Travaris Robinson…
"He brings a lot of confidence, a lot of good energy, and a lot of experience. He's coached at, I don't know, three, four, maybe five places in our league. He played at one, and I know he's coached at three or four of them, so he understands our conference. I think that's important in recruiting. He understands the footprint. He's developed some really good defensive backs at the places he's been before he got here. He brought an experience of guys he coached that have been all over the National Football League. His reputation kind of speaks for itself, and he's a team guy. He doesn't really care who gets the credit. He's worried about doing his job and doing his job to the best of his ability every day."
On WR Isiah Canion…
"Just create more weapons. He's a size, speed guy. He's got toughness. He's got experience, which in the beginning of the year we were lacking. He brings an awareness of coverage having played in an offense very similar to ours from where he came from a lot of the same language. He was able to transition nicely and easily. He's a great competitor. I'm happy for him that he's healthy and is getting to compete like he wants to and knows he can."
On the coordinators producing impressive statistics…
"They're leading. At the end of the day, you want good players, you want good leadership and you want buy in. We've got really good coaches that have really good staffs. They have a plan they implement. We have a lot of scheme. At the end of the day, you have got to be careful that you don't get away from fundamentals, and I think both those coaches, as much as they like ideas and they're like different things, they don't get away from the fundamentals of what it takes to be successful. They have got good players, so players and staff have a lot to do with it."
On the importance of reserve players getting snaps in games…
"I can't put it in words because I don't know the value of it. I could make a case there's more value in playing. It's valuable for the guys that might have to play later to learn and gain experience certainly, but to what extent? The value is that's it's hard for me to comprehend. I'm glad we were able to. I'd like to say that it keeps us healthier, but maybe we're healthier not conditioned as well. There's always a flip side."
