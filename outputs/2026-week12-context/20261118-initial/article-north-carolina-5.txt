Football's Defense Dominant In Defeat
November 15, 2026
|
Football
Share:
UNC’s defensive effort against one of the nation’s top offenses was a bright spot for the Tar Heels.
By:
Brennan Doherty
Heading into UNC's matchup against Louisville, head coach
Bill Belichick
was asked whether his team was ready to open up the playbook and embrace a high-scoring shootout against the Cardinals. Louisville entered averaging 40.8 points per game (sixth nationally) and 7.8 yards per play, the most in the country.
Belichick said the Tar Heels weren't going to simply "concede" points to the Cardinals, and he was right. Saturday's visitors had to work for everything.
While UNC came up short in its 16-14 loss at Kenan Stadium, a stellar effort from the Tar Heel defense kept UNC in the game and gave the offense a chance to attempt a game-winning drive in the final minutes.
UNC held Louisville to season lows in points (16), total offense (302) and yards per play (4.7).
With as many playmakers as the Cardinals have, "you have to play team defense," Belichick said. "I thought our guys did a pretty good job of that. Tackling is hard against them because they've got a lot of good players. But we hung in there and were able to play pretty good in the red area and pretty good on third down."
While the Cardinals scored on their first three drives, only one ended in a touchdown, and that came on a drive that started at the UNC 21-yard line. After that, Louisville scored only once on its final eight possessions. The Cardinals struggled to get into a rhythm against the Tar Heels, who kept the ball in front of them, wrapped up well and avoided big plays.
Louisville has gashed opponents with big gainers this season. The Cardinals' 64 plays of 20-plus yards (an average of eight per game) prior to Saturday were the most nationally. UNC allowed just one, a 26-yard gain on the first play from scrimmage. After that, Louisville didn't threaten vertically and seldomly extended plays with broken tackles.
"I think we all rally to the ball," said UNC linebacker and captain
Derek McDonald
, who finished with a game-high 11 tackles. "Obviously, you don't want one missed tackle to turn into an explosive play. They had a ton of explosive plays on the season, so just making sure we have a bunch of guys around the ball. If someone misses a tackle, it's no big deal. There's another guy right there to clean it up."
A tackle for loss by McDonald and defensive lineman
Leroy Jackson
on Louisville's last full drive of the night helped force the Cardinals to punt the ball away with just under two minutes remaining, giving UNC a chance to engineer a comeback.
Operating behind center at that point for the Tar Heels was true freshman
Travis Burgess
, who replaced injured starter
Billy Edwards Jr.
In the third quarter.
While UNC's final drive ended with an unlucky interception, Burgess impressed in his collegiate debut.
His first pass almost resulted in a TD, with wideout
Trech Kekahuna
appearing to haul in a diving catch, only for it to be ruled incomplete.
On his second and third drives, Burgess showed off his legs and finished with 35 rushing yards on eight attempts. Excluding sack yardage, he gained 52 yards on five rushes, including four first-down runs. Burgess, a Grayson, Georgia, native, completed 6-of-11 passes for 59 yards, with five of those completions coming on the Tar Heels' second scoring drive of the night, which brought UNC within two thanks to running back
Benjamin Hall's
second TD.
"I thought he came in there in a tough situation and made some plays," Belichick said. "Made a couple of plays with his legs on the scramble, made a couple good throws and took us down for a touchdown."
Wide receiver
Jordan Shipp
, who caught a team-best seven passes for 61 yards, applauded Burgess for how he "took command of the huddle" while put into a high-stakes situation.
Sticking to the theme of UNC's season, Saturday's loss was yet another game that went down to the wire. Seven of the Tar Heels' nine contests against FBS opponents have been decided by one score.
Whether they won a close game or came out on the losing end, the Tar Heels have shown an ability to quickly shift their focus to the game ahead.
That's once again the challenge as UNC heads to Virginia next week with only two games remaining.
"Just stay where your feet are," Shipp said. "That's one of the biggest things that's echoed to us — and just control what you can control."
