CHAMPAIGN, Ill. —
After back-to-back Friday games, the Illinois football team returns to Gies Memorial Stadium for a Saturday afternoon matchup against #13 Iowa. Kickoff is scheduled for 1:30 p.m. CT on Big Ten Network.
Illinois (6-4, 4-3 Big Ten) will celebrate one of its winningest senior classes in program history, including five members that have worn the Orange and Blue for all five years of college football:
Matthew Bailey
,
Ben Clawson
,
James Kreutz
,
Aidan Laughery
, and
Xavier Scott
. This year's seniors have won 38 games over the last five seasons, Illinois' most wins over a five-year stretch since 1905.
Iowa (8-2, 5-2 Big Ten) has the #1-ranked scoring defense in the nation (11.7 points per game allowed), as the Illini will face its third straight top 10 scoring defense: Iowa (#1), UCLA (#6), and Nebraska (#10). The Hawkeyes' impressive defensive also include top-10 national ranks in rushing defense (#6, 83.6), third down defense (#7, 30.4), and total defense (#10, 279.3).
Jeff Levering (play-by-play), Jake Butt (analyst), and Brooke Fletcher (reporter) have the the national television broadcast on Big Ten Network. The Busey Bank Illini Sports Network has the state-wide radio call with Brian Barnhart (play-by-play), Carey Davis (analyst), and
Kevin Mitchell
(sideline).
B1G Tailgate will also air live from Champaign on Saturday from 9-11 a.m. CT with on-site host Camryn Irwin alongside analyst Tyvis Powell. Big Ten Network will bet setup inside Grange Grove.
Limited tickets are available at
FightingIllini.com
.
Press Conference
Notes
Illinois, Iowa Set for Senior Day Matchup in Champaign
After back-to-back Friday night games, Illinois returns home to Gies Memorial Stadium to take on Iowa on Saturday at 1:30 p.m. CT for its final home game of the 2026 season.
Over the last three seasons, Illinois and Iowa have identical 25-11 records, ranking fourth in the Big Ten in victories. Each program also has 19 Power-4 wins in that span, also tied for the fourth-best in the Big Ten.
Illinois snapped a four-game Big Ten winning streak last week in a 22-14 loss at #4/4 UCLA.
Illinois posted four straight victories over Michigan State, #-/23 Oregon, Maryland, and Nebraska to make the Illini bowl eligible for a third straight season.
Illinois will face its third straight top 10 scoring defense: Iowa (#1), UCLA (#6), Nebraska (#10).
Illinois will celebrate Senior Day and one of its winningest classes in program history during a pregame ceremony.
Illinois will honor five seniors who have worn the Orange and Blue for the last five seasons: DB
Matthew Bailey
, DB
Ben Clawson
, LB
James Kreutz
, RB
Aidan Laughery
, and DB
Xavier Scott
. (Fifth-year
Jordan Anderson
has another year)
Illinois is 24-10 at Gies Memorial Stadium over the last five seasons.
No player in Illini history has ever been a part of as many wins as this year's seniors. Illinois' 38 wins over the last five seasons are the most in a five-year span for the Fighting Illini program since 1905.
No player in Illinois has ever won three bowl games. This class will look to become the first in Illini history.
Gies Memorial Stadium: Rising Attendance and Wins
Illinois is 16-4 at Gies Memorial Stadium over the last three seasons (2024-26).
Illinois ranks 16th in the nation in home wins since 2024 (16).
Illinois has seven sellouts over the last two seasons, including three sellouts this season (Duke, Southern Illinois, Purdue).
Illinois has set the program record for the most wins at Gies Memorial Stadium over a three-year period (16).
Illinois Offense Notes
WR
Collin Dixon
is one of the top wide receivers in the nation:
One of only five players in the nation to rank in the top 10 in receptions, receiving yards, and receiving touchdowns, along with Jesse Legree (Oregon State), Jeremiah Smith (Ohio State), Malachi Toney (Miami), and Easton Messer (FAU)
Third in the nation in receiving touchdowns (13), behind Ohio State's Jeremiah Smith and Oregon State's Jesse Legree
Illinois' single-season receiving touchdowns record holder (13)
Illinois' Gies Memorial Stadium receiving touchdowns record holder (11)
Eighth in the nation and second in the Big Ten in receptions (72)
10th in the nation and third in the Big Ten in receiving yards (946)
Dixon
and
Hudson Clement
rank third among Big Ten duos in receiving yards (1,548), behind only Indiana's Charlie Becker/Nick Marsh (1,699) and Ohio State's Jeremiah Smith/Devin McCuin (1,694).
Clement
ranks 19th in the Big Ten in receptions (40) and 15th in receiving yards (602).
QB
Katin Houser
currently has the seventh-best passing efficiency in Illinois history (140.0). He is one touchdown pass away from Illinois' all-time single-season top 10 list.
Aidan
Laughery
has 100+-yard games against two ranked opponents - Ohio State (113) and Oregon (123). He ran for 113 yards at OSU, the first opposing player to rush for 100+ at Ohio Stadium since Michigan's Kalel Mullings (116 yards) on Nov. 30, 2024.
Illinois' offensive line ranks among the best in the nation:
12th in the nation and second in the Big Ten in fewest tackles for loss allowed (34)
21st in the nation and third in the Big Ten in fewest sacks allowed (12)
Illinois Defense Notes
Illinois has held four straight Big Ten opponents to 20 or fewer offensive points for the first time since 2022. UCLA scored 22 points, but two points were on a safety.
Illinois is holding opponents to 15.5 points per game in the last four games (since Illinois' bye week).
The Illini's third-down defense is 37.5% (21/56) in the last four games, after being 50.5% (31/61) in the first five games against FBS competition (excludes SIU).
DB
Matthew Bailey
is the only player in the nation returning after leading his team in tackles in 2024 and 2025. Bailey is Illinois' leading tackler again this season with 75, including a career-high 14 at Ohio State.
LB
James Kreutz
has 52 tackles in the last five games after having 19 tackles in the first five games.
DL
Will Holmes
(26) and DL
Pat Farrell
(17) rank second and 12th in the Big Ten, respectively, in pressures among interior defensive linemen, according to PFF. Only Indiana and Washington also have two players in the top 12.
CB
Juice Clarke
ranks fifth in the nation and leads the Big Ten lead in pass breakups (11). He ranks seventh in the nation and leads the Big Ten in passes defended (12).
In the last four games,
Clarke
has allowed only 26.8 completion yards per game (107 total yards) on 24 targets (per PFF).
DB
Tanner Heckel
has allowed only 134 completion yards on 243 coverage snaps (26 targets) this season, per PFF.
Heckel'
s playing time has increased and his snaps played have more than doubled during the last five games.
