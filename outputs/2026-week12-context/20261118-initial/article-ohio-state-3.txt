Jeremiah Smith Named Semifinalist for Biletnikoff Award
11/16/2026 12:05:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
Junior wide receiver Jeremiah Smith was named a finalist for the Biletnikoff Award in 2025 and a semifinalist for the award in 2024.
By:
Andrew Kovar
TALLAHASSEE, Fla. –
Ohio State junior wide receiver
Jeremiah Smith
was named a 2026 Biletnikoff Award Semifinalist, as announced Monday afternoon.
The Biletnikoff Award annually recognizes the college football season's outstanding FBS receiver. Any player, regardless of position (wide receiver, tight end, slot back, and running back) who catches a pass is eligible for the award.
This marks the third consecutive year that Smith has been named a semifinalist for the Biletnikoff Award. He was also named a finalist for the award in 2025.
Additionally, this is the third national award that Smith has been
named a semifinalist as he was also a Maxwell Award Semifinalist
on Tuesday last week and a
Walter Camp Award Semifinalist
on Wednesday last week.
JJ4ItAll
"JJ 4 It All" champions
Jeremiah Smith's
pursuit of college football's highest individual honors while celebrating what drives him most: winning with his teammates and for The Ohio State University. This mission connects his quest for the Heisman Trophy, Fred Biletnikoff Award, Maxwell Award and Walter Camp Player of the Year Award with Ohio State's top goal every season of winning the rivalry game at the end of the regular season.
For Jeremiah, individual recognition reflects the work, preparation, and commitment to excellence he brings to the field every day while also competing with his teammates to win a second pair of Gold Pants.
Ohio State and the Biletnikoff Award
The Biletnikoff Award's semifinalists, three finalists, and award recipient are selected by the highly distinguished Biletnikoff Award National Selection Committee, a group of 650 prominent college football journalists, commentators, announcers, Biletnikoff Award winners, and other former receivers.
Three finalists will be announced on Nov. 24
The recipient of the Biletnikoff Award will be announced on the ESPN College Football Awards Show on Friday, December 11.
Ohio State is one of eight schools nationally to win the Walter Camp Award multiple times:
1995 Terry Glenn – WR
2023 Marvin Harrison Jr. – WR
More on
Jeremiah Smith
Entering Week 11 of the 2026 college football season,
Jeremiah Smith
has 69 receptions for 1,200 yards and 14 touchdowns, averaging 17.4 yards per catch and 133.3 yards per game.
Entering Week 11, Smith leads the Big Ten in all five categories. Nationally, he leads the nation in receiving touchdowns, ranks second in receiving yards and receiving yards per game, and he leads all Power 4 receivers in each of those three categories.
For his career, Smith has 232 receptions for 3,758 yards and 41 touchdowns. Smith is the Big Ten all-time leader in receiving yards and touchdowns while also being all-time Ohio State leader in all three categories.
Smith's production this season includes the two highest receiving yardage totals of his career: 217 yards against Illinois on Sept. 26 and 206 yards at No. 14 Iowa on Oct. 3. He also recorded 151 receiving yards against both Ball State and No. 4 Texas.
Following the victory over Illinois, Smith was the consensus National Player of the Week, being named AP, Walter Camp and Maxwell Player of the Week. Additionally, Smith has won Big Ten Offensive Player of the Week twice this season after his performances against Illinois and Iowa.
His performance has earned midseason All-America recognition from The Associated Press, The Athletic, On3 and Sports Illustrated. He also was named to a semifinalist for the Maxwell, Walter Camp and Biletnikoff awards and received Big Ten preseason honors.

In Saturday's game vs. Northwestern, Smith finished the game with 11 receptions for 117 yards and one touchdown.
Smith now needs 117 yards to move into the top 25 in FBS history in receiving yards. Stanford's Troy Walters (1996-99) currently ranks 25th in career receiving yards with 3,986 career yards.
Smith also needs 125 to reach 4,000 career yards. He would become the first Big Ten receiver with 4,000 receiving yards. Additionally, he would be just the fifth Power-4 receiver ever to reach 4,000 career receiving yards joining
Ryan Broyles (Oklahoma) - 4,586 yards (2008-11)
James Washington (Oklahoma St.) - 4,472 yards (2014-17)
Rashaun Woods (Okla. St.) - 4,414 yards (2001-03)
Kendall Wright (Baylor) - 4,004 yards (2008-11)
Smith is the only Ohio State receiver and became the second receiver in Big Ten history to have three 1,000-yd receiving seasons, joining Braylon Edwards (Michigan).
