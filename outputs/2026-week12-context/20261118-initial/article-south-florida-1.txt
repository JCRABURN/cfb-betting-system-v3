TAMPA, Fla
- After 29 seasons, the South Florida Bulls' familiar home of Raymond James Stadium is on the clock. Only two games remain at Ray-Jay before the program moves into its sparkling new on-campus stadium for the 2027 season.
Thursday night will be South Florida's final night game at Ray-Jay (covering all our bases, the Dec. 18 Gasparilla Bowl, if it comes to that, begins at 2:30 p.m.). It's the Bulls (6-3, 2-3) against the potent Memphis Tigers (7-2, 4-1) in ESPN's American Conference showcase game.
Do the Bulls have another memorable under-the-lights moment left in their tank? Historically, when the sun goes down, South Florida football has made some magical memories at home with four victories against ranked opponents from the Football Championship Subdivision (including No. 1 Troy State in 2000) and eight wins against ranked opponents from the Football Bowl Subdivision (including No. 5 West Virginia in 2007).
Unranked Memphis was picked second in an American Conference preseason poll conducted by media members.
"Really solid program,'' Bulls head coach Brian Hartline said. "One of our biggest challenges without a doubt.''
Although he has only been a Bull for one season, defensive coordinator Josh Aldridge said he will have fond memories of Ray-Jay.
"I hope our players appreciate the great players who have played in that stadium,'' said Aldridge, who grew up in the Memphis area. "I see (former Tampa Bay Buccaneers linebacker)
Derrick Brooks
out in the hall every day, so that's still pretty cool. Just think about all the great players who have played in that stadium and not take it for granted that it's (one of) our last games there before you're about to go play in a place (on-campus stadium) that people have dreamed about forever.''
"It's a great environment,'' Bulls offensive coordinator Tim Beck said. "It has been a really cool experience and it's one that our young players will probably never forget.''
Regardless of the venue or opponent, Aldridge said he's hopeful that the discipline of offseason training and the weekly process routine will pay dividends down the stretch.
"You play well early in the season because of your summer training, but I think you play well late in the season because of the consistency you operate with,'' Aldridge said. "We're not looking to peak at a certain time. We're trusting our process, how we do business. If all of a sudden we've got to get up for a big game or think we have to do something different, then your process isn't right. Playing well is a tribute to the process we take every single day.''
Here are South Florida's keys for success against Memphis:
1. Close The Margins
In a word, here is South Florida's biggest need against Memphis: Efficiency.
The Bulls must win in all the margins and close the gap in those areas. That means staying away from penalties. That means consistently good gains on first-down plays and steering clear of desperation down-and-distance situations. That means making big plays on special teams, finding some hidden yardage and staying in favorable field-position areas. That means not making mistakes and getting some takeaways (the Bulls have just two defensive interceptions and both picks occurred in the first game).
That means a crisp and clean performance in the "middle eight'' (final four minutes of the second quarter, first four minutes of the third quarter). And it probably means taking a long-and-hard look at manageable fourth-down situations, living on the edge a bit, to keep drives alive.
2. Dee-fense! Dee-fense!
The statistics indicate an outstanding season by South Florida's defense.
The Bulls are ranked No. 25 nationally (allowing just 312.7 yards per game). They have permitted 136 points — the lowest total through nine games in program history. That's 15.1 points allowed per game (10th nationally). They are forcing three-and-outs on 24.2 percent of drives (second best in the American and 39th nationally). They're on pace for the program's most sacks produced in nine seasons and the tackles for loss should finish in the program's top 10.
South Florida's defense will need all of those bells and whistles — and more — against Memphis.
It will be a huge test for the Bulls' defense, which has been haunted by some late-game breakdowns (twice losing in the game's final 15 seconds while permitting another go-ahead touchdown with 3:07 to play). There can be no such slip-ups against Memphis. From start to finish, it must be a suffocating effort.
3. Make Some Explosive Plays
This area has been severely lacking. In 2025, the Bulls registered 117 explosive plays (15 yards or greater) during the regular season. This season, with three games remaining, South Florida's total is 47.
It's not exactly an apples-to-apples comparison because ultra-experienced quarterback
Byrum Brown
ran a juiced-up, high-tempo offense last season. But the Bulls must get better at stretching the field or delivering a breakaway run. Those plays erase a lot of mistakes and put a defense on its heels.
South Florida's longest run was a 45-yarder by
Chase Garnett
(who hasn't had a carry since) against Delaware State. Its longest pass play was a 42-yard touchdown from Michael Van Buren to
Bryson Rodgers
against UTSA. There were six explosives against ECU — passes of 28, 27, 26 and 23 yards, plus runs of 25 and 23 yards — but this game probably requires double-digit explosives by the Bulls. That was achieved only once this season — 13 against Football Championship Subdivision opponent Delaware State.
4. Control The Clock
Memphis has the nation's No. 5-ranked offense (513.3 yards), led by potential quarterback Marcus Stokes (69.1-percent completion percentage, 25 touchdowns, six interceptions) and shifty running back Jaylin Carter (929 yards, 6.2-yard average, 10 touchdowns).
The Bulls likely can't win a scoring duel against the Tigers. It didn't happen in 2023 (59-50 defeat) or last season (34-31 defeat), either. So that Memphis offense must somehow be neutralized. And the best way to do that is keeping the Tiger firepower on the sideline.
The Bulls can't repeatedly go three-and-out.
Repeat: The Bulls can't repeatedly go three-and-out.
South Florida must possess the ball with time-consuming drives. It needs to finish with touchdowns, not field goals. It will require a consistent offensive look that we haven't seen often — maybe in the final five minutes against Army and other assorted flashes — but this game calls for South Florida's best effort of the season.
Joey Johnston
Athletics Senior Writer
