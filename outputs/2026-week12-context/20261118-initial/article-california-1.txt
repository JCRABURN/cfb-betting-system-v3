Bears Host Stanford In The Big Game Next Saturday
By:
Cal Athletics
CHARLOTTESVILLE, Va.
– Virginia returned two interceptions for touchdowns and knocked off California football 36-7 on Saturday.
Trailing 9-0 early in the second quarter, Cal moved from its 25 to the UVA 4 before its drive stalled. The Bears tried a fake field goal, but the play backfired as Brandyn Hillman intercepted
Mason Mini's
pass and returned it 100 yards for a touchdown.
The Cavaliers (5-5, 3-4 ACC) had increased their lead to 16-0 with 2:02 left in the half.
California (3-7, 2-5) responded with a nine-play, 60-yard drive for a touchdown. The Golden Bears converted a 3rd-and-7 at the Virginia 31 on a 19-yard completion from
Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele
to
Mark Hamper
. Hamper finished with six receptions for 66 yards.
Then on 3rd-and-8 at the UVA 10, the Bears got a break on a roughing-the-passer penalty, giving them 1st-and-goal at the 5. On the next play,
Adam Mohammed
bulled over the right side and into the end zone. The TD with 14 seconds left in the half cut Cal's deficit to 16-7, which was the score at halftime.
It was 19-7 late in the third quarter before Kam Robinson picked off a short Sagapolutele pass and raced 33 yards for a score to make it 26-7.
Cal linebacker
AJ Tuitele
recorded a team- and personal-high 10 tackles.
The Bears play their final two games at home, beginning with next Saturday's matchup against Stanford in the 129th Big Game.
Competing in the Big Game "is an extremely special opportunity and that's what I ended with in the locker room tonight," Travers Family Head Football Coach
Tosh Lupoi
said.
"We have something very special to prepare for, what this game means to myself, what it can mean to this team and certainly (to) a lot more people that care deeply about this game. So, going to put a lot of focus into that and doing everything possible to go win."
Cal center
Bastian Swinney
is in his sixth year in the program. The Bears earned the Axe in each of his first four Big Games, but the Cardinal prevailed 31-10 at Stanford Stadium last season.
In the coming days, Swinney plans to let his teammates – many of whom will be playing in their first Big Game – know the significance of the California-Stanford rivalry.
"I'm a Cal Bear for life," Swinney said. "I've proven that to everyone here. So, I'm definitely going to emphasize how important this game is not only to me, but to the other seniors in the room, to the other seniors in the building to just everyone involved with Cal and our program and our institution.
"So, yeah, I'm really going to drive that home for them."
Kickoff is set for 4:30 p.m. and the game will air on ACC Network.
