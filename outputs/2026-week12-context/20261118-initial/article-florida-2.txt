One Gators Legend To Honor Another: Ron Coleman Is 'Mr. Two Bits' For Vanderbilt Game
Tuesday, November 17, 2026
|
Football, Scott Carter
Share:
By:
Scott Carter, Senior Writer
GAINESVILLE, Fla. — The moment you start tossing questions at 76-year-old Ron Coleman, his sharp memory shines through.
Does Coleman remember the first University of Florida football game he attended?
Ron Coleman
"I do. It was when I was a youngster,'' he said. "One of my uncles from Ocala asked me if I wanted to go to a football game, and I said sure. Well, I thought it was in Ocala, and I thought it was going to be a little local high school or whatever. But he kept driving and driving. Ended up in Gainesville on the UF campus, and I'm like, 'Whoa, this is huge.' And that game was really a momentous occasion in my life. It was something I had never witnessed before, of course, and just made a huge impact."
Coleman later attended UF, making headlines as the first black scholarship athlete in UF history when he signed with Florida's track and field team on May 22, 1968.
Gators football coach/athletic director Ray Graves, track coach Jimmy Carnes and Coleman's father, Cranford Coleman Sr., watched as Coleman, a standout at Ocala High School, signed the scholarship papers.
Does he remember much about that day? Ha, silly question.
"I'm going to give you the lead-up to it," he said. "So that day just happened to be coincidentally senior skip day at Ocala High School. Myself and a carload of kids jump in a car and off we go to Daytona Beach. I know I have to be back at the signing ceremony by a certain time, and boy, oh boy, was it a challenge because they didn't want to leave. I was determined to get back, and I'm like, 'Okay, you guys gonna have to find a way to get home because I'm leaving right now.'
Ron Coleman
signs a scholarship with the Gators on May 22, 1968, at Ocala High School.
Ray Graves
,
Jimmy Carnes
and
Cranford Coleman Sr.
watch. (Photo: St. Petersburg Times via Newspapers.com.)
"We pulled into the parking lot [at school]. I'm buttoning up my shirt, putting on my tie, all at the same time, getting out, and I walk in there, and there's my dad. My dad was there because my mom died when I was 15, but my dad was there. Several of my other relatives were there, and I was getting the look of, 'Boy, where you been?' You know the look. It was quite the occasion. And to me, what made it so much more meaningful than the fact that I was the first black athlete at UF … it had even more significance in that it gave me the opportunity to go to college."
Coleman endured many of the racial slurs and threats of the time, but he persevered and became a standout performer for the Gators on the track and in the classroom. He graduated as a six-time Southeastern Conference champion, was inducted into the UF Athletics Hall of Fame in 2004, and is a decorated U.S. Navy veteran.
Coleman continues to live a full life and attends almost every Gators home game.
But on Saturday, when Florida hosts Vanderbilt in the regular-season home finale, Coleman will have a different view at the start.
He is serving as Honorary Mr. Two Bits.
Ask him how familiar he is with Mr. Two Bits and the tradition the Gators started in 2013 to honor him, and well, Coleman chuckles.
"That is going to be one of the most awesome experiences I've had in quite a long time,'' he said. "I mean, it brings me back to the days when I had to perform on the track in front of large SEC crowds, SEC meet, and that was quite the thrill as well. However, this tops them all absolutely.
"I look forward to it every game. 'Who's going to be Mr. Two Bits today?' "
Coleman, a trailblazer in the history of Gators athletics, knows all about George Edmondson, the fan who started the tradition in 1949. Edmondson died at age 97 in 2019 but remains vivid in Coleman's memory.
On Saturday, one Gators legend will honor another.
"I want to do it exactly the way Mr. George Edmondson did it. I'm probably going to take a look at some videos and make sure I got it the way he did it,'' Coleman said. "Most people do it that way in general. I want to do it exactly the way he did it."
