'Catch The Same Bus': Buffs Aim To Take Houston Formula On The Road
November 17, 2026
|
Football
Donate To Football
Buy Football Tickets
Watch Press Conference
Share:
By:
Curtis Snyder, Associate AD/Athletic Communications
BOULDER — Deion "Coach Prime" Sanders summed up what the Colorado football team wants to carry out of Friday's 42-32 win over Houston in one word: unity.
"Offensively, supporting the defense wholeheartedly. Defensively, supporting the offense, and both supporting special teams, and everyone showing up at the same time," Coach Prime said Tuesday as the Buffaloes began preparations for Saturday's 10 a.m. MT game at Cincinnati. "When we all catch the same bus, we could play this game at a high level."
Colorado got there against Houston. The Buffs ran 39 times for 249 yards and five touchdowns, scored by three different backs —
Richard Young
,
DeKalon Taylor
and
JaQuail Smith
— and the offensive line was named the Big 12 Offensive Line of the Week. On the other side,
Randon Fontenette
was named Big 12 Defensive Player of the Week after a career-high 14 tackles, including a strip-sack that set up a quick answer to start the second half. He is the third straight Buff to win the weekly defensive award.
"Three in a row for CU. That's unbelievable," Coach Prime said. "And it's their goal, it's their aim to make it four."
For the players, Friday wasn't a revelation.
"It's not necessarily what we can be. It's what we already knew we were," Taylor said. "It was just exciting to finally put it all together and show people, like, 'Hey, we are who we say we are.'"
Linebacker
Tyler Martinez
, who returned from injury against Houston, watched much of the season from the sideline and saw how close the Buffs had been.
"We were right there, right there, right there, and just didn't know how to give that finishing blow," Martinez said. "We played as one unit — special teams making plays, offense making plays, defense making plays. We played as one team, and I think we figured it out on Friday night."
**A physical opponent at Nippert**
Cincinnati (5-5) is 5-1 at home this season. Coach Prime called the Bearcats a physical, run-first team that makes good halftime adjustments, and he had praise for fourth-year head coach Scott Satterfield.
"He's been several places, and he's been successful, tremendously successful, at all of them," Coach Prime said. "I have the utmost respect for Coach Satterfield."
The Buffs' answer, he said, is to set the tone.
"We're tired of allowing the opposing team to dictate what we do," Coach Prime said. "We want to dictate what they do by being aggressive on both sides of the ball."
Defensive lineman
Santana Hopper
said the plan up front won't change: stop the run first, then get after the passer.
"That's really our mentality, and we ain't gonna change that for nobody," Hopper said.
As for the road, Coach Prime said he has asked his staff to bring the same practice habits, preparation and physicality that produced Friday's performance.
"Routine never beat me," he said. "It's the players, man, and the coaches. I've got to do a better job of rallying this team together, making sure they're in the right places, the right personnel is on the field at all times."
Punter
Damon Greaves
agreed there is no shortcut.
"Winning D1 college football games is hard, whether you're home or away," Greaves said. "Our emphasis is going to be on playing to our strengths and playing with the confidence that we gained from just this week."
**Answering adversity**
The Buffs' response to the opening play of the third quarter stood out. After an interception, the defense took the field and got the ball back right away. Fontenette forced the fumble, and Hopper recovered it.
"All I see is Rando come through in a blur, and just bang," Hopper said. "I just see the ball. I just jump on it, and we just run to the sideline. Everybody happy."
Coach Prime said that moment is something the program works on every day.
"We harp on: How do you respond to adversity? Not just adversity on the field, but how do you respond to adversity in life?" he said. "That was just one of those adverse times that we were prepared for, emotionally and physically."
He said the group's character has held up all season.
"These guys haven't quit. They haven't shut it down," Coach Prime said. "I love what I see on a daily basis. These are some really good kids, and they're fighting to the end."
**Around the Buffs**
Coach Prime said
Gideon ESPN Lampron
has lived up to everything the staff expected and more.
"He's so thankful and appreciative of the opportunity and the moments, and he has not taken one thing for granted," Coach Prime said. "Great, great, great guy to coach."
Greaves, a fourth-year starter, hit a career-long 75-yard punt against Houston, though it rolled into the end zone for a touchback.
"I'd like it inside the 10 rather than a touchback," Greaves said. "At the end of the day, I'm trying to put my defense in the best position possible."
He said his time in Boulder will stay with him.
"I'm going to be proud to be a Buff for the rest of my life," Greaves said.
Colorado plays at Cincinnati on Saturday, Nov. 21 (10 a.m. MT, ESPN+), before closing the regular season at home against UCF on Nov. 28 (noon MT, TNT).
