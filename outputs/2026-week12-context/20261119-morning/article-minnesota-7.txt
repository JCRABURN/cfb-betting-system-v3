Johnson Named Outland Trophy Semifinalist
11/18/2026 7:00:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
Minnesota has had three individuals win the Outland Trophy
University of Minnesota center
Greg Johnson
was announced as one of seven semifinalists Wednesday for the 2026 Outland Trophy, presented to college football's most outstanding interior lineman. The announcement was made by the Greater Omaha Sports Committee and the Football Writers Association of America.
The FWAA's All-America Committee selected five offensive linemen and two defensive tackles, with at least one semifinalist at each eligible position.
Johnson started the first three games at left guard, then moved to center, where he has played since. He has allowed five quarterback pressures and no sacks in 345 blocking assignments this season. Johnson earned Outland Trophy National Player of the Week honors for the weekend of September 26.
Three finalists for the trophy will be announced Tuesday, Nov. 24. The winner will be announced during The Home Depot College Football Awards, airing live on ESPN on Dec. 11. The trophy will be presented at the Outland Trophy Awards Dinner on Jan. 27, 2027, in Omaha, Neb. Werner Enterprises sponsors the dinner, which is produced by the Greater Omaha Sports Committee.
Joining Johnson as a semifinalist are: Anthony Boswell (Houston), Xavier Gilliam (Tennessee), A.J. Holmes (Texas Tech), Trevor Lauck (Iowa), Kade Pieper (Iowa) and Zereoue Williams (Utah).
Minnesota has had three players win the Outland Trophy in Tom Brown (1960), Bobby Bell (1962) and Greg Esliner (2005).
The FWAA All-America Committee considers votes from the full membership in selecting its 26-player All-America first team. The committee selects three Outland Trophy finalists from that team, and committee members then select the winner by individual ballot. The award is open to interior offensive and defensive linemen. Ends are not eligible.
First presented in 1946, the Outland Trophy is the third-oldest major college football award. Dr. John Outland, an All-American at the University of Pennsylvania in the late 1890s who later practiced medicine in Kansas City, Mo., gave the FWAA a financial contribution to establish the award. An avid outdoorsman, Outland believed linemen deserved more recognition and wanted an award that honored their play.
The Outland Trophy is a member of the National College Football Awards Association (NCFAA), which encompasses college football's most prestigious awards. The NCFAA's 25 awards have honored nearly 1,000 recipients since 1935. For more information, visit ncfaa.org.
