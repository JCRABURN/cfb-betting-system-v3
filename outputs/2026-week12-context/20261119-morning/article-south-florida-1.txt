30th Anniversary Series: Essential Special Teams Players
November 15, 2026
|
Football
Share:
Joey Johnston
/
Athletics Senior Writer
Editor's Note: To commemorate the University of South Florida's 30th football season,
GoUSFBulls.com
is presenting a 12-part series that covers the sights, sounds, milestones, biggest games, top performers and unforgettable personalities from the program's history. The installments appear each Sunday. Today's Part 10 is South Florida's Essential Special Teams Players.
Coach Willie Taggart's first South Florida team was 2-10. Those 2013 Bulls scored just 11 offensive touchdowns in 12 games. There weren't many positives.
But place-kicker Marvin Kloss had a season to remember. He made 18 of 23 field-goal attempts — hitting 13 consecutive to set a South Florida record — and led the nation by making 11 field goals from 40 yards or greater (and a nation's best four from 50 yards or greater). The Bulls didn't score an offensive touchdown in either of their two victories, relying on Kloss and scoring by their defense.
Kloss became the program's first finalist for a national award, joining Florida State's Robert Aguayo and Texas' Anthony Fera and the three players in contention for the Lou Groza Award, given to the nation's top place-kicker.
"I couldn't have imagined this a few months ago,'' said Kloss as he appeared at ESPN's College Football Awards Show in Lake Buena Vista. "I'm just enjoying this moment.''
Two days earlier, at the Breakers Resort in West Palm Beach, the three kickers were celebrated during the Lou Groza Award banquet. Kloss said he had "some of the best food I ever had in my life'' and he was provided a black tuxedo.
"I felt like James Bond,'' Kloss said.
Aguayo, who was 19-for-20 on field-goal attempts and 90-for-90 on extra-point attempts for the eventual national champion Seminoles, became the Lou Groza Award winner. But he appreciated the season of Kloss, who had become a close friend during kicking camps in Florida.
"He gets more chances to kick field goals and I get more chances to kick extra points,'' Aguayo said. "That's my downfall. Their offense stalls at the 35 and they send him in for the 50-yarders He meant a lot to his team.''
"Marvin's stats are very impressive,'' Fera said. "He has four field goals over 50 yards and I have one. He's not just about making short field goals. They kept throwing him out there for the log ones. And he kept making them.''
In a severely offensively challenged season, Kloss provided some memorable moments for the Bulls.
"I am thankful for Marvin Kloss,'' Taggart said upon hearing that his kicker was a Lou Groza Award finalist. "Whenever you have something positive to build on, it's huge. He has put a smile on my face the whole year. He's a winner. He's a competitor. He's passionate. And he's willing to do whatever it takes to help this football team win.''
Here are some other Essential Special Teams Players in South Florida's 30-season history:
Rodney Adams (2014-16) —
The wide receiver became one of USF most potent kickoff returners. He had a 29.1-yard average in 2015 and a 25.5-yard career average, second all-time at USF. In 2015 he opened the game at Navy with a 97-yard touchdown return.
Brian Battie (2020-22) —
He became a consensus first-team All-American in 2021, when he averaged 32.5 yards on 20 kickoff returns, including a pair of 100-yard touchdowns against Houston. He also had a 100-yarder at Tulsa, giving him a program-best three kickoff-return TDs for a season (and career). He had a 24.2-yard career average, ranking fourth all-time at USF.
Maikon Bonani (2008-12) —
The place-kicker from Lake Wales High School is the only USF player to become the team
'
s scoring leader in four different seasons. Connected on a 43-yard walk-off field goal to defeat No. 11-ranked Kansas 37-34 in his 2008 college debut. Has a USF-record 69 career made field goals. Has the USF record for most consecutive made PATs (62 from 2010 to 2012), including a 10-for-10 night against Florida A&M in 2011. Had a five field-goal game against Syracuse in 2012. Made 12 consecutive field-goal attempts in a span during the 2011 and 2012 seasons. Made a career-high 50-yard field goal against UConn in 2012, while connecting on five others of 47 yards or greater. Two-time All-Big East Conference and first team in 2012.
Justin Brockhaus-Kann (2009-12) —
He was the Big East Conference's second-team punter in 2012, when he completed a career with a then-record 68 punts downed inside the 20-yard line and had his longest punt (70-yarder at Cincinnati). He had a USF-record 30 punts inside the 20 in 2011. In 2010, he became the first USF punter with zero touchbacks in a season.
Mattias Ciabatti (2012-15) —
He had the program's second-best career punting average (42.5 yards) and the third-best season (44.4 in 2014). No USF punter had more career yardage (8,333). He was the first-team American Conference punter in 2014.
Bill Gramatica (1998-2000) —
The left-footed place-kicker finished with 36 made field goals, now tied for fourth all-time at USF. He made eight attempts in a row and still holds the mark for USF's longest field goal, a 63-yarder against Austin Peay in 2000. He was named Football Championship Subdivision All-American by the American Football Coaches Association (first team, 1998) and the Associated Press (second team, 1998). He also became the first USF player to compete in the East-West Shrine Game.
Nico Gramatica
(2024-present) —
He already has the USF career record for most made field goals of 50-plus yards (58, 53, 52, 52, 51 and 51 yards) and could be the best Gramatica in Bulls' history, surpassing all kicking marks of his two uncles, Bill and Santiago. He made 12 consecutive field goals, falling one short of the USF record and will be always remembered for his 20-yard walk-off field goal in Gainesville that beat the Florida Gators in 2025. As a sophomore in 2025, Gramatica the selected as the American Conference's second-team place-kicker.
Jimmy Horn Jr. (2021-22) —
The wide receiver from Sanford Seminole High School also became an electrifying kickoff-return man with a 29.9-yard average in 2022, including an 89-yard touchdown at Temple. He was the first-team American Conference kickoff returner in 2022.
Terrence Horne (2018-20) —
The wide receiver from Miramar High School caught lightning in a bottle during the 2018 game against Georgia Tech, when he had back-to-back kickoff-return touchdowns (98 and 97 yards) during a wild first quarter. He suffered a season-ending injury later that month and never reached those heights again, but his one-game performance was unforgettable enough.
Lindsey Lamar
(2009-12) —
The running back/receiver from Hillsborough High School had 3,162 all-purpose yards — and he still ranks first all-time in kickoff return yards (1,816). He had two touchdowns on kickoff returns (100 yards at Louisville, 94 yards against Syracuse, both in 2010). Selected Big East Conference Special Teams Player of the Year in 2010.
Emilio Nadelman (2014-17) —
The place-kicker holds USF's record for most points scored in a season (115 in 2017) and he's second for a career (286). He hit 13 consecutive field-goal attempts in 2017 — tying for another USF record — and his five made field goals against Temple tied another program mark. He was selected as the American Conference's first-team place-kicker in 2017.
Ean Randolph (2006) —
The wide receiver from Durant High School was the Big East Conference's Special Teams Player of The Year in 2006 when he averaged 14.8 yards on 25 punt returns, including a 76-yard touchdown against FIU. That was actually part of four consecutive punt returns for a touchdown by Randolph, but three of them were negated by penalty.
DeAndrew Rubin (1999-2002) —
The wide receiver from Dixie Hollins High School was a spectacular return man with a career 1,534 yards on kickoff returns and 1,049 on punt returns. He holds USF records for highest career average on kickoff returns (25.6) and punt returns (16.4). Had four career touchdowns on punt returns (91 yards against Utah State in 2001, 81 yards against James Madison in 2000, 56 yards against Florida Atlantic in 2002 and 55 yards against Northern Illinois in 2002) and one on a kickoff return (82 yards against Charleston Southern in 2002). Honorable mention All-American by CNN/Sports Illustrated in 2002.
Trent Schneider (2018-20) —
The Australian import holds a litany of USF punting records, including the highest average for a season (46.0 in 2019) and career (43.9), plus the most yards in a game (464 on nine punts against Memphis in 2019) and the highest average in a game (52.8 on five punts against BYU in 2019). He's also tied for the longest punt (72 yards against East Carolina in 2018).
Spencer Shrader (2019-22) —
He connected on 28 field-goal attempts, fifth best in a USF season, and made nine straight in 2021. At Memphis in 2020, he became the first Bulls' place-kicker to make four 40-yard field-goal attempts in one game (49, 47, 46, 41). He had a career-long 52-yarder against Temple in 2021, when he was the honorable mention American Conference place-kicker.
Andrew Stokes
(2021-24) —
The Australian import tied for the program's longest punt — 72 yards — on his last career effort during the 2024 Hawaii Bowl. He finished with a career 42.0-yard average and had a 51.0-yard average on seven punts during the Hawaii Bowl and had a 45.5 average in his final season. He also set a program record with the fewest career touchbacks (six) and had zero during the 2023 season. He was the first-team American Conference all-league punter in 2024.
Tony Umholtz (1997-99) —
The program's inaugural punter had three solid seasons, including a 41.2-yard average and a 49.7-yard average on seven punts against James Madison in 1999, when he was named first-team Football Championship Subdivision All-American by the Associated Press.
