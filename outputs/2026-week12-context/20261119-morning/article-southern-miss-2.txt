Blake Anderson Weekly News Conference – The South Alabama Game
11/16/2026 4:18:00 PM
|
Football
Media Availability
Share:
HATTIESBURG, Miss. –
Southern Miss football coach
Blake Anderson
addressed the media on Monday, Nov. 16, in the Duff Athletic Center ahead of the Golden Eagles' Saturday final home contest of the season against South Alabama. Game time is set for 3 p.m. at Renasant Stadium.
Southern Miss (3-7 overall, 2-4 Sun Belt) looks for their first-ever victory in seven overall meetings with the Jaguars  (5-5, 3-3) in a series dating back to the 2020 season.
The game can be seen via ESPN+, while the Southern Miss Sports Network's radio pregame show begins at 2:30 p.m. with the Voice of the Golden Eagles,
John Cox
, analyst Lee Roberts on the call.
Here is what Anderson had to say to the media Monday morning:
Opening Statement
"I mean, really short and simply. We just got to be better. We got to coach better. We got to play better. We've got to improve. And it's frustrating, because it's taken longer than anybody wants it to, including us. And we have seen it at time glimpses of us playing better than we did. And in the first half yesterday, we gave ourselves zero chances, and that's really from top down. So everywhere we can affect that we need to schematically, teaching, simplifying. Obviously, doing our job better technically and exceptionally. I mean, like it falls from top to bottom, and so you know maybe this is right where we're supposed to be in year one. It's just not any fun. I did think that La Tech did a great job. They made very few mistakes, played really, really well. And for us, kind of where we're at and what we're trying to do, it's honestly a sign of hope that this is what you can do within this league if you have some patience with the process, you know, we kind of looked at their roster. I think of their 22 starters, 11 of those guys from what we can see, been there for three to four years. I think just give or take seven or eight JC guys, 10 to 11 or 12 high school guys, and really only four transfers in their starting kind of lineups, and you've watched those guys progress over a four-year period with their program through some really frustrating times to get to the point where they're playing great ball right now. And so for us, what we're trying to do is just kind of reminder of to be patient in this process. You know, fight through it. Keep trying to speed it up as much as you can. But, there is a you can do it, and the way we're trying to do it, and it's just not fun. It's just not fun to take your lumps the way we are. My frustration is that I would like to see us improving at a faster pace. We're still making mistakes at this point in the season that we made earlier. We cannot find consistency, and it's hard to beat anybody, much less a really good football team in LA Tech, who's got a you know great chance to win this league here in a couple weeks. So, you know, ton of respect for what they've done, told Sonny (Cumbie) that before the game, and several of their players as well, that they really respect how they've done, gone about doing it, and got to give their administration credit for allowing them to fight through it and get to this point. So, I feel that support here. I know our administration is on the same page. We got the same vision. Just frustrating for our kids and our fan base to get to that point, but and we'll try to speed it up as much as we can, man. We try to speed it up as much as we can. The goal is to find a way to win this week. You know, I know the history here with South Alabama. It's not good. We need to turn that in our favor, and so our focus is on playing a complete game. We've seen it at times. Finding a way to get that Saturday, honor our seniors' last home game. Find a way to get a win, and we'll worry about you know the next one after that. But really, just focus on this week, getting better, being better, doing our best. So, what questions you have?"
Q:
About Coach Cumbie and their NIL, does it seem like they use it more to retain the players they have?
"There's been a ton of emphasis on retention. Their GM and I are friends from college. You don't ever just open the hood up and show everything, but even talking to recruits we're both recruiting, it's obvious that retention is a big key for them. It's kept their group together. I think some of that is just the kids they recruited. Listening to Sonny talk and talking to Brian about their building and the guys in their building, they're fortunate they built around guys who had pride in staying, who didn't chase money that would have been out there. They stayed for what they were getting, and the program took care of them at the best level they could. Similar to the rest of us in the league, there's more money out there if that's what a guy is chasing. But their culture shows a group of guys who stuck together and saw the value in that. They're going to have a chance to reap the rewards, which is not real common in today's landscape. I told our team very openly when it was over that this is what's possible if we stay patient in the process and keep working. They've been right where we're at right now, so we'll see how that goes. Retention has been a big emphasis for them. It is for us, too. We want to retain as much of this roster as we can. Financially, you have to make a commitment to do that without bankrupting yourself, so you can still acquire talent to fill gaps and holes. It's a delicate balance."
Q:
Coach you talked about accelerating the process a little bit, with these last few weeks, now that bowl eligibility is out and all that. Are you going to kind of shift towards devilment and get some guys in there that haven't had a chance yet?
"I'm going to try to do what it takes to win. I'd like to find a way to win these last two. I'd much rather start 5–7 instead of 3–9. That just tastes better going into the offseason. Every time we go out, I'm going to try to win, are we open-minded about using every available person who could potentially help us? Yes. Is this spring ball? No. We're not just going to throw guys out there and evaluate them. We need to feel like they're ready to help us and impact us in a positive way. We've been open-minded about using every available guy for a while, and we've got some guys who are banged up, too. So, there may be a few guys you see who you haven't seen so far because of injuries alone, but I'm definitely not taking the approach that this is spring ball and the results don't matter. I don't think that's fair to the fan base, our kids, or our seniors. We want to win. We work too hard not to want to win. I want to make sure we're in the right frame of mind about what we're trying to accomplish here. Think of it like the first two games of next season: let's find a way to win these suckers and get better as we do it. I don't know if that makes any sense or not, but my logic is twisted."
Q:
Your message to John (White) as he comes over during the game, and then taking that from the game and into film sessions?
"Oh yeah, and we're watching every single snap of these, I mean, there's not a snap that's getting missed at any position, especially quarterback. I am really frustrated with the two balls John forced. You know, the RPO world is a little bit risky anyway. Give them credit. I thought the technique that they used too, they know we're RPO based team. They are too. They threw a few RPOs throughout the game too. I thought the technique that their linebackers and overhang players use was really, really well played to commit you to the RPO and then be able to rally and get a hand on the ball. That scares you every time you call an RPO. You know that's always at risk. Linebacker, D lineman getting their hand up, and we see it so much now defensively, and the defenses that we're playing against see it so much now. That is something that you are teaching, to you know to squeeze to the ball, be in your gap, but then recognize and not everybody does it as well as they did it. So, two balls that go up, read wise, that ball was going exactly where it's supposed to go. It's going to be a tight window, but it's going where it's supposed to go. It's the two interceptions that he threw, leaving the pocket, making poor decision on one. That's just got to be run out of bounds, throw the ball away, punt the ball. All right, you cannot force the ball back in the middle. The trick play, it's going to be second and 10. Throw the ball away. Like he can't do that. So yeah, we're watching every snap, trying to coach him through poor decisions. He did not have a particularly good day, but he had a lot of help in that. They played good defense, and we did not play well. Really up front, got people to him too much, too much pressure. Some of the misses, he was pressured, had to get rid of it before he should have. Like, there's a lot going on. He gets too much credit and almost probably too much criticism as well. Those two completely his fault. Several of the others, there's a lot going on, and so he'll learn from it. Nothing's changed about my opinion of him. To be honest with you, I know it's easy looking stat line and just say you know he can't do it. That's not the case, and he'll learn a lot from it. I would expect he'll play much better because of how that game how that game went. Play really good defense. You're going to have to earn it. You cannot force it. And he clearly did, and we can't have that. We got to punt. We got to kick field goals. We got to kick extra points. Like we got to end every drive in a kick, and it's got to be in a kick. And he knows that. Matter of fact, I heard him say that in his interview. I guess that means he's listening. You know, we got to do better there, and he's more than capable of doing better. But he needs help across the board at every position. We got to get better.
"
Q:
Coming into the season, you know, there's a lot of new on the defensive side of the football with all the new players, new starters, new defensive coordinator. How much do you take into that? You know, for all the for the numbers on the defensive side of the ball, you know, moving forward or what you want to do in the off season as far as whether or not staff changes, whether or not it's personnel.
"I'm not firing staff. Just everybody that wants my staff fired not happening. Now, is everybody on a clock next year? Yeah, like we know that this is a foundation. Go in and really, really heavily assess where we are. Be critical. Make changes where needed, but you know this is the first step of a very difficult, frustrating process, and you know I think if we were just schematically unsound, that would be a different thing. That's not the case. We need to get everybody on the same page, and this is offense and defense and special teams. We're not playing well in any phase consistently, as we know. I think that it's no different. You always hear you should make the most improvements from week one to week two in in football games. Well, year one to year two and a rebuild. That's where we got to see dramatic improvement. And this time next year I'm not going to be, I mean I have a completely different approach. Right now, this is the foundational piece. Let's learn as much as we can. Let's retain as much as we can. Let's recruit to fill holes and develop together. And maybe that means schematically there's some adjustments that need to be made to do that, but I'm not going to make an emotional decision in year one when I think if anybody was really, really transparently honest, like we knew this was going to be really hard, and that when you play in a good league, which we do, that there's going to be some days it doesn't look real pretty. So, you know, I'm very clear with my staff. Stick to what we're doing. Continue to work on being better. All of us, including me, I'm going to help them where I can. Let's retain, recruit, develop, and we have to have a great off season, preparing into next year, so that we're not playing mistake football. I mean, like we're a bundle of mistakes right now, and I think some of those are on the staff and where we need to simplify and maybe scale back in areas. Some of that falls on players not doing what they're asked. Some of it is physically they can't do it. Some we got to find what they physically can do. Like there's a lot going into this process. Hard to do that in a week to week to week. Our off season is going to be critical for where we move. If hopefully that makes sense. What I'm explaining. It's a much bigger process than right now. Love where we're at. I love where we're at in recruiting right now. Last year, it's hard to say because there was just so much uncertainty. Even at this point in time in the year, there was kind of a theme in the air. I think we all kind of knew what was going on. And truthfully, this just wasn't Coach Huff's deal was we're going to replace issues in the portal like that's just where his mind was. I don't want to live on the portal. I want to still recruit a high school, good solid JUCO guys and use the portal where needed. So, we have picked up I don't know six or seven commitments in the last few days. Another one last night. Expecting several more this week. A huge weekend coming this weekend, you know this is the lifeblood of you know of who we're going to be, and we're starting at home. We're starting in a footprint about three hours around town, and having, I think, very real, transparent conversations with guys about what this year is and where we want to go, where we want to be, and what my history as a head coach would tell you, we're capable of doing inside this culture. So I feel really good about that. And part of it is recruiting our roster to stay. Like they're getting blown up every day by agents all over the country promising them the world, you know. And so part of it is just conversations with our guys to retain. We won't bat 1000, but hopefully we can keep that number really, really high."
