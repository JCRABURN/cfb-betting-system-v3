Emmanuel Okoye's Sees Beauty In His Journey From Nigeria To Berkeley
By:
Steve Kroner
Three East Coast trips – including this week's for Saturday's game at Virginia – a trip to Dallas and one to Las Vegas give the California football team a total of more than 20,000 air miles traveled this season.
If there's one player on the Golden Bears' roster who particularly can appreciate traveling long distances, it's outside linebacker
Emmanuel Okoye
. His home of Lagos, Nigeria, is nearly 8,000 miles from Berkeley.
"It's been a journey," Okoye said in an interview in California Memorial Stadium on Tuesday. "Every time I look back, it's just looking at the beauty of all the steps that I went through to get here. It just seems so crazy when I tell the story to people."
Okoye's story on how he got to Cal began more than four years ago when he was 17 and knew nothing about football. Now listed at 6-foot-5, 245 pounds, Okoye was a shooting guard/small forward in basketball.
"I could do a lot of things with the ball," Okoye said.
His basketball coach provided Okoye with the idea of playing football. Then Osi Umenyiora, the two-time Super Bowl-champion defensive end for the New York Giants, invited Okoye to "The Uprise" football talent-identification camp in Abua, Nigeria.
"Everything came to me from Osi Umenyiora," Okoye said.
After "The Uprise" camp, Okoye went to NFL Africa's talent-identification camp in Ghana. He did well enough there to earn a scholarship to the NFL Academy in England. Though he played in only two games at the NFL Academy, he began the process of learning the game and trying to get a scholarship offer to play college football.
"I just wanted to put myself out there the best way possible," Okoye said.
He would get video clips of himself in practice and post them on Twitter/X. He would try to contact coaches. "I was just pressing the buttons any way I could," Okoye said. He wound up receiving 18 scholarship offers, per an April 2023 ESPN.com story.
Cal was listed as one of those 18 schools, but Okoye chose Tennessee. He began his career with the Volunteers as a tight end in 2023 but did not play. Okoye moved to defensive end in the spring of '24, but a knee injury in preseason camp forced him to miss all of that season.
He played sparingly last season before entering the transfer portal and coming to Cal. Throughout his time in Knoxville and Berkeley, Okoye has tried to balance his aspirations to become a high-level player with the realization that he's competing with and against players who have roughly a decade's more experience in playing and understanding the game.
"There are a lot of moving parts to football and it takes time," Okoye said. "That's one thing that I've kind of come to peace with myself. In the past, I was kind of impatient. Just not getting what I want out of myself.
"But over time, I've just learned to give myself grace and to make sure I stay consistent with trying to learn and keep picking up as much as I can and just making sure that every day I show up, I know there's a chance to get better."
Okoye made the SEC Academic Honor Roll last year. He's majoring in legal studies at Cal, and the university's academic prestige was a reason he chose to come to the Bay Area.
"My family is one that holds education as a priority," Okoye said. His parents, Lawrence and Ifeoma, and three of his siblings -- brother Augustine and sisters Nwadinma and Chiagozie – live in Nigeria. Another sister, Onyedika, is a nurse in Minnesota; she came to Cal's game against visiting Clemson in September.
In the more than four years since he went to the NFL Academy in England, Okoye has not returned to Nigeria. He nevertheless remains close with his family.
"I make sure to be in touch with (my parents) almost every day," Okoye said. "My mom will probably call me today." Okoye said his family members "are the people that keep me grounded."
Cal players and coaches also help Okoye adjust to life so far from his homeland. "The guys on the team just make you enjoy being here," Okoye said. "We're all just like a whole brotherhood. That's one thing I like about this place."
Over the past four decades, Nigerians have made their mark in the NFL. Running back Christian Okoye (1987-92), wide receiver Nelson Agholor (2015-24) and linebacker Samson Ebukam (2017-present) are three of the 41 Nigerian natives listed on pro-football-reference.com as having played in the league.
Two Cal alums, wide receiver Iheanyi Uwaezuoke (1996-2000 in the NFL) and running back Adimchinobe Echemandu (2004-07), are on that list as well.
Still a relative football newcomer,
Emmanuel Okoye
hasn't yet received considerable playing time in college, much less in the NFL. But he certainly possesses the physical attributes to become a major contributor for the Bears – and he also doesn't lack for ambition.
"I have a very high ceiling," Okoye said. "Where I see myself becoming is probably way farther than what people might see.
"A lot of things are beginning to click. I'll just keep growing and showing up every day. Just expect to see a lot of good things."
