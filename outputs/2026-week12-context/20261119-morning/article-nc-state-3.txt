Michael Named Offensive Coordinator
11/18/2026 1:05:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
NC State head football coach Philip Rivers has announced that Jason Michael has joined his staff and will serve as offensive coordinator. Michael brings 23 years of coaching experience to his new role with the Wolfpack, including two decades in the NFL.
"I'm really excited to add Coach Michael to our staff," said Rivers. "I have history with him from my time with the Chargers and with the Colts and we are aligned in our vision for building this program. His expertise and experience in the offensive system that we are going to implement here at NC State is second to none, and I can't wait for him to get started."
Most recently, Michael coached the tight ends for the Philadelphia Eagles for five seasons under Nick Sirianni (2021-25). In 2024, the Eagles' offense, which ranked eighth in total yards per game, defeated the Kansas City Chiefs in Super Bowl LIX. Tight end Dallas Goedert hauled in 59 passes for 711 yards (12.1 avg.) and three scores, including team highs in receptions (17) and receiving yards (215) during the postseason. Michael also guided Grant Calcaterra to a breakout campaign, totaling 298 yards on 24 catches (12.4 avg.) in 17 games (13 starts).
"I'm excited and grateful for the opportunity for me and my family to join the Wolfpack family!" Michael said. "The awesome game of football is what connected Philip and me 15 years ago in San Diego, and although it's taken us both many places since then, I'm thankful for the chance to reunite at the place it all started for him.
"Philip's leadership, winning mentality, and vision for NC State football are what made this decision a no-brainer for me.  I want to thank him,
Boo Corrigan
and all of those involved for their trust and confidence in me.  I'm looking forward to getting to work, building something special, and helping bring success to NC State."
In 2023, Michael navigated Goedert to a career-high 59 receptions for 592 yards and three touchdowns in 14 games. Goedert ranked ninth among NFL tight ends in catches per game (4.2), while also logging the fifth-most yards after the catch per reception (5.4) at his position.
During the 2022 campaign, when Philadelphia advanced to Super Bowl LVII, Michael coached Goedert to his second consecutive season with 700- plus receiving yards (702). In his first year with the Eagles, Michael coached Goedert to a career season, as he tallied the fifth-most receiving yards (career-high 830) among NFL tight ends and led his position in receiving first-down percentage (75.0 pct.) and ranked second in yards per catch (career-high 14.8).
During the 2019-20 seasons, Michael served as the Indianapolis Colts' tight ends coach, working under Sirianni. Over that two-year stretch, Indianapolis' tight ends combined to rank fifth in the AFC among their position group in receptions (168, 10th in the NFL) and yards (1,862, ninth in the NFL).
In 2020, the year Rivers joined the Colts, the team posted the 10th-ranked offense (378.1 ypg) in the league. Michaels also coached the tight ends to a productive year in 2019, with Jack Doyle earning a Pro Bowl nod after starting all 16 games and totaling 43 receptions for 448 yards and four touchdowns.
Michael served as the tight ends coach for the Arizona Cardinals during the 2018 season, when Ricky Seals-Jones registered career highs in both receptions (34) and receiving yards (343).
Prior to Arizona, Michael spent four seasons on the Tennessee Titans' staff as the quarterbacks coach (2016-17) and offensive coordinator (2014- 15). He played an instrumental role in developing quarterback Marcus Mariota, whom the team selected with the second-overall pick in 2015.
From 2015-16, Mariota ranked first in the NFL in red zone passer rating (114.6) after accounting for 33 red zone touchdown passes with zero interceptions. As a rookie in 2015, he became the first player in NFL history to record a perfect passer rating (158.3) in their starting debut.  As offensive coordinator in 2015, the Titans established a team record with 12 different players catching a touchdown.
Michael first worked with Rivers during his stint as the San Diego Chargers' tight ends coach from 2011-13, where he guided Antonio Gates to the fourth-most receptions (190) among NFL tight ends, including 77 catches in 2013. He also helped Gates earn a Pro Bowl selection in 2011.
Michael spent two years with the San Francisco 49ers (2009-10) as an offensive assistant and worked with the team's quarterbacks in 2010. Before joining the 49ers, he coached the tight ends at the University of Tennessee (2008), where he originally began his career as a graduate assistant with the secondary and special teams units (2003-04).
Between stints in Knoxville, Michael served as a tight ends coach (2007) and quarterbacks/quality control coach (2006) for the New York Jets. In 2006, he helped Chad Pennington earn
Associated Press
NFL Comeback Player of the Year accolades. He began coaching in the NFL as a quality control coach with the Oakland Raiders during the 2005 season.
Michael was a two-time captain at quarterback for Western Kentucky after beginning his collegiate career at Army. As a senior in 2002, he guided the Hilltoppers to the NCAA I-AA title and was named the school's Male Athlete of the Year.
A native of Louisa, KY, Michael earned a bachelor's degree in civil engineering technology from Western Kentucky, posting a perfect 4.0 GPA. A second-team Verizon Academic All-District IV nominee, he was also named a I-AA Athletics Directors Academic All-Star.
Michael and his wife Jamie have a son, Wyatt, and a daughter, Charlie.
