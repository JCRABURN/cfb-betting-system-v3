WVU Concludes Home Slate With Meeting Against 20th-Ranked Houston on Saturday
November 18, 2026 01:13 PM
|
Football
West Virginia Game Notes
Houston Game Notes
Share:
By:
John Antonik
MORGANTOWN, W.Va. – Two teams looking to rebound from disappointing performances last weekend will meet in Morgantown on Saturday afternoon when West Virginia plays host to 20th-ranked Houston.
The Cougars were in the College Football Playoff conversation until last Friday night's 42-32 loss at Colorado, while West Virginia was unable to get untracked in a 37-14 defeat to Kansas and is still hunting for a sixth victory to become bowl eligible.
That road won't be easy for 5-5 West Virginia with Saturday's game against 7-3 Houston preceding a meeting at second-ranked Utah to conclude the regular season.
Houston will be making its first-ever visit to Milan Puskar Stadium this Saturday eager to make amends for last year's 45-35 loss to the Mountaineers in Houston. The Cougars, under second-year coach Willie Fritz, are unbeaten at home, but  just 2-3 when going on the road, although two of those road defeats have come against Texas Tech and Utah.
The Cougars, led by senior quarterback Conner Weigman, have the best third-down offense in the country this week, converting 58.9% of their third-down tries this season. Houston employs a methodical, ball-control attack that is racking up 35.1 points and 470.9 yards per game, while dominating time of possession by holding the football for an average of more than 31 minutes per game.
Weigman is completing 65.6% of his passes for 2,552 yards and 25 touchdowns with just three interceptions. He is also contributing 308 yards and seven touchdowns on the ground.
The Cougars have a two-pronged rushing attack that features Makhi Hughes (712 yards and five touchdowns) and Re'Shaun Sanford II (643 yards and 5.6 yards-per-carry average) and is averaging 207.4 yards per game.
All-Big 12 wide receiver Amare Thomas is the team's top outside receiver, showing 53 catches for 764 yards and nine touchdowns, while UTSA transfer tight end Patrick Overmyer has been another reliable target with 52 catches for 614 yards and eight scores.
UH's third receiving option is senior Trent Walker with 36 receptions for 477 yards and three touchdowns.
"They've got a very experienced quarterback and a lot of guys who have been in their system for a couple of years who have been highly productive against everybody this year, so it should be a great challenge," West Virginia coach
Rich Rodriguez
said. "We've struggled against everybody pretty much this year, so it's going to be a big game for our defense."
Defensively, the Cougars' beefed-up front seven is limiting opponents to an average of 121.5 yards per game on the ground, and combined with Weigman's outstanding ball security, have produced a plus-six turnover margin so far this season.
"They're really athletic," Rodriguez says of Houston's defense, "but they don't blitz a whole lot, although they have been blitzing a lot more the last couple of weeks. They've got really good athletes up front, which we struggled with last week somewhat, and they've got some experience in the secondary and they've got a really good defensive coordinator (Austin Armstrong) who does a lot of stuff."
Houston's issues have been a vulnerability to the pass, giving up 249.3 yards per game through the air, red zone struggles permitting teams to score on 88.9% of its scoring opportunities and a lack of explosive plays. The Cougars rank 40
th
in 20-plus-yard plays in 2026, which should help West Virginia's struggling defense that is giving up an average of 7.6 explosive plays per game.
The Mountaineers' 76 explosives allowed are the most in the country this year and equal the 76 the 2012 defense permitted in 13 games. That figure represents the most explosive plays surrendered since West Virginia joined the Big 12 Conference.
Clouding West Virginia's outlook for Saturday is the health status of starting quarterback
Michael Hawkins Jr.
, whom Rodriguez labeled as "questionable" on Tuesday after Hawkins suffered a bone bruise to his ankle late in the Kansas loss.
Hawkins has already established a WVU record with 20 rushing touchdowns and can become the school's first 2,000-1,000 player in passing and rushing with 43 more rushing yards.
If Hawkins can't go, Rodriguez indicated sophomore
Scotty Fox Jr.
will get the nod. Fox quarterbacked West Virginia to its 45-35 victory over 22
nd
-ranked Houston last year by accounting for three touchdowns, two coming on the ground.
Meanwhile, Weigman passed for 308 yards and four touchdowns against the Mountaineers last year in Houston, although he was picked off twice.
West Virginia's
Cam Cook
is coming off his worst rushing performance of the season against Kansas with just 16 yards on nine attempts, which could prompt some blocking adjustments up front.
WVU is operating without senior right guard Bubba Grayson, who was injured in the Texas Tech loss.  The starting offensive line West Virginia used against Kansas included freshman
Kevin Brown
at left tackle, sophomore
Josh Aisosa
at left guard, senior
Wes King
at center, junior
Nick Krahe
at right guard and junior
Carsten Casady
at right tackle.
For the Mountaineers to pull off another upset, they must maximize their offensive possessions and keep pace with Houston's efficient offensive attack on Saturday. Rodriguez also believes a big play on special teams might also be required.
Oddsmakers are giving Houston an 8½-point edge and are anticipating a high-scoring affair with one of the highest over/under point totals in the country this weekend at 70½ points.
Therefore, points should come in abundance, which will keep fans interested and engaged throughout.
Twenty West Virginia University seniors are expected to be recognized before the start of Saturday's game, which kicks off at 1 p.m.
Saturday will also be the final game for the current Milan Puskar Stadium press box, scheduled to be razed at the conclusion of the season to make way for a new $150 million structure slated to be completed before the 2028 football season.
TNT, TruTV and HBO MAX (JB Long, Mike Golic Jr. and Allie Laforce) will provide live television coverage. Mountaineer Sports Network radio coverage with
Tony Caridi
, Dwight Wallace and
Jed Drenning
begins at noon, two hours after the GoMart Mountaineer Tailgate pregame coverage, on stations throughout West Virginia, online via WVUsports.com and the Varsity Network and WVU Gameday apps.
Tickets remain on sale through the Mountaineer Ticket Office and can be purchased by logging on to
WVUGAME.com
.
