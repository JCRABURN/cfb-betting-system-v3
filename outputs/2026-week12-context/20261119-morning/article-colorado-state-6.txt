Better Late Than Never for Martinez
11/18/2026 12:00:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
Freshman energized by first game action
By:
Mike Brohard
A simple run down the middle, he read, reacted and made the stop with Xavier Winbush. A play seen a thousand times over by most, but it was a first for
Kallen Martinez
. It came in week nine of his first college season, but at the  base, it felt very natural.
"I was just going through my read steps and just seeing the flow of it, and it popped out of me, it just felt like second nature,"  Martinez said. "It felt like exactly what I'd seen in film, exactly what we did in the walkthrough. So seeing it and being able to react on it was definitely a great feeling."
Be ready at any time. Really for anything if you're a member of Colorado State's secondary. Martinez spent the majority of the season playing corner, but his first action came at nickel.
By design, nickels coach
Matty Ah You
said the position wasn't a foreign concept to the youngster from Tampa, Fla. Secondary skills can translate, so those in the backend stick together while learning specifics.
"One thing that we do here that Coach (Jim) Mora has really helped us with is he wants all DBs to meet all together. So every meeting that we have, all the DBs are here, all the same thing," Ah You said. "So they're learning corner, they're learning nickel, they're learning safety and then after that, after we get all the installs and the corrections, then you can get into your individual groups and separate. But we always start together and we always end together."
There's no hiding the fact Mora and the defensive staff have been seeking answers and solutions in a season were the Rams rank last in the nation in points allowed per game (47.6). The 476 allowed is closing in on the school record, one they'd rather not own.
In the second half of the Washington State game, four freshman picked up substantial playing time, and Mora singled them out for their willingness to step in and make plays.
For Xavier Winbush, it was his fifth game. Linebacker
Jaylen King
(eight games) and
Jaden Hernandez
(nine games) have seen most of their action on special teams. The four of them, including Martinez, combined for 15 tackles, all in the second half.
This season, 18 freshmen have played for Colorado State, accounting for 78 games played. Two –
Cedric Ross
and
Desman Manuel
– have played in all 10. Two – Ross and Wimbush – have started games.
Martinez had dealt with injuries – he laughed about practicing with a 5-pound club on one hand for a spell – and spent most of the season running the defense of the next opponent. Even in those periods, he would still draw back on what he was being taught during individual drills.
Ah You said that is crucial for all players, especially those with the least inexperience.
"So we do a great job here of making sure that they split the reps. So, not the whole time they're going to be on scout team, they come and get some live reps," he said. "Someone like Kallen, who has spent the majority of the time on scout team, he's been able to come and get reps inside the classroom and learn from really taking all that stuff that we do inside the meeting rooms and outside the walkthroughs and carrying it over.
"It's been a little easier for Xavier because I've kept him with me majority of the time and had him split maybe 60-40 with the actual defense because he's been in the two-deep. But, really, that's where it comes where the classroom and what we do and the walkthroughs, before we come out on the football field, that's where that takes really important."
Martinez said no matter his situation – early in camp or late in the season, running nothing but scout or recovering from injury – he kept his mind focused and never lost hope. He's just not built that way.
In his mind, if he approached it right, his time might come.
"I've always been a big believer in just always staying ready and always being prepared. I mean, the best ability is being available," he said. "So it was just getting those mentor reps, watching my teammates go in the film room, going through my iPad and just taking those reps on scout team and just staying ready.
"Since fall camp, I've been in my notebook. I've been studying it, looking it over. I mean, the process of how I've went over my notes hasn't really changed. I've always been really good at going back into my notebook, going into my iPad, so the process has kind of always been the same with that. In regard to the scout team stuff and the defense, I mean, it's not the same defense, of course, but a lot of the techniques are very similar, so just honing in on what the call is, what kind of technique I'm playing, and just being very intentional with that."
Throughout the season, Martinez said his surroundings have been nothing short of positive and encouraging. It's been the veteran players in the secondary helping his mind and the rights and wrongs, telling him the next rep would be better.
It was also assistants
Drew Anderson
and
Miles Fishback
, helping with scout, telling him he was doing a good job and giving good looks. Word spreads when players work hard, and hard work leads to the depth chart.
As Martinez hoped – and now knows – that can come at any point of the season.
With only three games remaining, getting told by the staff to be ready was the type of encouragement which was next level.
"For sure. I mean, I'm willing to do whatever I need to do to help the team win and go in a positive way," Martinez said. "If that means I have to play nickel one week, safety the next week, corner the next week, I'm more than willing to do any of that. And I'm more than willing to work with any piece of material that I need to work with in order to get the mental aspect of it and just be prepared to do that."
