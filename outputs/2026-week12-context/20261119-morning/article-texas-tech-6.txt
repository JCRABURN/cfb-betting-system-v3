Holmes named Outland Trophy semifinalist
November 18, 2026
|
Football
Share:
LUBBOCK, Texas –
Texas Tech senior defensive lineman
A.J. Holmes Jr.
was named Wednesday one of seven semifinalists for the 2026 Outland Trophy, presented annually to college football's most outstanding interior lineman.
The Greater Omaha Sports Committee and the Football Writers Association of America (FWAA) announced the semifinalists, which include five offensive linemen and two defensive tackles. The FWAA's All-America Committee selected at least one semifinalist at each eligible position. The Big Ten and Big 12 have three semifinalists apiece, while the SEC has one.
Holmes has recorded 45 quarterback pressures this season, the most among FBS interior defensive linemen and is second among all FBS Power 4 players in that category. Holmes has recorded at least three pressures in nine of 10 games thus far as his 90.1 overall pass rush grade leads his position group. The lone exception was against Sam Houston when he played a season-low 34 defensive snaps.
Holmes leads the Red Raiders with 8.0 sacks, including 5.5 combined in the past five games alone. He has recorded at least a share of a sack in seven of 10 games, continuing his strong play from a year ago that landed him All-America honors. Holmes currently ranks tied for 12th in the FBS for sacks and leads all interior defensive linemen overall.
Three finalists for the Outland Trophy will be announced Tuesday, Nov. 24. The winner will be announced during The Home Depot College Football Awards, airing live on ESPN on Dec. 11.
2026 Outland Trophy semifinalists (listed alphabetically by last name)
Anthony Boswell, Houston, C
Xavier Gilliam, Tennessee, DT
A.J. Holmes Jr.
, Texas Tech, DT
Greg Johnson, Minnesota, C/G
Trevor Lauck, Iowa, OT
Kade Pieper, Iowa, G
Zereoue Williams, Utah, OT
First presented in 1946, the Outland Trophy is the third-oldest major college football award. Dr. John Outland, an All-American at the University of Pennsylvania in the late 1890s who later practiced medicine in Kansas City, Missouri, provided the FWAA with a financial contribution to establish the award. An avid outdoorsman, Outland believed linemen deserved more recognition and wanted an award that honored their play. The award is open to interior offensive and defensive linemen. Ends are not eligible.
