JOHNSON'S JOURNAL - Four-Time Cancer Survivor; Bryce Steele Takes Nothing for Granted
11/18/2026 10:59:00 AM
|
Football
Share:
By:
Jon Johnson
As a four-time cancer survivor, Troy senior linebacker
Bryce Steele
never takes a day - or time on the football field - for granted.
"I'm still able to wake up and call my mom, call my brother and still put on a football helmet and go out there and compete, whereas people who were in the same situation as me are no longer on this planet," Steele said.
As a 17-year-old who was a big-time college football recruit, Steele had his world rocked after a malignant tumor was found behind his heart.
"Nobody in my family had dealt with cancer," Steele said. "I never had been personally impacted by it, so I really never knew how severe it could be.
"I was still trying to play football. That's all I really cared about at the time."
In the years to come, Steele would come to understand the magnitude of the disease better than he could have ever imagined.
While football would still be the driving force of his recovery, Steele's purpose in life became much more than playing a sport.
"Ever since I was a little kid, my goal has been to play college football and be in the NFL," Steele said. "But I realized that may not be God's plan for me in life, and that I was put on earth by Him and everyone on this planet has a purpose, so it's just up to Him, and I to figure out what that purpose is. I accepted that."
How it was discovered
In August of 2019, Steele was getting ready for his junior season at Episcopal High School - a boarding school in Alexandria, Virginia - when he started experiencing a constant cough and chest pain.
"A couple of thoughts kind of crossed my mind," Steele said. "One, I just slept in an awkward position, and you sometimes wake up with lower back pain or something like that.
"Obviously football is a physical sport, so I thought I had gotten hit wrong, or landed on the ground wrong. Cancer or a tumor was definitely the very last thought in my mind."
The diagnosis began when an X-ray revealed a shadow of something behind his heart.
"My school doctor had told me he didn't know what it was, but it looked like a mass that's not supposed to be there," Steele said. "I didn't really think that it was cancer.
"I was just like, whatever it is, it can't be that bad because I was still able to put on a football helmet and go out there and run around. I was still in pretty good shape; it was just that I had the constant cough.
"I definitely didn't think I would be out for the entirety of my junior season and then having to deal with it five years later."
Surgery was required to successfully remove the tumor, followed by 30 rounds of radiation.
It was the first step in wiping out the cancer, but unfortunately not the last.
Settling on a college
After missing his junior season, Steele recovered and was in good enough shape to play as a high school senior before the COVID-19 pandemic canceled the season.
It meant Steele would miss two years of playing football, which would also highly affect colleges recruiting him.
"When I didn't play, I didn't really go public about why I wasn't playing," Steele said of his missed junior season. "I don't really like people feeling sorry for me, so I didn't really tell anybody.
"Coaches were reaching out to me asking why they weren't seeing any film or seeing any stats. I didn't know what to tell them, so I just ignored them.
"Ohio State, Michigan, Notre Dame … all of those schools kind of stopped talking to me."
Word eventually surfaced about Steele's physical situation, but South Carolina and then head coach Will Muschamp continued to recruit him.
"That showed to me they still valued me as a person; still valued me as a player," Steele said.
Steele, a native of Raleigh, N.C., committed to play for the Gamecocks before things abruptly changed.
"Coach Muschamp got fired, and I was kind of scrambling for a couple of weeks or so," Steele said. "I didn't really want to stay home in North Carolina and go to UNC or NC State.
"Having gone to boarding school, it kind of opened my eyes up to wanting to go see different places in the country and experience a different lifestyle."
Jeff Hafley, who was the defensive coordinator at Ohio State and had recruited Steele, became the head coach at Boston College and offered Steele a chance to follow him there.
"It sounds kind of ignorant to say now, but when Boston College offered me after my freshman year, it wasn't a school I was interested in," Steele said. "Then, having gotten sick and having football taken away from me, it kind of changed my perspective on knowing I could have football taken away from me at any given moment.
"I obviously needed to have a degree from a good school to give me an opportunity to be successful in something other than football."
With the 2020 high school season being canceled, Steele signed with Boston College and enrolled early in 2021, believing his health problems were behind him.
More bad news
After removal of the tumor, Steele was scheduled for check-ups every three to six months as a precautionary measure.
"For that whole 2020 year, I was good," Steele said. "Then, that spring of 2021, I was going in for my normal CT (imaging test), and they found a couple of nodules (showing cancer).
"They went in and removed the nodules. I was only sidelined for a couple of weeks. It wasn't a big surgery."
Just knowing cancer had returned was discouraging, but Steele wasn't too concerned.
"It definitely hurt, but it was small nodules," Steele said. "You go in and cut them out, and you'll be back in a couple of weeks or so.
"Obviously I was annoyed because I was going to be two or three weeks away in terms of training for my opponents, but that was kind of how my mind was operating."
Steele played in 11 of 12 games for Boston College the following fall as a true freshman, recording 27 tackles.
The next spring, cancer was once again discovered in some nodules that were removed with another minor procedure. Steele returned to the field in short fashion and played in 12 games as a sophomore, recording 51 tackles.
What followed in the spring of 2023 ahead of his junior season would be the biggest challenge of Steele's life.
"I had a great spring physically, and I know for a fact I was in the best shape of my entire life," Steele said.
Yet when Steele went in for his scheduled scans, cancer had returned and spread throughout his chest cavity and ribs.
"I'm not actually sure how it grew that fast during that time period of a year, but it did," Steele said. "When they first told me, I really didn't even let the doctor finish explaining what happened. I was like, 'OK, when can I have surgery … how quickly can I get back out on the football field?' because it was like a routine to me.
"That was when my surgeon said, 'Well, you may not be able to play football again.' I was like, 'What do you mean?' She showed me the scans and where everything was growing. She thought the best approach was to cut open my sternum and go in like that. She was saying if she did it that way, I wouldn't be able to play football again."
Steele wasn't ready to consider that option.
"I told her, 'Well, we're going to have to find another way because there's no way I'm going to do that,'" Steele said. "This wasn't like when I was first diagnosed in 2019, when I had the cough and chest pains. I had no signs … I felt incredible.
"I played a decent amount as a freshman and as a sophomore had an even better year. Physically, I felt like I had finally reached my grown man body, and I was really ready to take that next step and try to get recognized on a national level in college football.
"I even asked her if there was a possibility that I could play that season and revisit my health a year from now just to see. It was a slow-growing cancer. She was like, 'I don't think that's a good idea, because the more time it grows, the more it's going to spread over your body and it's going to be even a more complicated surgery.'
"I was like, 'I'm not going to let you cut my sternum open. I'm sorry, but you're going to have to go back and talk to my oncologist and the other doctors on the board and figure something else out.'
"That's when she came back and said we're going to try a couple of rounds of chemo and try to kill off some of the cancer cells in the body and see if that makes the surgery less extensive."
A long road back
In October of 2023, Steele was back under the knife for a surgery he said, "Changed my life forever, if I'm being honest."
Prior to the extensive surgery, Steele got in the best shape possible so he wouldn't have as much of a gap to close once he was able to train again for a return to football.
"But boy, I was a bit over my head," Steele said. "I thought the recovery process was going to be a lot easier than it was."
For the first time, Steele contemplated quitting football. If not for the encouragement from his mother, he might have given up the sport.
"I was in the hospital for about a month-and-a-half," Steele said. "For those first three weeks, it just felt like I was getting bad news after bad news. One of my cardiologists came in, and he was like, 'How much do you love football?' I was like, 'A lot, why?' He was like, 'Well, you might want to try and find something else to do.'
"I was on a lot of drugs at the time, so mentally I wasn't in the right headspace. I just remember looking at my mom and her looking at me, and I just immediately started bawling. He took my mom to the side and was explaining to her why he came in and said that. That was the second or third doctor that told me you should give football up.
"My mom came back into the room after speaking to him, and kind of wiped my tears, and was like, 'I believe you will be back on the field one day. It might not be this season, but I believe you will put on a helmet again, and you have to believe that yourself.'
"From that day on, I accepted that physically I might not be able to do some of the things I was able to do before. But one of the reasons I continued to play was to prove those doctors wrong."
Steele was a shell of himself after the surgery.
"When I went into the surgery in October, I weighed about 235 pounds," Steele said. "Then when I left, I dropped down to like 190 pounds. I was skin and bones. I had no muscle definition. I couldn't walk 20 feet without being tired and extremely fatigued."
Back on the field
Steele received a medical redshirt for the 2023 season and began the long process of trying to play football at some point the next year.
Hafley left as head coach of Boston College in February of 2024 to become defensive coordinator in the NFL for the Green Bay Packers and Bill O'Brien was hired in his place.
"I met with Coach O'Brien earlier in the season and said, 'Listen Coach, I feel like right now I'm not able to contribute to the team like I want to," Steele said. "Can I go on the scout team and compete against our offense; try to give them a good look and get myself into football shape?'
"He was like, 'Yeah, but what if we need you to play?' I said, 'If you guys need me to play, then obviously I'll be ready to play.' That time came."
Injuries hit the Boston College linebacker corps hard and Steele was needed with four games left in the 2024 season.
It was during the first half of a game against SMU in Dallas that Steele returned to the field for the first time since the major surgery.
"They were ranked then and the top team in the ACC," Steele said of SMU. "It might have been the second quarter that I came in and made my first tackle.
"I didn't hit the guy as hard as I wanted to, or at least as hard as I thought I could at the time, but I made a tackle, and it was just nice to be back on the field."
While happy to be back playing football, it was frustrating for Steele not being in prime shape.
"Physically, I wasn't quite there yet," Steele said. "I think I was able to maybe play three or four plays without being gassed."
Steele returned full speed for the 2025 season at Boston College and played in 11 games with two starts. He recorded 16 tackles, one fumble recovery, one pass break up and one quarterback hit. His best game was a start against the University of California in which he tied a career-best with seven tackles.
Fresh start at Troy
After earning his bachelor's degree in communications at Boston College, Steele entered the transfer portal for his final year of football eligibility.
"I wanted to go somewhere where I had the opportunity to play more," Steele said. "I wanted to go somewhere warm compared to being up in Massachusetts. I came down here and felt immediately at home."
Troy has been a good fit for Steele on and off the field.
"I grew up in Raleigh, N.C., which is like a suburban area," Steele began. "I lived in Boston the last five or six years, and now I'm down here living in Alabama. It's pretty cool to live in all those places and experience different ways of living.
"I'm grateful for the opportunity to be down here. I've made a lot of great relationships with my teammates that will carry on past football.
"I'm getting my MBA right now as well (master's in business administration). I'm trying to get as many degrees as possible and still playing football and enjoying it while I can."
Steele has recorded 26 tackles and recovered two fumbles in 10 games for the Trojans this season.
Just like has always been the case, his mother is present to cheer on her son.
"She doesn't miss a game," Steele said. "One of the main reasons I still play football now is also because I know how much it makes my mom smile for her to be able to see me out there out on the field competing."
Although thankful to be playing, Steele can't help but think about what might have been.
"Not to reminisce, but I watch my film from before I got sick and then after I got sick and there's been times I start crying," Steele said. "It's frustrating, because there are certain things I know my body was once able to do and then now when I try to do it even now, I can't."
Keeping the faith
Brought up in a Christian household, Steele relied on his faith during his cancer battles.
"I'm not going to lie, it was definitely tough," Steele said. "My relationship with God was kind of a roller coaster at the time because a lot of it early on I felt like, 'Why me? Is this a punishment for the sins I committed in the past?'
"I felt like I was a pretty good person, but I was trying to think back in my time of all the people I had potentially done wrong, and is this God punishing me for doing that to people?"
"I kind of just started reading the Bible. I have this Bible app on my phone and you can choose a specific category of love, hate, jealousy … whatever it may be … and you can pull up different verses and kind of talk about that topic.
"I realized that I was more than just a football player and everything happens for a reason, and that I just have to trust the process."
Looking ahead
With his football career coming to a close, Steele is grateful for the journey despite his many obstacles.
"The two years that I missed from cancer I kind of gained them back on the back end, if you think about it," Steele said. "A lot of my friends I came into college with are either in the NFL or working jobs.
"We'll get on a FaceTime call and those guys who are working are like, 'I wish I could still go out there and put on the helmet one more time.' Everybody calls me Grandpa because I'm 24 and still playing college football."
Steele hopes to get into real estate development once the final whistle blows on his football career.
"A part of me early on was worried about what life after football would be like for me, but I've put all my trust in God and I feel like He's going to guide me in the right direction with my life," Steele said.
"I don't feel like my time here is going to be wasted."
