Klanderman trying to ‘teach these guys not to have a victim mentality’
Three Takeaways: Nov. 16
Klanderman trying to 'teach these guys not to have a victim mentality'
Director of Sports Journalism
Jerry Hill
gives his three takeaways from Monday's weekly press conference with interim head coach
Joe Klanderman
talking about the Bears' 28-7 road loss at 10
th
-ranked BYU and Saturday's matchup between Baylor (6-4, 4-3) and No. 11 Texas Tech (9-1. 6-1), which kicks off at 2:30 p.m. Saturday at McLane Stadium and will be broadcast by FOX:
NO EXCUSES, NO VICTIM MENTALITY:
With the FAA grounding and the Bears traveling to Provo, Utah, on game day, it would have been easy to use the travel issues as an excuse for the Bears' loss at BYU. But Klanderman said it would be wrong "for me to sit up here and say that was a big deal if I'm trying to teach these guys not to have a victim mentality. I think that's a problem for kids today, there's a victim mentality. Anything goes wrong, it's not my fault, it's my circumstances that created that. If there is one thing I'm trying to teach these guys in my time with them is that that's not right. I don't believe in that, to my core."
'DIFFERENT IN THEIR APPROACHES':
Since benching pocket passer Will Hammond for dual-threat QB Tommy Castellanos, Texas Tech's offense is "probably more similar to BYU than they were prior to that," Klanderman said. But where BYU is "going to try to muscle you with size, with huge receivers, big tight ends and humongous running backs, Texas Tech's approach is a little bit different, a lot more speed, a lot more athletes in the open field. The emphasis this week is trying to be great in the open field. We're going to have to be great on the perimeter." While quarterback Bear Bachmeier is BYU's leading rusher and had three rushing touchdowns against Baylor, Tech running back J'Koby Williams is fourth in the Big 12 and top 15 nationally with 1,078 yards and eight TDs on the ground.
THE PLACE TO BE:
Noting how "awesome" the crowds were for the TCU game and the Homecoming game against Iowa State, Klanderman said he is "hoping we can keep these students engaged. I think they're doing a great job of making it fun for the students. When you go to BYU, you notice the atmosphere. There are so many raffles going on and all these things they're doing, so there's something else going on there besides the game. . . . It's our job as a football program to give them something to rally around. I'm hoping it's a bigger thing moving forward, that this is the place to be on Saturdays. Not just the students, but the people in the community, too. 'Baylor's playing at home, we're there.' That's the goal."
