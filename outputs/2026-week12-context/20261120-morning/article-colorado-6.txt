Buffs’ Special Teams Remain A Bright Spot
November 19, 2026
|
Football
Buy Football Tickets
Donate to Football
Share:
By:
Marcelo Brant, Athletic Communications Student Assistant
BOULDER – The Colorado Buffaloes' special teams unit played a crucial role in Friday night's upset victory over No. 14 Houston, making several key plays to help the Buffs control field position.
"Special teams played a role," Deion "Coach Prime" Sanders said after the game. "The offense showed up, the defense showed up, special teams showed up. So we all were here."
Punter
Damon Greaves
has been an unsung hero for the Buffs this season. The Australian punted three times against the Cougars, totaling 164 yards.
"As a punter, you've got to be one of the more consistent players on the team," Greaves said. "Putting your team in the best field position you possibly can, and just being a consistent player for the team, is what you're meant to do. So I feel I've been able to do that quite well this year, and these last two games, it's certainly a big emphasis to continue that."
After his first punt of the game traveled 47 yards and pinned Houston at its own 15-yard line, Greaves delivered an even bigger effort in the third quarter with a booming 75-yard punt from the CU 25-yard line into the Houston end zone, marking the longest punt by a Buffalo in the 21st century.
"Sometimes I go out there and just sort of execute my punt," he said. "I didn't really realize it went 75 yards in the end until someone told me. So that's pretty cool, to have a career long of 75."
The kick marked the first time a Buffalo recorded a punt of at least 70 yards since Alex Kinney did it against Texas State in 2017. It was also just the 27th 70-yard punt in program history, making Greaves the 18th Buffalo to reach the mark and only the fourth Colorado player to do so this century.
Consistently solid performances for the Buffs have earned Greaves national recognition. The senior was included in the Ray Guy Award midseason watchlist, a prize given to the best punter in the country.
"It's obviously great to get that recognition outside of the facility here," he added. "But I'm not able to get that without a good snapper and a good bunch of guys in front of me blocking for me, and covering for me, too. So I've got to give credit to those guys, because they've been awesome this year. Without them, I wouldn't be on that watch list."
Wide receiver
DeAndre Moore Jr.
also made an impact on special teams against Houston, recording a 34-yard punt return – a season-long for the Buffs –  that helped spark a second-quarter touchdown drive for the Buffs heading into the half.
Kicker
Daniel Gerlach
, who stepped into the starting role after kicker Elliot Arnold was injured on a late hit against Arizona State, went 6-for-6 in extra points, marking another solid performance by a CU specialist on Friday night.
"For the kids, they've been expecting that type of performance from one another all season long, offensively, defensively, as well as special teams," Coach Prime said. "So they got what they've been desiring. It just all came together."
