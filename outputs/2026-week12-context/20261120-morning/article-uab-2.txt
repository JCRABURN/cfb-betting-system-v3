Blazers Turn Attention to UTSA for Senior Day at Protective Stadium
11/16/2026 6:05:00 PM
|
Football
UAB Game Notes vs. UTSA
UTSA Game Notes vs. UAB
Share:
BIRMINGHAM, Ala.
– With bowl eligibility secured for the first time since 2022, the UAB football team returns to Protective Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 21, for a pivotal American Conference matchup against UTSA. Kickoff is scheduled for 7 p.m. CT on ESPNU.
The Blazers (6-4, 4-2 American) are coming off a 36-10 victory at Temple that secured their sixth win of the season and first road victory of 2026. However, head coach
Alex Mortensen
made it clear during Monday's press conference that the program's focus has already shifted toward the challenges ahead.
"I told the players I'll celebrate it after the season," Mortensen said of becoming bowl eligible. "We've got more work to do. I'm excited about getting on the practice field, getting back in the meeting room, teaching, coaching and going to work."
UAB faces one of its toughest remaining tests in UTSA (8-2, 5-1 American), which enters Saturday's contest tied for first place in the conference standings following a 35-16 victory over North Texas. The Roadrunners have established themselves as one of the league's most consistent programs under head coach Jeff Traylor.
"Coach Jeff Traylor has done a great job there and has been able to sustain success for a long time," Mortensen said. "They're strong in all three phases and have done an excellent job with their roster. It'll certainly be a challenging game."
UTSA's offensive versatility presents a significant challenge for a UAB defense that leads the nation in passing yards allowed and ranks first in the American in total defense. The Roadrunners average 33.2 points and 401.9 yards per game, utilizing a balanced attack behind quarterback Owen McCown and a talented group of running backs.
Most recently, running back A'Marion Peterson rushed for 179 yards and a school-record four touchdowns against North Texas, earning American Conference Offensive Player of the Week honors.
"Balance is part of what has made them good," Mortensen said. "They have really good run-pass balance. You can see that within a game, or it might vary from game to game. Owen McCown has played really good football for them at quarterback. They're a challenge not only to prepare for but also to deal with in the game."
Mortensen also emphasized the physicality of UTSA's defense, which limited a North Texas offense that entered last week's game leading the nation in total yards to a season-low 304 yards.
"I have the utmost respect for the program they've built and the way they play," Mortensen said. "They have a physical team, and that physicality isn't just at the line of scrimmage. It extends beyond the line of scrimmage to all three levels. That's something I admire in football teams, and we certainly want to have very physical teams here, too."
For UAB, Saturday also represents an opportunity to complete an undefeated home season. The Blazers are 5-0 at Protective Stadium this year and are seeking their first perfect home record since 2019.
The home finale will also serve as Senior Day, with UAB recognizing its graduating student-athletes before kickoff. Mortensen credited the team's veteran leadership with helping establish the culture and chemistry that have contributed to the Blazers' turnaround.
"We've got really good leadership on this team, and a lot of it is senior-driven," Mortensen said. "We talked about having a team, not a collection of individuals. They all bought into that. I'd argue they were a major driving force behind creating that."
Mortensen noted that Saturday will carry additional meaning for those making their final appearance at Protective Stadium.
"For their last opportunity to be in Protective Stadium, it's a big deal for them," Mortensen said. "At the same time, we're not going to let it become a distraction."
Running back
Rod Robinson II
, who rushed for a career-high 202 yards and three touchdowns against Temple, echoed his coach's emphasis on preparation and the importance of maintaining a team-first mentality.
"Every week you want to go 1-0," Robinson said. "You have to win Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, not just Saturday. It's about getting better every day, preparing for the game and earning the right to play fast. Personal goals are much easier to accomplish when the team goals are accomplished first."
Robinson also credited the close relationship among UAB's running backs for their collective success. Alongside
Bam McReynolds
and
Ja'Vin Simpkins
, Robinson has helped lead a rushing attack averaging 173.7 yards per game.
"Me, Ja'Vin and Bam are all super close," Robinson said. "Whenever one person is having a great game, it feels like we're all having a great game. We stick up for one another, and we look at the rushing game as our game. No matter who's carrying the ball, it's a reflection of all of us."
Robinson said the Blazers understand the challenge awaiting them against a physical UTSA team.
"They play super hard. I love what they have going on. They're a great team," Robinson said. "We've got to go out and do what we do: play hard every snap, produce positive plays, cut back on some of the negative ones, and continue to play fun and fast. I think it'll be a good game."
Special teams could also play an important role in Saturday's matchup. Punter
Oliver Straw
, who ranks among the nation's leaders in punting average, emphasized the importance of field position against a talented offense.
"The defense has been stellar all year long. A big part of my role is helping them with field position," Straw said. "If I can put them in position to make plays and put pressure on the offense, that's something I think about."
Straw, who joined the Blazers shortly before the season after spending four years at West Virginia, said the opportunity to contribute to UAB's success has made his final collegiate season especially rewarding.
"It's been a great year," Straw said. "Everyone has been really welcoming, and I've loved it."
While the Blazers have already accomplished one of their goals by securing bowl eligibility, Mortensen emphasized that the team's approach remains unchanged as it enters the final two games of the regular season.
"We really want to create a team that's intrinsically motivated and focused on chasing its best, chasing perfection, if you will, and continuing to get better," Mortensen said. "We need to sprint to the finish line. We're not where we want to be yet."
Saturday's game will also feature recognition of the 2026 UAB Athletics Hall of Fame class, including former football standout Rodregis Brooks. With students preparing for fall break, Mortensen encouraged the UAB community to support the Blazers in their final home appearance.
"I know we've got fall break coming up, but I want to encourage all our students to hang around one more day and come out to the game," Mortensen said. "We're playing at night, and hopefully it'll be a good environment. We're excited to be back home."
THREE QUESTIONS WITH COACH MORTENSEN
Q: How much do you rely on running backs coach
Hindley Brigham
to manage the rotation during games, and how much input do you have in those decisions?
Mortensen:
"I have a ton of confidence in Hindley and really respect his feel for the game and for the backs. He's got a great history with running backs, and a lot of our fan base is familiar with it. He's recruited, coached and developed some great runners. He's a great technician, he's great with the psychology of it, and he's got a really good feel for how and when to play them.
"We talk during the week and have a plan going into the game. If we feel the need to deviate during the game, he and I will speak. Sometimes he'll make decisions in the game. We try to communicate as much as possible, but I trust him tremendously to make a lot of those calls."
Q: How important was it to get
Adam Lepkowski
and
Payton Kirkland
back in the offensive line rotation?
Mortensen:
"Having
Adam Lepkowski
back at center is really good. He's a leader on our team, a really good player, and he does a really good job for us. I can't tell you how much I've enjoyed coaching him.
"Having
Payton Kirkland
back is also very helpful. Payton was starting for us at one point, and losing him was disappointing. I hope he can continue to accelerate and get better. I also want to say we're playing other guys we like. We like
Barrett Maddox
and
Cooper Young
. All these guys can help us, and they're still getting better. Having Adam and Payton back in the rotation was really helpful."
Q: With Saturday's 7 p.m. kickoff, how does the team's game-day routine change compared to an earlier start?
Mortensen:
"We do have a different routine based on kickoff time, and we've played at different times all year. With a later game, you're able to get more meals in, and you can vary the rest, meetings and things of that nature when you've got more time."
