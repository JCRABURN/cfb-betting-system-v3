Carter's Corner: Remembering Charley Casey
Wednesday, November 18, 2026
|
Football, Scott Carter
Share:
By:
Scott Carter, Senior Writer
GAINESVILLE, Fla. —
Charley Casey
and
Steve Spurrier
will be forever linked.
Casey, an All-Southeastern Conference receiver, caught Spurrier's first official college touchdown pass. The 8-yard reception was the final score in Florida's 24-8 victory in the 1964 season opener against Coach
Hayden Fry
's SMU team and marked sophomore Spurrier's varsity debut with the Gators.
Meanwhile, Casey's last touchdown catch with the Gators came on a pass from Spurrier in the Sugar Bowl following the 1965 season. As the Gators mounted a late comeback against Missouri, Casey made a twisting catch in the end zone after a Tigers defensive back tipped the ball. Casey's 21-yard touchdown pulled Florida within two points, but the Gators failed on the two-point conversion and lost.
As Casey walked off the field at Tulane Stadium that afternoon, he did so as the SEC's single-season record holder for receptions (58) and yards (809) in a three-yards-and-cloud-of-dust era. During their two seasons together in the mid-1960s, Casey and Spurrier helped usher in a new era of SEC football. Casey led the SEC in receiving during his junior and senior seasons, only the second player in SEC history at the time to do so.
Gators
Charley Casey
, left, and
Steve Spurrier
receive ceremonial kisses when the team arrived in New Orleans for the Sugar Bowl after the 1965 season. (Photo: St. Petersburg Times via Newspapers.com.)
More than three decades later, with Spurrier then Florida's head coach and a national champion, Casey was among the close friends and former teammates in a New Orleans Hilton ballroom in the wee hours of Friday, Jan. 3, 1997. They celebrated along with UF President
John Lombardi
, Gators athletic director
Jeremy Foley
and
Ray Graves
, Florida's head coach during the days when Spurrier and Casey formed the SEC's most dangerous passing duo.
It was a celebration the two had dreamed of as players.
Casey, one year ahead of Spurrier in school, joined Florida's junior varsity team in 1962 after graduating from Brown High School in Atlanta. Most expected Casey to sign with hometown Georgia Tech, but a famous Yellow Jacket alum prevented it.
Charley Casey
Pepper Rodgers
, the former Georgia Tech star, was Florida's offensive coordinator under Graves. He persuaded Casey to sign with the Gators.
"I guess Tech just got a little complacent about Casey,'' Rodgers told the
Jacksonville Journal
in 1964. "Tech never doubted Casey would sign with them. They didn't work as hard on him as they should have. I sold him on our football program and on our engineering school. Casey's switched to business administration since entering school, but the football part of it has worked out every bit as well as we could've hoped."
Rodgers made those comments following Florida's 37-0 victory over South Carolina, a game Florida's quarterback recalled decades later.
"I remember Charlie Casey caught a post pass, and it was one of the best spirals I ever threw,'' Spurrier said a few years ago as South Carolina's coach. "We won pretty easily."
Casey caught seven passes for 102 yards and a touchdown. He began to garner significant national attention afterward and was named a second-team All-American after the season. The next season, both Casey and Spurrier were named first-team All-Americans, the first players at their positions in UF history to earn the honor.
Casey finished his college career with 114 receptions for 1,614 yards and 14 touchdowns, eye-popping numbers for a receiver during his era.
The Atlanta Falcons drafted Casey in the 1966 NFL Draft, but he suffered a leg injury and never played in the NFL. He entered the business world and spent the latter portion of his career as a Fort Myers financial advisor.
He remained close to Spurrier through the years, and they played golf together a couple of times each year.
Casey had seats in the south end zone at Ben Hill Griffin Stadium on the night of Dec. 1, 2001. The Gators lost to Tennessee in what turned out to be Spurrier's final home game as Florida's head coach.
Casey was 57 at the time and was asked how the teams he and Spurrier starred on would fare against the current Gators.
"It might be a good scrimmage for about half of a quarter,'' he told the
Fort Myers News-Press
. "These guys are so much bigger, stronger and faster."
Charles Arthur Casey
quietly passed away
Sept. 3 at the age of 82. His death only gained notice on social media earlier this month and was not mentioned in the sports pages or websites of any of the state's major media outlets.
On a day the Florida Sports Hall of Fame announced its 2026-27 inductees –
including former UF running back Neal Anderson
– it seems fitting we remember Casey.
He was
inducted into the Florida Sports Hall of Fame
in 1968 and the
UF Athletic Hall of Fame
the same year.
Charley Casey may have assessed his teams would be unable compete with Spurrier's Gators, but he had long since made his mark nonetheless, carving out a place in program history alongside the most famous Gator of them all.
