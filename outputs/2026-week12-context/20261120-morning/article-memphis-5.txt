No. 24 Tigers Head East to Annapolis for Saturday Game at Navy
Nov 19, 2026
|
Football
Media Center
Share:
ANNAPOLIS, Md. –
The University of Memphis (8-2, 5-1 American) football team is set for its final road game of the regular season, traveling to Annapolis for an 11 a.m. kickoff at Navy (3-6, 2-4 American) inside Navy-Marine Corps Memorial Stadium.
The game will kick off at 11 a.m. CT on ESPN2 with Anish Shroff on play-by-play, Andre Ware on color and Morgan Uber on the sideline. The game will also be available to listen to on 98.9 THE ROAR OF MEMPHIS, Memphis' Athletics flagship station for Tiger Sports Network, with Dave Woloshin (play-by-play), Jarvis Greer (analyst) and
Greg Gaston
, Sr.
(sideline) on the call.
SETTING THE SCENE
• Memphis heads into its penultimate regular season matchup with the path to a conference championship appearance becoming clearer.
o The Tigers would clinch a spot outright by winning out and having North Texas or UTSA lose one of their last two games.
o If the season ended today, tiebreakers using rankings kept by the American Conference would place Memphis as the host of the LLH Healthcare American Conference Championship, with the UTSA Roadrunners coming to the 901.
• After a strong performance in a 52-41 win over South Florida, the Tigers continue to boast one of the best offenses in the country, ranking third in total offense (520.1), eighth in rushing offense (227.3) and scoring offense (39.0), along with being ranked 12th in passing offense (292.8).
• Defensively, Memphis ranks 20th in the country in sacks per game (2.80) and 10th in both third down defense (30.9%) and red zone defense (72.2%).
• QB
Marcus Stokes
sits as one of the nation's top passers with the third-best passing efficiency (177.0), fourth-most passing touchdowns (28) and eighth-most passing yards (2,845).
• RB
Jaylin Carter
became the 12th FBS player to reach 1,000 rushing yards with 109 against South Florida and currently ranks 14th in rushing yards (1,038), 12th in rushing touchdowns (12) and 22nd in yards per carry (6.03).
• With Memphis' aerial attack, four receivers have at least 28 catches for 400-plus yards, led  by
Jermane Hayes
' 38
grabs and and
Jemyri Davis
' 561 receiving yards and seven touchdowns.
o In an offense that leads the country in explosive plays (110), five Memphis receivers (
Jemyri Davis
,
Jermane Hayes
,
Hunter Tipton
,
Stonka Burnside
,
Terrell Timmons Jr.
) have a catch of at least 65 yards on the year.
• Memphis' pass rush has been led on the edges, as ends
Elijah Kinsler
(7.5),
J'Mond Tapp
(6.0) and
Kadan Spratling
(3.0) have combined for 18 sacks, with eight coming up the middle from defensive tackles
Taylor Burton
(4.5),
KJ Miles
(1.5),
Jaden McKinney
(1.0) and
Xavier Miles
(1.0).
• The 2026 Memphis Tigers roster features a grand total of 81 newcomers, split between 20 true freshmen and 61 transfers, with 27 returners from last season.
• Huff brings along 17 players from his 2025 Southern Miss squad, including All-Sun Belt performers: DE
J'Mond Tapp
, LB
Mike Montgomery
and SAF
Ian Foster
.
o The list also includes 5 players who played for Huff at both Marshall and Southern Miss.
• Memphis returns just four players (CB
Chauncey Logan Jr.
, DL
Taylor Burton
, WR
Brady Kluse
, OL
Parker Mitchell
) that started at least one game for the Tigers in 2025.
• The Tigers currently have 115 victories since 2014, the 10th-most in the nation during that stretch and the most among current American Conference schools.
•  Dating back to a win over UAB in the 2023 season, Memphis has forced at least one turnover in 34 of its last 42 games. Over that stretch, the Tigers have created 76 takeaways (43 INT, 33 FR).
INSIDE THE SERIES
•  Memphis meets Navy for the 12th year in a row, leading the all-time series 6-5 and currently sitting on a two-game losing streak.
•  Last season's matchup was the nail in the coffin of what was a once-promising Tiger season, completing a four-game collapse after starting the year 8-0.
•  In that loss, Memphis was held scoreless in the second half after holding a slim, 17-14 lead going into halftime. In the defensive battles, both sides combined for just 588 yards of offense, with Navy reaching exactly 300 and Memphis stuck with 288.
SCOUTING THE MIDSHIPMEN
• The Midshipmen sit in the bottom half of the American Conference standings, coming in with a 2-4 mark in league play after picking up a 42-35 win at home against Temple last Saturday.
• QB Jackson Gutierrez, a member of the Davey O'Brien QB Class of 2026, has been dominant as a rusher, following in the long line of successful mobile QBs at the Naval Academy, logging 1,159 rushing yards with 18 touchdowns on the ground this season. `
o Navy's other QB, Braxton Woodson, also has 391 yards on the ground with five touchdowns, second-most on the team.
• Gutierrez has also been strong through the air, tossing eight touchdown passes on just 42 completions for 895 yards.
• The only receiver above 200 receiving yards this season is Jordan Collins, who has four touchdown receptions and 324 yards.
• Defensively, Navy has been stout with the No. 27 rush defense in the FBS, led by a linebacking tandem of Coleman Cauley and MarcAnthony Parker, combining for 128 tackles on the season.
• On the back end, Giuseppe Sessi leads the team with a pair of interceptions, also having 54 tackles.
• Cauley and Parker also have 3.5 and 4.0 sacks, respectively, ranking as the top two on the team, with Cauley tied with Aiden Lyczek at 3.5.
"THREE THINGS IN LIFE THAT'S CERTAIN: DEATH, TAXES AND THAT THE TIGERS GONE' HUNT" - W, 27-21 at UNLV
• The Tigers opened the
Charles Huff
era with a statement win over RV/RV UNLV, a program who entered the season with higher odds than Memphis of becoming the Non-P4 representative in the College Football Playoff.
• Memphis entered the half trailing 14-7 after allowing a pair of explosive UNLV plays, only countered by the first of many connections from QB
Marcus Stokes
to TE
Hunter Tipton
on a 16-yard passing touchdown.
• In the second half, Stokes knotted the game up at 14 apiece on a 48-yard pass to WR
Stonka Burnside
, with RB
Jaylin Carter
later scoring on a 36-yard run to make it 21-21.
• The Tiger defense stood tall in the second half, limiting UNLV to just seven points in the final 30 minutes, forcing a pair of turnovers-on-downs and sacking Jackson Arnold six times, highlighted by 2.5 from DL
Elijah Kinsler
.
• Stokes finished his Memphis debut 15-for-26 for 223 yards and a pair of touchdown passes, also netting 45 yards on the ground.
• In a display of pure dominance at the point of attack, the Tigers recorded six sacks, with DL
Elijah Kinsler
(2.5), DL
J'Mond Tapp
(1.0), DL
Taylor Burton
(1.0), DL
Joseph Head Jr.
(1.0) and DL
Jaden McKinney
(0.5) all wreaking havoc in the backfield.
CHRISTENING THE STADIUM WITH A RIVALRY WIN - W, 42-24 vs. ARKANSAS STATE
• In front of a sold-out crowd of 45,632 in the newly renovated Simmons Bank Liberty Stadium, the Tigers put on a dominant display with a 42-24 win over Arkansas State in the Paint Bucket Bowl.
• RB
Jaylin Carter
shined for a second week in a row, this time going off for 183 rushing yards and four touchdowns, matching
Mario Anderson Jr.'s
TD total against North Texas in 2024 and the most rushing yards since
Greg Desrosiers Jr.'s
204 last season at Florida Atlantic.
o Carter was named the American Conference Offensive Player of the Week for his performance.
• Carter wasn't the only Memphis running back to show out as
Manny Covey
had 15 carries for 103 yards, giving him his first career 100-yard rushing game as well.
• QB
Marcus Stokes
became the first Memphis Tiger with 2 or more passing TDs in his first two starts since Riley Ferguson in 2016, finding
Jermane Hayes
and
Aidan O'Callaghan
in the end zone for each's first collegiate touchdown.
• After Memphis' defensive line starred against UNLV, the Tiger secondary stole the show against the Red Wolves as SAF
Dylan Smith
, SAF
Ahmere Foster
and LB
Zach Ruffin
each picked off Arkansas State QB Trey Owens a total of four times.
o Smith picked off Owens twice, the first time a Tiger had multiple INTs in a game since Sylvonta Oliver in the 2022 SERVPRO First Responder Bowl against Utah State.
A TALE OF TWO HALVES - L, 20-38 at BOISE STATE
• The Tigers suffered their first loss of the season in a battle of two of the frontrunners for the top Group of Six program in a matchup that saw a contrast in play style and two completely different halves of competition.
• Memphis, a program formerly known for dominating time of possession, led 20-17 at the half with two of its scores just taking 33 seconds off the clock on an 87-yard reception by TE
Hunter Tipton
and an 80-yard run by RB
Jaylin Carter
.
o Memphis' four scoring drives, all coming in the first half, took a total of 4:33 off the clock.
o Comparatively, Boise State's scoring drives took a combined 21:14 of game time.
• After carrying a 20-17 lead into the half, Boise State took a commanding lead after a pair of defensive stops and scores, including the first punt-return touchdown allowed by Memphis since 2021.
• Memphis was shutout completely in the second half, with Boise State scoring the final 21 points of the game.
• QB
Marcus Stokes
had a career night in the loss, passing for 384 yards and connecting on three passes of 40-plus yards.
• TE
Hunter Tipton
had one of the best single-game performances by a Memphis tight end in program history, finishing the game with nine catches for 229 yards and a touchdown, en route to being named the John Mackey Award Tight End of the Week.
o It was the most receiving yards by a Tiger since Calvin Austin III's 200 against Tulsa in 2021.
• SAF
Dylan Smith
led Memphis with 12 tackles in the loss, a new career high for the Southern Miss transfer.
A FULL SUPPORTING CAST PUT ON A SHOW - W, 45-21 vs. UT MARTIN
• The Tigers took care of business at home, putting up a third-straight 500-plus yard performance against UT Martin.
• QB
Marcus Stokes
became the first Tiger since
Seth Henigan
in 2024 to pass for 4 TDs in a game, also tying his own career-high of 26 completions.
o Stokes was named an American Conference Weekly Honorable Mention for his performance.
• A multitude of backs featured for the Tigers, with true freshman
Jordan Thompson
gaining 110 yards with one touchdown on just six carries.
• Memphis took a balanced approach passing, with 12 different players catching a pass, the most in a game since 2021.
o Included in those receivers were RB
Jaylin Carter
, WR
Joshua Joseph
and TE
Jonathan Harding
, who all logged their first career touchdown receptions.
• The Tiger defense was also balanced, with no player making more than five tackles, but with 22 different players recording a tackle.
A MONSOON OF SCORING - W, 59-8 at CHARLOTTE
• Memphis opened American Conference play with a 59-8 rout of Charlotte in an adjusted matchup, with kickoff moved up by four-and-a-half hours due to inclement weather in the Charlotte area.
• Before the skies opened up in the Queen City, QB
Marcus Stokes
went off in the first half with three passing touchdowns to
Jermane Hayes
,
Jemyri Davis
and
Hunter Tipton
, with his pass to Hayes going for 93 yards - the second-longest passing touchdown in program history.
• Memphis' defense also came through in the first half, forcing turnovers on back-to-back possession with an interception by
Ian Foster
and a fumble forced and recovered by
Chauncey Logan Jr.
• The Tigers ended the first half with a 24-0 lead after
Philippe Laforge
connected on a 20-yarder as the clock hit zero.
• Stokes followed up with another pair of touchdowns in the second half, finding Tipton again and
Stonka Burnside
, with Burnside 'Mossing' a corner to come down with the catch.
o Stokes finished the game 20-for-25 for 318 yards and five touchdowns, with the touchdowns matching his career high.
• Memphis continued the scoring with three more touchdowns in the second half, one from
Air Noland
to
Brady Kluse
and a pair of rushing touchdowns by
Dallan Hayden
and
Jordan Thompson
.
• LB
JJ Clayton
also came up big in the second half, recovering a pair of fumbles on a botched snap and on a kickoff return, with the latter forced by
Tylon Nash
.
• WR
Jermane Hayes
logged his first career 100-yard receiving game, finishing with 123 on six catches.
• TE
Hunter Tipton
continued his strong campaign for being the top tight end in college football, hauling in three passes for 60 yards, including a pair of scores.
• WR
Stonka Burnside
had season-high marks with four catches for 58 yards, including a 19-yard touchdown catch.
BRINGING THE BONES HOME - W, 38-13 vs. UAB
• After dropping last season's matchup in Birmingham, Memphis made sure to leave no doubt when returning the Battle for the Bones Trophy to the 901, beating the Blazers by four scores.
• Memphis leaned on its rushing attack and defense in the win, with RB
Jaylin Carter
tallying 132 yards on the ground with two touchdowns.
o TE
Hunter Tipton
even got in on the ground attack, scoring on a leaping goal line rush.
• QB
Marcus Stokes
managed the Tiger offense well, finding
Tychaun Chapman
and
Aidan O'Callaghan
through the air from 45 and 13 yards out.
• Memphis' defense wreaked havoc on the Blazers, registering eight tackles for loss with sacks by
Zach Ruffin
,
Elijah Kinsler
and
J'Mond Tapp
, along with two forced fumbles by
Jabari Ishmael
and Tapp, and an interception by
Dylan Smith
.
• It was the third time this season that Memphis had at least two passing and rushing touchdowns.
WIPED OUT - L, 17-31 at TULANE
• Despite committing the most turnovers since 2006 (6), the Tigers made it a one-score game with Tulane up until the final 94 seconds.
• The Tigers' six turnover came on three interceptions and three lost fumbles, while having six total fumbles forced in the game.
o Memphis also caused two turnovers of its own on an
Ahmere Foster
interception and a
Jaylen Lewis
kickoff fumble recovery.
• WR
Stonka Burnside
had a career night against the Green Wave, catching seven passes for 151 yards and a touchdown, being named an American Conference Weekly Honorable Mention for his performance.
• Even with being held out of the end zone, RB
Jaylin Carter
had a career-high 23 carries for 135 yards, his third-career 100-plus rushing yard game.
• WR
Jemyri Davis
also had a solid game, hauling in four passes for 89 yards and a score.
• Memphis' defense kept Tulane at bay, only allowing just over 300 yards of total offense and recording seven TFLs, led by
Elijah Kinsler
's 2.0, followed by
JJ Clayton
and
Chauncey Logan Jr.
with 1.5 each.
PLUNDERED - W, 41-34 (OT) vs. EAST CAROLINA
• After entering the second half at a 13-point deficit while only scoring on a
Philippe Laforge
field goal, the Tigers stormed back with four touchdowns in the second half to force overtime and eventually win.
• Memphis scored three times in the third, ending with a 24-20 deficit after passes from
Marcus Stokes
to
Jermane Hayes
and
Jemyri Davis
and another field goal by Laforge.
• Memphis took its first lead of the game with just 3:31 left in the fourth quarter on a two-yard run by Stokes to go up 34-27 before East Carolina scored to send it to overtime.
• In the extra frame, Memphis took just two plays to score with Stokes flipping into the end zone.
• On East Carolina's first and only possession, a Pirate WR tripped on a fade route, with
Ellis Ellis Jr.
making the easy interception to seal the deal.
• QB
Marcus Stokes
was named to the American Weekly Honorable Mentions List, Davey O'Brien Great 8 and Manning Stars of the Week after totaling 351 yards of offensive and being responsible for all five Memphis touchdowns.
• Memphis' defense kept Tulane at bay, only allowing just over 300 yards of total offense and recording seven TFLs, led by
Elijah Kinsler
's 2.0, followed by
JJ Clayton
and
Chauncey Logan Jr.
with 1.5 each.
KNIGHTY KNIGHT - W, 49-22 vs. ARMY
• Scoring in every quarter, with multiple touchdowns in three of four, Memphis dominated in all phases en route to a Halloween night victory against the Army Black Knights.
• QB
Marcus Stokes
found
Jermane Hayes
twice in the first quarter for scores, with another touchdown pass to
Jemyri Davis
and a rush by
Jaylin Carter
giving the Tigers a two-score lead going into the half.
• The second half opened with more of the same as Stokes found Davis again in the end zone.
• Entering the fourth quarter with a 13-point lead, rushing scores from Stokes and Carter put the game far out of reach.
• Defensively, DL
Joseph Head Jr.
recorded the only sack & TFL for the Tigers, with
Chauncey Logan Jr.
and
Ellis Ellis Jr.
both recording an interception for the second game in a row.
OUTRAN THE BULLS - W, 52-41 at SOUTH FLORIDA
• Memphis and South Florida combined for 93 points, the most scored in a Thursday night American Conference matchup this seaosn, with the Tigers pulling away with a 52-41 win inside Raymond James Stadium.
• The Tigers jumped out to a 21-0 lead in the first quarter after a pair of rushing touchdowns by QB
Marcus Stokes
, with a passing TD from Stokes to
Hunter Tipton
in between.
• Memphis tacked on another touchdown on a goal line rush by
Jaylin Carter
before going into the half up, 28-17.
• After the Bulls cut Memphis' lead to four points, the Tigers scored on back-to-back possessions on a pass from Stokes to
Stonka Burnside
and a field goal by
Philippe Laforge
, ultimately entering the fourth quarter knotted up at 38-38.
• The Tigers came out dominant in the fourth quarter, scoring on a 76-yard touchdown from Stokes to
Jemyri Davis
to retake the lead after USF opened the quarter by taking its first lead of the game.
• After forcing back-to-back turnovers on downs, Memphis put the game away with a 26-yard rushing touchdown by
Jaylin Carter
, putting him above 100 in the game on his second score on the ground.
• With 386 yards of total offense, three passing touchdowns and two rushing touchdowns, QB
Marcus Stokes
became the first player since Bo Nix in 2023 to account for five or more touchdowns in back-to-back-to-back games.
• RB
Jaylin Carter
logged his fourth 100-yard rushing game of the year, reaching 1,000 on the season with 109 in the win, only needing 71 to hit the mark.
• WR
Jemyri Davis
had a record night, registering a career-high 161 receiving yards with a touchdown.
• Memphis' defense wreaked havoc on a run-heavy USF offense, totaling 13 tackles for loss, a season-high, while also logging seven sacks, the most since 2017.
• Leading the way for the Tiger defense was LB
Mike Montgomery
, whose 19 tackles were the third-most by a player in an FBS game this season at the time, and marked the 12th time in American Conference history that a player from an American school had at least 19 in a game.
• Memphis' edge rushers terrorized QB Luke Kromenhoek the entire game, with
J'Mond Tapp
logging 2.5 sacks,
Elijah Kinsler
with a pair and freshman
Kadan Spratling
with a solo sack.
