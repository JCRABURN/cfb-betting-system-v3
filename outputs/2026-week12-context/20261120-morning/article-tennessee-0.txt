#8 Vols Emphasize Team Growth, Versatility Ahead Of Senior Day Showdown
November 17, 2026
|

KNOXVILLE, Tenn. –
Tennessee's success on the gridiron this season has been built on contributions across the roster, with the tight ends helping power the ground game and a veteran secondary emerging as a playmaking unit.
Tight ends coach
Alec Abeln
and co-defensive coordinator/secondary coach
Anthony Poindexter
met with the media Tuesday ahead of the No. 9/9/8 Volunteers' regular-season home finale against No. 24/23/21 LSU. The Tigers visit Neyland Stadium for a 3:30 p.m. ET kickoff Saturday on ABC.
Abeln emphasized the importance of the tight ends' work as blockers, a role that has helped make Tennessee one of the SEC's most productive rushing offenses. He said the group has embraced the physical demands of the position and understands how its work up front helps define the offense.
"I do think there's no greater feeling than taking a man from point A to point B against his will," Abeln said. "I think, you know, when you feel the guy across from you spit it out, that's a tough feeling to beat in a lot of ways."
Abeln also praised
Trent Thomas
for his consistency, toughness and understanding of his assignments, calling him a player who does his job at a high level in the run game and in pass protection.
On the defensive side, Poindexter credited the secondary's growth to its ability to communicate, understand opposing offenses and make plays when opportunities arise. He pointed to the relationships built among the players, including offseason additions, as a key factor in the group's cohesion.
"You never know how they're going to gel together," Poindexter said. "Kudos to them. You're around these kids, and they act like they've been brothers forever and real brothers, like they mess around with each other, they have fun with each other, and you can really see it translate to the game."
That chemistry has translated into production on the field. Poindexter highlighted
TJ Metcalf's
timely playmaking, including his ability to capitalize on an opportunity when the ball came his way, and praised
Qua Moss
for his toughness and knack for making plays as a blitzer.
The secondary's depth and experience have also remained important beyond the players seeing the field each Saturday. Poindexter noted the continued contributions of
Edrees Farooq
and
Dylan Lewis
through their preparation, knowledge and presence in the safety room despite being out due to injury.
Full comments from Abeln and Poindexter can be viewed below.
Tennessee Football Press Conference | Nov. 17, 2026
Tight Ends Coach
Alec Abeln
On
Luca Wolf's
biggest areas of growth since the start of the season…
"I've talked about the art of contact a lot, and there's a lot of timing and sequencing that goes into that, and I think he's taken steps there. I think, like any young player, the more you play, the more confident, comfortable you get out there. And I think he's taken strides in that as well. Done a really good job."
On preparing for LSU's defensive front…
"The guys are dynamic and explosive and really good pass rushers, make a ton of plays. They're good against the run as well. It'll be a real challenge, but I think everybody in the room understands how important that is for us to be successful, is doing our part, regardless of trying to take care of those guys."
On if Mike Mathews' absence could lead to more opportunities for the tight ends…
"Certainly, it takes all of us stepping up, and it is a next man up mentality. I feel like this year we've had to say that a lot, and it's just the way it is but understanding there's opportunity for guys to step up and make plays (in) my room, (and) other rooms. Excited for those guys that are prepared to step up and fill in that void."
On tight end's enthusiasm for contributing to the run game…
"It's something that doesn't sell very well in recruiting, and it's not something that receiving-type guys necessarily think about when they're coming out of high school. But, I do think there's no greater feeling than taking a man from point A to point B against his will. And I think, you know, when you feel the guy across from you spit it out, that's a tough feeling to beat in a lot of ways. I think, certainly understand that having the ability to do that makes us a different team and they've certainly taken pride in it."
On how the team has fought through adversity…
"Every year is its own set of challenges, and we've certainly had some adversity this year. I think that it speaks to the culture of the building that it doesn't matter, nobody feels sorry for you. There is no asterisk at the end of the year on the record, it's a production and results-based industry. Nobody cares. I think they've embraced that, and I think we have guys that have been preparing and ready for their moment. That's really I think more of what it says, but every year has challenges, this year is certainly no different."
On
Trent Thomas's
involvement in the offense…
"I mean, talk about just doing your job and certainly makes a difference for us as a plus in the run game. He does a great job in protection, he's where he's supposed to be. There's value in guys that do their job, are consistent and you can trust them. Obviously, other guys in our room we trust as well, but I think just the meat and potatoes of a lot of the assignments, he just does his job at a really high level, and he's tough as hell. He's really smart, he's a great teammate, really everything you could ask for from someone you want to coach."
Co-Defensive Coordinator/Secondary Coach
Anthony Poindexter
On how LSU might challenge the secondary differently than other opponents this year…
"They have three very skillful wide receivers, really good tight ends, and the quarterback is fantastic. I think the quarterback can make all the throws, he can beat you with his legs, and he can fit balls in where most quarterbacks can't."
On what has prepared the guys to create opportunities in space and ball skills…
"You do ball drills, and you practice the plays, and you try to tell them to make the plays during practice so they can see themselves making them in the game. To the kids' credit, they make the plays. When the plays come to them, we have been fortunate this year that they have been making the plays. They are doing a nice job of taking the game plans, understanding what the routes are that the other team is trying to present to us. So, it's a lot on the kids. They're just doing a nice job."
On how
TJ Metcalf
has grown over the course of this season...
"They all have just been getting better each week within the scheme, and with what we've asked them to do. It was great to see him make that play. Sometimes, as a DB, the balls come to you, sometimes they don't. Dejaun (Lane) had a lot of opportunities to make plays deep down the field. TJ not quite as many, but it was great to see that he made a play on the ball when we needed it the most."
On
Qua Moss
and being a good blitzer…
"First off, he's just a really good football player, and he's tough, he's physical. He's not the biggest person, but he just has a mentality. This dude wants to make plays, and Coach (Jim) Knowles is doing a great job of putting him in a position to make plays and he is making the most of his opportunities."
On the growth in the secondary with offseason acquisitions…
"We felt good about the guys we went and got. We felt good about the collection of kids that we put together. You never know how they're going to gel together. Kudos to them. You're around these kids, and they act like they've been brothers forever and real brothers, like they mess around with each other, they have fun with each other, and you can really see it translate to the game. So, they know how to communicate with each other, and I think that starts with just their relationship off the field."
On
Edrees Farooq's
involvement…
"Obviously, he's in all the meetings, and then on game day, he's there at the game. So, he's still an important part of our secondary, important part of our safety room. Played a lot of football, he just has a lot of knowledge, he can help them. He's watching the tape just like they're watching the tape, he can give them a little tidbit, so he's just been an important part of our secondary, him and D-Lew (
Dylan Lewis
), they've both been important parts of how we're playing right now. Just their presence and what they're seeing as well."
