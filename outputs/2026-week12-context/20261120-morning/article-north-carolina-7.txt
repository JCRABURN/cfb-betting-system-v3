FB Freshmen Embrace Earned Opportunities
November 18, 2026
|
Football
Share:
Travis Burgess and Jaden Jefferson are the latest examples of the youngest Tar Heels contributing in meaningful ways.
By:
Brennan Doherty
As much as the Tar Heels hated to see veteran quarterback and team leader
Billy Edwards Jr.
leave this past Saturday's game with a third-quarter injury, they knew what the freshman QB replacing him had in his repertoire.
They face
Travis Burgess
on the practice field and see the arm talent and running ability that make him a dual-threat signal caller.
"I've played a lot against Travis in practice, and he's a fun guy to play against," said linebacker
Derek McDonald
. "He's pretty electric. Super fast kid back there. He has a ton of talent, and I think it's just exciting for everyone to see a little bit of that."
Burgess impressed in his collegiate debut, completing 6-of-11 passes for 59 yards. He was excruciatingly close to a touchdown on his first pass, led drives of 60 and 75 yards (the latter ending in a TD) and showcased his athleticism by running for 35 yards on eight carries, including four first-down rushes.
The game action was the first in over a year for Burgess, who missed the majority of his senior high school season because of injury. Belichick explained how Burgess progressed from being limited during spring practice to being fully cleared during fall camp, which is "a lot different" for a QB.
"He's done a good job" with reacclimating to the physical nature of the game and the constant under-pressure state QBs are expected to excel in, Belichick said. "I thought he stepped in there in a situation Saturday night and competed."
The strong debut from Burgess is the latest example of young players stepping up for the Tar Heels and a testament to how members of the Tar Heels' 40-player freshman class are prepared when their number is called. The likes of wideout
Nyqir Helton
and guard
Cristian Alvarez
have done the same on offense. Defensive back
Kenton Dopson III
and linebacker
Calvin Thomas
both have had key contributions defensively.
One freshman with an ability to contribute in all phases of the game is
Jaden Jefferson
, a special teams ace who was recruited primarily as a defensive back but is now factoring into the Tar Heels' offensive game plan.
Following Jefferson's 98-yard TD return on a kickoff against Notre Dame,
Jordan Shipp
said, "I know we're going to find more ways to get him the ball."
The Tar Heels are doing just that now. In addition to his kick return and kick/punt coverage duties, Jefferson has worked his way into the wide receiver rotation and has recorded 15-yard receptions in consecutive games. Against Louisville, Jefferson showed his track star speed — he broke the California state record with a 10.01 time in the 100-meter — on a pitch pass that moved the Tar Heels into the red zone.
"We saw his value in all three phases," Belichick said. "He's a unique player with elite speed, and if the situation presents itself and if we can use him productively, we'll to try to use him."
Carolina Quotable
"If you look from a year ago to where we are now, the growth is just so large. Even if the record isn't showing it, we can see the growth, whether that's in the weight room, whether that's on the field. We're playing much better football. We're in tight games, and we just got to turn the corner on these tight games. Every game this year has been tight at the end. We just got to put it all together to close out these games better." —
Benjamin Hall
Three and Out
A trio of additional notes from Monday's news conference
1. Tar Heels excel on the road
The Tar Heels hit the road this weekend and head up to Charlottesville. Playing away from home hasn't fazed UNC, which is 4-1 in neutral site and away games. In wins against TCU, Pittsburgh and Duke, the Tar Heels played great under pressure, with the Tar Heel defense making lead-preserving defensive stops on the opponent's final drives. UNC posted a season-high 38 points at Connecticut in its last road contest.
Belichick said his program prioritizes keeping the same routine for players on game day, which helps away games feel the same as ones at home.
"Whether it's on the road in California like it was last year at Cal or in Ireland or up the road at Charlottesville, we try to keep the routine the same for the players in terms of their pregame meal to pregame meetings to warmup to play — and then you just work," Belichick said.
There are plenty of fine details coaches and players assess once inside a new stadium, from the thickness or wetness of the grass and where the play clocks are to how the sun is shining.
It goes without saying that the executing the game plan is the biggest factor in a team's success no matter where the game's held, "but those other things do factor into it a little bit," Belichick said.
2. Running backs aid air game
Tight end
Jelani Thurman
and wideout
Jordan Shipp
account for the majority of UNC's receptions and receiving yardage, but in recent weeks the Tar Heels' running backs have chipped in and assisted in the passing game.
The trio of
Jaylen McGill
,
Demon June
and
Benjamin Hall
combined for 20 receptions and 203 receiving yards in the past three games. QB coach
Matt Lombardi
said a few weeks ago that the backs' use in the passing game is at times "an extension of the run game."
"Running backs can catch. We have hands," said a smiling Hall, who's caught a pair of TDs this year. "We can look the ball in and go downfield. A lot of the times (running backs) go unnoticed or we're not the first option, but even if we're the last option, we're going to get you yards."
3. A homecoming for Seelmann
As far as out-of-state road trips go, Saturday's is a short one. Scott Stadium is only 149 miles away from Kenan Stadium as the crow flies. The quick trip will be a homecoming for six Tar Heels from the state of Virginia, including linebacker
Peyton Seelmann
.
Seelmann, a junior transfer who played his first two years at Richmond, hails from Mechanicsville, Virginia, about an hour away from Charlottesville. The game against the Cavaliers is an opportunity for Seelmann to continue his stellar first year in Carolina Blue. He's second on the team in tackles (85) and sacks (4.5) and third in tackles for loss (5).
"It means a bunch going back," Seelmann said. "I'm really excited for it. I've got a bunch of friends and family going, probably like 10 people. I'm looking forward to it. It's going to be fun."
