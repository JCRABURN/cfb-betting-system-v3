Fontenette, Offensive Line Earn Big 12 Weekly Honors After Win Over No. 14 Houston
November 16, 2026
|
Football
Donate To Football
Buy Football Tickets
Share:
By:
Curtis Snyder, Associate AD/Athletic Communications
BOULDER — Colorado safety
Randon Fontenette
was named the Big 12 Defensive Player of the Week and the Buffaloes' offensive line was named the Big 12 Offensive Line of the Week, the conference announced Monday, following the Colorado football team's 42-32 win over No. 14 Houston on Friday night at Folsom Field.
It is the third straight week a Buff has been named the league's Defensive Player of the Week, after linebacker
Gideon ESPN Lampron
won the award following the Kansas State and Arizona State games, and Colorado's fourth this season, joining
Lamont Lester Jr.'s
honor after the opener at Georgia Tech. The offensive line's award is the first for the unit this season; the Buffs' defensive line was the Big 12's Defensive Line of the Week in Week 1.
Fontenette, a fourth-year safety from Freeport, Texas, set a career high with 14 tackles, including a career-best 12 solo stops, the most solo tackles by a Buff since Juwan Mitchell had 13 against Colorado State on Sept. 16, 2023.
He also made the play that swung the game. Houston intercepted
Isaac Wilson
on Colorado's first snap of the second half and took over at the CU 36, trailing 21-20. On the Cougars' first play, Fontenette sacked Conner Weigman for a 9-yard loss and forced a fumble that
Santana Hopper
recovered at the CU 45. Three plays later, Wilson hit
Joseph Williams
for a 53-yard touchdown and a 28-20 lead.
"Man, that play was important," Fontenette said. "When I went out there on the field, I really was kind of shedding tears. I wanted the win so bad, and it was just something inside of me, like, burning."
In the fourth quarter, after Houston scored to pull within 35-26, the Cougars motioned their tight end to Fontenette's side on the two-point try. He came downhill to take away the switch release, and the pass fell incomplete.
"I just came down, made contact, and Conner Weigman, he looked the other way, and I mean, it worked out," Fontenette said.
Fontenette has started all 10 games and has 90 tackles, second on the team to Lampron, with five double-digit tackle games this season. The Houston-area native said the setting added to the night.
"I knew everybody from back home was watching," he said. "But I just went out there and had fun with my guys."
Deion "Coach Prime" Sanders put Fontenette in select company afterward.
"That's who he is," Coach Prime said. "That's what he's capable of doing. He's been probably the most consistent player, along with Gideon, on the defensive side of the ball — that has been there every game."
Up front, left tackle
Xavier Payne
, guards
Chauncey Gooden
and
Leon Bell
, center
Sean Kinney
and right tackle
Larry Johnson III
paved the way for 249 rushing yards and five rushing touchdowns. The 249 yards were Colorado's most since it ran for 281 against Northern Colorado in 2021 and its most against a ranked opponent since 258 against No. 21 Washington State in 2016. The five rushing touchdowns were the most since a five-touchdown game against UCLA in 2020.
Colorado did not allow a sack, gained 466 yards at 7.3 yards per play — its most total yards since 471 against Oklahoma State in 2024 — and converted 10 of 14 third downs.
The line did its loudest work in the second quarter. Colorado rushed for 165 yards in the first half, the most in any half of the Coach Prime era, and scored on touchdown runs of 38 yards by
Richard Young
, 67 by
DeKalon Taylor
and 32 by
JaQuail Smith
. It was the first time three different Buffs scored on runs of 30 yards or more in the same game since 1958, and only the second time in school history since 1950 that they did it in the same quarter. The other was also in the second quarter, against Kansas State in 1954, on runs of 95, 50 and 46 yards.
Young finished with 21 carries for 99 yards and two touchdowns, and
Kaneal Sweetwyne
added a 14-yard touchdown run in brief relief of
Isaac Wilson
.
"We changed it up a little bit," Coach Prime said. "We opened up the offense a little bit. We showed them different looks that I'm pretty sure they wasn't ready for, especially at the onset of the game."
Young was quick to share the credit.
"I can't keep having games like this without my offensive linemen, though," he said. "I love them all. We are a family at the end of the day. We are brothers."
Gooden and Kinney have started all 10 games, Bell is a sixth-year veteran, Johnson III made his fourth straight start at right tackle and Payne, a first-year, made his third straight start at left tackle.
Colorado (4-6, 2-5 Big 12) plays at Cincinnati on Saturday, Nov. 21, needing wins in its final two games to become bowl eligible.
