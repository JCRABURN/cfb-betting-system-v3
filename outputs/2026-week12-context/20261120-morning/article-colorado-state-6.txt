Memorable Moments: Rams Triumphant in Bowl Return
11/19/2026 2:00:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
Johnson’s total-yards showcase highlighted New Mexico Bowl
By:
John Hirn
CSU officially received their invitation to the 2008 New Mexico Bowl on Dec. 7
th
, the first time in school history a first-year coach had been invited to a bowl game. The Rams were pitted against the Fresno State Bulldogs who had started their season off well, but lost key WAC games to finish their regular season 7-5. The Bulldogs were considered heavy favorites, and it was also rumored they remained upset they did not receive an invitation to join the Mountain West conference 10 years earlier.
Coach Steve Fairchild prepared his team and utilized the extra practice time to try out new players and bring back injured men as well. After a mid-season injury, Mike Pagnotta was not expected to return for regular season games, but the bowl game gave him a finale for a career to remember. Klint Kubiak, who briefly returned in the Air Force game only to injure his knee, returned for the bowl game, but his brother Klay suffered a setback and required surgery on his shoulder.
Four days before the New Mexico Bowl, Mychal Sisson received the school's first "Freshman All-American" player award from the FWAA. Sisson recorded 99 tackles on the year with 54 as solo stops. Along with Sisson's All-American Award, senior Jeff Horinek made the final list for the coveted Draddy Trophy.
The Rams traveled to Albuquerque on a chartered Frontier Airlines jet as many of their fans drove 511 miles, or 7 hours and 36 minutes from Fort Collins for the game. An excellent campaign by the CSU athletic department allowed the Rams to sell more than 5,100 tickets while Fresno State sold slightly more than 600 tickets. The CSU alumni tailgate tent filled to capacity as more CSU fans attended than Fresno State fans.
Nearly the entire east side of New Mexico's University Stadium had a sea of Green and Gold as Ram fans supported their team as if it was a home game. All over Albuquerque, Colorado license plates were seen with carloads of Ram fans while the Albuquerque airport flowed with many CSU faithful. Ram fans helped a local choir to finish the National Anthem when the public address system failed. At the same time, two cargo planes and a hybrid helicopter/ airplane flew over the stadium to ignite pre-game excitement. CSU returned to their first bowl game since 2005.
Every Ram on the field contributed to the New Mexico Bowl effort, but Gartrell Johnson III played before a national television audience and made the day for the CSU football team. Fresno State scored first with a touchdown on their opening drive. The Rams wasted no time as they too drove the field and used some of the unusual plays Fairchild broke out during the season.
Backup quarterback Grant Stucker bootlegged the ball from the 18-yard-line to score the first CSU touchdown. The real magic began on this drive when Gartrell Johnson set up Stucker's touchdown after he rushed 57 yards to get the team into position.
Fresno State answered with a touchdown of their own to take the lead 14-7. The Rams then ended the first quarter with a Jason Smith field goal and another 31-yard rush by Johnson. After they stopped the Bulldogs on the next series, the Rams and Johnson III drove the field after Johnson received a shuffle pass and ran 57 yards to set up another field goal to make it 14-13 in favor of Fresno State.
The Bulldogs answered with a 75-yard, 10-play drive to take a 21-13 lead, but the Rams came back down the field late in the second quarter. Led by a series of Billy Farris passes, a last minute drive capped an amazing corner of the end zone catch by Kory Sperry. Sperry barely made the catch but remained in-bounds and CSU headed to the locker room behind by one point at the half.
In the second half, Fresno State scored a touchdown with 8:31 left in the third quarter to take a 28-20 lead. After a long back-and-forth series, the score at the end of the third quarter remained in the Bulldogs' favor. The momentum swing could be felt throughout the stadium with the large number of CSU fans in attendance and the players excited for their fans.
Less than two minutes into the fourth quarter, that excitement turned to elation when Tommie Hill intercepted a crucial third-and-11 pass at the Fresno State 32-yard-line. Four plays later, Johnson punched it in from the 1-yard-line to score six points. A missed two-point conversion made the score 28-26 in favor of the Bulldogs. After the Bulldogs were forced to punt on the next series, Rashaun Greer lit the Ram fireworks. Farris connected to Greer for a 69-yard touchdown pass to leapfrog the Rams on top for the first time all day with just seven minutes left in the game.
The Bulldogs tried desperately to drive the field, but the Ram defense held strong and Fresno State had to punt. The Rams took over at their own 15-yard-line with 2:43 left on the game clock. After a gain of 8 yards on a run up the middle, Johnson ignited out of the gate and ran 77 yards for his second touchdown of the day. Although "The Predator" had been caught from behind on his other long runs, Johnson refused to be caught as he broke the school record for the most rushing yards in a bowl game.
Fresno State mounted a quick comeback to pull within five, but Kyle Bell recovered the onside kick, and the Rams won their first bowl game since 2001. In his first season as head coach, Fairchild amassed a 7-6 record and the New Mexico Bowl Championship. Thousands of CSU fans poured onto the field to congratulate their team on an amazing season. It was almost insurmountable to think that most of these same players ended the previous season 3-9.
Johnson broke the NCAA record for all-purpose yards in a bowl game at 375 and just missed the NCAA record for rushing yards in a bowl game. His 285 rushing yards ranked him second most in a single game behind Tony Alford's 310 yards in 1989. The upset gave the Rams great momentum as they began their preparation for the 2009 season and Johnson celebrated a great career when the San Diego Chargers drafted him in the fourth round of the 2009 NFL draft.
