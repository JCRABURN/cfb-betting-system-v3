Pokes Insider: Wyoming cramming for weighty Mountain West final exam
11/16/2026 4:17:00 PM
|
Football, Pokes Insider
Share:
Cowboys host New Mexico Friday in game with championship implications
By:
Ryan Thorburn
LARAMIE – The Cowboys pulled an all-nighter.
And then the cramming for their Mountain West final exam week began.
Wyoming, which won its fifth consecutive game with a 27-15 victory over UTEP late Saturday night at the Sun Bowl, landed back in Laramie at 4:30 a.m. Sunday.
The team had meetings and a walkthrough Sunday evening before returning to the field for its weekly "Bloody Tuesday" practice, which was moved up to Monday.
The Cowboys (7-3 overall, 6-1 MW) will rely on adrenaline and championship motivation to get through a short week of preparation for one of the most important home games in program history against New Mexico on Friday night at War Memorial Stadium (7 p.m., FS1).
"I'm excited for this week because of what this is for the state of Wyoming, for the University of Wyoming, for our fans," UW head coach
Jay Sawvel
said. "It's a late-November big game. From what people tell me, it's the biggest game that's been around here for 10 years."
A decade ago, the Josh Allen-led Pokes hosted San Diego State in the 2016 Mountain West championship game.
A victory over the Lobos (8-2, 5-1) would allow UW to host the 2026 MW championship game again at War Memorial Stadium.
It won't be easy.
"We will have to play great Friday night to win," Sawvel said.
New Mexico ranks second in the FBS in scoring defense (11.9 points per game) and total defense (248.9 yards per game). Senior defensive end Darren Ago leads the MW with 16.0 tackles for loss, and senior defensive end Brett Karhu ranks third with 10.0 tackles for loss.
The Lobos rank 14th nationally in rushing offense (212.1 yards per game) and lead the nation in turnover margin (+19).
New Mexico was ranked No. 23 in the Associated Press poll and in the mix for a possible berth in the College Football Playoff before suffering a 21-17 loss to UNLV last Saturday in Albuquerque.
"They're a really, really good football team that left one on the table on Saturday," Sawvel said. "We know that we're going to get a great shot from them, and we have to be ready for it."
Starting quarterback
Tyler Hughes
is "doubtful" for the game, according to Sawvel.
Landon Sims
has an impressive first start under his belt after completing 19 of 25 passes for 289 yards and a touchdown.
Tight end
Kyle Frendt
caught three of those passes for 113 yards, while running back Samuel "Tote" Harris had 82 yards receiving, 66 yards rushing and two touchdowns.
Markell Holman
had 41 yards rushing and posted the best passer rating on the Pokes after flipping a 3-yard touchdown pass to tight end
Landon Pace
.
UW's defense, which gave up an early 80-yard touchdown run but held UTEP to 225 yards on its other 54 plays, will have to rise to the challenge to help the program pass this test.
"We've been on a good streak here, and because of that, we put ourselves in a situation where we have an important game on Friday night against a tremendous opponent," Sawvel said. "Important games are supposed to be against uphill-climb opponents, and this is certainly that. We have a tremendous amount of respect for New Mexico."
