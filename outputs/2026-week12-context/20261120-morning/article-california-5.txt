Smith Named 2026 Recipient Of Glenn Seaborg Award
11/18/2026 11:55 PM
|
Football
Share:
Former Cal Receiver Went On To Become Nationally Recognized Surgeon And Healthcare Leader
By:
Cal Athletics
BERKELEY –
Dr. Michael A. Smith, a former California wide receiver who went on to become a nationally recognized cardiothoracic surgeon, academic physician, and healthcare leader, has been named the 2026 recipient of the Glenn Seaborg Award.
Named in honor of the Nobel Laureate and former UC Berkeley chancellor, the Seaborg Award is presented annually to a former Cal football player for his career accomplishments and who represents the honored Cal principles and traditions of excellence in academics, athletics, leadership and attitude.
Smith played for the Golden Bears from 1987-89 and recorded 55 catches and six touchdowns. He was a Pac-10 All-Academic selection and won the Oscar Geballe Award for his superior combination of athletic and academic performance. He also won the Andrew Latham Smith Award for academic excellence and was named the most outstanding graduate by the Black Board of UC Berkeley.
After his time in Berkeley, Smith earned his medical degree from the University of California, San Francisco, and completed his general surgery and thoracic surgery training at Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis. During his surgical residency, he also pursued basic science research supported by competitive research training awards, including a National Institutes of Health (NIH) National Research Service Award. He is board-certified in both general surgery and thoracic surgery.
Smith currently serves as the John Norton Chair in Thoracic Surgery, Chief of Thoracic Surgery and Surgical Director of Lung Transplantation at the Norton Thoracic Institute, Dignity Health St. Joseph's Hospital and Medical Center in Phoenix, Arizona.
"Earning this award is so humbling for me," Smith said. "To have a personal relationship with Mr. Seaborg when I was at Cal and to serve in the sciences just like him makes this award mean so much more to me."
Smith also holds the academic rank of Professor of Surgery at Creighton University School of Medicine's Phoenix Regional Campus.
Throughout his career, Smith has remained dedicated to advancing the science and practice of thoracic surgery through clinical excellence, surgical innovation, academic research, and the education and mentorship of future surgeons. His career reflects a sustained commitment to improving patient outcomes, developing the next generation of surgical leaders, and advancing the standards of care in cardiothoracic surgery.
