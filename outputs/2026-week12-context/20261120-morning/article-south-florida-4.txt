Bulls Head South to Face Florida Atlantic on Saturday
November 19, 2026
|
Football
Share:
Joey Johnston
/
Athletics Senior Writer
One week after falling short in a memorable shootout, the South Florida Bulls (6-4, 2-4 American Conference) could be faced with a similar challenge on Saturday afternoon when they travel to face the offensively potent Florida Atlantic Owls (6-4, 3-3).
The Bulls, who have dropped two straight games and were blitzed for 581 yards in a 52-41 home defeat against Memphis, must cope with Owls quarterback Caden Veltkamp (69.2-percent completions for 3,086 yards, nation-leading 27.6 completions per game, 29 touchdowns and six interceptions) and a fleet of talented receivers.
"This team (FAU) is very explosive,'' Bulls linebacker
Zavier Hamilton
said. "They can be super explosive. Explosive plays normally happen from two things — either missed assignments or missed tackles. So it's going to come down to us honing in on our details and making sure that we're good across the board.''
Head coach Brian Hartline said it's obvious that South Florida's defense must greatly improve, especially against an FAU passing attack that ranks No. 6 nationally (320.8 yards per game). It features prolific receivers Easton Messer (86 catches for 1,033 yards and 13 touchdowns) and Kelby Valsin (76 catches for 923 yards and seven scores).
"We've got to make sure we have more contested catches,'' Hartline said. "We've got to do a better job affecting the quarterback. We need more of the (pass) coverage and (pass) rush working together. If he (Veltkamp) is standing back there all day and leaving the DBs out to dry, that's not good, either.
"If you're going to speed up his process and make the quarterback uncomfortable, then we've got to do something, whether it's D-line or linebacker pressures or sticky coverage. To allow guys to go with routes on air with no impact on the quarterback or no impact on the receivers, that's just not good football. We're going to find ways to enhance those areas. The total yards (by Memphis) were just shy of 600 yards, so there are plenty of things to fix.''
There have been plenty of encouraging moments by South Florida's defense, but also a lack of production at the end of games, allowing late scores against Temple, UTSA and East Carolina to decide the outcomes. The defensive performance against Memphis, which took a 21-0 first-quarter lead, was more of an all-encompassing problem.
"You identify the things that have going well, you find the consistencies that have happened, and then you have an honest conversation about (the things that haven't gone well),'' Hartline said. "If you're shy about having the conversation, it's hard to get things fixed.
"The understanding about the end of the games (with close outcomes) … it's the job of the offense to finish with the ball and take a knee and it's the job of the defense to get the ball back so the offense can take a knee. You can always point fingers at where (the problems) have happened. But you've also got to do a great job of pointing (out) the things that have gone right. We've got to attack it, whether it be end of half or end of game. These things have shown up too often, both offensively and defensively.''
Offensively, the Bulls are probably as confident as they have been all season. The Bulls rolled up 552 yards (502 in the final three quarters) against Memphis, Quarterback
Luke Kromenhoek
accounted for 417 total yards (146 rushing, 271 passing on 22-for-37 completions), while running back
Jason Collins Jr.
contributed 108 rushing yards and a 75-yard touchdown.
Kromenhoek's fiery presence has made an impact on the team, Hartline said.
"The amount (that) he cares is pretty much second to none,'' Hartline said. "What he's willing to do to accomplish to goal, I think, is second to none. There are a lot of strides. So the question is: Can we keep enhancing? Can we keep growing? … I'm very pleased with the way Luke is willing to take the coaching, willing to prepare, willing to enhance and his desire to be everything you want him to be. He's a tough dude and those are the kind of guys you (want) to go battle with.''
Kromenhoek got his opportunity on Oct. 31 when Michael Van Buren, the starter in the season's first eight games, was injured against UAB.
"Obviously, Luke had been chomping at the bit,'' Bulls tight end
Luke Clyburn
said. "We have a very talented quarterback room. Luke has this unwavering confidence about himself that you know he's going to go make a play. And he's going to allow us to make a play, too. We just have to let him do that.''
Joey Johnston
Athletics Senior Writer
