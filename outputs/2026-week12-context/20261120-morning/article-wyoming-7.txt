Cowboys to play in most important matchup in 96 year history against Lobos
By:
Mason Riding
Wyoming and New Mexico became conference foes in 1951, but the series between the schools began in 1930. Ever since the Lobos joined the Skyline Conference in 1951, the two schools have been in the same league.
The Cowboys lead the overall series 42-35, but the Lobos have had the advantage in Mountain West (MW) play, leading UW in MW games 14-12.
While many games worth of history have been played between these two schools, no contest may be more important for UW than this Friday's matchup. With a win, the Cowboys will move to 7-1 in MW play and secure first place in the league standings, which will allow them to host the MW Championship Game, slated to be played Dec. 4.
The last time these two teams found themselves atop the league standings was in 2016, the last time the Cowboys played for a MW Championship Title. Both teams, alongside Boise State, finished in a three-way tie for first in the Mountain Division. Although the Lobos defeated the Cowboys in both teams' regular season finale, the Cowboys held the tiebreaker and advanced to the championship game.
Nov. 26, 2016
While a win over UW would allow UNM to slide into a three-way tie for first place in the Mountain Division, it meant little in the grand scheme of things.
UW was already confirmed to be in the MW Championship Game earlier in the week after Boise State lost to Air Force the night prior. Because the loss moved Boise State to 3-2 against divisional opponents and UNM was also 3-2 against the Mountain Division, UW was confirmed as the representative from the Mountain Division in the MW Championship because of the Cowboys could only at worst be 4-1 against divisional opponents.
Those circumstances didn't cause an upstart UNM team from losing any motivation, however, as the Cowboys found themselves on the wrong side of a record-breaking performance inside University Stadium.
UNM roared out to an explosive start offensively, finding the end zone on five consecutive drives. The Cowboys were unable to force a punt until a little more than two minutes left in the second quarter, and by that point all the momentum had fallen out of their favor. By the time the first half came to a close, UW was behind by a four-possession deficit as the Lobos led 35-7.
UNM tacked on another touchdown in the beginning of the third quarter to take its largest lead of the evening at 42-7. The Cowboys would eventually find a spark offensively, cutting into the lead with two straight scores to make it 42-21. It was too little too late, though, the Lobos would hang on to take a high-scoring affair, 56-35.
UW allowed a staggering 568 rushing yards against the Lobos, the most an opponent has had against the Cowboys since Wisconsin ran for 548 yards against the Pokes in 1973. In total, the Lobos amassed 690 yards of total offense while only attempting five passes, with the 690 yards of total offense also being a record against the Pokes.
Oct. 28, 2017
The UW defense definitely took its showing against the Lobos in 2016 personally.
After a spectacular offensive display that resulted in a 56-point performance by the Lobos the year prior, a stalwart UW defense completely shut down the Lobo offense and held UNM to just a single field goal in a 42-3 rout inside War Memorial Stadium.
"We showed up with great focus and there was a lot to prove. I know last year was last year, but that didn't set well with us," then UW head coach Craig Bohl said postgame. "Our guys had a great week of practice.  We were focused, came out fast and played an explosive second quarter."
Program records were again shattered for UW in this one, but this time in favor of the Cowboys. UW's defense forced a then program record of seven turnovers, a record that only stood for a little more than a month after UW forced eight turnovers against Central Michigan in the Famous Idaho Potato Bowl later that season.
The Cowboys held the Lobos to just 184 yards of total offense, as the Pokes scored 42 straight points before the Lobos finally answered with a field goal. All 42 of UW's points were scored in the first half, which is the last time the Pokes have ever scored 40 or more points in a half.
Three separate Cowboys recorded an interception, as junior safety Andrew Wingard snagged two interceptions and junior defensive end Carl Granderson and junior safety Marcus Epps both came away with an interception each. All three are still enjoying successful NFL careers to this day.
