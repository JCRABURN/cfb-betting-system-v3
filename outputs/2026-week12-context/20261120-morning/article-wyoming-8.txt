Pokes Insider: O-line preparing to hold its ground vs. New Mexico
11/19/2026 9:32:00 AM
|
Football, Pokes Insider
Share:
Joe Tropodi pleased with progress as Wyoming gears up for dominant Lobo defense
By:
Ryan Thorburn
LARAMIE – How did the Cowboys turn things around so dramatically on offense?
After averaging 14.2 points and 150.6 rushing yards during a 2-3 start, Wyoming has averaged 30.6 points and 247.0 rushing yards during the team's current five-game winning streak.
Christian Taylor
's beautiful play-calling mind. Dual-threat quarterback
Tyler Hughes'
explosiveness. A healthy Samuel "Tote" Harris pairing with bruiser
Markell Holman
in the backfield. The dominant tight end room.
Landon Sims
filling in efficiently behind center.
All of the above have been factors during this remarkable turnaround, but it always starts up front, and the offensive line has improved week by week.
The unit suffered an early setback when starting center
Jason Maciejczak
sustained a season-ending injury during the second game against Northern Colorado. Chandler Donoway, who missed part of fall camp and was working his way into the rotation at guard, has been coming on strong at center, where he has started the last eight games.
Senior left tackle
Rex Johnsen
and sophomore right tackle
Braylon Jenkins
have made some mistakes but also provided stability at those critical positions. Sophomore right guard
Giovanni Panozzo
and redshirt freshman left guard
Kalvin Janssen
have helped improve the offensive line's consistency, emerging as solid starters. Redshirt freshman
Johnathan Bush
, sophomore
Caleb Hall
and junior
Josiah Petaia
have added quality depth.
Now the
Cowboys face their most difficult challenge of the season
against New Mexico on Friday at War Memorial Stadium (7 p.m., FS1).
The Lobos (8-2, 5-1 Mountain West) rank No. 2 in the FBS in both points allowed (11.9 per game) and yards allowed (248.9 per game). Senior defensive end Darren Ago leads the conference with 16.0 tackles for loss, while senior defensive end Brett Karhu ranks third with 10.0 tackles for loss.
New Mexico ranks 14th nationally in rushing offense (212.1 yards per game) and leads the nation in turnover margin (+19).
The Cowboys (7-3, 6-1) can clinch the regular-season title and will host the MW championship game Dec. 4 with a victory, but the offensive line will have to raise its level of play for the occasion.
I caught up with UW offensive line coach
Joe Tripodi
this week for a Q&A ahead of the high-stakes matchup:
Thorburn:
How rewarding has this season been to see this group, which has some talented guys who have developed in the program and some new guys from the portal, get better and better as the season has progressed?
Tripodi:
That process of getting better has been enjoyable because it's a fun group to coach and they're working hard. We still have a long way to go, so it's fun to see the improvement, but we still have some other stuff to get done. We still have to keep working.
Thorburn:
How has Rex grown as a leader?
Tripodi:
Rex has matured. Obviously, he had a couple of false starts (at UTEP). Two years ago, if he had that, he probably would have imploded. He's grown up a lot; he cares a lot. He's a senior, and I think he's starting to realize that there's only so many guaranteed opportunities left. He's improved, and it's been fun to see him grow for sure.
Thorburn:
Where would you guys be without Donoway's versatility?
Tripodi:
Oh man, I can't say enough about Chandler being able to play multiple spots and his leadership as well. I think he's complemented Rex really well because they're different. Chandler has played a lot of football, and he's been a really positive light for our room.
Thorburn:
How are the guys balancing the excitement of Friday night's opportunity with preparing to face one of the nation's elite defenses?
Tripodi:
Well, that's what you have to do. I mean, they're well-coached, they're physical, they're a good defense. I think the guys have to accept the challenge. Everybody's excited, but it's that same mentality, right? You turn the page each week, and they have to go back to work.
Thorburn:
Jay (Sawvel) said it's difficult enough to get 10 yards against this defense, so it will be important not to make it easier on the Lobos with false starts or other penalties. How do you approach getting the O-line focused on eliminating mistakes?
Tripodi:
You approach it like any other week. What we've really talked about in the O-line room specifically is that you have to practice hard. You have to practice the right way. They're learning how to practice hard. They have practiced hard, and that gives you a chance to go out there on Friday night and have some fun with it. The workload is there. They're a very sound defense. They're very good, they're physical, they've got a bunch of seniors, and they're coached the right way. They play football the right way. So, we have to bring it.
