What You Need to Know
Michigan will honor its seniors prior to Saturday's game against No. 4 AP/No. 4 CFP UCLA.
This will be the 12th meeting between the Wolverines and Bruins.
Michigan leads the all-time series, 8-3, with the two programs meeting for the first time since 2000.
Saturday's game will be the first meeting between the programs as Big Ten members.
Michigan ranks ninth nationally in total defense (276.5 yards per game) and 13th in pass defense (173.4 yards per game), ranking among the top 25 in six defensive categories.
U-M has allowed the fourth-fewest runs of 10-plus yards nationally this season (21 in 10 games), trailing only Iowa (16), Notre Dame (19) and Miami (19).

Michigan vs. UCLA
The Wolverines and Bruins will compete for the first time as Big Ten Conference opponents on Saturday at Michigan Stadium.
Michigan and UCLA will meet for the 12th time overall and for the first time since 2000.
In the last meeting, the 14th-ranked Bruins defeated the third-ranked Wolverines, 23-20, in temperatures that exceeded 100 degrees at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, Calif. (Sept. 16, 2000).
U-M leads the all-time series, 8-3, with each team winning a bowl matchup. Michigan claimed a Bluebonnet Bowl victory in 1981, while UCLA earned a Rose Bowl win in 1982.
The Wolverines won the first five meetings between the programs and also had a three-game winning streak during the series.
Michigan holds a 5-1 record against UCLA at Michigan Stadium, including victories in the last two meetings (1990 and 1996).
The Maize and Blue have a 2-1 road record against the Bruins, posting wins in 1972 and 1989 before falling in 2000.
Team
Michigan returns to Ann Arbor for this weekend's matchup with UCLA. U-M is 4-4 in its last eight league games at Michigan Stadium and is 4-3 overall this season at home.
All-time, Michigan leads the series against UCLA, 8-3. U-M has won five of six matchups at Michigan Stadium.
UCLA and U-M last met in 2000 (Sept. 16), when the No. 14-ranked Bruins beat the No. 3-ranked Wolverines, 23-20. This will be the first-ever meeting between the programs as Big Ten Conference opponents.

Offense
U-M has at least one scoring drive of 10-plus plays in eight of 10 games this year. The offense also has at least one scoring drive of six plays or fewer in nine games. Last weekend, U-M scored on a two-play drive capped by
Savion Hiter
's 70-yard score.
A balanced stable of contributors has scored for the maize and blue so far, with eight different touchdown scorers (five players with rushing touchdowns, three receiving).
The Wolverines continue to work toward improvement in their third-down conversion rate, now at 40.8 percent (53-of-130). The offense has been better on fourth down, with a 57.1 percent conversion rate (8-of-14), a top-50 mark nationally.
Michigan's offense is seeking consistency and balance in the explosive play (20-plus yards) department. The offense has generated 24 explosives, including 18 via the passing game.
JJ Buchanan
(nine) and
Andrew Marsh
(four) continue to lead the team in explosive offensive plays, all receptions. Marsh led U-M in 2025 with 15 explosive plays on offense (14 receiving, one rushing), and another eight via special teams returns.
Jordan Marshall
(five touchdowns) has found the end zone most often on the ground, and Buchanan (five touchdowns) is the leading scorer through the air.
Buchanan also leads the team in receiving yards (646, 57th nationally). Buchanan has 1,069 career receiving yards after his 427-yard debut season at Utah.
Marsh also reached 1,000 career receiving yards during the 2026 season; he has 1,110 for his career across 22 career games (459 this year).
The duo has combined for four 100-yard games this season in Buchanan (126 vs. WMU, 130 vs. Iowa) and Marsh (105 vs. UTEP, 106 vs. Iowa).
Of the team's 11 pass-catchers so far this year, four are freshmen (
Mason Bonner
,
Savion Hiter
,
Travis Johnson, Salesi Moa
). Bonner (27 yards), Moa (44) and Johnson (21) have registered explosive plays in recent weeks.
Marshall is also closing in on a career milestone, with 1,404 career rushing yards. With 96 more, he'll reach 1,500 for his career.
Running back
Savion Hiter
’s 70-yard touchdown run at Oregon marked the longest touchdown run by a freshman back since Tyrone Wheatley’s 74-yard touchdown against Northwestern in 1991.
Hiter’s touchdown was U-M’s longest offensive play this year and a career-long rush for the first-year back, who scored the fourth touchdown of his freshman season (all rushing). He finished the day with 13 carries, a career-high 107 rushing yards and the score.
The play helped lead to U-M's best rushing output on the season (215 yards) and the second game this season with 200-plus rushing yards (Penn State, 211).
The offensive line continues to grow; six players have now made double-digit starts at Michigan: left guard/tackle
Evan Link
(27 starts, 26 at tackle), center
Jake Guarnera
(20 consecutive; 10 at guard), tackle
Blake Frazier
(16, including six at right tackle), right tackle
Andrew Sprague

The line permits an average of 4.7 negative plays per game, ranking 49th nationally in TFLs allowed, and has employed six different starting lineup combinations across 10 games.
First-year quarterback
Tommy Carr
made his first career start against Indiana. He is the sixth first-year signal caller to start for U-M, joining teammate
Bryce Underwood
(2025), Tate Forcier (2009), Chad Henne (2004), Rich Hewlett (1979) and Rick Leach (1975). Carr and Leach are the only two among those six who did not start the season opener.
Carr went 22-of-27 passing at Rutgers in his first career win as a starter, marking the highest single-game completion rate (81.5 percent) for a freshman starting quarterback in U-M history (minimum 20 attempts).
In three starts, Carr is 60-of-97 (61.8 percent completion rate) for 616 yards through the air. He has two passing touchdowns and three rushing touchdowns on the season.
Carr and Underwood split time at Oregon. Carr started the game before Underwood entered after two drives and played the remainder of the first half. Carr returned to start the third quarter before Underwood re-entered in the fourth quarter.
Defense
The defense is ranked ninth nationally in total defense, allowing 276.5 yards of total offense per game to opponents.
The defense excels in all situations. U-M has allowed the 10th-fewest first downs (153, 15.3 per game) in the nation and ranks 38th nationally in third-down conversion rate allowed (34.5 percent).
Through 10 games, the U-M defense has yielded 15 touchdowns and ranks 15th overall in scoring defense (17.7 points per game allowed). Oregon (21, first half) is the only team to score more than 14 points in a half against U-M this year.
That scoring defense has been consistent; six opponents have been held to 18 points or fewer, and only Oregon (31) has exceeded 21 points.
The rush defense has kept opponents below 100 yards on three occasions, allowing 103.1 yards per contest, on average (21st nationally).
U-M has allowed the fourth-fewest runs of 10-plus yards against them this year (21 in 10 games). Only Iowa (16), Notre Dame and Miami (19 each) have allowed fewer.
Linebacker
Troy Bowles
leads the defense in tackles (79), 11 clear of the next-closest defender.
Chris Bracy
(68) leads the secondary and
John Henry Daley
leads the defensive linemen (33). Bowles is sixth in the Big Ten in total stops.
Twelve (12) different players combined for 11 tackles for loss against UTEP, the most by a U-M defense in a single game since 2022 (11 against Colorado State, Sept. 3). Nineteen (19) different players have at least a share of a TFL through 10 games.
John Henry Daley
leads the defense with 9.0 TFL (1.0 per game, 39th nationally). Daley is also the team's leader in sacks, with 6.0 (0.67 per game, 35th nationally). Twelve (12) different players have at least a share of a sack so far this season.
The defense ranks 13th in passing yards per game allowed (173.4). Opposing passers have thrown 12 touchdowns to seven interceptions.
With his first-quarter breakup against the Spartans,
Zeke Berry
reached 27 career pass breakups, tying him with Jeremy LeSueur (2000-03, 27) for eighth-most all-time at Michigan. James Whitley (1997-2000, 28) and Lavert Hill (2016-19, 29) are next up on the top-10 list.
The defense has forced nine turnovers through 10 games, including seven interceptions, two of which have been returned for touchdowns. Three interceptions have been returned 24 yards or more.
Those two defensive touchdowns make U-M one of 16 teams in the nation with multiple scores from the defense.
Jonah Lea'ea
recorded the shortest pick-six in program history when he returned an interception nine yards for the score against UTEP to end the nonconference slate. At Minnesota,
Nathaniel Staehling
recorded the longest pick-six on the road in program history when he returned an interception 79 yards for a touchdown.
Seven different defenders have an interception overall.
Jyaire Hill
recorded the first interception of the season against Oklahoma.
Zeke Berry
,
Chris Bracy
,
Rod Moore
and
Smith Snowden
each have one apiece. U-M has generated 152 return yards on its interceptions this year.
In three games this year (Oklahoma, Minnesota, Penn State), Michigan's defense has forced multiple turnovers. The defense has recovered both of its forced fumbles (both forced by Bracy).
Twenty (20) different players recorded a pass breakup or intercepted a pass in 2025, led by
Zeke Berry
(11, including one interception). Fifteen (15) players have recorded at least one so far this season, led at the top by Berry and
Jyaire Hill
(five each).
Special Teams
Punter
Cam Brown
averaged 43.9 yards on 22 punts during his freshman season at Utah and has averaged 42.3 yards per punt so far this season. U-M ranks top-15 in punt return defense (2.73 yards per return allowed), in part due to Brown's hangtime and consistency.
Brown has punted 46 times this season, with 20 punts inside the 20-yard line. He has forced 21 fair catches and has had three punts downed at the one-yard line. Against Oklahoma, he pinned the Sooners inside their 20 on six of seven attempts; at Oregon, Brown allowed minus-six return yards on five punts.
Kicker
Jacob Baggett
has generated 24 touchbacks on 36 kickoffs.
Freshman
Salesi Moa
had his first game as punt returner for U-M against Penn State. Moa had two fair catches and one return for nine yards. He added another two returns for 25 yards against Indiana and an eight-yard return against Michigan State.
