The Extra Down: Diego Aviles Turned Patience Into Opportunity
11/19/2026 11:15:00 AM
|
Football
Share:
By:
Caitlyn Griffin
STATESBORO
– For fifth-year linebacker Diego Aviles, the road to Georgia Southern football was anything but conventional. A lifelong basketball player, Aviles didn't begin taking football seriously until his junior year of high school.
After earning first-team all-district honors in football and following another successful campaign as a senior, Aviles began to see the potential in a sport he had only recently picked up.
I played basketball my whole life, and I didn't really start playing football until my junior year of high school, Aviles said. Once I accoladed first-team all-district honors in my first year, and then followed that up with another year, I started to see the potential I had. I think after seeing that, it motivated me to keep going.
Although he also played tight end, Aviles found his home on the defensive side of the ball, drawn to the aggressiveness and physicality of playing defense.
That decision eventually took him to Wingate, where he began navigating the challenges of college football and life away from home.
The transition from high school to college brought an entirely new challenge. The speed of the game increased, the playbook became more complex and understanding his responsibilities within the defense required a new level of preparation.
When I first got there, I was handed this huge playbook, and I had people who had been there for years telling me that sometimes even they didn't understand it, Aviles said.
Aviles spent four seasons at Wingate, gradually developing his game and learning from the veteran players around him. His first significant opportunity came during his third season, when he appeared in all 11 games, recording 34 tackles and three tackles for loss.
He followed that performance with an even stronger final season, playing in 10 games and finishing with 40 tackles, 3.5 tackles for loss, a sack, three pass breakups and a forced fumble.
While his production continued to grow, Aviles credits much of his development to the people who helped him along the way.
Being able to learn from the veterans and take in the things they had learned and passed down to me taught me a lot, Aviles said.
Those lessons eventually became something he could pass along to others.
Aviles transferred to Georgia Southern in January 2026.
The opportunity to continue his career in Statesboro was an appealing one, particularly with former teammates Davon Gilmore and Justin Rhodes already familiar with the program. Their experiences helped make Georgia Southern an easy choice, but what Aviles found after arriving exceeded his expectations.
My former teammates, Davon Gilmore and Justin Rhodes, had been here, and they already gave me the background knowledge and the experience that they have here, and it just overall sounded like a great place to be here, Aviles said. And when I got here, it was everything that they said.
From the coaching staff to the support staff throughout the building, Aviles immediately felt a sense of connection.
From the coaches and the way they bond with you and communicate with you to the staff and everybody in the building, it all feels like one family, Aviles said. I felt that was true as soon as I got here.
The move to Statesboro also gave Aviles an opportunity to apply the lessons he learned throughout his time at Wingate.
Having once been the younger player learning from experienced teammates, he now finds himself in a position to provide that same guidance to others.
Aviles has embraced a leadership role within Georgia Southern's linebacker room, helping younger players understand assignments, correct mistakes and learn from his experiences on and off the field.
Being able to teach them things that I know and share some of the mistakes I've made in the past, on and off the field, gives me an opportunity to pass along some knowledge, Aviles said. I think it's about being able to learn from me and the experiences I've had, and hopefully learn from some of my mistakes.
That willingness to help others reflects the same mindset that has carried him throughout his own journey: staying prepared, continuing to learn and being ready whenever his number is called.
For Aviles, that opportunity arrived during Georgia Southern's matchup against Houston following an injury to RJ Myles.
Despite primarily coming off the bench earlier in the season, Aviles approached each week with the starter mindset. He made the most of his practice repetitions and studied as if he would be the one leading the defense on game day.
When I was getting the reps I had, I was always trying to give it my all, Aviles said. So when I transitioned into being a starter, it was more like, 'It's here now. It's your job, and you have to execute it to the best of your ability.'
The opportunity didn't catch him unprepared. It was the moment he had been working toward.
But football has never been Aviles' only motivation.
Behind the preparation, the statistics and the pursuit of playing time is a family that continues to push him forward. His mother, father, siblings and young niece all serve as reminders of why he continues to work, especially when the journey becomes difficult.
Being able to wake up every morning, knowing I have my family and a purpose in life, motivates me, Aviles said. I have a little niece who looks up to me, and being able to wake up another day knowing I can take care of her, my mom, my dad, my brother and my sister, those little things keep me going.
That motivation is rooted in the challenges he faced long before arriving in Statesboro. There were moments along the way when the uncertainty became overwhelming, and returning home seemed like the easiest option.
Coming from Division II football, dealing with injuries and really having to work my way up, there were many times when I would call my mom and tell her I was about to come home, Aviles said. I was 18 years old, had just moved to North Carolina by myself, didn't have a job and was really just trying to survive.
Those experiences helped shape his perspective, making his current opportunity one he refuses to take for granted.
It's been a lot, but I'm just thankful for everything, Aviles said. That's why, if you ever hear me talk, I always say how thankful I am to be where I'm at. It's been a hard struggle to get here, and it's truly a blessing. Every day I wake up, I thank God because this is amazing compared to where I came from.
For the younger athletes hoping to follow a similar path, Aviles' advice extends beyond football. Reflecting on his own experiences, he recognizes that growth comes not only from the decisions made on the field but also from learning how to handle adversity away from it.
Don't let your emotions stink for you. Because I was very, I can say that I had very bad emotions, and I think I was just so quick to speak when I was mad and when I felt some type of way that it led to things that I should have never led to in my life, or actions really my actions, Aviles said. Think twice before you do something or you speak. That's the younger advice I give myself.
From picking up football late in high school to navigating the challenges of college football and earning his opportunity at Georgia Southern, Aviles' journey has been built on perseverance, preparation and the people who have helped him along the way.
Now, with an opportunity to make an impact in the Eagles' linebacker room, he hopes to be that same source of encouragement for the next player coming up behind him.
