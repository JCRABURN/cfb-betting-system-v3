‘Top-Notch Back’ – Khobie Martin Glad to be ‘Back in the Loop’
Pete DiPrimio | IUHoosiers.com
BLOOMINGTON, Ind. --
Couch potato? That’s the last thing you can expect from
Khobie Martin
. He’s a 6-foot, 204-pound fierce-running football force who is back from injury and ready to make defenses pay.
Last Saturday, USC felt that force to the tune of 82 yards and a touchdown on 12 carries. Martin came in with just three carries and seven yards, having basically missed the first eight games with an arm injury.
“It was real good to be back on the field,” he says after a recent practice. “Being out, sitting on the sideline that long and watching my teammates put in that hard work, was a hard feeling.
“I felt like a little bit of a couch potato, but I wasn't. I was still rehabbing, still in the weight room doing what I needed to do to get back on the field.”
What did Martin do to stay fit while sidelined?
“I was able to do some lifting with it, really just rehab. I was actually able to do a lot of stuff. I had to make sure my body was conditioned; my legs were conditioned. I was still on my diet with my meal plan. It wasn’t holding me back from a lot of things. I was able to do a lot of things.”
Martin was a much-needed second-half force against USC. He showed power, speed and agility, especially on a 37-yard run that just missed being a touchdown.
“It feels really good to play like that,” he says. “It feels like I’m finally getting back into the loop of things. That’s how every game for me is supposed to go. I’m going to keep working hard and keep my head down.”
This was what the Hoosiers expected after his breakout sophomore season when, as the backup to
Roman Hemby
and
Kaelon Black
, he rushed for 505 yards and six touchdowns while averaging 6.5 yards a carry.
“When the season started,” head coach
Curt Cignetti
said, “I felt like we had three starters at running back and could divvy up the carries. He went down the second play of the game in the opener and is just starting to come back.
“So, No. 1, he's fresh and healthy. But he's a really good player. He can run with speed. He can run with force. He can make you miss. He's agile. He's got good hands. He's got good balance and body control.
“He’s shown lots of improvement since he's come to us. I think he’s a top-notch back.”
While veteran
Turbo Richard
(745 yards, six touchdowns) is out with injury, Martin and
Lee Beebe Jr.
(786 yards, four touchdowns) still provide a formidable rushing tandem.
“Me and Lee know what’s ahead of us and that we don’t have Turbo with us,” Martin says, “but we have some freshmen (Sean Cuono and Jayreon Campbell), who can carry the load if they need to. We’re not worried about anything.”
Beebe Jr. shares that confidence.
“If we can do anything, we can do the impossible. With the three of us (when Richard returns), I feel like we can just be that (dominant) running back group.”
That’s why Beebe Jr. provides constant reminders to Martin to “keep finishing our plays; keep doing what we’re doing, all the little details. Make sure we do those things and make sure we’re doing what we need to do.”
On Saturday, IU will face a 6-4 Washington team that allows 21.1 points and 132.4 rushing yards a game. The Huskies are 5-0 when holding opponents to 20 or fewer points.
“They’re a hard-playing team,” Martin says. “They have two really good linebackers (Alex McLaughlin and Rylon Dillard-Allen). The D-line is good, as well.”
The Hoosiers will be ready, he adds.
“I think me and Lee will be able to get done a lot. With our O-linemen, we trust in them, believe in them. We know Coach Cig and Coach Shanahan call really good plays to put me and Lee in good positions to make something happen.”
