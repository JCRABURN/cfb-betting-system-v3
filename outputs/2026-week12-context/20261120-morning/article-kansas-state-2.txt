K-STATE CONCLUDES HOME SLATE WITH ARIZONA
Kansas State is looking to send its seniors out on a high note and get back on the winning track as the Wildcats host No. 19 Arizona Saturday night at Bill Snyder Family Stadium. The game will kick at 6 p.m. and be shown on FS1 with Eric Collins (play-by-play) and Spencer Tillman (analyst) on the call, while the K-State Sports Network begins its coverage at 4 p.m. (CT), with
Wyatt Thompson
(play-by-play), Stan Weber (analyst) and Matt Walters (sidelines) calling the action.
K-STATE TO HONOR 13 ON SENIOR NIGHT
Kansas State will honor 13 players prior to Saturday night's game for what is anticipated to be their final contest inside Bill Snyder Family Stadium. Those players include defensive lineman
Travis Bates
, offensive lineman
Michael Capria
, safety
Jet Dineen
, quarterback
Jacob Knuth
, wide receiver
Isaac Koch
, wide receiver
Sterling Lockett
, linebacker
Mekhi Mason
, tight end
Garrett Oakley
, offensive lineman
John Pastore
, cornerback
Kaleb Patterson
, linebacker
Gabe Powers
, linebacker
Rex Van Wyhe
and wide receiver
Brandon White
.
McCLANNAN HAVING ALL-BIG 12 SEASON
Punter
Simon McClannan
has been outstanding in 2026 as he ranks sixth nationally and second in the Big 12 Conference with a 47.6-yard average, a mark that is currently atop the K-State record book. A Ray Guy Award semifinalist, McClannan is also tied for first in the Big 12 with 18 punts that have traveled at least 50 yards – including a Big 12-best five at Colorado – while he is tied for second in the league with 19 punts that have ended up inside the opponent 20-yard line
SEVEN FOR SATURDAY
• K-State is fifth nationally in fewest penalties (41) and 12th in fewest penalty yards (392).
• Kansas State ranks eighth in the nation by converting on 51% (75-of-148) of its third-down attempts.
• The Cats rank 25th nationally and fifth in the Big 12 with 6.3 tackles for loss per game.
•
Garrett Oakley
has 15 career receiving touchdowns, which ranks third among all active tight ends in the country.
• Punter
Simon McClannan
ranks sixth nationally and is currently tops in school history with a 47.6-yard average this season.
• K-State ranks 13th nationally in net punting with a 42.93-yard average this season.
•
JoJo Scott
ranks fifth nationally among all freshmen and sophomores with nine pass breakups this season.
TEAM NOTES
RECENT RECAP
• K-State has dropped four-consecutive games following a road loss to TCU (20-10), home loss to Oklahoma State, a 27-24 overtime loss at Colorado and a 28-17 decision at Arizona State.
• The four-game losing streak is the longest since the Wildcats dropped the last five games of the 2020 season.
• The current skid is coming on the heels of K-State's 18th-straight win over in-state rival Kansas in a 35-24 victory. The Wildcats ran for 359 yards against the Jayhawks, which included 159 yards from
Joe Jackson
and 132 yards from
Avery Johnson
. It was the most rushing yards in a Big 12 game this season and the ninth most offensive yards in a Big 12 game this season (568).
• It was K-State's fifth-most rushing yards and total offensive yards ever in a Big 12 contest.
NEWCOMERS MAKING AN IMPACT
• Of the 220 available starts on offense and defense this season, 47.3% (104-of-220) are made up of transfers.
• The biggest area in which newcomers are making a difference is sacks and tackles for loss. Of the Wildcats' 23 sacks, 12 (52.2%) are from newcomers, while 47% (29.5) of their 63 tackles for loss are from those new to the program.
• Kansas State's 2026 roster features 58 newcomers, equating to 52.2% of its roster.
• The 58 newcomers on the roster rank seventh in program history. The school record for newcomers in a season is 72 in 2002.
LETTERWINNER TURNED COACH
•
Collin Klein
, the former Heisman Trophy finalist and starting quarterback of the 2012 Big 12 Championship team, is in his first season as head coach in 2026.
• He is the first former K-State letterwinner to serve as head football coach since Ellis Rainsberger from 1975 through 1977.
• In fact, Klein is one of only four former letterwinners to take the helm of the K-State program as he and Rainsberger are joined by Lud Fiser (1945) and Ralph Graham (1948-50).
THE BIG 12'S BEST
• Kansas State has totaled 150 Big 12 victories since the conference's formation in 1996, which stands as the most among active Big 12 programs.
• The Cats are 14 wins ahead of Oklahoma State (136) and 18 ahead of Texas Tech (132). The Wildcats, Cowboys and Red Raiders are the only three Big 12 teams with over 100 conference wins.
• K-State is also tied for first among active Big 12 members in winning percentage since non-divisional play began in 2011 (minimum 50 games). The Wildcats sit at 57.0% (81-61) – tied with Oklahoma State (57.0%; 81-61) – while TCU is third (54.9%; 73-60).
VERY FEW PENALTIES
• Since 2022 – the year that current head coach
Collin Klein
took over as offensive coordinator – the Wildcats have averaged only 42.16 penalty yards per game (62 games) to rank eighth among Power 4 teams (min. 50 games).
• K-State enters the week ranked fifth nationally in fewest penalties per game (4.10) and 12th in fewest penalty yards per game (39.20) this season.
THE NATION'S LEADER IN NON-OFFENSIVE TOUCHDOWNS
• K-State leads the country in non-offensive touchdowns over the last 28 seasons as it has 141 since 1999, seven more than the team in second place (Alabama – 134).
• K-State recorded five non-offensive scores in both 2024 and 2025, and they got on the board this year with a
Brandon White
punt return score against Nicholls. Then, against Oklahoma State,
Logan Bartley
returned an interception 18 yards for a touchdown. It was K-State's first pick-six of the season and first since Des Purnell returned an interception 25 yards for a score last year against TCU.
A REGULAR IN THE POSTSEASON
• Kansas State has qualified for postseason play in 14 of the last 15 seasons (excluding COVID) as the Wildcats earned a 6-6 record in 2025 but opted to not participate in a bowl game due to the change in coaching staff.
• The Wildcats are one of only 15 Power 4 teams – and the only Big 12 program – to be eligible in 14 of the last 15 seasons, joining Alabama, Clemson, Georgia, Iowa, Louisville, LSU, Miami, Michigan, Notre Dame, Ohio State, Oklahoma, Oregon, Texas A&M and Washington.
O CAPTAIN! MY CAPTAIN!
• Prior to the opener, Kansas State announced six team captains for the 2026 season in defensive ends
Jordan Allen
and
Travis Bates
, running back
Joe Jackson
, quarterback
Avery Johnson
, tight end
Garrett Oakley
and linebacker
Gabe Powers
.
• Each of them were voted captains for the first time in their careers except for Johnson, who is now a captain for a third time. He is the ninth player in school history to be named a captain on three occasions and the third quarterback, joining current head coach
Collin Klein
(2010-12) and Skylar Thompson (2019-21).
OFFENSIVE NOTES
KLEIN'S OFFENSES
• K-State head coach
Collin Klein
has been one of the top offensive play callers in the nation over the last four-plus seasons.
• Since 2022, Klein's offenses have averaged 32.7 points per game, which ranks 17th among Power 4 programs.
• Additionally, his last five offenses have combined for 426.2 yards per game, which ranks 15th among Power 4 teams.
• His teams the last four-plus years have combined for 297 plays of at least 20 or more yards (203 pass/94 rush), good for an average of 4.7 per game.
HIGH POINT TOTAL vs. BIG 12
• When
Collin Klein
took over the K-State offense in 2022, it began a trend of averaging 31.2 points per game in conference contests, which ranks second in the league. Additionally, the Wildcats rank 10th among Power 4 teams in points per game in conference contests since 2022 (min. 30 games).
• The Cats have hit the 30-point mark in 24 of their 43 Big 12 games since the start of 2022.
• K-State had a four-game streak of scoring at least 30 points last year against UCF (34), Baylor (34), TCU (41) and Kansas (42), its longest streak in conference play since doing so in six-straight games during the 2003 season.
SUCCESS ON THIRD DOWN
• The Wildcats enter the week ranked eighth nationally by converting on 50.7% (75-of-148) of their third-down attempts.
• K-State is one of five Big 12 teams that rank in the top 12 nationally in third-down conversion percentage (Houston – 1st; Utah – 3rd; BYU – 9th; Arizona – 12th).
• K-State has not finished a season 50.0% or better on third downs since 2012 (50.0% – 80-of-160).
FOUR GAMES, FOUR STARTERS
• K-State has had a different starting quarterback in each of the last four games as
Avery Johnson
started against Kansas,
Jacob Knuth
started at Arizona State,
Dillon Duff
started at Colorado and
Blake Barnett
against Oklahoma State.
• It was the first time in school history the Wildcats had four different starting quarterbacks in four-consecutive games. In 1983, Doug Bogue, Donnie Campbell and Stan Weber each made starts in three-consecutive games.
• Entering the month of November, the Wildcats were one of seven FBS teams this season to start three different quarterbacks in consecutive games (Delaware, Nevada, Oregon, Pittsburgh, Syracuse and UMass).
• Although Barnett did not start at Colorado, he played the final three quarters of the game, engineering a fourth-quarter touchdown drive that forced overtime.
AN EXPERIENCED SIGNAL CALLER
• Quarterback
Avery Johnson
was in his third year of starting duties in 2026 until an injury sidelined him and snapped a streak of 32-consecutive starts. It was tied for the most-consecutive starts by a K-State quarterback in school history (Josh Freeman – 2006-08).
• Johnson has led the Wildcats to 20 wins as a starting quarterback, which ranks sixth in school history. Skylar Thompson (2017-21) holds the top mark with 24, followed by a tie for second with 22 wins by Ell Roberson (2001-03), Michael Bishop (1997-98) and
Collin Klein
(2010-12), while Johnathan Beasley (1999-2000) led the Wildcats to 21 wins.
• Johnson is one of only two quarterbacks in the Big 12 in 2026 to have started for multiple years at the same school. The other is Noah Fifita, who has made 44 starts over the last three-plus seasons at Arizona.
MULTIPLE 200/100 GAMES
•
Avery Johnson
has recorded two games this season with at least 200 passing yards and 100 rushing yards as he did so against Tulane (227 pass/152 rush) and Kansas (209 pass/132 rush).
• He is the fourth Wildcat in the Big 12 era to have two such games in one season, joining Michael Bishop (1998),
Collin Klein
(2012) and Jake Waters (2014).
HIGH AVERAGE FOR A QB
•
Avery Johnson
has rushed for 462 yards this season on only 51 carries, good for a 9.1-yard average.
• He leads all qualified quarterbacks nationally in rushing average this season and is 3.0 yards per carry better than the next best Power 4 signal caller (South Carolina's LaNorris Sellers).
IN A CLUB ALL BY HIMSELF
•
Avery Johnson
had a historic effort against Tulane, throwing for 227 yards and rushing for 152 yards with two scores.
• He became the first player in school history, just the 14th in Big 12 history and one of only three players this season (Texas State's Brad Jackson vs. Colorado State and Utah's Devon Dampier vs. Houston) to throw for at least 200 yards and rush for at least 150 yards in a single game.
• Johnson's 379 yards of total offense against the Green Wave tied for the 15th most in school history, while his 152 rushing yards were the 10th most ever by a K-State quarterback.
THE SCHOOL'S ALL-TIME LEADER
•
Avery Johnson's
first touchdown pass of the season carried significant weight as he broke the school's career record, a mark that now stands at 58. He is the only K-State quarterback with more than 50 touchdown strikes in a career.
• Johnson ranks in the top 10 in program history in multiple career categories (see pages 59-60).
THE FIRST TO 6,500/1,500
•
Avery Johnson
is the only player in school history to throw for at least 6,500 yards and rush for 1,500 yards, and he is one of only 10 quarterbacks in Big 12 history to reach those two career milestones, including the fourth since 2016.
• He is also one of just eight active players to accomplish the feat, including two from the Big 12 in Texas Tech's Tommy Castellanos and Utah's Devon Dampier.
• Johnson is on the doorstep of reaching 7,000 passing yards and 2,000 rushing yards in his career as he needs 123 yards through the air and 160 yards on the ground to hit those marks.
• He is looking to become fourth player in Big 12 history to hit those two marks as he would join Missouri's Brad Smith (2002-05), Baylor's Robert Griffin III (2008-11) and TCU's Trevone Boykin (2012-15).
ONE OF THE NATION'S BEST TIGHT ENDS
• Senior
Garrett Oakley
is toward the top of several K-State records among tight ends, and he is one of the best active players at the position in the nation.
• The career record holder for touchdown catches by a tight end with 15, Oakley ranks third among all active tight ends in the country in career receiving scores.
• The Columbus, Nebraska, native became the fifth tight end in school history to reach 1,000 career receiving yards, hitting the mark at Colorado. He ranks ninth nationally among active tight ends in career receiving yards, and his 97 catches are tied for seventh.
TIBBS ON A ROLL
• Wide receiver
Jaron Tibbs
leads the team with 53 receptions for 536 yards and five touchdowns.
• The Indianapolis, Indiana, native has averaged 4.55 catches over his 22 games as a Wildcat to rank sixth in school history.
• Tibbs is coming off a career-high performance of 115 yards on eight catches, which included a career-long catch of 53 yards that went for a touchdown. It was his second 100-yard game of the season as he posted 114 yards against Tulane.
SALLEY JR. WITH ANOTHER 100-YARD GAME
•
Derrick Salley Jr.
joins
Jaron Tibbs
with a pair of 100-yard receiving games this season when he hit the century mark with two touchdowns on only three catches against Oklahoma State.
• His first touchdown was on a 73-yard pass from
Blake Barnett
, the Wildcats' longest pass play of the season and the eighth-longest pass play in a Big 12 game this year.
• Salley Jr. enters the week ranked 24th nationally by averaging 18.46 yards on his 24 catches this season. He is only 0.79 yard per catch shy of entering the school's top-10 list for a season.
DEFENSIVE NOTES
TOP-TIER PASS DEFENSE
• Kansas State enters the Arizona game owning the nation's 19th-ranked pass defense, allowing only 178.2 yards per game.
• The 178.2 passing yards allowed is the Wildcats' eighth-best mark in the Big 12 era (since 1996).
• K-State opened the season by allowing only 30 passing yards to Nicholls, the fewest since the 2016 Missouri State game (24 yards) and the fewest in a full game since the 2000 Ball State contest (5).
• K-State followed that up by limiting Washington State to only 65 passing yards, marking the first time since the 2000 Ball State and North Texas games the Cats allowed less than 100 passing yards in consecutive games.
BEHIND THE LINE
• The Cats are one of the nation's best teams in terms of tackles for loss, ranking 24th in the country and fifth in the Big 12 with 6.3 TFLs per game (63 total).
• It is the second time in the last three seasons the Wildcats tallied at least 63 tackles for loss through nine game (66 in 2024).
• Defensive end
Wendell Gregory
ranks 24th nationally and fourth in the Big 12 with 1.10 tackles for loss per game (11.0 total).
Jordan Allen
,
Jayden Bryant
,
Gabe Powers
,
Rex Van Wyhe
and
Darien Whitaker
Jr. have also recorded at least 5.0 tackles for loss this year.
LIMITED POINTS PER GAME
• Since the 2021 season (75 games), the Cats are allowing 23.2 points per game, which ranks third among current Big 12 teams and 25th among Power 4 teams (minimum 40 games).
• Since 2019, the Wildcats have held 55 of their 69 Big 12 opponents under their season average at the time it faces the Wildcats (excluding 2025 season opener against Iowa State).
BREAKING UP THE PASS
• Sophomore defensive back
JoJo Scott
enters the week tied for third in the Big 12 with nine pass breakups this season, a mark that ranks fifth nationally among freshmen or sophomores.
• A native of Lakeland, Florida, Scott has had seven pass breakups over a four-game stretch, knocking away two passes apiece against Houston, Kansas and Arizona State before tallying another one at Colorado.
• He was the first Wildcat with at least seven pass breakups in a four-game span since Duke Shelley had eight over the first four games of the 2018 season.
A SUCCESSFUL FIRST START
• Freshman defensive back
Nick McClellan
made his first-career start against Kansas a successful one as he recorded three tackles – two of which went for a loss of 14 yards – forced a fumble on a punt attempt that resulted in a safety and broke up a pass with 1:15 remaining as the Jayhawks were in K-State territory trying to cut into an 11-point deficit.
• A native of St. Louis, Missouri, McClellan was honored for his play against the Jayhawks as he was named the Big 12 Freshman of the Week.
IMPACT ON THE EDGE
• Defensive end
Wendell Gregory
enters the week ranked 24th in the nation and fourth in the Big 12 with 1.10 tackles for loss per game (11.0 total TFLs).
• The 2025 Big 12 Defensive Freshman of the Year, Gregory has recorded 0.96 TFLs per game over his 24-game career to rank ninth nationally among active players.
SPECIAL TEAMS NOTES
SCORING IN THE THIRD PHASE
• A big part of K-State's non-offensive touchdown success comes from special teams as the Wildcats lead the nation in total kickoff- and punt-return touchdowns since 2005 with 64.
• That mark is 24 more than the second-best teams in Alabama and San Diego State (40) and 30 more than the closest Big 12 programs, Oklahoma State and TCU (34).
• Of the 64 total returns, a nation-leading 32 are on kickoff returns, which are nine more than the second-best team (San Diego State).
19 AND COUNTING
• K-State has blocked at least one punt, field goal or extra point every year since 2008, which includes the 2026 season after
Jordan Allen
blocked a Washington State field goal attempt.
• The Wildcats were one of five FBS teams entering 2026 to have blocked at least one punt, field goal or extra point every year since 2008, joining Boise State, Indiana, Marshall and Texas A&M. K-State, Boise State and Marshall have each extended its streaks this season.
EIGHT STRAIGHT
• The Wildcats entered the 2026 season having blocked at least one punt in eight-straight seasons, which is the longest streak in the nation and the longest by K-State since at least the 1970s.
• Toledo and Michigan are second on the list by blocking a punt each of the last six years, which includes 2026.
McCLANNAN'S HIGH AVERAGE
• Punter
Simon McClannan
has been outstanding in 2026 as he ranks sixth nationally with a 47.6-yard average, a mark that is currently first in school history.
• A native of Omaha, Nebraska, McClannan posted a career-best 53.67-yard average at Colorado, which was the third-best mark in school history and the ninth-best nationally this season (minimum four attempts).
• It is not just the high average that has allowed McClannan to be one of the best punters in the nation. He is also tied for the Big 12 lead with 18 punts that have traveled at least 50 yards – including a Big 12-best five at Colorado – while he is second in the conference with 18 punts that have ended up inside the opponent 20-yard line.
• McClannan holds a 43.96-yard average over his 135 career punts, which ranks second in K-State history and 14th nationally among active players.
HITTING THE MARK
• Place kicker
Luis Rodriguez
ranks first in school history for a career with an 86.7% (26-of-30) conversion rate on field goals, just 0.3% ahead of Matthew McCrane, who held an 86.4% mark from 2014 through 2017.
• Among active kickers who have attempted at least 30 career attempts, Rodriguez ranks sixth nationally in career field goal percentage.
4-FOR-4
•
Luis Rodriguez
was successful on all four of his field goal attempts and each of his three extra point attempts in K-State's 11-point victory over Kansas as he went on to earn Big 12 Special Teams Player of the Week honors.
• His four made field goals tied for the second most in a Big 12 game this season, tied for 11th most in the nation and tied for the second most in a game in school history.
FROM DOWNTOWN
•
Luis Rodriguez
connected on a 55-yard field goal at TCU. It was a career-long field goal, tied for the sixth longest made field goal in school history and was the longest by a Wildcat since Jeff Snodgrass was true on a 57-yard field goal against North Texas in 2005.
• His long connection at TCU was the third-longest field goal made during a Big 12 game this year.
• Rodriguez, who booted a 51-yard field goal against Kansas, has two field goals of 50 or more yards this season to tie for fourth in school history. His three career makes from that distance is tied for fifth.
