‘Fired Up’ Hoosiers Ready for Final Regular Season Road Trip
Pete DiPrimio | IUHoosiers.com
BLOOMINGTON, Ind. --
Noise once again rocks Merchants Bank Field at Memorial Stadium and spills out past Simon Skjodt Assembly Hall and beyond.
Indiana is locked into preparations for Saturday’s game at Washington’s 72,000-seat Columbia Bank Stadium, including shouting and cheering fan challenges. Practice rolls on and plays are called and executed amid the noise, some better than others, and coaches push for more under a darkening sky and a bright half-moon.
Head coach
Curt Cignetti
observes it all, pacing the field, taking notes, occasionally injecting himself in for hands-on emphasis when necessary because this is a big opportunity to find the level of consistent excellence that made the Hoosiers last season’s national champion.
IU (9-1 overall, 6-1 in the Big Ten) is ranked No. 6 and No. 5 in the national polls, and No. 8 in the College Football Playoff ratings.
Everything depends on beating Washington (6-4, 3-4) and then Purdue (3-7, 2-5) at Merchants Bank Field at Memorial Stadium in the last two regular season games.
The road is a wonderful thing if you have the right attitude, the
Jamari Sharpe
attitude, and embrace the challenge.
Take, for instance, the Washington trip.
“It gets us fired up,” the veteran cornerback says. “When we’re away, it definitely gets us pumped up.”
A pumped-up IU defense is an intimidating force. The Hoosiers have forced nine turnovers in the last four games -- three interceptions last Saturday against USC, forcing and recovering a fumble against Minnesota, intercepting a pass against Michigan, and intercepting two passes and forcing and recovering two fumbles against Ohio State.
The reason, Sharpe says, is “we’re constantly putting ourselves in position for those. It’s trusting everybody to do their job; trusting the D-line to get home.”
IU’s relentless pressure on quarterbacks -- it has 32 sacks, 41 quarterback hurries and 80 tackles for loss -- means, “The ball has to come out fast half of the time because we have a nice D-line. When the quarterback drops back, we just play off the quarterback's vision.”
Washington has a mobile quarterback in Demond Williams Jr. He’s thrown for 1,955 yards and 13 touchdowns, and run for 273 yards and two TDs. Cignetti has made it clear to defensive coaches he doesn’t want to be “watching a running quarterback run all over the field against us.”
Sharpe says the Hoosiers want to contain Williams Jr. by keeping him in the pocket.
“He wants to extend plays and make plays down the field.”
That makes it difficult on defensive backs, Sharpe adds, “Because once he gets out of the pocket, we have to plaster to our receiver and make sure he’s not open. Stay on our guy.”
That’s a challenge given five Huskies have caught at least 20 passes for 235 yards and two touchdowns. Dezmen Roebuck leads with 62 catches for 565 yards and four touchdowns. Chris Lawson has 54 catches for 851 yards and five TDs.
“They have a couple of great receivers who can make plays,” he says.
Williams Jr. has thrown four interceptions. IU has 10 interceptions. What could that mean for Saturday?
“I feel, at the end of the game,” says Sharpe, who has three of those interceptions, “when the ball is in the air, it’s ours, not theirs.”
