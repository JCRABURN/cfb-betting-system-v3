K-State Previews Home Finale During Weekly Press Conference
Nov 16, 2026
|
Football

MANHATTAN, Kan.
– Kansas State head coach
Collin Klein
and select players met with the media on Monday inside the Vanier Family Football Complex to discuss the Wildcats' home finale this Saturday against No. 19 Arizona. Links to video and audio of Klein's press conference – which was streamed live on ESPN+ – are above in addition to an audio link of the player breakout interviews. A transcript of Klein's press conference is below.
COLLIN KLEIN, HEAD COACH
Opening Statement…
"Good morning, good to see you. After going through it, not all that much different than what we thought going into that game or even after coming out of it. We knew it was going to be a close game, knew it was going to be a one- or two-possession game down the stretch. I thought that, defensively, we did enough to win. I was proud of those guys and the steps that they made and that we took from a mentality standpoint, a physicality standpoint, an attention to detail on some of the run fits – holding the team under 100 yards rushing – and how we just competed through the game. We put them in a bad spot with the turnover in the first half that made points easy for them. We came out flat in the first half, offensively. We couldn't really get a rhythm, couldn't get going. We had some conversion downs and some situational football where we were not able to stay on the field. That really bit us. We were not able to establish a consistent ground game to complement and to help a young quarterback. Going into the second half, the drive right out of halftime, there were two 3rd-and-long conversions that we did give up on defense and kept the drive going. Offensively, we had a little bit of life there in the third (quarter), but we were not able to finish on a couple of drives and in a couple of situations. Some really good learning lessons in some situational football that we'll be able to carry forward and move forward with us.
"Special teams, we weren't able to get a difference-maker play. We're close a couple of different times to being able to have a potential game-changing play. We weren't able to get on the right side of it. We were not able to flip the field like we have at times this year. We didn't strike the ball overly well there to get some fields flipped. You go on the road, you turn the ball over twice – one in minus field position giving them easy points, one with points in hand in a two-possession ball game there at the end, and we rush for 58 yards. That's not a recipe to be able to win a tight ball game in this conference.
"I was proud of the guys, how they came out this morning and how we keep attacking. I've told them, and the message has been, we have to keep grinding. We have to keep improving. We have to keep pushing forward and building on some of the things that we are doing in a positive way. Some of the throws that Dillon (Duff) was able to make, some of the things we did in the RPO game, were very positive and showed signs of growth offensively. We talked about the strides on defense, and more than anything, continuing to build a competitive nature, a competitive culture that we can move forward and become everything that we want to be. That doesn't happen overnight. The mentality can't change through the course of the ups and downs, and we've had our share of downs the last four weeks That's very unfortunate and painful all at the same time.
"Big challenge this week with Arizona coming in here at home. They are very explosive on offense, elite quarterback play, talented and skilled at the wideout position. Tons of respect for Coach (Brent) Brennan and the job he's done there in the three years he's been there, and how hard his guys play, how detailed they are schematically. Defensively, they throw everything at you. Lots of different front structures, lots of different pressure structures. They thrive in creating chaos for you offensively, and give you a lot that you have to deal with. It's going to be a big challenge for us. We got to focus on ourselves and doing the things that we can to get better and play winning football."
On if the plan is to start
Dillon Duff
at quarterback again this week…
"That would be the plan as of now."
On who filled in to pass at practice while
Blake Barnett
was sick…
"Avery (Johnson) was able to take some and spin the ball around a little bit, and then Coach (Christian) Ellsworth isn't that old, so he was able to spin it around as well for us."
On if
Collin Klein
thew at practice…
"I'm older than he (Ellsworth) is, so, no."
On if he will go back to calling offensive plays this year…
"I don't know the answer to that question. I think it's one step at a time right now, like it's been the last two weeks. I've got to focus on getting a lot of the pieces around just the simple play-calling piece right, so that whoever's doing that job, we build around that."
On
Dillon Duff's
ability to compete on the road…
"I thought he showed toughness throughout the game. There were some mistakes made, things weren't going well in the first half, but it felt like he didn't waver. I felt like his approach was very consistent, and his competitive mindset was very consistent through all the ups and the downs. He was able to deliver the ball under duress a couple of different times. I think pocket presence is something that he will continue to improve on as he continues to play and face some of those live bullets and live situations. But, some of the throws he was able to make, and I thought there were some good steps forward there."
On what the defensive personnel changes bring to the team…
"Competitive nature, accountability and consistency in schematic assignment, and being great teammates. That's what those guys have earned."
On why it is so tough for first-year head coaches in the Big 12…
"We got to finish strong, first and foremost. I think every situation is different, every dynamic is different. So many twists and turns as you go on the journey of a season. I think all of those attribute to it. I said it a few weeks ago – and we've had conversations as a staff and our players going through – losing four in a row is not easy. It's hard on everybody. It's frustrating for everybody. It's unacceptable. Period. No one knows and understands the expectations of this place, the history of this place, and what we're striving to build and even raise those expectations here more than I do. Those aren't easy. That's what we're in. That's what we're focused on. How do we get that fixed right now? Every situation, there are some head coaches that have better first years. Some don't. All the reasons that go into that, it's hard to put your finger just on one or one area. We've had opportunities to overcome some of the curveballs that we've gotten and haven't been able to get on the right side of it. I've been proud of our staff and been proud of our guys of how we've hung together and continue to compete. There are things that we are improving on. We just have to put them together on both sides of the football in both halves to get where we want to go."
On the struggles in the run game…
"Last week, first of all, you have to get credit to TCU. That's, statistically, the best defense in the conference. You've got to start there. In the first half, in particular, there were a couple of times and a couple of what-ifs that were one block away here and there from really getting some explosive plays in the run game going. We weren't able to get that done. It was a little bit variable as to why, but we got to get on the right side of those inches. It's 11 guys being able to get their job executed correctly. It's our job as coaches to do a better job of making sure they're in an advantageous position and all doing that at the same time to get that going."
On discussions over playing time week by week and looking ahead to next season…
"The mentality thing, the culture thing, the accountability thing, the disciplined football and playing it how we want to play it here, those are constantly non-negotiables. That's where some of that has happened. It's hard to project like that because there are so many variables. It's no different than what we're asking our players to do, and we (coaches) have to finish and make sure that all of our focus is directed the right way to making sure that we are putting them in as good a position as we can. We continue to grow and develop, and then, yes, is there prep work and forecasting? Sure. Some of that is a little bit like the Kansas weather."
On playing younger players in game situations…
"It's a little bit back and forth. Do they fit those characteristics and metrics of who has earned and taken that playing time? If they have, then sure. But, it's also what gives us the best chance who's earned the right and continues to earn the right to give our team the best chance to win that football game. In this conference – and I told our team last week – the level of intensity, mental focus, competitive fortitude, snap in and snap out, week in and week out to win and win consistently and create consistent play in this conference is not easy. So, whoever fills that void, that's what we have to look at."
On the decisions he had to make with travel logistics on Friday…
"It was an interesting afternoon. I was first and foremost just grateful that we were able to keep everybody safe through that situation and the unknowns that were happening. We were five minutes from getting in the air, and our pilots having to fly completely on their own eyes and all of that, which I'm sure they would have done a great job, but it's a crazy deal in today's age. So, I was grateful that they handled it as safely and as wisely as they could on that end. They communicated with us. We handled it. I've never been a part of a pregame routine quite like that before, but our guys responded. They handled it as well as you could in that situation. Is it ideal? No, but we stayed together, and everyone did the best they could."
On how special Arizona quarterback Noah Fifita is…
"He's got elite arm talent. He can fit the ball into small windows. He extends plays with his arm, with his legs. He's very hard to bring down. He's very slippery. They create a lot of explosive plays. They put a lot of pressure on you and how they space the field. Coach (Seth) Doege and I played against each other and have known each other for a long time, and I respect the heck out of the job that he's done with that offense the last two years."
On how special Senior Day at K-State is...
"It's a special day. It's a culmination of a lot of hours and a lot of hard work and a lot of blood, sweat and tears and a lot of ups and downs for a lot of those guys. There will be a handful of them that have been here their entire career. In today's world, there's going to be some guys that come, and there's going to be some guys that go for different reasons and different periods of time, and that's okay. I think the K-State family is special. The culture that we are building here and has been here for a long time is unique. It's special. It's real. We want to have a higher retention rate than however the market average goes in this and as the world continues to change, because we want to find the right people that have the right mindset, that have the right makeup, that have the right value system, that appreciate that on the other side that they get here and how they're treated and how they're pushed and how they're developed. That will lead to a higher percentage there, which makes that day even more special for those guys that have invested and been a part of something bigger than themselves for an extended period of time that has greatly benefited their life while they've also contributed to greatly. So, very proud of those guys, and something that we want to be special here for a long time."
On what he remembers most about his Senior Day…
"I don't know that there was one thing in particular. I know we beat Texas, which is always a good thing. That's probably what I remember the most."
On if he remembers hoisting the Big 12 Championship trophy after his Senior Day game…
"Well, that was the result of that. I remember winning."
On if the team was able to stay on schedule on Friday despite not traveling…
"Friday stayed pretty much the same from a routine standpoint. We were probably off by about 30 minutes. We able to do everything here. That kind of functioned like normal. Saturday morning, not so much. That got really condensed just because we wanted to give them as much sleep as we could. It was wise, plus game time and trying to manage that with getting down there. So, Saturday was affected quite a bit and quite a bit different than what we would normally do."
On what
Avery Johnson
is doing at practice…
"He's active, but he's not fully practicing at all."
On what went into switching
Oliver Miller
back to his offensive line number…
"Yeah, good question. So, TCU – when you put an ogre sixth lineman on the field – they put another defensive lineman on the field. So, there was not a strategic advantage doing that like there had been in certain weeks. We had projected and wanted to get him some more snaps. It didn't happen. I see him getting some more snaps in the future still doing both roles. "
On if he admires
Sterling Lockett
for staying in one place for his entire collegiate career…
"Absolutely, and he's been a big part of our program, invested tremendously and is a big part of our leadership culture and backbone and has the last few years. I appreciate the heck out of him and everything that he's done."
On
Ben Osueke's
competitive nature at TCU…
"You said it. His competitive nature, his energy, his passion. For doing so arriving here a couple weeks into this season, I respect the heck out of that. He's earned everything he's gotten, he continues to compete and battle. That type of energy, that type of fire, that type of passion has affected our defense in a positive way."
On Arizona's defense…
"They fly around. They cause a lot of chaos. They're stemming, twisting, pressures – a lot of different looks. So, you got to be fundamentally sound. You have to find ways to quiet that down as best you can. Then, when you get opportunities, you have to be able to take them."
On what he has seen from
Avery Johnson's
mentality standpoint as he has been sidelined…
"I think this is one of the first times in his life that he's probably missed this much practice time, game time or having dealt with something like this. It's not easy on him. He's a competitor. So, he's handled it well. He's been awesome with his teammates and the offense, whether it's holding somebody accountable here, encouraging somebody there. He's asking questions in the meetings and has been engaged like he would be starting the game and helping show some of those young guys the way. So, as well as you can, obviously, while being frustrated he's not out there."
On how close the team was to bussing to and from TCU…
"It was scheduled. It was scheduled. It was kind of crazy just with how everything blew up. They're like, 'Well, we might be able to get you down there at 8:00 in the morning, but we don't know if we're going to be able to get you back,' with where the plane might be and the pilot hours and all that stuff that's over my head. So, we were a little bit jumping, saying we'll figure out the back half as we go. So, that was crazy."
