Behind Enemy Lines: Louisville
11/18/2026 2:04:00 PM
|
Football
Share:
PITTSBURGH—
Each week, as Pitt prepares for its next opponent, we'll look at what the other side is saying about the Panthers. Here's a snapshot of how the Cardinals are sizing up Pitt ahead of Saturday night's matchup in Louisville.
Preparing for Pitt's Pressure
Louisville head coach
Jeff Brohm
opened his weekly press conference by emphasizing the challenge Pitt's defense presents.
"We've got a good team coming in, outstanding on defense," Brohm said. "They have been every year. They play aggressive, like to get in the backfield, stop the run and bring a lot of heat."
Brohm said the Panthers have built their defensive identity around creating pressure and negative plays.
"That's what they've made their name on—getting after the quarterback, sacks, tackles for loss, constant blitzing and trying to play a scheme behind it that doesn't give up the big play," he said.
Louisville's offense will need to recognize and defeat those pressure packages throughout the game.
"You've got to be able to defeat their blitz and their aggressive approach," Brohm said. "Pitt has always been a tough challenge for anybody to play. Whenever you find a way to win, it's tough."
Why Pitt Is So Difficult to Run Against
Pitt enters the matchup ranked second nationally in fewest rushing yards allowed. Brohm believes the Panthers' success begins with their willingness to attack the line of scrimmage.
"They have an aggressive package that's sound, that attacks and penetrates the line of scrimmage no matter what the call is," Brohm said. "They're blitzing linebackers a lot and guys off the edge. They don't necessarily show it a lot. They'll hang in there and come."
The pressure forces quarterbacks to make quick decisions, but holding the ball in search of an explosive play brings its own risks.
"They play a scheme behind it that has vision on the quarterback and makes you get it out quick," Brohm said. "If you can hold it longer, sometimes you can create a big play, but if you hold it too long, you're going to get sacked."
Brohm said Pitt's consistency stems from a system that has remained largely unchanged.
"That package has been really good for them," he said. "It hasn't changed a whole lot because they're really good at it, and every year they're near the top in stopping the run and getting tackles for loss and quarterback pressures."
Preparing for Multiple Quarterbacks
Louisville is also preparing for uncertainty at quarterback after Pitt used multiple players at the position in recent weeks.
"Whoever is the starter, I'm sure they've got another guy as well," Brohm said. "You have to be able to adapt your defense to the brand of football that quarterback's going to play."
The Cardinals faced four quarterbacks across their previous two games, giving their defense experience adjusting to different playing styles.
"Whoever's in there, we have to have a plan for that and make sure we can execute it," Brohm said. "I think we have seen a lot of different types of quarterbacks, and I think we know which ones give us problems."
"Hopefully, we have some answers for that, no matter who's on the field."
A Scary Team When All Three Phases Connect
Brohm believes Pitt is at its most dangerous when its offense complements the pressure and disruption created by its defense.
"When they're really good is when their offense is working with them," he said. "When that happens, they're a scary football team."
Louisville will try to prevent the Panthers from controlling the game with sacks, tackles for loss and turnovers.
"We've got to find ways to stop their offense," Brohm said. "We've got to find ways to get a lead. We've got to find ways to not allow them to be super precise at creating negative plays, tackles for loss, turnovers and sacks."
The Cardinals enter their final home game riding a five-game winning streak, but Brohm said their attention remains entirely on Pitt.
"It's one game on Saturday night against a really good football team that gives a lot of teams problems," he said. "They're hard to beat, and we're going to have to have a good plan and be able to execute it."
