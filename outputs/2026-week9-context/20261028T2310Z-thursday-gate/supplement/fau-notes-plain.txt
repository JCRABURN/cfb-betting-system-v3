Conference Affi   liation and History 
Florida Atlantic University began playing football as an FCS member 
in 2001. In 2003, the Owls became the youngest team to earn an 
FBS victory (game 21), and the youngest to earn a playoff   berth. FAU 
hosted and were defeated in the semiﬁ  nal game by Colgate. 
That same year, Florida Atlantic declared its intentions to move to 
the FBS level and joined the Sun Belt Conference. The Owls would 
begin playing an FBS schedule but did not reach full status until 
2007. That same season FAU won its ﬁ  rst conference championship, 
becoming the youngest startup program to earn a bowl berth, a bowl 
that the Owls won. 
The Owls moved to Conference USA in 2013 and just ﬁ  ve seasons 
later were atop the league with a perfect regular season, a CUSA 
Championship victory  and the program’s third bowl berth. FAU 
repeated its championship run in 2019. 
2023 marked the third league for the Owls as they began to 
represent the American Athletic Conference, just 20 years after its 
ﬁ  rst championship season.

Cris Carter 
 Pro Football Hall of Famer Cris Carter joined the Florida Atlantic 
football staff   as the Executive Director of Player Engagement in 2023. 
  Carter, a Pro Football Hall of Fame 2013 Inductee, amassed 13,899 
receiving yards, 1,101 receptions, and 130 touchdowns playing 16 NFL 
seasons with the Philadelphia Eagles (1987-1989), Minnesota Vikings 
(1990-2001) and the Miami Dolphins (2002). 
  Also known for his various television appearances, Carter was a 
two-sport high school athlete with scholarships to play both, but unlike 
his brother Butch, a basketball star at both Indiana and the NBA, Cris 
elected to play football at the Ohio State University where he became 
a consensus All-American selection following his junior campaign. The 
longtime Boca Raton resident has been instrumental as a mentor, moti-
vational speaker, coach and television personality. 
MORE ON THE OWLS 
Familiarity and Impact: Newcomer and senior WR Kelby Valsin is 
poised to make an impact in the Owls’ receiver room. The 
transition from Texas Tech has been simple and aided by the fact he 
was recruited by Head Coach Zach Kittley and played for the Red 
Raiders for three years. 
Division II All-American Stands Tall: Fifth-year RB Leonard 
Farrow earned All-American status as a running back for Fairmont State. 
Shortly after his recognition, he was on an offi   cial visit in Paradise. 
Always seeking challenges for himself, he made the transition to 
Division I and has ﬂ  ourished. 
Following Well-Deﬁ  ned Paths: Senior EDGE Jay Crable saw fellow 
Ohio defensive lineman CJ Doggette Jr.’s 2025 season in Paradise 
from afar and elected to follow his former teammate for the 2026 sea-
son. Crable comes to FAU after appearing in every 2025 Bobcat game. 
He tallied 33 tackles and is expected to add something the Owls  priori-
tized in their defensive additions: someone to disrupt the quarterback. 
Crable brings 8.0 tackles for a loss of 54 yards, including 6.0 sacks for 
a loss of 49 yards, to the ﬁ  eld. Following in great footsteps is something 
Crable is accustomed to as he followed in the footsteps of his father 
who was a captain for the Michigan football team before playing for the 
New England Patriots and the New Y ork Jets. 
Well Acquainted: Redshirt senior TE Marcus Vinson joined the Owls 
after three seasons at UTEP, where current FAU tight end coach Jajuan 
Dulaney served on the coaching staff  . Dulaney moved to Paradise for 
the 2025 season as the tight end coach and Vinson moved to New 
Mexico where he saw action in one game. In January, the two were 
reunited and began working together to build a productive tight end 
room. 
It’s All About the Comics: Junior DL Gabriel Birnbaum joined his 
hometown team as a defensive lineman in 2024 and remained an Owl 
through the head coach transition. Birnbaum not only grew up in Para-
dise, it is where he developed his business model, started his company, 
rented a 4,000 square foot facility and grew his team of employees to 
18 individuals. Doing what you ask? Selling comic books. 
Son of a Coach: Zach Kittley openly credits his father, Wes, as the 
“best man I know.” If he is going to emulate someone’s career, his 
father’s is the perfect example. Wes is in his 28th season as Texas Tech’s 
men’s and women’s track and cross country coach and has coached at 
the collegiate level for more than four decades. Kittley moved to Lub-
bock after leading one of the most successful programs in the history 
of collegiate track and ﬁ  eld. While at Abilene Christian University, Kittley 
won an unprecedented 29 national track and ﬁ  eld championships, the 
most in Divisions I and II combined. Kittley won his ﬁ  rst national champi-
onship in 1985 when his women’s Wildcat squad won the Division II out-
door championship, a title he would win again in `86, `87 and `88. When 
ACU combined their programs in 1993, Kittley was selected to head 
the men’s team, as well. He wasted no time taking a ﬂ  oundering men’s 
program to an elite level, winning the Division II indoor title in his ﬁ  rst 
season. His 15-year tenure at ACU included coaching 10 Olympic quali-
ﬁ  ers, three Pan-American Games athletes, ﬁ  ve World University Games 
athletes, 16 athletes who have qualiﬁ  ed for the world championships 
and three United States national champions. In the fall of 2006, Kittley 
was honored by the Lone Star Conference for his coaching eff  orts while 
at ACU as he was named to the conference’s Hall of Honor.

Team – In the age of transition, the Owls return 64% of the team’s

Graduate QB Caden Veltkamp completed his 2025 campaign ranked 
among the nation’s top 25 in six categories, including No. 1 in comple-
tions per game (28.76), No. 3 in passing yards per game (303.4) and 
No. 6 in total off  ensive yards per game (307.4). Veltkamp’s number 
one receiver, ﬁ  fth-year WR Easton Messer, was the top receiver in the 
nation with 8.67 receptions per game. Messer ranked No. 10 nationally

II All-American Leonard Farrow. A cast of new receivers have been 
added to the off  ensive side of the ball led by senior Texas Tech transfer 
Kelby Valsin (a previous Kittley recruit).

graduate DT CJ Doggette Jr., who led the team in sacks year ago. The

than Bax from TCU and senior Jay Crable from Ohio. The linebacker

defensive backﬁ  eld is expected to be a blend of experienced returners, 
led by graduate Damarius McGhee as well as newcomers including JK 
Johnson and Tyson Rooks.

In-Season Accolades 
 Graduate QB Caden Veltkamp earned three major honors 
following his Week 2 performance, winning the American Confer-
ence Offensive Player of the Week and being selected to the Davey 
O’Brien Award Great 8 as well as a Manning Award Star of the Week. 
Veltkamp led the charge for Hagerty Family Head Football Coach 
Zach Kittley’s Owl Raid offense, completing 27 of his 32 pass at-
tempts for 386 yards and ﬁve touchdowns in the 38-30 win over RV 
Navy. 
 His ﬁve touchdowns tie the single-game program record, while 
his 84% completion rate is second-best in a game, and the 386 yards 
are now ﬁfth all-time in program history. 
 Senior DB and Shula Bowl XXIV MVP Chris Tooley III has been recog-
nized by the American Conference as a weekly honorable mention honoree 
(9/21/2026). 
 Tooley III, who has spent all four seasons of his collegiate career in 
Paradise, helped the Owls reclaim the Shula Bowl trophy in a 16-10 victory 
over FIU, the lowest scoring game and FAU’s tightest margin of victory in the 
all-time series against the Panthers. 
 Among three FAU interceptions in the ﬁrst quarter, it was Tooley III’s 
42-yard return off his takeaway that set up the Owls’ only touchdown of 
the night. He also added ﬁve tackles, including one for a loss, and forced a 
fumble. 
 Zach Kittley/Brett Dewhurst Recognized -After a statement 16-10 win 
over FIU in Shula Bowl XXIV, Hagerty Family Head Football Coach Zach Kit-
tley and Defensive Coordinator Brett Dewhurst were named by CBS Sports 
as American Conference Coaches of the Week. 
 Kittley was selected as the American’s top head coach for the second 
consecutive week while Dewhurst was named the best coordinator. 
 In the lowest scoring Shula Bowl and tightest margin of victory by the 
Owls,’ Dewhurst’s defensive unit surrendered just 10 points, least amount al-
lowed by Florida Atlantic against an FBS opponent since Nov. 12, 2022. 
 The game was bookended by the Owls’ defense logging three intercep-
tions in the ﬁrst quarter and three defensive stands in the fourth, with the ﬁnal 
coming in the red zone as time expired. Florida Atlantic’s three picks matched 
the total for the entire 2025 season. 
 Reed Chandley, a junior punter on the Florida Atlantic football team, has been named to the Ray Guy 
Award’s Ray’s 8 for Week 3, announced on Monday. 
 The eight best punters in Division I are named to the Ray’s 8 each week. This marks Chandley’s ﬁrst such 
honor of his collegiate career, coming off playing an essential role in the 16-10 victory over FIU in Shula Bowl 
XXIV. 
 In a game that was decided by defense and special teams, Chandley’s perfectly placed punt with less than 
two minutes remaining was downed at the FIU 1-yard line, requiring the Panthers to go the length off the ﬁeld to 
score a winning touchdown. The Owls’ defense was then able to stave off the potential game-winning drive in 
the red zone to return the Shula Bowl trophy to Boca Raton. 
 Chandley ﬁnished the night with four punts and an average of 48.2. Two of them went over 50 yards, includ-
ing a season-long 56, and two landed inside the 20-yard line.

In-Season Accolades 
Easton Messer, a ﬁ  fth year WR, was selected as the American’s Special Teams Player of the Week, announced by 
the league on Monday. 
 Messer earned the weekly honor after recording a 73-yard punt return touchdown in the Owls’ 45-17 win at ULM, 
a highlight reel moment that was ranked No. 3 in SportsCenter’s daily Top 10 plays. He ties the second longest punt 
return in program history, shared with Lucky Whitehead who later had a three-year NFL career. He is the ﬁ  rst player in 
the American Conference to return one for a touchdown since 2024, while becoming the ﬁ  rst Owl to run a punt back 
since LaJohntay Wester, currently with the Baltimore Ravens, in 2023. 
 This marks the third consecutive week in which an individual Owl has been recognized by the conference. 
Graduate QB Caden Veltkamp earned American Off  ensive Player of the Week following Week 2 and senior DB Chris 
Tooley III was named an honorable mention after Week 3. 
 A Paul Hornung Award watch list member in both years as an Owl, Messer is the only player in program history to 
have recorded all of a passing, rushing, receiving, and return touchdown. He is currently the FBS leader among active 
players in receptions (229) and is No. 2 in all-purpose yards (3,769) and receiving yards (2,725). 
 After a strong start to the 2026 season, Florida Atlantic football’s senior WR Kelby Valsin is one of 14 
wide receivers added to the in-season Biletnikoff   Award Watch List, announced on Wednesday. 
Kelby Valsin added to Watch List 
 The award is given annually at the end of the season to the most outstanding receiver in college 
football. Valsin earned his spot of consideration through ﬁ  ve weeks after leading the Owls with 40 
receptions, 512 yards, and ﬁ  ve touchdowns. These categories rank No. 4, No. 10, and No. 11 nationwide, 
respectively. 
 His most recent outing saw him record a career-high 129 yards and three touchdowns, all coming in 
the ﬁ  rst half of a 66-10 blowout victory over Texas Southern. The performance earned Valsin honorable 
mentions from the American Conference and the Tyler Rose Earl Campbell Award. 
 Valsin joins his teammate, ﬁ  fth-year Easton Messer, who was a preseason honoree. The duo are 
the only members of the American Conference represented on the prestigious watch list. Messer is right 
behind Valsin in receiving yards with 445, No. 20 in the nation and second in the conference. 
 As an off  ense, Florida Atlantic is putting up 37.3 points per game and are No. 17 in the FBS with 
304.4 yards per game coming through the air. Now in their bye week, the Owls hold a four-game win 
streak and are on top of the American standings. 
 Fifth-year RB Leonard Farrow has received the American Confer-
ence’s Special Teams Player of the Week honors. Additionally, senior WR 
Kelby Valsin and DL Wilky Denaud were named honorable mentions 
by the conference, announced on Monday, Oct.. 5. Farrow also was 
named to the Jet Award on Tuesday, Oct. 6. 
 The Owls (4-1) earned these recognitions fresh off   recording the 
largest margin of victory in program history, a 66-10 victory over Texas 
Southern. For the ﬁ  rst time at FAU, all three phases: off  ense, defense, 
and special teams, scored touchdowns. The trio of American honorees 
combined for six TDs. 
 Farrow’s opening kickoff   return for the 98-yard score was impressive 
on its own, tied for the second longest return in team history and making 
him only the second Owl to take a kickoff   to the house, alongside NFL 
pro Kerrith Whyte. 
 But it was his very next return six minutes later where he solidi-
ﬁ  ed his name in the NCAA record book with a 92-yard score, tying the 
single-game FBS mark and becoming the ﬁ  rst player with two kick return 
touchdowns in one game since 2023.

touchdown. Under Special Teams Coordinator Tyler Schovanec, the 
Owls have now scored both a kick and punt return touchdown in the 
same season for the ﬁ  rst time in program history.

yards and three touchdowns. All three of his scores came in the second 
quarter to give the Owls a program-record 49 points at halftime.

Head Coach Zach Kittley 
Zach Kittley was named Florida Atlantic’s 
newest Hagerty Family Head Football 
Coach on December 2, 2024, putting at 
the helm one of the brightest and most 
innovative offensive minds in college 
football. Kittley came to Boca Raton after 
three seasons as the offensive coordi-
nator and quarterbacks coach at Texas 
Tech. 
Kittley brought 12 years of Division I 
coaching experience to Florida Atlantic, 
beginning with his time as a student 
assistant and graduate assistant coach 
at Texas Tech from 2013 to 2017. His ﬁrst 
full-time coaching appointment came at 
age 26 when he was named offensive 
coordinator and quarterbacks coach at 
Houston Christian (Houston Baptist) from 
2018 to 2020. He moved to Western Ken-
tucky as the offensive coordinator and 
QB coach in 2021 and returned to his 
alma mater in 2022 at the same position. 
A highly-regarded developer of quar-
terbacks, Kittley, 35 (Aug. 14, 1991), has 
been the architect of the proliﬁc offenses 
at each of his stops while coaching the 
likes of future NFL signal-callers Patrick 
Mahomes II, Bailey Zappe, and Davis 
Webb. In his eight seasons as an offen-
sive coordinator, quarterback coach, and 
play-caller, Kittley’s teams have averaged 
456.1 yards and 34.0 points per game. 
Four of his last ﬁve seasons have pro-
duced bowl-eligible teams, beginning in 
2021 with Western Kentucky beating Ap-
palachian State, 59-38, in the Boca Raton 
Bowl at Flagler Credit Union Stadium to 
cap a 9-5 season. Texas Tech was bowl 
eligible in each of the last three years, 
with the Red Raiders beating Ole Miss in 
the Texas Bowl in 2022, beating Califor-
nia in the 2023 Independence Bowl, and 
going 8-4 this past season. 
FLORIDA ATLANTIC (2025) 
Taking on the role of not only a ﬁrst-time 
head coach, but also playcaller and 
quarterbacks coach, Kittley transformed 
Florida Atlantic’s passing game into one 
of the best in the nation. Not only did the 
team produce its ﬁrst season with over 
4,000 yards through the air in program 
history, the Owls also led the nation in 
passing yards per game at 340.2, an 
average of 22 more yards than second 
place. 
Kittley added quarterback Caden 
Veltkamp from Western Kentucky, who 
he previously recruited. Under Kittley’s 
coaching, Veltkamp led all Division I 
quarterbacks with 326 completions and 
became the ﬁrst Owl to record over 
300 passing yards per game. The “Owl 
Raid” offense set single-season records 
in passing completions (385), attempts 
(582), completion percentage (66.2), 
and broke or tied single-game records in 
completions (42), attempts (57), passing 
yards (494), passing touchdowns (ﬁve), 
and receptions by one player (15). 
Four players: Jackson Lee (LS), Easton 
Messer (WR), CJ Doggette Jr. (DL), and 
Leon Hart Jr. (LB) were named to the 
American’s All-Conference team in 2025. 
Under Kittley, the Owls are the only team 
in Division I to return their entire full-time 
coaching staff and starting quarterback 
in 2026. 
TEXAS TECH (2022-24; 2013-17) 
Kittley was named the offensive coordi-
nator and quarterback coach at his alma 
mater on December 6, 2021, returning 
to Lubbock after beginning his coaching 
career as a student assistant in 2013 and 
rising to graduate assistant and assis-
tant quarterbacks coach his ﬁnal three 
seasons. 
Working alongside former Tech head 
coach Kliff Kingsbury in the develop-
ment of Mahomes, Kittley tutored the 
future NFL MVP into becoming the 
nation’s leading passer in 2016 and an 
eventual top-10 selection by the Kansas 
City Chiefs. Future NFL draft pick Davis 
Webb, now the quarterbacks coach for 
the Denver Broncos, was also in Lubbock 
during Kittley’s ﬁrst stint. 
In Kittley’s ﬁrst season as the offensive 
coordinator in 2022, he cemented his 
reputation as a quarterback developer as 
Texas Tech’s offense managed consistent 
numbers despite numerous injuries be-
hind center. Each of the three Red Raider 
starters at quarterback threw for at least 
1,000 yards as Texas Tech became just 
the second team since 1996 to have 
three different QBs throw for at least 
1,000 yards in the same season. 
Texas Tech averaged 34.2 points per 
game in 2022 en route to an impressive 
8-5 record that included a win in the Tax-
Act Texas Bowl over Ole Miss. The Red 
Raiders led the Big 12 and ranked ninth 
nationally in passing offense in 2022, 
averaging 302.0 yards per game through 
the air as part of a unit that was in the 
top-25 nationally and second in the Big 
12 for total offense. 
With signiﬁcant injuries under center 
again in 2023, Kittley adapted his of-
fense to a more balanced attack with 
junior running back Tahj Brooks, who be-
came only the ﬁfth Red Raider in program 
history to rush for at least 1,500 yards in 
a season. Brooks, an All-Big 12 ﬁrst team 
honoree, ranked fourth in the nation in 
rushing yards to push the Red Raiders 
to another 5-4 mark in Big 12 play and a 
victory in the Radiant Technologies Inde-
pendence Bowl over Cal. 
In 2024, with a healthy Morton starting 
all 12 games at quarterback and Brooks 
amassing another 1,500 yards on the 
ground, the Red Raiders are 8-5 and 
ranked top-10 nationally in nearly every 
offensive metric, including 10th in total 
offense, eighth in scoring and ninth in 
passing. The team averaged 459.8 yards 
in total offense and scored 38.6 points 
per game to lead the Big 12 in each. Indi-
vidually, Morton was top-10 nationally in 
yards per game and touchdowns, while 
Brooks amassed 1,505 yards on the 
ground to rank third nationally in rushing 
average at 136.8 yards – the ﬁrst back-to-
back 1,500-yard rusher at Texas Tech in 
school history. 
In total, ﬁve different quarterbacks have 
started under center during Kittley’s 
38 games as offensive coordinator the 
past three seasons, including two true 
freshmen at the time in Morton and Jake 
Strong. In games that Morton started last 
season and did not leave due to injury, 
the Red Raiders were 6-1 as he garnered 
honorable mention All-Big 12 accolades 
by the conference coaches after 
throwing for 1,757 yards and 15 touch-
downs over his eight starts.

WESTERN KENTUCKY (2021) 
Behind the arm of Zappe, who followed 
Kittley from Houston Christian to WKU, 
Kittley transformed the Hilltoppers into 
one of the nation’s most explosive of-
fenses, averaging an impressive 44.2 
points per game and 535.3 yards of 
total off  ense. Western Kentucky closed 
the 2021 campaign as the FBS leader in 
passing yards per game (433.7) and ﬁ  rst 
downs (384) and ranked second for both 
scoring off  ense and total off  ense and 
then 13th in red zone off  ense after scor-
ing in 90.9 percent of their appearances 
inside the 20. 
Just the sixth quarterback in FBS history 
to record at least 5,000 passing yards 
and 50 touchdowns, Zappe was named 
the Conference USA Most Valuable 
Player as well as the winner of the Earl 
Campbell Tyler Rose Award. He was also 
a ﬁ  nalist for the prestigious Manning 
Award and a semiﬁ  nalist for both the 
Davey O’Brien and Walter Camp Player 
of the Y ear awards. His 5,967 passing 
yards and 62 passing touchdowns were 
each single-season FCS records. 
Zappe was the highest-drafted quar-
terback in WKU history when he was 
selected in fourth round of the 2021 
NFL Draft by the New England Patriots. 
Zappe has spent three years in the NFL 
with Patriots, Kansas City Chiefs and the 
Cleveland Browns. 
The year prior to Kittley’s arrival at WKU, 
the Hilltoppers went 5-7, averaging 19.0 
points and 290.3 yards per game. 
HOUSTON CHRISTIAN (2018-20) 
Kittley was hired as the off  ensive coordi-
nator and quarterbacks coach at Hous-
ton Christian in 2018 and spent three 
seasons constructing one of the top 
off  enses in the Football Championship 
Subdivision (FCS). 
In the ﬁ  rst season under Kittley, the 
Huskies doubled their off  ensive produc-
tion from the year prior, racking up 4,337 
yards of off  ense, 301 points and 41 touch-
downs – 2,975, 150 and 18, respectively, 
in 2017. 
Zappe led the nation with 35 passing 
touchdowns in 2019 as Houston Christian 
improved its record from 1-10 in 2018 to 
5-7 in 2019. He ranked second in passing 
yards per game for a Huskies off  ense 
that was third nationally in passing of-
fense with 334.3 yards per game through 
the air. Houston Christian also ranked 
second in the Southland Conference and 
12th nationally after posting 426.6 yards 
of total off  ense that season. 
During its COVID-shortened 2020 
season, Houston Christian continually 
frustrated FBS opponents as Zappe, who 
later transferred to Western Kentucky 
after Kittley’s hire, completed 65.6 per-
cent of his passes for 1,833 yards and 15 
touchdowns with only one interception 
over four games.

Florida Atlantic University 
 Florida Atlantic, with its largest campus in Boca Raton, Florida, is a 
dynamic, national public research university with campuses and sites 
strategically located along Florida’s southeast coast stretching from Fort 
Pierce to Fort Lauderdale and situated between America’s Everglades 
and the Atlantic Ocean. It is recognized as a university of ﬁrst choice for: 
•  Excellence in undergraduate education and the student experience; 
•  Comprehensive graduate education; 
•  Visionary and globally relevant research; 
•  Transformative engagement with its global communities 
 Florida Atlantic is one of only 21 institutions nationwide recognized 
by the Carnegie Classiﬁcation as both an ‘R1: Very High Research 
Spending and Doctorate Production’ university — the highest distinction 
for research excellence — and as an ‘Opportunity University’ in the new 
Student Access and Earnings category. With one of the nation’s most 
diverse student bodies, Florida Atlantic offers 170 undergraduate and 
graduate degree programs to more than 31,000 students, with over one-
third of those students being ﬁrst-generation college students. 
 Florida Atlantic is designated a Hispanic-serving institution and 
ranked as a top public university by U.S. News & World Report. To 
enhance interdisciplinary and collaborative research, Florida Atlantic 
has established four university-wide research institutes focused on the 
University’s strengths. These are dedicated to improving and address-
ing diseases of the brain, enhancing human health and quality of life, 
protecting the environment locally and globally, and using sensors to 
monitor the world around us. 
 Florida Atlantic’s 10 colleges are: the Dorothy F. Schmidt College of 
Arts and Letters, the College of Business, the College of Education, the 
College of Engineering and Computer Science, the Graduate College, 
the Harriet L. Wilkes Honors College, the Charles E. Schmidt College 
of Medicine, the Christine E. Lynn College of Nursing, the Charles E. 
Schmidt College of Science, and the College of Social Work and Criminal 
Justice. Additionally, the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute, the largest and 
most successful program of its kind in the United States, offers non-credit 
courses on a wide variety of subjects to people of retirement age.
