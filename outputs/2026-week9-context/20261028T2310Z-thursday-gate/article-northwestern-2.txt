Wildcats Propelled by Highest-Scoring Big Ten First Quarter Since 2011
Share
Twitter
Facebook
Email
By Yanyan Li, NUSports.com
EVANSTON, Ill.
– Fueld by its early execution, Northwestern (6-1, 3-1 Big Ten) clinched bowl eligibility with a victory against Rutgers (2-5, 1-3 Big Ten), earning a 42-14 victory on Saturday at Ryan Field.
Northwestern’s offensive and defensive efficiency helped build a 21-3 lead in the first quarter and a 28-6 lead at halftime. The 21-point first quarter marked NU’s highest-scoring first quarter in a Big Ten game since 2011. The fast start set the tone for one of Northwestern’s top scoring performances of the year.
“It’s huge, especially when you’ve got a home crowd and you’ve got a lot of energy, and Wildside is going crazy,” Dan and Susan Jones Family Head Football Coach
David Braun
said. “What you saw of our football team, and our offense specifically, is there was urgency, there was focus, there was execution, and it was a great way to start off the game.”
The 42 points that NU scored on Saturday are second only to the 45 points it scored against Ball State for its highest single-game total this season, and are the most it scored in a Big Ten game since beating Illinois 45-43 in November 2023.
Northwestern grabbed momentum early, scoring three touchdowns on three consecutive offensive possessions. The Wildcats' first and second drives were powered by 30 and 56-yard rushes, respectively, from running back
Caleb Komolafe
. It took just two plays for NU to score on its third drive, after
Jamaal Johnson
recovered a Rutgers fumble forced by
Michael Kilbane
at the Scarlet Knights' six-yard line to put the ‘Cats in scoring position.
Komolafe continued to make explosive plays in the second quarter, rushing for a 33-yard touchdown to put the ‘Cats up 28-3 with 1:46 left to play until halftime.
“Overall, we just stuck to our execution, stuck to our job, trusted our preparation, and just went out here and played free, played confident,” Northwestern quarterback
Aidan Chiles
said.
Meanwhile, Northwestern’s defense was eager to get the ball back to the Wildcats early on. After giving up a field goal in the opening possession, NU forced a fumble before handing the Scarlet Knights back-to-back three-and-outs, followed by another drive that ended with a punt from the Rutgers 40-yard line.
As a result, four straight offensive drives for the Scarlet Knights across the first and second quarter combined for just three total yards.
Northwestern’s early performance set the tone for the remainder of the game, where the ‘Cats scored 14 more points in the fourth quarter. Though Rutgers eventually scored a touchdown in the third quarter and intercepted Chiles for the first time in 2026, Northwestern never lost control of the game en route its larget margin of victory in Big Ten play (28-point margin) since a 43-3 win over Maryland on Oct. 23, 2020.
Komolafe, as well as wide receiver
Griffin Wilde
, were critical to establishing Northwestern’s advantage. The ‘Cats' leading back rushed for 123 yards in the first half, while Wilde caught quarterback
Aidan Chiles
’ first touchdown pass and racked up 103 receiving yards by halftime.
“My mentality is just if the ball’s up in the air, it’s got to be mine,” Wilde said. “50-50 as a wide receiver shouldn’t exist. That ball should be yours, and you have to do whatever you can to get to them.”
Chiles himself had a standout performance as well. He completed five of his first six passes as part of his overall 19-for-25 performance, finishing with 219 passing yards, 83 rushing yards, three passing touchdowns and two rushing touchdowns.
And while Northwestern started strong, Braun emphasized the need to close out just as well. He opened his postgame press conference by praising his team’s 14-point fourth-quarter performance and hammered home the desire for a “closer’s mindset.”
“I like the way that our team responded in the fourth quarter. This group still needs to find its closer mentality when you have someone on their heels,” Braun said. “You can’t take your foot off the gas, and I think we felt that and saw that in the fourth quarter. That’s something that we need to learn from and know that we have the ability to do, and make sure that it carries with us here throughout the rest of the season.”
