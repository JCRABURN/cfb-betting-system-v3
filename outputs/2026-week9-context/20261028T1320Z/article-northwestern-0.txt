October 24, 2026
No. 24 ’Cats Start Fast, Knock Off Rutgers 42-14
Share
Twitter
Facebook
Email
Box Score
EVANSTON, Ill. —
Behind its highest-scoring first quarter in Big Ten play since 2011, Northwestern knocked off Rutgers 42-14 on Saturday at Ryan Field.
With the win, the Wildcats (6-1, 3-1 Big Ten) started a season 5-0 at home for the first time since 1996. Northwestern has now secured bowl eligibility in back-to-back seasons for the first time since 2017-2018. The ’Cats have won three of their past four meetings with the Scarlet Knights (2-5, 1-3 Big Ten).
“We’re just carrying ourselves in a different way, with humility, but also a sense of confidence and belief,” Dan and Susan Jones Family Head Football Coach David Braun said. “For us to even acknowledge the bowls right now would distract us from what’s right in front of us.”
Braun is the second coach in program history to reach at least six wins in three of his first four seasons at the helm, joining Pat Fitzgerald.
“The consistency of our preparation is something that’s a reflection of great player leadership and that needs to continue,” Braun said. “It speaks to our depth and speaks to our coaching in terms of guys coaching this entire roster.”
Senior quarterback
Aidan Chiles
completed 19-of-25 passes for 219 yards and three touchdowns. He rushed for 83 yards and two touchdowns. Chiles is the only player in the country to have multiple games of at least three passing touchdowns and two rushing touchdowns this season.
Junior wide receiver
Griffin Wilde
recorded nine receptions for 133 yards and a touchdown. Wilde became the first Wildcat to log at least 500 receiving yards and five touchdowns in consecutive seasons since
Jeremy Ebert
did so in 2010 and 2011.
Pacing the ground game, redshirt junior running back
Caleb Komolafe
rushed for 150 yards and a touchdown on 13 attempts (11.5 yards per carry). He equaled his career-long on a 56-yard rush.
While the Northwestern offense surpassed 500 yards for the second time in 2026, the Wildcat defense forced three takeaways in a complementary display.
“I should probably keep this a secret, but
Tim McGarigle
is one of the best defensive minds in the country, hands down,” Braun said. “I love working with him. He’s a Wildcat through and through. He bleeds purple. We got someone special in him.”
Graduate student linebacker
Braydon Brus
posted a team-high 10 total tackles, adding a sack and two quarterback hits. Graduate student linebacker
Kobie McKinzie
, senior defensive lineman
Michael Kilbane
and redshirt sophomore linebacker
Matthew Smith
each registered a sack.
Graduate student defensive back
Montae Pate
and redshirt junior defensive back
Jalen Lewis
collected their first interceptions as Wildcats.
Chiles led the ’Cats to touchdowns on their first two drives of the opening quarter, building an early 14-3 lead. He first found Wilde for a 13-yard passing score, then Chiles followed up a Rutgers field goal with his eighth rushing score of the season.
Kilbane’s second strip sack in as many weeks set up the Wildcat offense in the red zone. Just two plays later, Chiles hit graduate student tight end
Alex Honig
for an 8-yard touchdown pass to extend the NU advantage to 21-3.
Late in the first half, Komolafe broke free for a 33-yard rushing score, helping the ’Cats build a 28-3 lead. Rutgers’ Dane Pizzaro kicked a field goal in the final seconds to cut the halftime margin to 28-6.
With eight seconds left in the third quarter, Scarlet Knight Antwan Raymond rushed in a 46-yard score to bring the game back within two possessions. Northwestern answered on its ensuing drive, with Chiles connecting with
Drew Wagner
for a 4-yard score to stretch the edge to 35-14.
A 47-yard Chiles rush on a zone read bookended a banner performance for the Wildcats, who surpassed the 40-point mark for the third time this season.
“I really liked the way our team responded in the fourth quarter,” Braun said. “This group still needs to find its closer’s mentality. When you have someone on their heels, have someone on the ropes in this league, you can’t take your foot off the gas.”
