Northwestern Defense Delivers in Win Over Rutgers
Share
Twitter
Facebook
Email
By Charlie Perlman, NUSports.com
Dan and Susan Jones Family Head Football Coach
David Braun
couldn’t keep his praise for defensive coordinator
Tim McGarigle
under wraps following Northwestern’s 42-14 win over Rutgers.
“I should probably keep this a secret, but
Tim McGarigle
is one of the best defensive minds in the country, hands down,” Braun said. “I love working with him. He's a Wildcat through and through. He bleeds purple. We got someone special in him.”
The Wildcats’ defensive success has backed up Braun’s praise. Northwestern has allowed 18 points or fewer in each of its first five home games, an opening stretch of defensive consistency the program had not matched since 1956.
Against the Scarlet Knights, Northwestern’s secondary limited Rutgers to 100 passing yards and nine completions. Graduate student defensive back
Montae Pate
and redshirt junior defensive back
Jalen Lewis
each recorded their first interceptions as Wildcats, adding to a strong performance by a defense that has relied on contributions across the unit.
“We got a lot of young guys on the team, but the coaching point is the same,” Pate said. “Your moment could be at any point.”
The secondary’s takeaways were complemented by pressure from Northwestern’s linebackers and defensive linemen. The Wildcats recorded four sacks, their most in a Big Ten game since 2023, with four different players bringing down the quarterback.
Senior defensive lineman
Michael Kilbane
delivered one of the defense’s biggest plays, recording a sack that forced a fumble recovered by Northwestern. With the performance, Kilbane became the first Wildcat since 2017 to record at least one sack and one forced fumble in consecutive games.
“I think every single week, we're just dialed in,” Kilbane said. “Coach McGarigle, Coach Kolodziej, and all our defensive staff are so good at just putting together a plan that creates strong pressures and rushes, and attack weaknesses that they may see on film of offensive lines or schemes.”
Senior wide receiver
Griffin Wilde
said the impact plays made by Kilbane and the rest of Northwestern’s defense not only halt opposing drives but also help energize the Wildcats’ offense.
Wilde contributed to Northwestern’s offensive production with 133 receiving yards, helping the Wildcats secure their largest point differential against a Big Ten opponent since 2020.
“Our defense as a whole, we have so much faith and trust in them,” Wilde said. “We feed off of them when they go out on the field and they get a three-and-out, or they go get stops. We use that energy to take it to the offense.”
As Northwestern secured bowl eligibility for the second consecutive season and did so in October for the first time since 2015, its defense has continued to deliver consistent performances under McGarigle.
For Kilbane, the defense’s success starts well before game day. The preparation and game plan provide a foundation, but he said the unit’s chemistry and enjoyment of playing together help translate that work into results.
“Coach McGarigle always says, ‘You practice how you play,’ and I think we had a really great week of practice and the coaches put together a great game plan,” Kilbane said. “When we’re just having fun and we’re living for the moments that we’re going to look back on, we all come together and we just put together a great effort.”
