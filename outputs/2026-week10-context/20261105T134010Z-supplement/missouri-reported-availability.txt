Missouri football,
again, has a clean-looking availability report to open a game week.
Mizzou published its first of four SEC-mandated availability and injury reports on Wednesday, Nov. 4, ahead of its game this coming Saturday against Texas, and added no new names to begin the week. That marks the second straight week that the
Tigers
have opened a game week without any injury concerns beyond players previously ruled out for the year.
The major injury news comes out of Austin, Texas,
as the Longhorns listed multiple starters and key players with availability designations.
On offense for Texas, starting wideouts Cam Coleman and Ryan Wingo were listed as questionable, and rotational running back Hollywood Smothers has been ruled out.
On defense for the Longhorns, star defensive end Colin Simmons is questionable. Starting nickel Graceson Littleton and cornerback Kade Phillips, who have played the fifth- and sixth-most defensive snaps for the Longhorns this season, respectively, also were questionable.
As it has since SEC play started, Mizzou ruled out running back Malae Fonoti, who is out for the season, right tackle Josh Atkins, who Eli Drinkwitz said he does not expect to be available this year, and tight end Gavin Hoffman.
Missouri's top-20 game against Texas is scheduled to kick off at 11 a.m. Saturday, Nov. 7, on Faurot Field in Columbia.

What is the availability report?
The SEC-mandated reports are required of member organizations every night beginning Wednesday in the leadup to an in-conference matchup, with the final report publishing 90 minutes before kickoff.
The possible designations, from most likely to play to least likely, are as follows: available; probable; questionable; doubtful; out.
The reports are not technically injury reports, which some teams — Mizzou often included — provided in years prior. Instead, they are lists that include any players ruled unavailable or possibly unavailable for upcoming games. That could be for any number of reasons, including but not limited to injuries.
The reports are only mandated before in-conference matchups.
Inaccurate or late reports in football are subject to fines ranging from $25,000-$100,000 and can include "accountability measures" for head coaches.
