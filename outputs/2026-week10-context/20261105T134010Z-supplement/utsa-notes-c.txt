SETTING THE SCENE
Riding a five-game winning streak and tied atop the American Conference standings,
UTSA will return from its lone idle week to face Florida Atlantic on Thursday, Nov.
5, in Boca Raton, Fla. Kickoff is set for 7:30 p.m. ET/6:30 p.m. CT at Flagler Credit
Union Stadium, and the game will be televised nationally on ESPN. The Roadrunners
ran their record to 7-1 overall and 4-0 in the league with a 42-1 9 road triumph over
Tulane on Oct. 24. The Owls suffered a 48-41 setback to North Texas on Oct. 29 to
move to 5-3 overall and 2-2 in the conference. FAU holds a slim 3-2 advantage in
the all-time series, but UTSA has won each of the past two meetings in 2023 (36-10)
and 2024 (38-24).

LAST TIME OUT
UTSA tied school records with six takeaways and six rushing touchdowns in a 42-1 9
victory over Tulane in American Conference action on Oct. 24, snapping the Green
Wave’s 10-game winning streak at Yulman Stadium. The Roadrunners recovered
four fumbles and picked off two passes in running their record to 7-1 overall while
remaining atop the league standings at 4-0. Camron Cooper, RJ Lester Jr., Zach
Morris and Elijah Newell all had fumble recoveries, while Morris and Willie Nelson
III both had interceptions. Meanwhile, Will Henderson III logged his second 100-
yard game of the season and A’Marion Peterson scored two touchdowns on the
ground to help lead a potent rushing attack that churned out 223 yards and six TDs.
Henderson went for 102 yards and a TD on 1 5 carries en route to his fifth career
100-yard outing. Peterson rushed for 5 1 yards and his two scores, while Brandon
High Jr. added 50 yards and a TD on 1 2 totes. Owen McCown passed for 1 73
yards and rushed for 20, including one score, while Mark Wiepert added a late
rushing touchdown to complete the scoring for UTSA, which converted 1 2 of 16
third downs on the night. UTSA held Tulane to 332 yards of offense, including 67
rushing. Ahamad Chapman led the defense in tackles with six, including five solo
stops, while Owen Pewee posted five tackles, three sacks, three quarterback hurries
and a pass breakup en route to American Conference Defensive Player of the Week
honors. Darion White also tallied five tackles, Jameian Buxton added four to go with
his forced fumble while playing in his hometown and Morris totaled four stops and
a PBU in addition to his two takeaways.
PEWEE HONORED BY AMERICAN CONFERENCE
UTSA linebacker Owen Pewee was named American Conference Defensive Player
of the Week on Oct. 26 for his performance in the 42-1 9 road triumph over Tulane.
He registered three sacks as part of five total tackles —all unassisted — to go with
three quarterback hurries and a pass breakup, helping lead a defense that came up
with a school record-tying six takeaways and limited the Green Wave to 332 total
yards, including only 67 on the ground. His three sacks combined for a total loss of
26 yards, as he had a 10-yard sack in the third quarter and a pair in the final frame
that went for nine and seven yards. This marks the third conference player of the
week honor for the Roadrunners this season, as Darion White was named American
Conference Defensive Player of the Week on Oct. 5 and Caile Hogan earned the
special teams award on Oct. 1 9.

UTSA O-LINE NAMED TO JOE MOORE AWARD MIDSEASON HONOR ROLL
UTSA’s offensive line was named to the Joe Moore Award Midseason Honor Roll, which recognizes
the nation’s top offensive line units. The Roadrunners are one of 25 teams selected for the midseason
honor roll for the award, which is named after Joe Moore, widely regarded as one of the best offensive
line coaches in college football history. The semifinalists for the Joe Moore Award will be announced
on Nov. 1 1. The UTSA o-line has paved the way for a balanced offensive attack that is averaging
409.3 yards per game, 1 84.9 rushing and 224.4 passing. UTSA has surpassed 500 yards of offense
twice this season with 542 in the 59-45 victory over Colorado State and 527 in the season-opening
triumph over UTRGV. The Roadrunners have racked up 200-plus rushing yards four times, headlined
by a season-high 277 in the 3 1-24 victory against South Florida.
SWEET SIXTEEN
The Roadrunners is in its 16th season of football this year. UTSA started its program from scratch and,
following a practice year in 2010, played its first season as an FCS Independent in 201 1 before
joining the Western Athletic Conference for the 201 2 campaign. UTSA moved into Conference USA
in 201 3 and became a full-fledged FBS member starting with the 201 4 season. The Roadrunners set
NCAA modern startup program records in 201 1 by drawing 56,7 43 fans to their inaugural game
against Northeastern State and by averaging 35,52 1 fans for their six home contests that season.
UTSA made its first postseason appearance at the 2016 New Mexico Bowl in its sixth season of
play. The Roadrunners made history in 202 1 by capturing their first conference championship with a
49-42 decision against WKU and capping a 1 2-2 ledger with their third bowl game. They repeated
as league champions with a 48-27 win over North Texas in 2022 and made their third straight
and fourth overall bowl appearance, ending the year with an 1 1-3 mark. In 2023, the Roadrunners
notched their first bowl victory with a 35-1 7 decision over Marshall in the Frisco Bowl to cap a 9-4
ledger that include a 7-1 mark in their debut in the American. The 2024 campaign saw UTSA win four
of its last five games, starting with a 44-36 victory over No. 25 Memphis for the program’s first-ever
top-25 win and ending with a second straight bowl victory, a 44-1 5 triumph over Coastal Carolina in
the Myrtle Beach Bowl, to finish at 7-6 overall. The Roadrunners capped a seven-win season in 2025
with a 57-20 rout of FIU in the First Responder Bowl. With the 38-34 home win against Navy on Oct.
1 7 , they now have registered at least six wins in 1 1 of the 16 seasons in program history. Following the
42-1 9 road triumph over Tulane, UTSA now has reached the seven-win plateau nine times, including
in each of the first seven years of the Jeff Traylor era.
THE TRAYLOR ERA
Seventh-year head coach Jeff Traylor has taken UTSA to new heights during his time in San Antonio.
The Roadrunners are 60-27 (.690) under his direction, the most wins among current American
Conference teams, the second most among state of Texas squads and the 1 1th most among FBS
programs during that timeframe. Traylor also has helped turn the Alamodome into one of the toughest
places for visiting teams to play, as the Roadrunners are 37-5 (.88 1) at the Alamodome since 2020.
UTSA’s 37 home victories are the most among both current American Conference members and state
of Texas teams and tied for the sixth-most home wins among FBS programs dating back to 2020.
Since Traylor took over the program, UTSA has captured a pair of conference championships, earned
six straight bowl berths with three consecutive bowl wins and made a combined 23 appearances in
the three major national polls. A 2025 inductee into both the Texas High School Coaches Association
Hall of Honor and the Texas High School Football Hall of Fame, he has coached 10 All-Americans,
three national award finalists, 97 all-conference selections and a trio of NFL Draft picks at UTSA.
UTSA IN FOURTH SEASON IN AMERICAN CONFERENCE
UTSA is in its fourth season as a member of the American Conference and seventh year under the direction of
head coach Jeff Traylor. The Roadrunners welcome back 49 members of last year’s team that capped a second
straight seven-win campaign with a 57-20 victory over FIU in the First Responder Bowl, their third consecutive
bowl win. UTSA lists 10 returning starters with six on offense, two on defense and a pair of specialists.

INTERVIEW POLICY
All interviews with UTSA student-athletes and coaches must be arranged at least
24 hours in advance by contacting the Athletics Communications Office. Telephone
interviews are available to the media on a “call-back” basis ONLY and at a time that
is convenient for the interviewee. Contact information (i.e., cell phone numbers) for
student-athletes and coaches WILL NOT be provided to the media.
MEDIA AVAILABILITY
During the season, UTSA head coach Jeff Traylor will be available during a Monday
media session. Coach Traylor and student-athletes will be available post-practice on
Tuesdays and Wednesdays. All interview requests for the week should be made by
3 p.m. on Mondays.

Practices are closed during the season. Questions pertaining to practice schedules
and specific media information regarding coverage for all other sports may be
directed to Kyle Stephens.

student-athletes will be made available for interviews.

Named UTSA’s third head coach on Dec. 9, 201 9, Jeff Traylor has had
an immediate impact on the program, university and city of San Antonio
during his tenure.
Traylor has turned around the UTSA football program by instilling
his 2 10 Triangle of Toughness culture, which has led to positive results on
and off the field. The Gilmer, Texas, native took over a team that won a
combined seven games in the two seasons prior to his arrival and has led the
Roadrunners to a 60-27 record, the most wins and best winning percentage
(.690) by a coach in program istory.
Traylor also has helped turn UTSA’s home facility — the Alamodome —
into a true homefield advantage with a 37-5 (.88 1) home record during his
tenure. Additionally, the Roadrunners are 27-1 (.964) against conference
opponents at home.
During the Traylor era, UTSA has captured a pair of conference
championships, earned six straight bowl berths with three consecutive bowl
wins and made a combined 23 appearances in the three major national
polls. He has coached 10 All-Americans, three national award finalists, 97
all-conference selections and a trio of NFL Draft picks.
That success is not
limited to just the gridiron. The
Roadrunners also have received
numerous honors for their work
in the classroom under Traylor’s

NEW ORLEANS —  UTSA tied school records with six takeaways and six rushing touchdowns in a 42-1 9 victory over Tulane
in American Conference action Saturday night, snapping the Green Wave’s 10-game winning streak at Yulman Stadium.
The Roadrunners recovered four fumbles and picked off two passes in running their record to 7-1 overall while remaining atop
the league standings at 4-0. Camron Cooper, RJ Lester Jr., Zach Morris and Elijah Newell all had fumble recoveries, while
Morris and Willie Nelson III both had interceptions.
Meanwhile, Will Henderson III logged his second 100-yard game of the season and A’Marion Peterson scored two
touchdowns on the ground to help lead a potent ground game that churned out 223 rushing yards and six TDs. Henderson
went for 102 yards and a TD on 1 5 carries en route to his fifth career 100-yard outing. Peterson rushed for 5 1 yards and his
two scores, while Brandon High Jr. added 50 yards and a TD on 1 2 totes.
Owen McCown passed for 1 73 yards and rushed for 20, including one score, while Mark Wiepert added a late rushing
touchdown to complete the scoring for UTSA, which converted 1 2 of 16 third downs on the night.
UTSA held Tulane to 332 yards of offense, including 67 rushing. Ahamad Chapman led the defense in tackles with six, including
five solo stops, while Owen Pewee posted five tackles, three sacks, three quarterback hurries and a pass breakup. Darion White
also tallied five tackles, Jameian Buxton added four to go with his forced fumble while playing in his hometown and Morris
totaled four stops and a PBU in addition to his two takeaways.
Despite the Roadrunners’ takeaway parade, it was the Green Wave who logged the first takeaway of the night, doing so on
the first play from scrimmage when Anthony Rogers picked off a pass in the flat and returned it 22 yards for a touchdown.
The Roadrunners knotted things up with an eight-play, 56-yard drive that was capped by a 1-yard TD rush by Peterson with
eight minutes left in the first quarter.
UTSA took the lead late in the opening frame when Peterson reached paydirt for the second time on a 16-yard dash. That drive
was set up by a fumble recovery by Cooper near midfield on an errant option pitch by the Tulane quarterback.
The defense rose to the occasion again early in the second, as Buxton forced a fumble that was recovered by RJ Lester Jr. in the
end zone, halting a drive that had reached the 2-yard line.
The Roadrunners pounced on a third fumble of the first half when Morris recovered one at the Tulane 46-yard line that was
forced by Eddy Smith. Eight plays later, McCown scored on a 4-yard keeper around the left end and Michael Petro’s extra
point gave UTSA a 2 1-7 advantage with 4:23 left until halftime.
Tulane pulled to within 2 1-1 3 at the break after Cooper Helmke made field goals of 38 and 33 yards, the last as time expired
in the half.
UTSA padded its lead on its first possession of the second half, driving 72 yards in seven plays, the last a 9-yard sprint to the
end zone by Henderson.
High then put an exclamation point on a 1 3-play, 63-yard march with a 5-yard touchdown rush, pushing the lead to 35-1 3
with 2:41 left in the third quarter.
Tulane used an 8 1-yard run by Johnnie Daniels on fourth down deep in their own territory to set up his own 7-yard touchdown
rush. The two-point conversion attempt failed, leaving the score at 35-1 9 with 7:37 remaining.
Wiepert’s 1-yard touchdown rush at the 2-minute mark of the fourth quarter put an end to a quick, four-play, 41-yard drive that
was set up by a 34-yard scamper from Henderson.
