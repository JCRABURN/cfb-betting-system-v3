Know Your Personnel: Florida State
November 04, 2026
|
Football, #ForBoston Files
Share:
FSU's high-velocity offense arrives for a key matchup against the Eagles.
By:
Dan Rubin, BCEagles.com contributor
When Florida State and Boston College take the turf at Alumni Stadium for Saturday's ACC matinee, the kickoff will be about teams looking to start fast and fresh, with game nine serving as a brand new opportunity for both sides.
The juncture adds layers to a game already dotting a race for bowl eligibility among the ACC's ultra-competitive ranks, but the question marks of facing the Seminoles in the aftermath of their decision to part ways with head coach Mike Norvell leaves an unexpected blank slate and rebirth of a team that's going to enter Chestnut Hill intent on restoring its pride.
"I think Mike Norvell is a great football coach," said head coach
Bill O'Brien
during Tuesday's media availability. "They went 13-0 [in 2023], and it's hard to do that. But that's the way that it is. We all know what we sign up for, and Rick Stockstill is a great coach. I've known him for a long time. I have a lot of respect for him, and we're just going to prepare for what we see on film."
FSU entered 2026 with massive internal expectations after improving to last season's 5-7 record. Graduate transfer Ashton Daniels arrived with 4,000 yards passing at both Stanford and Auburn as a one-year stabilizer for highly-touted passer Jaden O'Neal, and Lafayette transfer Dean Denobile offered a capable backup with approximately 7,000 yards and 50-plus touchdowns across three years for the FCS-level Leopards. Nehemiah Chandler gave the defensive backfield a young All-Sun Belt cornerback after he chose to leave South Alabama, and Mikai Gbayor and Quintrevion Wisner were two of the more regarded four-star portal players after leaving North Carolina and Texas, respectively.
The high-velocity scheme, though, failed to generate forward momentum despite its steps forward. The 34-17 win over New Mexico State featured 115 yards rushing from running back Ousmane Kromah while Daniels passed for 202 yards and a score, but Trey Hedden alarmingly led the Aggies with 272 yards and two scores during a game that remained within one score for much of the first half and third quarter. One week later, Kevin Jennings passed for 400 yards in SMU's 27-24 win - a number that overshadowed the 199 yards rushing or the four caused turnovers that handed the Seminole a decisive advantage in time of possession.
A 50-36 loss to Alabama then continued the bad news, and the sturdier wins over Charleston Southern and Virginia failed to generate momentum when road losses to Louisville and Miami preceded last week's 36-24 defeat against Clemson.
Even with those setbacks and the coaching change, FSU remains one of the more multi-dimensional offenses within the ACC, and the issues facing the Seminoles had little to do with their ability to put points on the scoreboard. Through their first eight games of the year, a 30.6 points per game average stemmed from an offense with 414 yards per game, and a 60-40 pass-rush attack was more than capable of creating deep balls for quarterbacks who averaged approximately 10 yards per attempt. Their spread option and direct-snap quarterback zone-reads inside of the red zone accounted for massive shares of their touchdown production, and their 15.3 yards per completion number offered a tempered stretching to defenses that dared to step into the box against Daniels and a battering ram attack spearheaded by Kromah and Wisner.
"You have to treat every week the same," said safety
Carter Davis
. "We just go in each and every week, ready to put in the work. No matter if we lost or if we won, we treat it all the same. We treat wins the same. We treat losses the same. We go and look at the film, watch it, get better, and go to work all week."
The wideouts, meanwhile, forced multiple looks against defensive backfields. While losing Duce Robinson to a season-ending injury is a major issue for any team, Jayvan Boggs and Micahi Danzy both averaged similar yardage on similar reception numbers. With Robinson now subtracted, a higher emphasis on Jasen Lopez or tight end Desirrio Riles will open more underneath routes while a heightened red zone efficiency remains one of the critical constraints on an opposing defense.
"We're just working on ourselves this week," explained Davis. "We're preparing for the game like we would with any other week. Florida State is a very talented team, so we know it's going to be a test."
Containing Daniels is unquestionably a primary area of focus, and the defensive ends and linebackers ultimately need to hold edge discipline against his direct snap designed runs. Where that might be easier to simulate when the Eagles run a similar scheme with
Mason McKenzie
, eliminating the explosive boundary plays to Boggs and Danzy is going to prove tricky, especially when the players and coaches are galvanized by the transition state of a program that's attempting to end its season on a high note despite the challenges and changes inherent to its coaching room.
"I think we need every game," said running back Bo MacCormack. "That's how we look at it. We're preparing to win the same way that we prepared to win our other games, and we're going to continue to prepare to win every week. It's definitely a must-win game because I think every game is a must-win."
BC and Florida State kick off on Saturday at 12 p.m., from Alumni Stadium in Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts. The game can be seen on ACC Network with streaming coverage available through ESPN's family of Internet and mobile device apps for cable subscribers with access to the channel.
