No. 10/11 Football’s Simmons named Lombardi Award semifinalist
11.04.2026
|
Football
Lombardi Award semifinalists
Share:
Colin Simmons is the second Longhorn in the last three years to be selected as a semifinalist for the award.
By:
Grace Tafolla
AUSTIN, Texas –
No. 10/11 Texas Football junior EDGE
Colin Simmons
was named one of 12 semifinalists for the Lombardi Award, the Lombardi Award committee announced Tuesday. In memory of coach Vince Lombardi, the Lombardi Award was established in 1970 and is presented to outstanding linemen who best epitomize his values of leadership, courage, performance, and discipline on and off the field.
Simmons, who was placed on the 2026 Lombardi Award preseason watch list, is the second Longhorn in the last three years to be selected as a semifinalist for the award. In 2024, former Longhorn left tackle Kelvin Banks Jr. took home the award in addition to the Outland Trophy. He became the fourth Longhorn to win the prestigious award, becoming the first recipient since defensive end Brian Orakpo in 2008. Defensive tackles Tony Degrate (1984) and Kenneth Sims (1981) won it in the 1980s. Nine other Longhorns have been finalists, including Justin Blalock (2006), Rodrique Wright (2005), Derrick Johnson (2004), Cory Redding (2002), Doug Dawson (1983), Steve McMichael (1979), Brad Shearer (1977), Bill Wyman (1973) and Jerry Sisemore (1972).
The Dallas native has appeared and started in six games this season. Despite missing the last two games due to injury, Simmons leads Texas with 10.5 sacks and 16.0 tackles for loss to go along with 25 total tackles (17 solo), two forced fumbles, one fumble recovery and four quarterback hurries. Simmons leads the FBS in total sacks (10.5), sacks per game (1.75), tackles for loss (16.0) and tackles for loss per game (2.67). Simmons also ranks second in the SEC and 19th nationally in forced fumbles per game (0.33).
In Texas' season opener against Texas State on Sept. 5, Simmons compiled a half tackle for a loss of two yards, a quarterback hurry and made a fumble recovery. In UT's win over No. 1/1 Ohio State on Sept. 12, Simmons had two tackles, including 2.0 tackles for a loss of nine yards and 1.0 sack for a loss of eight. Simmons followed up that performance with one sack for a loss of five yards against UTSA on Sept. 19. Simmons swept the major National Player of the Week honors, earning recognition from the Chuck Bednarik Award, Bronko Nagurski Trophy and Walter Camp Award on Sept. 29 following his record-breaking performance at No. 14/13 Tennessee. Simmons set the Texas school record for sacks in a game with 5.5 for a massive 55 combined yards lost, while also forcing a fumble and recording nine tackles (six solo) and a pressure in leading the Longhorns to a 20-17 victory at No. 14/13. With the 5.5 sacks, Simmons became the first FBS player in the last 20 seasons to have 5.0 or more sacks in a game against a ranked team. Simmons' 5.5 sacks tied the SEC single-game record, which was set by Mississippi State's Greg Favors against Ole Miss in 1996. It was just the sixth time since 1975 a Texas player has had 4.0-plus sacks, with the last being 4.5 by Alex Okafor against Oregon State in the 2012 Alamo Bowl. In the Allstate Red River Rivalry against Oklahoma on Oct. 10, Simmons posted four tackles, including 3.0 tackles for a loss of 16 yards, 2.0 sacks for a loss of 15 yards and forced a fumble. Simmons has recorded a tackle for loss in all six games this season and at least one sack in each of his last five games.
Simmons is part of a Texas defense that ranks seventh nationally in team sacks (3.25), eighth in team passing efficiency defense (106.91), 10th in total defense (273.1), 10th in defensive touchdowns (2), 12th in passing yards allowed (170.8), 16th in red zone defense (0.750), 19th in scoring defense (17.1) and 24th in rushing defense (102.4).
Up next, Texas hits the road to Columbia, Mo., to take on the No. 19/19 Missouri Tigers at 11 a.m. CT on Saturday, Nov. 7, at Faurot Field. The game will broadcast on ESPN with Joe Tessitore (play-by-play), Jesse Palmer (analyst) and Kris Budden (reporter) on the call.
